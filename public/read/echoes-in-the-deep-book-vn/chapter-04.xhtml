Silence Protocol

Cormorant, 08:12

Ambience: near-silence, scrubbers whispering, one slow pump note, a single ping ringing in headphones

Red emergency lamps. The passive array's green glow paints Lian's face. Neve sits with headphones on, eyes closed. Kofi has his back against the patched frame, tape roll in his hands.

On screen: Neve (headphones on, eyes closed), Lian (tablet turned down to nothing), Kofi (patched frame at his back, tape in hand), Rivas (at the console, pen in fist)

LIAN That's her. Halcyon. She's pinging again.

RIVAS On whose order?

LIAN Not yours. Somebody on that boat reached for the switch in the dark.

Sound: one clean bright ping through the headphones

Neve flinches, then goes very still. The ping goes out into the black, comes back, and after it comes something else. Low. Slow. Patient.

KOFI (astonished) She didn't flex. Doctor, it answered and she didn't flex.

NEVE Because we're not adding to it. It answered Halcyon with Halcyon's own sound. We put nothing in, so nothing came back at us.

NEVE Lian. What's it doing with the pattern?

Lian listens with her head tilted, then makes a small unhappy sound.

LIAN It's learning. Same as it learned ours. It's taking her ping and running it, over and over.

LIAN Two seconds between phrases when it started. Now one.

RIVAS Plain words. What does that mean?

NEVE It means Halcyon is feeding it. Every ninety seconds a clean bright ping goes down, and the thing below practises it and gets faster.

NEVE That's not a rescue signal any more. That's a metronome. And it won't stop on its own.

RIVAS Then tell them to stop.

NEVE The acoustic phone works. The trouble isn't the channel. It's the volume. The phone puts a voice into the water the way the array puts out a ping.

NEVE I called Achebe three times and stopped before I finished the word. A whisper through a megaphone is still a shout.

RIVAS You didn't tell me that.

NEVE I'm telling you now.

RIVAS You should have told me ten minutes ago.

NEVE Ten minutes ago you were counting air and I was counting what I could hear.

Rivas is quiet. Then he sets his pen down.

RIVAS Then we go over and knock on the door.

NEVE With what? Thrusters surging, frame strapped. The last time we moved we made the crack worse.

NEVE And if we go active to find her in the dark, we ring the bell again. I don't want to ring the bell again.

RIVAS So we do nothing, and listen to it ring for us.

NEVE For now. Until we find another way.

RIVAS How long since we cut the array?

LIAN Nearly two hours. It stopped tapping twice. Now it's not tapping at all.

RIVAS Stopped. Meaning what?

NEVE Meaning it listened. Then it got bored, or it got full, or it decided we weren't worth the trouble. From here those look the same.

RIVAS And if it's just resting. Gathering itself.

NEVE Then we'll hear it gather. We hear everything in this trench now. It hears nothing from us. That's the trade we made.

Cormorant, 09:30

Ambience: a steady quiet hum, the thruster note suddenly smoother

Kofi comes up backwards through the galley hatch, hands black to the wrist, a length of cable over one shoulder.

On screen: Kofi (hands black, cable over shoulder, pleased), Rivas (turning in his seat), Neve (watching him), Lian (half-smile)

KOFI Commander. The number two thruster's been surging because the controller's confused, not because the motor's dying.

KOFI It's been taking its timing off the sonar clock. Every time the array clocked, the controller hiccupped, and every time it hiccupped, the thruster burped.

KOFI I can cut the sonar clock out. Run her on her own crystal. Steady as a rock, quiet as one too.

RIVAS Do it.

KOFI And there's a second thing.

He crouches by the console and lowers his voice.

KOFI The work lamp on the belly. Own circuit, doesn't touch the array, and it's a light. Nothing down here can hear a lamp.

RIVAS So?

Kofi looks at Neve.

NEVE Halcyon's got a lamp on her sail. She's had it burning since the first hour. If it's still on, she can see ours.

KOFI And if she can see it, she can read it. Long and short. Morse, if there's a signaller awake over there.

RIVAS You want to talk to a submarine that's been pinging for two days. At four kilometres. In the dark. With a lamp.

NEVE You want to keep letting it ping. We don't have a phone we can use without feeding the thing. We do have a lamp.

NEVE The question isn't whether we can speak to Halcyon. It's how loud a whisper we can manage.

RIVAS One flash. That's my condition.

NEVE One flash, then listen. Then we decide what comes next together.

Rivas sits back. He pulls up the log.

Close-up: the ship's log on the console, two handwritten lines in two hands beneath a fresh blank one

The first line reads: RECOMMENDATION: silence protocol, active sonar suspended. N. OKONKWO. The second: ACCEPTED, responsibility the commander's. S. RIVAS.

RIVAS We did this once already. I'm not writing a fresh version of a thing I already signed.

RIVAS But a light is a new decision. Recommendation is yours, acceptance is mine, and both of us say so in the book.

NEVE Agreed. Log it the same way.

He types it out with one finger. Then he looks up, and his voice changes.

RIVAS Doctor. I've got eleven people on that boat and four on this one. I've got a commander's oath that says a rescue is loud, and your word that a rescue is quiet, and a crack in my frame that says one of you is wrong.

RIVAS You've given me a theory, a rock and a metronome. What I don't have is a reason to trust the theory over the frame.

RIVAS Tell me why the scientist in this boat is worth listening to on the day the hull goes.

The cabin is very quiet. Faint and far, Halcyon's ping goes down into the dark, ninety seconds on the dot.

NEVE I'll tell you.

Cormorant, 09:38

Ambience: a low hush, only the scrubbers turning

Neve turns to face Rivas, hands together in her lap, and makes herself say it in order, the way you log a thing.

On screen: Neve (facing Rivas, hands folded, very still), Rivas (pen down, listening), Kofi (cable forgotten on the deck), Lian (stylus still, watching Neve)

NEVE Eight years ago I was on a survey in the Southern Ocean. A towed camera near a seal colony.

NEVE There was a species we had no name for. A benthic worm, blind and pink, about the length of my hand, growing in beds on the ledge where the seals hauled out.

NEVE Harmless. Very nearly the most harmless thing in the water. It couldn't bite, couldn't sting, had no skeleton worth the name.

RIVAS Go on.

NEVE One afternoon we had a bad sonar return. Dense, moving slow, too slow for a fish, too fast for a bed.

NEVE I was young and tired, and I had the log in my hand. I had to choose whether to take the camera down into a blind current or clear the area.

NEVE I looked at the return and I saw an animal in it. Something big. Something with a shape that could take a seal.

NEVE I called it a threat. I said, get clear, get the divers out, get everyone up.

RIVAS And?

NEVE And the divers came up too fast. Two of them came up too fast. One did not come up at all.

Neve says it flatly, because flatness is the only mercy she has ever been able to give it.

NEVE The thing on the sonar was a worm bank. It had a shape, and I gave the shape a name, and I named it before I had looked at it properly.

NEVE The panic was mine. I made it out of a return I didn't understand, and it killed a man.

NEVE What I learned is what I've been saying to you all day. I will not name a thing before I have looked at it. That's my whole method. That's my whole argument.

NEVE I have looked at the thing below. It has chosen, more than once, in the only language it has, not to hurt us.

The cabin is silent. Kofi has put the cable down. Lian watches Neve with an expression that is not pity, but recognition.

RIVAS That's a hard thing.

NEVE It is.

RIVAS And you're telling me so I'll follow you.

NEVE I'm telling you so that if I'm wrong, you'll know what wrong looks like on me.

NEVE I made a decision before the looking. I won't make one again. This is the looking. This is the part of the job I'm good at. The part where I wait, and listen, and do not flinch first.

Rivas looks at the oxygen panel, and Neve watches him read it and do the arithmetic he has been doing all day.

RIVAS Twenty hours and change on this boat. Halcyon's got better than thirty.

RIVAS One flash. Belly lamp, toward Halcyon. Then we wait. If they answer, we keep going. If they don't, I go active and take my chances, and we log that too.

RIVAS Kofi. Get the lamp hot and rig a shutter I can work from here.

KOFI A shutter.

RIVAS A thing that goes open and shut. You built a coffee maker out of a sonar housing. A shutter you can do.

Kofi thinks. Then he pulls tape from his pocket, tears three long strips with his teeth, and lays them over the lamp controller in a cross, leaving a gap the width of a coin in the middle.

KOFI Not a shutter. A shadow with a hole in it. But it'll make a spot of light, and I can switch her behind it, and that's a flash.

KOFI Commander, when I say go, you count.

RIVAS Count what?

KOFI Count three.

The Marrow Deep, 08:58. Four thousand metres

The belly lamp's beam goes out low across the trench floor and finds the Halcyon's sail far off, a coal dropped in a field of snow. The debris field stands in the light like a room with the curtains pulled back.

On screen: Neve (at the glass), Rivas (hand on the lamp switch), Kofi (by the taped shadow), Lian (headphones on)

KOFI Say when.

NEVE When.

Sound: a soft click, then a low beam of light across the sediment

The beam finds the bent sail, the tow line still fast to the bow, the long white scar in the sediment, the little red marker line on the railing. Then Kofi folds the shadow over the lamp and the dark comes back like water into a footprint.

RIVAS One.

Out across the floor the Halcyon's lamp burns steady.

RIVAS Two.

Nothing. The orange eye does not move. Kofi's tape roll creaks in his hand.

RIVAS Three.

The Halcyon's lamp blinks. Once. Twice. Then a pause, then once again, slow and deliberate.

LIAN (voice cracking) They're reading us. They've got someone on the sail.

NEVE Lian. Was that a yes?

LIAN It was a yes. It was a yes and a please.

Neve watches the orange eye with her mouth dry. Thirty seconds. Forty-five. Sixty. The passive array gives her the Halcyon's ping, clean and bright, and then nothing.

Picture: Neve and Rivas at the Cormorant's viewport in red emergency light, watching the Halcyon's small orange lamp blink twice and go dark four thousand metres down, with Lian and Kofi still behind them.

The ping comes once more. Then it stops. Ninety seconds. Another ninety. No ping comes.

LIAN It's over. They've gone quiet.

Out across the debris field the orange eye blinks twice more and goes out entirely. The trench goes black, and two boats lie in the dark together.

Then, from beyond the sonar, the thing in the trench answers the last ping it was given. Low and slow. Once. The Cormorant does not shudder.

KOFI (astonished) She didn't flex. Doctor, it answered and she didn't flex.

NEVE Because we added nothing to it.

The note holds, gentle as a hand on a rail, and then nothing at all comes back.

NEVE (cautious) It's quiet.

Rivas lets out a breath through his nose and puts his hand on the console. For a moment he sits quite still. Then he reaches for the log.

Close-up: the log page, three fresh lines in Rivas's blunt hand

The lines read: 08:58, one flash, belly lamp, toward Halcyon. Halcyon answered. Halcyon sonar off 09:02. Recommendation: keep silence. Accepted by commander. 09:09, source silent. Growth check, passive.

RIVAS Get on the passive array, Doctor. I want to know if the thing out there is still where it was, or if it's gone to sleep.

NEVE It isn't asleep. But there's nothing left for it to answer. Let me see if I can prove it.

She puts the headphones on. The cold plastic bites at her ears. The trench comes up into her skull, small and dark and full of shapes. For a moment the relief goes through her like warmth.

Then she sits forward, and the warmth goes out of her.

NEVE Commander. The near edge has moved.

RIVAS Moved where?

She reads it twice before she says anything, because she promised herself that much.

NEVE Not toward us. Not toward Halcyon either.

NEVE It's thinning where it lay under Halcyon's port side. It's thickening out past the debris line, on the eastern wall. And it's thickening upward.

Lian's stylus stops dead.

NEVE It isn't sleeping, Sal. It's climbing.

RIVAS Climbing what?

NEVE The wall that stands between both boats and the way out of this trench.

The cabin is very quiet. Out in the dark the sea says nothing at all.

RIVAS So we're not finished.

NEVE No. We've stopped it answering us. What we haven't done is find out what it does when there's nothing left to answer.

Lian slowly lifts her headphones, and her face has gone the colour of the screens.

LIAN Doctor. If it's climbing to the throat, and it's still growing on the way.

LIAN What are the numbers?

NEVE (pause) I don't know. That's the trouble. I don't know what it needs.

LIAN Then I'll listen until it tells us. But I need something from you first.

NEVE What?

LIAN A note I can aim at.
