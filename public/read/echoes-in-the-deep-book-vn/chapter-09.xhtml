The Note

Cormorant, 17:41

Ambience: the low uneven labour of two thrusters, one cold pump

Red emergency lamps only. The cabin has no heat left in it, and Neve's breath shows faintly at the console. Kofi's panel hangs open with a torch taped above it.

On screen: Rivas (at the throttles, jaw set, hands white on the levers), Lian (at the passive array, headphones on), Kofi (head down in the panel, torchlight on his face), Neve (wrapped hand cradled, watching the depth)

RIVAS Sixteen hundred metres up. Two and a half k to the surface.

KOFI Number two's still weak. We climb on one and three.

RIVAS Then we climb slow.

LIAN I've got the Halcyon. Above us and to port. She's pinging.

RIVAS How many?

LIAN Four. Once a minute. Steady. Not panic.

NEVE That's a survey boat doing what it knows. Ping, wait, ping.

RIVAS Is she getting answers?

LIAN (head tipped, listening) No. The trench under us is quiet. The field's still on the floor. It's not following.

RIVAS Then let her ping. She's earned the right to be a little noisy on the way home.

NEVE That's the first time I've heard you give permission for a sound.

RIVAS Don't get used to it.

Cormorant, 18:19

Ambience: a hard shudder in the frame, then a second, then a third

The lights flicker. A loose cup rattles in its gimbal and will not settle.

On screen: Rivas (hands fighting the throttles), Kofi (head coming up from the panel), Lian (hands flattening on the array), Neve (half rising)

Sound: a deep rhythmic shudder through the hull

RIVAS Kofi.

KOFI The patch is holding. The strap's holding. It's the thrusters. Number two's losing her bite. She's surging.

NEVE The controller? The sonar clock again?

KOFI No. That's fixed and it's been fixed since morning. This is the aft battery. She gave everything to the drum and the song, and now she's remembering she's a battery.

RIVAS Can you give me full on one and three?

KOFI Ninety, and she'll climb like a bear on a ladder.

RIVAS Slow is up. How much battery for the top?

KOFI Enough for the climb and the manoeuvring. Not enough for clever.

KOFI (stopping, hand on a cable) Commander. The transducer's still mounted on the frame ring.

RIVAS Then pull the lead.

KOFI It's already switched off. It's still drawing a trickle. It's not the draw that worries me.

LIAN Kofi. What's the field doing?

KOFI There's a standing charge in the frame ring. Residual, from the note. Steel rings for a long time.

KOFI She's been humming this whole climb. Too soft for us. And it's going down the hull into the water, a very quiet, very steady note.

NEVE And the thing in the trench hears it.

KOFI It's been listening to us crawl up its throat for two hours.

LIAN It's moving.

RIVAS Which way?

LIAN Up. The whole field is lifting off the floor.

LIAN Not growing. Lifting. Like a carpet lifting when something breathes under it.

RIVAS How long have we got?

LIAN We're at three one. It's four kilometres down. If we keep these speeds it comes up under us before two thousand.

LIAN It won't even have to hurry.

RIVAS Cut the charge. Ground the frame.

KOFI I can ground her, kill the standing charge in a minute. But a sub climbing makes noise whether I like it or not. I can't make her silent without stopping her.

RIVAS Then we go loud. We beat it to the top.

LIAN You can't beat it. It doesn't have to hurry. It'll close the throat at three thousand and we'll climb into the underside of it.

KOFI With what do we fight it? A cracked window and a battery with opinions?

NEVE Nobody fights it.

Silence. The shudder softens under Rivas's eased hand.

NEVE We spent this whole dive learning one thing and I keep forgetting it because I keep being frightened.

NEVE It doesn't want to hurt us. It wants the noise to stop. Every time we were quiet, it was quiet. Every time we sang clean, it opened.

RIVAS Doctor. It's coming up to meet us.

NEVE Because we've been humming at it for two hours and it wants to know who's there. Take away the hum and it goes back to sleep.

KOFI Lovely and clean and scientific. There's one problem.

KOFI Ground the frame and the charge goes into the hull, and the hull gives it back as a bang. Big, sharp, steel. Like slapping a sleeping dog with a frying pan.

KOFI It might go back to sleep. Or it might bite the hand holding the pan. We are the hand.

NEVE Then we don't ground it. We give it a proper note. Clean, once, down at it, before it gets any higher.

RIVAS We gave it a note. It nearly killed us.

NEVE It opened the way. We got it wrong because we gave it two hulls and a crowd of overtones and called that a song.

NEVE Lian found the one it trusts. It's in the throat right now, singing it to itself. Give it that same note back, clean, and it settles.

RIVAS And if it closes on us instead?

NEVE Then it was always going to close on us. This is the better chance, and you know it is.

KOFI With what power? A clean note comes out of the active array, and the array draws more than everything else combined.

NEVE Then we switch everything off.

KOFI Pumps? The heater? Stale air is dead air with a nicer name.

NEVE Not for the whole climb. Only long enough to sing and see if it settles.

KOFI And there's the bending. Cold water bends a note down. Up here it's the right note. Four kilometres down it lands as a stranger.

LIAN Then we bend it ourselves. We sing the stretched version. The one it will hear.

KOFI (a breathless laugh) We tune the array flat. Of course we do.

KOFI Doctor, I could kiss you and I could also strangle you.

NEVE Later. Which of us pushes the button?

KOFI Not you. It's a two-handed job. There's a hot leg off the emergency cells we never routed through the panel.

KOFI Left hand throws the switch, right hand holds the contactor shut. If the surge comes back through it, it comes back through you. We should do this with a fuse.

RIVAS We don't have a fuse.

KOFI We don't have a fuse.

Cormorant, 18:31

Ambience: a cold tick of contracting metal, no heater

On screen: Neve (holding up her stiff right palm), Kofi (staring at it), Rivas (watching her), Lian (quiet at the array)

NEVE You're the only one who can read what the array's doing. Lian's the only one who can hear what comes back. Sal flies the boat when everything else is dead.

NEVE And I've got a hand that already doesn't work properly.

NEVE If something gets cooked, let it be the hand I've already lost half of.

KOFI Doctor.

RIVAS Say it for the log. Your voice. Both names.

NEVE The recommendation is mine. One note, clean, bent for the depth, aimed down the throat before the field passes three thousand.

NEVE If it goes wrong, it goes wrong because I chose it. Neve Okonkwo, senior. Sal Rivas, as commander, concurring.

RIVAS Both names. Write it after.

RIVAS Kofi. Strip her down.

KOFI Everything that isn't the pumps and the array.

He works fast: heater, galley, work lamps, spare recorder, torch charger. Each click is a small loss, and the cold comes up through the deck.

LIAN The field's at three two four and rising. Slow. Like a hand coming up through the dark looking for the edge of a table.

KOFI Where's my target?

LIAN Sing at three thousand one, straight down. It'll meet the field's song about eight hundred under us. That's where the note lands.

KOFI That's the number.

RIVAS Do it.

Cormorant, 18:44

Ambience: a single clean held tone, low and steady

The note fills the cabin, deep and even, and goes away into the trench without a quaver.

On screen: Kofi (hands on the panel), Rivas (at the console), Lian (at the array), Neve (standing ready, wrapped hand at her side)

Sound: a bang and a hiss of light from the panel

KOFI (yelping, pulling back) Surge! It's coming back up the cable.

The note falters. The depth gauge drops half a metre.

NEVE Hold it.

KOFI I can't hold it, it's running through the panel.

NEVE Then I'll hold it.

She throws the switch with her left hand and lays her burned right palm flat on the naked brass of the contactor.

Sound: a bright buzzing crackle

The note comes back, steady. Neve goes rigid from the jaw down, her hand clamped to the brass.

KOFI Neve!

RIVAS She can't let go.

KOFI She's locked on. Not without killing her, not while the current's live.

RIVAS Then kill the current.

KOFI Kill it and the array dies and the note stops and the thing in the throat hears it stop.

A pause. Then Kofi moves.

KOFI All right. All right. Doctor, I'm going to bridge the contactor with my wrench. It'll take the current off your hand.

KOFI You won't like the look of it. Try not to watch.

Neve's eyes are open and fixed on nothing. The note goes on.

KOFI It's a good wrench. It's my favourite wrench. Hold on.

Sound: a sharp crack, a smell of hot metal

The current crawls over the steel and Neve comes free all at once, and drops onto the deck.

KOFI (hand still on the contactor, voice shaking) I've got it. I've got it. Keep the note going.

NEVE (croaking, from the deck) I'm all right. Keep the note.

LIAN It's listening. The field's stopped coming up.

LIAN It's holding station. It's just listening.

RIVAS Hold the note. Until I tell you.

Cormorant, 18:57

Ambience: the tone fading, then three people breathing

On screen: Kofi (crouched by Neve with a rag off his belt), Neve (on the deck, right palm red with a bright struck line), Rivas (turning from the console), Lian (listening, hand up)

LIAN It's settling. It's going back down over the floor. It's curling up the way it does.

LIAN Commander. It's going to sleep in the trench.

RIVAS Stop the note.

Kofi lets go. The silence is enormous.

RIVAS Pumps on. Heater on. Everything we've got.

KOFI She's a flat battery and a bad mood, but she'll climb. Thrusters are yours.

Kofi winds the rag round Neve's palm, and his own hands shake, and he does not mention them.

KOFI Don't use it. Don't hold anything with it. When we're up I want a doctor to look at it, and I don't mean you.

NEVE Thank you.

KOFI Don't. Not yet. I'm still cross with you.

RIVAS Depth is two thousand. Climbing.

The Marrow Deep, 19:24

Ambience: the steady labour of one weak thruster, water passing the hull

The Halcyon appears in the lamp-light at three hundred metres, a grey shape climbing patient and slow, her sail lamp a small orange star.

On screen: Neve (wrapped hand in her lap, in the co-pilot's seat), Rivas (at the throttles, flashing the lamp), Lian (at the array), Kofi (on the deck with a mug of water)

RIVAS Achebe, Cormorant. Two flashes. Read me.

LIAN Steady blink back. She's reading.

KOFI She'll beat us up by twenty minutes. Her boat climbs faster than this old cow even wounded.

NEVE Then tell her we're coming up behind her.

Kofi goes to the lamp.

LIAN It's going down. It's over the floor now. It's settling.

LIAN (turning) Doctor. I want you to hear this. With me.

Neve comes forward and puts her good hand flat on the console.

Sound: one soft long note rising up the trench behind them

LIAN One note. Long. It's the note you gave it, given back.

NEVE It's exactly what it did on the first day. The first ping.

LIAN And it isn't demanding anything. It's just answered.

The note fades away into the water.

LIAN Silence. It's dormant. It's done.

The Marrow Deep, 19:48

Ambience: wind, a hatch opening, the slap of water on a hull

Grey Atlantic light pours down the open hatch. Wet salt air comes in like a wave.

On screen: Rivas (hands still on the controls, head down), Kofi (laughing, arms thrown up), Lian (grinning, headphones off), Neve (standing, log against her chest with her good hand)

Sound: the hatch clanging open

KOFI We nearly came up under her bow! Twenty metres! Twenty!

RIVAS (not lifting his head) Twenty minutes of air. Most of it in the pipes. We'd have had the rest in the top of the boat before we surfaced.

NEVE We had thirty-six hours. That was the whole number.

RIVAS I know.

He lifts his head. His eyes are red.

RIVAS Write it down before you sit. All of it. Every name in it.

NEVE I'll write it.

RIVAS And Neve. The next time you tell me something about the deep, I'm going to remember that you held a live contactor in your hand and sang.

RIVAS And I'm going to believe you.

NEVE It was never the enemy, Sal. It was only ever frightened.

RIVAS I know. Write that down too.

Wind comes down the hatch and lifts the papers on the chart table. Alongside, the Halcyon's sail turns slowly in the light.

Close-up: Neve's log, open on the chart table, a line drying in fresh ink

It reads: 18:44. SUSTAINED NOTE, ARRAY TUNED FLAT, AIMED AT THROAT. LIVE CONTACTOR HELD BY HAND. NEAREST CALL OF THE DIVE. MY CALL, MY NAME FIRST. FIELD DORMANT 18:57. BOTH BOATS UP.

NEVE Lian. When we're alongside, take the array off and bring the recordings up in your own hands.

LIAN All two hundred hours of them?

NEVE All of it. Someone up there is going to want proof, and I want it to arrive with a witness still holding it.

LIAN And what will you do?

NEVE I'm going to sit down. And then I'm going to write down what the Halcyon's lamp did at every hour, and hope my hand stops shaking long enough to hold a pen.

The Halcyon's crew begin to appear on her deck, small against the grey, and one of them lifts an arm and waves.
