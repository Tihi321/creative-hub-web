Rockfall

Cormorant, 07:05

Ambience: a near-total hush, only the tick of cooling metal and the faint hiss of air

The cabin is dark except for one red clock face on the console. It stares, and everything around it goes darker. Kofi sits with his palm flat on the seam above the viewport. Lian has her headphones on, eyes closed, lips apart. Nobody has spoken in some while.

On screen: Rivas (rigid at the throttles, cap pulled low), Neve (still, watching the clock), Kofi (palm on the hull), Lian (headphones on, eyes closed)

RIVAS (whispering) Somebody say something. Whisper it. Anything. I can hear my own pulse and I do not like the company.

KOFI (whispering) Crack's stable. Surface crack in the resin, not the glass. Hasn't spread in forty minutes.

KOFI That's the hull telling us she's still got opinions. She's not panicking.

RIVAS The humming. Lian. What was it?

Lian takes her headphones off and sets them round her neck.

LIAN Three notes. The same three the Halcyon's ping came back with, only lower and slower. Like the echo decided to answer in its own voice.

LIAN (pause) It's still there. Quiet now. And when we cut the sonar it stopped moving toward us.

RIVAS So the doctor was right.

NEVE The doctor was right about the first thing. The second thing is that we can't do a rescue in total silence.

NEVE We need sonar to see the rockfall, and lamps to hook a line. I want to know how much noise we can afford before it comes back.

RIVAS Then give me a number, Doctor. Something I can write in the log. Not a feeling.

NEVE Twenty minutes of the passive array. And a look at that debris field.

NEVE A rockfall fell from somewhere. I want to see what's still up there, waiting to come down.

The Marrow Deep, over the trench floor at 07:24

Ambience: the steady thrum of thrusters at low power

The lamps come up one at a time to a quarter power, pale and careful. Dust hangs in the beams. Beyond them the slab lies across the Halcyon's forward hull, the colour of old bone, its broken end propped on a ridge of rubble.

On screen: Rivas (hands on the console), Lian (at sonar, headphones on), Kofi (beside the equipment locker), Neve (at the viewport)

RIVAS Lian, passive only. Count every moving thing between here and that slab.

RIVAS Kofi, the tow line. Doctor, you're on the debris with me.

NEVE I'm always on the debris with you.

RIVAS Achebe, this is Cormorant. Keep your people off the hull and your thrusters cold. And Captain, I need you to stop pinging.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (careful) Say again?

RIVAS Cut your sonar. All of it. We've had a response we don't like. Passive listening is fine. Sending out a ping is not.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (a pause, then flatly) Commander, we've pinged every ninety seconds for eleven hours. It's the only way anyone finds us.

RIVAS We found you. Something else found us. Cut the pings.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (longer pause) Ninety seconds from now. And if it gets strange out there, I want to hear about it.

RIVAS It's already strange.

Neve has her face close to the glass, cold against her cheek, reading the debris the way she reads a tide pool.

NEVE Commander. The slope's moving.

RIVAS Moving how?

NEVE Not fast. But the dust is fresh. A film of it over the Halcyon's upper surfaces and nowhere else. It came down recently and gently, a slow rain out of still water.

NEVE And look at the ridge under the slab's broken end. The stones are sorted. Big at the bottom, small riding on top.

NEVE That's not how a rockfall lands. That's how a rockfall lands and then keeps shifting.

RIVAS In English, Doctor.

NEVE The slab is propped on a pile that's still settling. It could come down on its own, any time. Or the moment we put a load on it.

NEVE Or the whole eastern shelf lets go and posts us all some more rock.

KOFI Hallelujah. What do we do about it?

NEVE Move fast and move gentle. Make the line fast to her bow and pull at an angle that lifts her forward end. Then what's under her midships is a slide, not a drag.

RIVAS The angle'll be shallow. We can't lift much.

NEVE Then we don't lift much. We lift enough that she can skate backward on her own thrusters.

NEVE (looking up at the fresh white scar on the wall) We have until that ridge finishes sorting itself. After that, we don't have at all.

Cormorant, bow to bow with Halcyon, 07:41

Ambience: the tow winch ticking over, the hull creaking under load

Through the viewport the Halcyon's bow fills the lamps: scratched paint, riveted steel, a work lamp taped with orange cloth that flutters in the current like a small bright flag.

On screen: Kofi (reeling out the last of the cable), Rivas (at the winch control), Neve (at the glass), Lian (at sonar)

KOFI Here's the thing about braided tow cable. Everyone thinks steel is the strong part. It isn't.

KOFI The weave is. You pull a braid and every strand grabs every other strand, and the whole thing gets stubborn.

KOFI A stubborn rope is what you want when you're dragging a submarine off a rock.

He clips the seaward end to the bow hardpoint, checks the shackle, checks it again, then a third time in a way that is not checking.

KOFI There. My masterpiece. If anyone asks who made it, tell them me. And tell them I was very brave.

LIAN You were on the floor the whole time.

KOFI Emotionally brave.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE Cormorant, Halcyon. I can see your line. I'll send a swimmer out.

RIVAS Negative. We're not putting a person outside. We'll shoot a messenger line across and you winch it in.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE My seabay's flooded, Commander. The hardpoint's outside and I've no remote winch.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (pause) Someone has to put their hands on it. I've asked. I have three volunteers and a fourth who didn't volunteer but is already in his suit.

Rivas looks at Neve. She looks back. Something passes between them that is not about the sea.

RIVAS One swimmer. Tethered. The moment that ridge so much as sighs, he's back inside.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE Understood.

The Halcyon's forward hatch opens. A figure in a hard suit comes out with a helmet lamp, trailing a tether, one hand on the steel at all times. He takes the messenger line, clips it to the hardpoint, and gives a jaunty thumbs-up.

KOFI Line's fair.

Kofi reaches for the winch control. The floor moves.

Sound: a low grinding, distant at first, then close

The sediment lifts in a wide pale breath. The boulders in the ridge shift and grate. Then the slab dips, and stones come down in a slow brown river that takes the light and eats it.

LIAN Swimmer's clear! He's at the hatch!

The Cormorant speaks. Not a groan this time. A crack, sharp and bright, like a gunshot in a small room.

KOFI (already moving, reaching for tape) New fissure! Starboard midships, above the ballast tank.

KOFI Commander, that's not the seal, that's the frame. A weld line's opened. I've got a pressure rise.

RIVAS Is the line made fast?

KOFI Made fast. But I need ninety seconds to patch—

RIVAS You've got forty. Grab the tape.

NEVE The slab's coming down. If it lands, it takes Halcyon's bow with it. We pull now.

KOFI We pull now and I tear my line through a broken weld. Which is worse.

RIVAS Then patch and pull. Doctor, how long?

Neve presses her fingertips to the glass and does the sum with her eye, and then decides not to say it aloud.

NEVE Less than a minute. Sal. It's going.

RIVAS Kofi, now.

KOFI Now.

He tears the backing off a strip and presses it across the fissure with both palms, slaps a second crossways, a third over that, then puts his ear to the hull and holds up one finger.

KOFI It's held. Go.

Rivas draws the winch in hand over hand. The line comes out taut and dripping, and the Cormorant leans against it with all her weight. The Halcyon shudders.

Sound: a deep clang through the hull as the slab drops

The slab drops hard. The Halcyon comes backward all at once, her bow digging a long white scar in the sediment, slewing, and then she is clear.

Picture: The Halcyon sliding free of the slab in a bloom of pale sediment, her bow carving a white furrow while the Cormorant's lamps catch the falling dust, four kilometres down in the Marrow Deep.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (loud) We're clear, we're clear, we're clear—

RIVAS (a short, disbelieving laugh) That was clean. Kofi, that line of yours—

KOFI I made that line. Somebody write it down.

The Cormorant's thrusters stutter. The sub yaws to port. A console light goes from green to amber and stays there.

RIVAS Thrusters are surging. Both sides. Intermittent draw. Kofi, what's eating my power?

KOFI Patch is holding, but the frame's flexing, and the line's still taut. She's loading our hull every time the motors catch.

KOFI We can't hold her like this. We need to slack off.

RIVAS If I slack off now, the floor under that slab is still falling. I'm not putting her back under it.

KOFI Then back us out. Astern, slow, give the line a long curve and get us clear of the slope.

Rivas doesn't answer. He looks at the amber light, then at the oxygen panel, and Neve watches him begin to count.

RIVAS Halcyon's air. Call it thirty-five of her thirty-six. Ours, twenty-two.

He taps the panel once with a flat nail, as if the number might flinch and give him back a better one.

RIVAS Twenty-two hours. Put it in the log where everyone can see it, because it changes every hour we sit here.

KOFI Twenty-two hours.

RIVAS And if those thrusters stop, we've got less. Then we're not a rescue sub, we're a second wreck.

RIVAS Back us out. Slow. Kofi, fix that stutter with what's on this boat.

KOFI Yes, Commander. But I need to say the thing I always say. I'm very good at this, and it's still going to take an hour.

RIVAS Take forty minutes. We'll need the other twenty.

Cormorant, drifting astern of Halcyon, 07:58

Ambience: the whisper of the scrubbers, the tick of a frame cooling, one soft stutter of the motors

The tow line lifts off the floor in a long slow curve. Through the viewport the Halcyon shrinks to a grey shape and then to a single work lamp. Neve leaves the glass and stands at Lian's shoulder.

On screen: Neve (one hand on Lian's chair), Lian (headphones pressed to both ears, face pale), Rivas (counting under his breath), Kofi (already at the engine bay hatch)

NEVE The echo. The way it answered before. Don't tell me what you hope. Tell me what the array says.

LIAN It's back. It's closer. And it's got more to it than three notes now.

NEVE More how?

LIAN It's patterned. It's not just answering. It's like a hand tapping a table. Tapping out a song it wants you to hear.

She turns her head, eyes wide in the red light.

LIAN Doctor. It's been waiting for us to stop making noise so it could start.

Close-up: the passive array's screen, two water traces side by side: three smooth crests, then a dense comb of small repeating teeth

RIVAS (without turning round) Twenty-one fifty. Twenty-one forty-nine.

KOFI (from the bay, muffled) Boss, I can hear it too. Through the frame. That's not the pumps. That's out there.

LIAN (barely aloud) It's not done. It's adding something. A slow part under the fast part, like a second hand on the same drum.
