The Answer

Cormorant, 08:14

Ambience: the whisper of the scrubbers, the faint high note of the pumps turning over, a frame creaking once and going quiet

The cabin is dark except for one small glow on the oxygen panel and the green wash of the passive array. Kofi lies on his back under the starboard frame with a magnetic work lamp above him. The patch is a pale band of resin and crosswise tape. Rivas sits at the throttles with his hands flat, not flying, just resting.

On screen: Kofi (on his back, work lamp above him, tape roll in hand), Neve (standing by the console, arms folded), Rivas (at the pilot's seat, hands flat on the console), Lian (at sonar, one headphone on)

KOFI Last roll. Second patch crossways. Frame strapped to the equipment rack with webbing. Put it in the record that this is the ugliest thing I have ever built.

KOFI I built a coffee maker out of a sonar housing once. That was prettier.

NEVE Four hours?

KOFI Four hours of hard work. Six of going gentle. The frame can't flex past the strap, so the choice is yours. Say it out loud, please.

RIVAS Then we go gentle. Decision's made. Put the tape away.

KOFI (sitting up) Aw. I had a whole speech.

RIVAS I heard the speech in the hangar before we launched. It was better then.

He turns from the console and looks at Neve. The dryness goes out of his voice.

RIVAS We go quiet, and we go slow. Half speed on the thrusters, no lamps in the water. And if the passive array gives me anything I don't like, I say so out loud and we argue again.

NEVE That's all I want.

RIVAS I'm not going to say the word. Not because I think you're wrong. Because I don't like a decision that costs me nothing to make.

RIVAS If it comes apart, it comes apart on my name and not on my silence. Both our names in the same line.

NEVE Both our names.

Cormorant, 08:40

Ambience: almost pure silence, a stylus ticking on a tablet, the soft slow tap of a copied phrase through headphones

Neve sits on the deck with her back against the console and a tablet on her knees. Lian has her headphones round her neck and a screen propped where she can see it. Rivas is awake at the throttles. Kofi is asleep against the equipment rack with his bandana over his eyes.

On screen: Neve (sleeves rolled, tired, tablet on knees), Lian (headphones round neck, stylus in hand), Rivas (awake at the throttles), Kofi (asleep, bandana over his eyes)

LIAN I'm going to play it. Softly. Is that all right?

RIVAS It's a recording. It can't hear us through a recording.

LIAN I know. I still wanted to ask.

She touches her screen. The cabin fills with a low rounded thrum, the Lattice filtered down to a quarter volume.

Sound: a soft, slow, three-note thrum repeating on the same slow interval

Sound: the same thrum again, closer and lower, a beat longer than before

KOFI (without moving his bandana) I'm asleep, and I have opinions. That's the boat's own idling heartbeat. She makes that with her pumps all night and never once cracks herself in half.

RIVAS Kofi.

KOFI Going back to sleep, Commander.

LIAN It isn't one note. Listen. There are three in it at once, and they fit. Play them apart and they're just sounds. Together they belong to each other.

NEVE A harmonic set. How did it get a harmonic set?

LIAN It got it from us. Second hour, before we backed off. Rivas fired one ping. I've still got the tremor through the frame when the answer came back.

LIAN The crack didn't open on the loud part. It opened on the third overtone, right where the frame's own resonance sits. Our ping and its answer landed on the same note.

RIVAS You're saying it aimed at us.

LIAN I'm saying it answered in the key we gave it. That's not aiming. That's a duet, Commander. It's agreeing.

RIVAS Or it found our weakest frequency and leaned on it.

LIAN Both readings fit. I've said that already.

NEVE Either way, the answer's the same. If it copies what we give it, then what we give it matters more than what we say.

Cormorant, 09:05

Ambience: the quiet tick of the hull cooling, one long creak from the strapped frame, then nothing

Neve reads the log page twice before she speaks. The two handwritten lines are still there, her recommendation and his acceptance. She turns the tablet round so Rivas can see them.

On screen: Neve (tablet turned round, one finger on the page), Rivas (pen in fist), Lian (watching), Kofi (awake now, bandana round his neck)

NEVE You said both our names in the same line. This line stops at the frames. It says what we did, not what we're about to do.

NEVE If we're quiet for the rest of this dive, then the quiet is the plan, and the plan needs an entry. A number we can defend when somebody in a warm room asks why we stopped shouting.

RIVAS The recommendation is yours. The acceptance is mine. That's the arrangement.

NEVE And the next entry is both. Half speed, no lamps in the water, and one sentence that explains why the biologist thinks the trench is listening.

RIVAS You want to write the reason in your own words, where the board can read it.

NEVE I want to write it in a way that helps, yes.

Rivas looks at the oxygen panel, then at the tablet, then at her.

RIVAS Write it. Then read it to me, and I'll say yes or no, and whichever it is, it goes in the book under both our names.

NEVE Agreed.

She writes with the stylus, slow and careful, crossing out nothing.

Close-up: the ship's log page on the tablet, a third line in Neve's small precise hand beneath Rivas's blunt one, with a space left at the end for a signature

The line reads: 09:02, recommendation: remaining dive conducted quiet, half speed, no lamps in the water, on evidence that the organism copies and returns our sound, and grows louder only when we do. N. OKONKWO.

NEVE That's the reason. That's all of the reason.

RIVAS It's honest. It's also a sentence that gets read in a room I'm not in.

NEVE Then you should read it twice.

Rivas reads it twice. Then he takes the stylus and signs his name under it, one line, no flourish.

RIVAS There. Now the quiet is ours, not just yours.

KOFI (quietly) Twenty-one hours. In this boat.

The number goes round the cabin and settles.

RIVAS Halcyon's got thirty-five and eleven people breathing it. I don't intend to be the reason that number gets smaller.

Cormorant, 09:22

Ambience: a hush, the scrubbers, and then a soft slow thrum that is not from the recording

Kofi sits cross-legged by the equipment rack with a screwdriver and a spool of speaker wire. He is smiling in a way that means trouble. Lian has her headphones on and one hand raised for quiet. The thrum outside is slower than the recording was.

On screen: Kofi (cross-legged, screwdriver and wire, grinning), Neve (crouched beside him), Rivas (turned in his seat), Lian (headphones on, one hand up)

KOFI Here's my stupid idea, and it's beautiful. The acoustic phone is a whisper. The array is a shout. But we've got a third mouth on this boat.

KOFI The dome speaker in the forward bulkhead. The one that makes the alarm noise. I can drive it at a volume you'd have to lean in to hear.

RIVAS You want to put a loudspeaker in the water.

KOFI I want to put a very quiet loudspeaker in the water. A hum through the hull. Nothing a whale would turn its head at.

NEVE Sal. If it copies us, we can copy it. Give it back its own harmonic set, even softer than it gave it to us, and see what it does.

RIVAS Or give it a new noise it's never heard and watch it come looking.

NEVE Then we start with what it already knows. Three notes it has heard from this boat all night. If the answer is louder, we stop. If the answer is quieter, we've learned something, and it's learned something too.

RIVAS Who turns it off? Say it in one word.

LIAN Me. I'm on passive. I hear the reply before anyone hears it in the floor.

RIVAS Doctor?

NEVE Lian calls it off. Any doubt, any thickening, she stops it, no argument, no asking twice.

RIVAS Kofi. How long to rig your quiet mouth?

KOFI Forty minutes of going gentle. Twenty if I go gentle and swear.

RIVAS (pause) Go gentle. We'll need the other twenty.

Neve looks at the black viewport, and the deep looks back with enormous interest.

NEVE Then we give it something it already knows. A song it has heard before, from us, and liked enough to learn.

LIAN (barely aloud) Thirty seconds.

RIVAS (quietly counting) Twenty-one oh five. Twenty-one oh four.

KOFI Then hold still. I'm going to teach a submarine to hum.
