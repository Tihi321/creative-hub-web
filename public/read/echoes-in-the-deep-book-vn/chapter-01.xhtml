The Descent

Cormorant, on the surface at dawn — 05:48

Ambience: the dry hum of a pressurised cabin and a distant pump

The cabin is cramped and grey. Green and amber instrument screens are the only light. Beyond the small observation viewport there is black water and nothing else.

On screen: Rivas (grey flight suit, cap low, hands on the console), Neve (navy jumpsuit, watching the screens), Kofi (orange coveralls, bandana, tools strapped to him), Lian (green sonar jacket, headphones round her neck)

RIVAS Fifty metres. Diving. Doctor, you can say goodbye to the daylight.

NEVE I'll say goodbye at two hundred. Light doesn't reach far here.

Sound: the hull creaks as pressure settles

RIVAS Two hundred. Pressure's climbing. Kofi, talk to me.

KOFI Hull's happy. Stress nominal. That's engineer for nothing has exploded yet.

KOFI (grinning) Give it time.

RIVAS Comforting. Three hundred. I've been to this depth eleven times and it never gets less strange.

RIVAS Going down here, the water stops being water and starts being a wall.

NEVE How's our patient?

LIAN Halcyon is holding. Still pinging. Every ninety seconds, regular as a heartbeat.

NEVE Good. That means they have power and someone's thinking clearly.

LIAN It also means they're scared. Same interval every time. Someone set a timer and is watching it.

RIVAS I'll take scared. Scared crews keep their helmets on.

LIAN (quietly, almost to herself) Or they run out of air on schedule.

Neve watches Lian, then turns back to the viewport seal.

RIVAS Six hundred. Lian, give me a course correction for the shelf. I want to come in wide of the wall.

LIAN Ten degrees port, then hold. There's a spur at eleven hundred that'll shadow the thrusters if we hug the rock.

RIVAS Copy.

KOFI She's a good girl, the Cormorant. A little stiff in the starboard flank, but she's holding.

KOFI I've got strain gauges on every frame ring. If she sings out of key, I'll hear it.

LIAN We'll all hear it.

NEVE Something wrong?

LIAN The trench wall should be layered sediment and basalt, and mostly it is. But there's a return that doesn't fit.

NEVE What kind of return?

LIAN A soft one. Like sonar hitting something that isn't quite solid. Could be a thermocline. Cold water bends sound. I'll keep listening.

RIVAS One thousand. Midnight zone now. No light at all down here.

NEVE That's very restful, Commander.

RIVAS I aim to soothe.

The Cormorant shudders, a small hiccup in the thrusters.

KOFI (head up) That wasn't me.

RIVAS Current. There's a cold waterfall dropping down the eastern side. It'll push us around a little.

KOFI It's a four-kilometre waterfall. Forgive me if I'm not comforted.

RIVAS Two thousand. Halfway. Air's good, cryo's good. Breathe normally, or breathe like you're being charged by the hour.

Neve's reflection hangs faintly in the inner pane as she looks at the seal.

NEVE (softly) A single ping can cross a valley down here and come back changed.

RIVAS Two thousand five hundred. Kofi, read on the frame.

KOFI Starboard flange is running warmer than I'd like. Not hot. Like a kettle you haven't switched on yet.

RIVAS Three thousand. Three-two.

The Cormorant groans again, and the sound sinks into the background and stays there.

NEVE Rivas, the Halcyon's pinned under a rockfall. We don't know the angle, we don't know the load, and we don't know what the debris is sitting on.

NEVE If the slope is loose, our thrusters could bring the whole face down on both of us.

RIVAS That's the cheerful version. Give me the other.

NEVE The other is that we have one hour to learn the ground. I'd like to spend it on science, not luck.

RIVAS (a small, not-quite laugh) You do the arithmetic I don't want to hear and make it sound like a lecture. Lights on.

The lamps come up. Black water turns a hard, cold blue, and the trench floor reaches out and gives up in the dark.

The Marrow Deep, over the trench floor at 06:32 — four thousand metres

Ambience: the deep background thrum of the hull under pressure

Beyond the glass is pitch-black water and pale sediment that the lamps light in narrow bands. Dust hangs in the beam like slow snow.

On screen: Rivas (checking gauges), Neve (leaning toward the glass), Kofi (squinting at a stress readout), Lian (headphones on)

RIVAS Four thousand metres. Welcome to the bottom of the world.

RIVAS Lian, where's our heartbeat?

LIAN Off to port, six hundred metres. Still on her ninety-second timer.

Lian routes it through the cabin speaker: a clean bright tone, then a long fall of echo.

LIAN Hear that? That's her.

RIVAS And the rockfall?

LIAN Big. She's under a slab. I can see the edge of it in the return. They're not going anywhere without help.

NEVE Look at that. A fish with a lure on a stalk. Patient as a fisherman.

KOFI Focus, Doctor. Boring is good. I'm rooting for boring.

RIVAS Halcyon, Cormorant. We have you visual. Do you read?

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (through the speaker, tired and steady) Cormorant, Halcyon. We read you. Are we glad to see you.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE This is Captain Achebe. Eleven aboard. Hull's holding, but we're pinned. We've tried reverse thrusters. The slab doesn't move.

RIVAS We'll come alongside and look at your tow points. Don't do anything energetic. We've got time.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE Thirty-six hours of it. We've been counting.

RIVAS So have we. Kofi, full stress read before we get closer. I don't like the way the debris is stacked.

KOFI On it.

Kofi moves down the rear bulkhead with a torch held in his teeth. Neve watches the sonar screen instead.

NEVE Lian. Play the last ping back. The one after.

Lian runs the recording. Clean tone. Echo. Then a soft pulse, at a regular interval.

LIAN That's not geology. Geology doesn't keep time.

RIVAS What is it, then?

LIAN I don't know. But it's close, it's getting closer, and it's wide. Like a whole cathedral wall of sound coming back at once.

CAPTAIN ACHEBE (voice tightening over the speaker) Cormorant, we're getting a vibration in the hull. It started about a minute ago. Is that you?

RIVAS Negative. We're holding station. Kofi—

KOFI I see it. Starboard flange just jumped two degrees. Commander, something's loading the hull.

RIVAS From where?

KOFI From outside.

In the lamp's reach, the sediment ripples in a slow, wide wave. The wave moves toward them.

Picture: The pale trench floor rippling in a widening wave toward the Cormorant's lamps, dust blooming out of the sediment in the dark, four kilometres down.

NEVE Commander. Turn off the sonar.

RIVAS What?

NEVE Active and passive. Now.

RIVAS I'm not flying blind in the middle of a rescue because you've got a feeling.

NEVE Every time the Halcyon pings, something answers. Every time we ping, something answers louder.

NEVE That ripple is not the trench and it is not rock. It's alive, and it's reacting to us.

RIVAS Something alive at four thousand metres. In the dark.

NEVE Yes. That's exactly where things live.

Sound: a small, sharp note rings through the hull

KOFI (out of his seat) That's the starboard sheer strake. The flange weld has opened a little. Nothing through.

KOFI I need everybody very calm and very still.

RIVAS Define still.

KOFI Still like a mouse in a room with a cat.

A hairline has opened in the paint along the weld, thin as a thread of light.

NEVE Sal. Please. Go quiet.

RIVAS Lian. One ping. Low, along the floor, aimed away.

NEVE Don't.

RIVAS I hear you, Doctor. I'm doing it anyway. Now.

The ping goes out. They hear nothing. Then its return strikes the hull like a bell, and the Cormorant rings from bow to stern.

KOFI (voice climbing) It's a working crack now. Every time the thing outside shouts, she flexes the frame.

KOFI I can patch this. I can't keep patching it if it answers us every time we say a word.

Rivas keeps both hands on the console and does not turn around.

RIVAS All stations. Silence protocol. Cut the sonar, cut the lamps. Nobody says a word until I say so.

The cabin goes dark. The screens go dark. The deep presses up against the viewport, black and total.

Lian lifts one flat hand: wait. Her eyes close behind her headphones.

Sound: a low, long hum like a finger drawn around the rim of a glass

LIAN (barely a whisper) It's not an echo. It's a note.

NEVE It sounds like a question.

The hum rises, falls, and rises again in the dark.

KOFI (hoarse, very quiet) Then what do we say back?

Cormorant, in the dark at 06:48

Ambience: a near-total hush, only the hull ticking as it cools

No lamps. The only light is the faint amber smear of a dead screen. Kofi kneels at the weld with resin tape coiled over his shoulder.

On screen: Kofi (kneeling, tape in his teeth), Neve (still, hands flat on her knees), Rivas (rigid at the console), Lian (headphones on, eyes closed)

KOFI (barely above a breath) Commander. If I don't tape this now, the crack decides for us.

RIVAS Do it. Quietly.

Kofi works. The tape comes off his roll with a soft, slow sigh.

Sound: the soft rasp of resin tape

KOFI There. Ugly as sin, but she'll hold.

RIVAS Lian. Talk to me.

LIAN The hum stopped. Three notes, then nothing.

NEVE It stopped because we stopped shouting.

RIVAS Or it's circling.

LIAN It's not circling. It's listening.

RIVAS You can't hear listening.

LIAN I can. It's like the pause after a piano chord when the room waits for the next one.

RIVAS (checking a gauge, whispering now) Thirteen hours of air. Say that again to yourself before you tell me about piano chords.

Close-up: a clipped page of the mission air budget, with the figure "36 HRS" circled by hand, and a second figure, "13.2", handwritten below it.

RIVAS The Halcyon has thirty-six. We have thirteen. That's the whole argument.

NEVE Then we have thirteen hours to be clever instead of loud.

Lian leans into her screen. In the dark its glow paints her face pale green.

On screen: Lian (headphones tight, frowning), Rivas (watching her)

LIAN It's coming back. Longer this time. And it isn't the same pattern.

NEVE Play it.

Lian routes the hum to the cabin speaker, very low.

Sound: three rising hums, the last one held

LIAN That's not a shout. That's a phrase. Three notes, the last one long, like a hand held out.

RIVAS Fingers or teeth?

NEVE Teeth don't keep time, Sal. That's what I told Rivas an hour ago and what I'm telling you now: it kept time when it answered, and now it's keeping time when it asks.

RIVAS (long pause) I cut the array on your word. Tell me why I shouldn't regret it.

NEVE I was on a survey off a warmer coast. A pod of something I'd only read about came through our dive line. I judged them dangerous. I called the evacuation too fast.

NEVE The panic hurt us worse than the animals ever did. A good man didn't come back up.

NEVE (pause) I was right about their teeth. I was wrong about what they wanted. I'd rather be quiet and wrong than loud and right again.

RIVAS You say that like it's a plan.

NEVE It's the beginning of one. Something down here answers sound. So we stop giving it sound to answer.

LIAN Then we have to reach the Halcyon anyway.

RIVAS To reach her, we ping. To ping is to feed it. That's a fine knot. Cut it.

NEVE We go in blind and slow. Lamps off. I'll steer us by the sonar we don't send — listen to the Halcyon's own pings and let them lead us in.

RIVAS That's six hundred metres of dead-reckoning in the dark at four kilometres.

NEVE Then I'd like your eyes, Commander. You've done this depth eleven times.

RIVAS Eleven times with an array, a lamp, and permission. Not one of those, right now.

RIVAS All right. Lamps stay off. Thrusters at a crawl. Kofi, tap the hull every thirty seconds, one tap, so the Halcyon knows we're alive.

KOFI One tap. Like knocking on a neighbour's door.

RIVAS If she's wrong about this, we all die polite. If she's right, I owe her an apology.

The Cormorant begins to creep through the black, and the hatch above the engineering bay ticks in the cold.

On screen: Kofi (tape in hand, still as a mouse), Neve (hand hovering over the dead console), Rivas (jaw set, counting under his breath), Lian (headphones on, lips moving with a sound no one else can hear)

RIVAS (whispering) Twelve hours ten. Twelve nine. Twelve eight.

KOFI (softly) Boss, don't.

RIVAS I count. It's how I stay honest.

LIAN Wait. It's moving again.

NEVE Away from Halcyon?

LIAN No. Toward us. And there's a new return behind the first, much bigger. Like the first one was only a fingertip of it.

NEVE A fingertip of it. Lian, give me the bearing. And somebody tell me that's not the sound of four billion years of patience turning its head.
