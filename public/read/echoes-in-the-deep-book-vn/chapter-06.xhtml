The Pulse

Cormorant, 12:01

Ambience: a galley timer's soft tick, the whisper of the scrubber

Red emergency lamps paint the cabin in dim bands. Kofi stands in the aft bay, holding the finished drum up in both hands like a man displaying a fish he is proud of. A galley timer is wired into the beacon's belly with red and blue leads, one strip of tape already lifting at the corner. Neve leans in the hatchway with her tablet. Rivas has the log open on the console. Lian watches from her seat with her head tipped.

On screen: Kofi (grinning, drum held high), Neve (tablet against her chest), Rivas (pen in hand), Lian (head tipped, listening)

KOFI Behold. The world's smallest, worst drum kit.

He presses the dial with his thumb. The beacon knocks softly against the deck, waits, knocks again.

KOFI Gap's set at eight seconds. This dial doesn't have numbers, it has moods, but I know the moods now. I've been living with it for an hour.

LIAN A heartbeat. A slow one. A resting one.

RIVAS Good or bad?

NEVE A start.

RIVAS Then it goes in the book, and the book says who called it.

Close-up: the log tablet, one careful line at a time in Rivas's blunt capitals

PLAN: BEACON ON WEIGHTED LINE, BELLY HATCH, 200 M TOWARD EASTERN WALL, AWAY FROM HALCYON AND FROM US. DRUM BEGINS 12:20, EIGHT-SECOND GAP, LOW POWER. IF FIELD TURNS TO DRUM, PAY LINE OUT, WALK IT. IF FIELD TURNS TO US, CUT DRUM, GO FLAT AND SILENT. AT SEVENTEEN HOURS AIR, ABANDON DRUM. RESPONSIBILITY: OKONKWO FIRST, RIVAS SECOND, MENSAH THIRD, WU FOURTH.

RIVAS That last line about seventeen hours, I wrote that one myself. Keep it in your head.

NEVE Seventeen. I have it.

RIVAS Say the rest of it.

NEVE If it goes wrong, the responsibility is mine first, logged in my name, then yours, then Kofi's, then Lian's. Because I called it.

RIVAS Because you called it.

The Marrow Deep, 12:14

Ambience: a faint knock every eight seconds

The belly hatch is open. The beacon goes down and away into black water on its weighted line, a small pale shape on the outside camera, then a smudge, then nothing. The line runs back to the Cormorant like the string of a kite. In the distance the Halcyon's sail lamp burns as a small orange coin.

On screen: Kofi (at the airlock control, paying out line), Rivas (at the console), Neve (headphones on, very still), Lian (at the array, headphones on)

KOFI Two hundred metres. Or thereabouts. The tape measure is my arm and my arm is lying.

RIVAS Stand by.

Sound: the first soft knock through the hull

The knock goes out into the deep. Eight seconds pass. Then a soft tap answers from the east, and the space between the two matches the dial exactly. Another crown answers, and another, and the answer runs along the field like a hand along a fence.

LIAN It's answering in time. Doctor, it's answering in time.

KOFI A metronome. I built them a metronome.

NEVE (quietly) Commander. The field's turning.

RIVAS Show me.

NEVE The leading edge has swung off the Halcyon and pointed east. Out toward the drum.

RIVAS It's working. Kofi, pay out another fifty metres. Slowly.

KOFI Paying out.

The line goes out. The beacon knocks, the field answers, and the two sounds walk on ahead of each other into the dark. For one whole minute the Cormorant feels like a boat that will be a boat and not a coffin.

LIAN Doctor. The taps. They're coming closer together.

NEVE Kofi. What's the gap?

KOFI Eight. I set eight.

NEVE They're coming at four.

Sound: the taps accelerating through the hull

LIAN It's on our bearing. It's coming toward us.

NEVE Kofi, kill the drum.

His hand is already on the dial. The taps stop. The silence comes in like water into a held breath, and for one second it is the good silence.

Sound: a rising buzz and tap through the deck plates

Then the floor begins to talk. The whole hull rings like a bell somebody touched in the dark. The boat shivers.

KOFI It's under the keel. The whole front of the field is under the keel, it's on the battery bay.

RIVAS Hull stress.

KOFI Left of the red. Left of the red, but it's climbing.

Cormorant, 12:29

Ambience: the hull's buzz and tap, a bead of air hissing

A white line runs out from the edge of the forward viewport, fine as a hair, and behind it a second line. The taped seal from this morning is spreading. A bead of air glitters at the worst of it.

On screen: Kofi (out of his seat, wild-eyed), Neve (palm flat on the glass), Rivas (rigid at the console), Lian (already moving for the patch kit)

Sound: a sharp crack, like a knuckle

KOFI Seal breach. Lian, the patch kit, the big one, the heavy one, the two-part resin—

LIAN They're both grey—

KOFI The one that's heavy!

She gets it. He tears the lid off with his teeth. Neve presses her palm flat over the crack and feels the little cold breath of the sea against her skin.

NEVE Pressure's holding. Sal, it's holding, but it's breathing.

KOFI I need the frame straight. Doctor, other side of the window, put your back to it and push the seal corner in by a finger's width when I say.

She braces her boots on the console foot and pushes. Kofi mixes the resin on the back of his glove, a grey paste going warm, and reaches around her and lays it on and smooths it with his thumb.

KOFI Hold. Count with me. Sixteen seconds.

RIVAS Seventeen hours of air. Sixteen fifty-nine. Hold it.

KOFI Sixteen. Fifteen.

They count it out. Neve's back aches, her boots slip, the cold breath of the sea comes against her shoulder.

KOFI Four. Three. Two. Set.

The cold stops. The resin hardens against her shoulder, and she stays with her back to it because her knees do not trust themselves.

KOFI (sitting down hard) It's holding. It's a patch on a patch on a crack, and the heavy resin is all gone now, every last squeeze, and the tape with it. Do not slam any doors.

RIVAS Where's the original crack?

Kofi turns his head to the starboard frame, where the webbing strap runs across the weld-line fissure.

KOFI Two fingers longer than it was. The strap's holding it. But tired metal doesn't get fresh again. I can tape a crack and I can strap a frame, and neither one is a repair.

RIVAS How much did it cost us?

KOFI The last of the resin, the last of the tape, and about four hours off the boat. After this she wants handling like a sleeping baby, and we are not going to handle her like a sleeping baby tonight.

RIVAS (a long beat) Sixteen hours. All right. It's holding.

Neve goes to the screen where the returns still come in and stands with her hands on the rail.

NEVE Listen.

RIVAS I'm listening to my boat being played like a—

NEVE No. It's not answering the drum any more. It's answering everything. The scrubber. The hull. Us.

RIVAS Everything?

NEVE The drum woke it up, and the crack woke it up, and now it's answering all of it at once. Lian, tell me I'm wrong.

LIAN (headphones on, eyes shut, hands flat on the console) You're not wrong. There are hundreds of them out there, all singing at once, all singing different things. Some are singing the drum. Some are singing the crack. It's not a choir, Commander. It's a crowd in a room with too many doors.

RIVAS And a crowd does what?

Before anyone answers, something moves against the Cormorant's flank that is not a tap. The whole boat lurches a hand's width to port and comes up shivering.

KOFI That would be a crowd pushing.

RIVAS Doctor. Tell me you have something that isn't another knock.

Cormorant, 12:41

Ambience: the scrubber's slow sigh, the hull's faint shiver

Neve sits on the deck with her back to the patched glass. Kofi sits on the floor opposite, the beacon in his lap. Rivas has the log tablet on his knee. Lian holds her headphones in her lap.

On screen: Neve (back to the viewport, chin up), Kofi (beacon in his lap), Rivas (tablet on his knee, pen still), Lian (headphones held, eyes down)

KOFI Doctor. You've got the face of a woman who has thought of a second idea and doesn't want to be seen thinking it. Say it. Save us the ten minutes.

NEVE The drum failed. I want to say that plainly before I go on. It brought it off the Halcyon and then it brought it onto us. It was too loud and too hard. Every knock was a hit, and a thing that lives by answering will answer a hit louder than anything.

RIVAS Four hours off a crack.

NEVE Those four hours are mine. But here is what I have not been able to stop thinking. It answered the drum in time. It took our eight seconds and gave them back to us. It sang our own boat back at us, round and soft, like a room rounding off a voice.

KOFI And?

NEVE A thing that hates noise does not sing in time. A thing that hates noise goes and hides. This thing learned our song and gave it back. It's not strength it hates. What if it doesn't want to be hit? What if it wants to be sung to?

RIVAS You've brought this boat to the edge of her life twice today. Once with the ping, once with the drum. Now you want a third noise at the same thing.

NEVE I know exactly what I'm asking.

RIVAS Then give me a reason.

NEVE Not from me. She lifts the headphones from the console and holds them out to Lian. From it. Tell him the thing you told me.

Lian takes the headphones and sets each word down as if it has to be carried somewhere.

LIAN Commander. Remember the silence. When we all went dark. The quiet was not an empty quiet. It was a long low note, and the low note had a shape. That shape is still in the water. It's underneath the crowd.

LIAN The crowd's on top. The long low note is underneath, and it comes and goes, and the crowd doesn't eat it. It's like a person in a loud room humming to herself.

LIAN That note is what it sounds like when it isn't frightened. I can't sing it. I haven't got the machine. But if Kofi can make the drum not knock, but hum, and the hum has the shape of the long low note, it will hear the shape it already knows. And it'll think, that's the thing that was quiet with me. Not the thing that shouted.

RIVAS Can you make the drum sing?

KOFI It's a buzzer. A buzzer with a gap is a knock, and a buzzer without a gap is a bad note somebody already complained about. He turns the beacon over. But the timer's got a setting where it doesn't open and close so much as lean. Like the galley bell. The one meant to be quiet. Give me twelve minutes.

RIVAS You have ten.

He types on the tablet, one key at a time.

Close-up: the log tablet, typed lines with two signatures below

FIRST PULSE FAILED. LOUD. LATTICE CAME OFF HALCYON AND TURNED ON US. VIEWPORT CRACKED AND SEALED; STARBOARD WELD LINE EXTENDED TWO FINGERS; LAST OF RESIN AND TAPE SPENT; FOUR HOURS AIR LOST. RECOMMENDATION OF OKONKWO: SOFT PULSE, THE SHAPE OF THE LONG NOTE. CALLED BY OKONKWO, FIRST. ACCEPTED BY COMMANDER IN HIS OWN NAME, SECOND.

RIVAS Read it. Then say your piece into the log, in your own hand.

Neve takes the tablet and types, slowly, because her hands are not steady.

NEVE I recommended the pulse drum. It drew it off the Halcyon, then turned it on us, and the pressure cracked the viewport and cost us four hours. My call, my name first. I don't withdraw it. I withdraw only the knock.

RIVAS That's the lot?

NEVE That's the lot.

RIVAS Then stop writing and start listening. We're going to sing to it. In all four names.

The Marrow Deep, 12:53

Ambience: a long low hum with a slow swell, repeating

The belly lamp shows the beacon on its line, a small pale shape against the black. Beyond it the crystal crowns glow faint pale blue along the eastern wall, leaning like grass under a slow wind. The Halcyon's sail lamp burns clear, the growth gone from her side.

On screen: Kofi (beacon held to the hatch), Neve (headphones on, hand on the line), Rivas (at the lamp), Lian (at the array, eyes closed)

KOFI Soft pulse going out. Long note, slow swell. If it leans on us at all, say the word and I cut it.

RIVAS Do it.

Sound: one long low hum through the hull, swelling gently, then again

The hum goes out into the dark. For forty seconds nothing happens. Then the nearest crown takes up the note, and the note runs along the field, not in a ripple but in a held breath. All of them hum together, swelling and settling, swelling and settling, the way the sea swells on a long slow day.

KOFI Hull stress coming down. It's coming off.

LIAN It's slowing. It's not stopped, it's slowed. It's turned toward the drum and it's going slow, the way you walk when you're listening to something.

The bloom thins under the Cormorant. The leading edge swings east along the wall after the beacon, and the space it was filling opens like a room after a crowd goes home. Along the Halcyon's side there is now nothing at all: just a wide pale vacancy and one orange coin of a sail lamp, still burning.

NEVE The Halcyon's clear. Sal, she's clear.

RIVAS We're not clear. But we're clearer. Sixteen hours and a soft bell. Keep it going, nice and slow, and tell me what it costs.

KOFI I can tell you now. He puts the torch to the power panel and takes it out of his mouth, and his face in the light has no joke left in it. The drum's pulling steady off the aft battery, and the aft battery is not getting any fresher. We can do this two hours. Then the drum takes the last of it, or the thrusters get it, and the thrusters are the thing that takes us up.

RIVAS Two hours of drum. And it's working.

KOFI It's working like a dream. It's working so well we're pouring our own air into the sound it makes.

LIAN (headphones lowered) Commander. If we starve the drum, it stops singing, and the crowd comes back. We can't just switch it off.

NEVE Two hours. Can we make two hours into more?

Kofi looks at the open panel, and the galley timer, and the ceiling, and something in his face changes.

KOFI Depends what you'd like to stop doing.
