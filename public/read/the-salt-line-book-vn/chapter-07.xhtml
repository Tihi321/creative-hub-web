The Salt Line

Meridian Dawn, night

Ambience: a slow gray water climbing, wind in the northeast

The sea climbs the freighter's side in the dark, inch by inch, with no wave to it. The Dawn holds her bow high and her stern down in the mud. The Ninth Hour lies jammed against her quarter, fenders crushed between the hulls. Lanterns bob on the flats where the town's boats wait.

On screen: Juno (in the tug's wheelhouse door, coat streaming, watching the water climb)

Juno keeps one hand on the frame and will not go below.

JUNO Come on, then. Show me what you've got in you.

The water licks at the freighter's rail and keeps coming.

Meridian Dawn, midnight

Ambience: a pump hammering, water going over the rail

The foredeck is black and wet. Teo climbs into the wheelhouse with his hands black to the wrist and a sheared bolt in his fist. A loose line worries at its cleat in the wind.

On screen: Teo (hands black, face drawn, red cap shoved back), Juno (turning from the rail)

TEO Number one pump's done. Winding's hot and the impeller's chewed to hell.

JUNO And the others?

TEO Portable's running off the Dawn's own emergency generator. The big one's still turning on the tug, but she loses her prime every twenty minutes.

JUNO Keep her at it as long as she'll go.

TEO She's a towboat. She's doing a thing she was never built for, with a cracked head and wire where the gasket goes.

He wipes his hands on his trousers, looks at them, wipes them again.

TEO She'll fail. The only question's what she does before she fails.

JUNO Then we'll do the same. Fail last.

Teo nods once and goes back down.

Meridian Dawn, night

Ambience: a pump hammering, water rising against a hull

The deck cants harder than it did at dusk. Barrow comes up fast, boots skidding, and puts his head into the wheelhouse where Voss sits with her hands folded and Juno stands at the glass.

On screen: Barrow (wet to the shoulders, breathing hard), Ines Voss (hands folded, face gray), Juno (turning)

BARROW She's making water faster. The seam's opened and the mat's worked loose at the bottom chain.

JUNO How loose?

BARROW The pump's holding it level. The way a man holds a door against a flood. Not long.

JUNO Teo. Go and look.

Teo goes forward with Barrow. The two of them come back with their faces set.

On screen: Teo (soaked through, jaw tight), Barrow (behind him, silent)

TEO The mat's torn away at the lower edge. I can't get it back on her in this sea.

JUNO Then we wait.

TEO I can't send a man over the side in the dark. That's a man drowned.

JUNO I said we wait. We don't send him. We hold what we've got and wait for the light.

Meridian Dawn, night

Ambience: a pump slowing, then a hard metal snap

On the foredeck Teo has the portable pump apart with a torch in his teeth. The big pump hammers below, and the whole tug shivers with it.

On screen: Teo (torch in his teeth, hands working), Juno (above him with a lantern)

Sound: a hard metal snap, then the pump winding down to nothing

The portable dies. Teo lifts the impeller out and holds it up: a twist of bright metal, no longer any use to anyone.

TEO That's the last of her.

He climbs to the wheelhouse and sets the sheared impeller on the chart table. It sits there, bright and useless, and the big pump hammers on.

TEO If the big one goes, I can't promise her past dawn.

JUNO Then she's got till dawn and so have we.

Meridian Dawn, night

Ambience: wind over the flats, water slapping a hull

Juno climbs to the tug's wheelhouse roof with a lantern and holds it up. The lanterns on the flats bob where the town's boats lie, waiting on a word from her she has not yet found how to give.

On screen: Juno (alone on the roof, lantern high, hair whipped flat)

Sound: radio static, three tries, then a voice thin through the weather

PELL (on radio) Reach Harbor. Ninth Hour, is that you?

JUNO Pell. Listen close, because I'll only say it once.

PELL (on radio) It's half past three in the morning. Say it.

JUNO The Dawn's taking water forward of her bulkhead and my pumps are failing one by one. If the forepeak fills and the bulkhead goes, the drums go in the estuary and up to you.

PELL (on radio) How long have I got?

JUNO Till the tide turns. Four hours, maybe.

A long pause, and the wind in the static.

PELL (on radio) Then I'll have them there. You keep that ship on the flats, Juno Halloway. I'll bring you the whole town.

JUNO Pell. Sandbags along the north lip, not across the channel. You'd never hold the channel.

Sound: radio click

PELL (on radio) I know my own flats. Reach Harbor out.

Meridian Dawn, before dawn

Ambience: boats coming out of the dark, a crowd on wet steel

They come at four, out of the dark: the pilot boat first, then a fishing boat and another, a scallop dredge, a flat-bottomed launch that has no business in a sea like that, and behind those the small boats, a dozen and then more. Lanterns run up the flats in a line.

On screen: Pell (coming over the rail with empty sandbags over his shoulder), Juno (at the rail, staring), the boatyard man (oilskins, hands raw, behind Pell), people crowding over the rail behind them

PELL Where do you want us?

JUNO The mouth. Not across the channel, you'd never hold it with bags.

She points out across the water.

JUNO Along the north lip, where the water goes over into the flats and down into the lens. Leave the channel running. The Dawn's sitting on the mud and the mud's holding her. Build me a wall behind her and the sea's got nowhere to go but out the channel.

PELL And the drums?

JUNO The drums stay in her and the hold stays dry. That's my job. Yours is the wall.

Pell wipes his hands on his coat, slow, and looks at the water and the lie of the flats. Then he turns to the crowd.

PELL You heard her. North lip of the channel mouth, behind the wreck. Fill them and lay them and keep laying them. The tide turns at seven and we've got no more than that.

They go. The boats break up and run up the channel with a lantern in the flat launch's bow, and the sandbags go up the flats in a chain of hands, and the wall begins.

Meridian Dawn, before dawn

Ambience: a pump losing its note, then dying, wind in the rigging

The big pump loses its prime and does not come back. Teo has it open before the smell of hot metal clears. Juno holds the torch while the wind worries at her coat.

On screen: Teo (over the pump in pieces, hands black), Juno (torch steady), Barrow (arriving at a run), Ines Voss (out on deck, face gray and set)

TEO Coupling's bent. Winding's black. That's the last of her.

JUNO What's left?

TEO The bilge pump and the hand pump. A toy and a prayer in a sea like this. Maybe an hour before the forepeak takes the bulkhead.

BARROW Captain. The water in the forepeak's taken the lower step.

VOSS How long?

TEO An hour. Maybe. She's a good bulkhead and she was built to be beat. But the water'll find a seam and come up in the lower hold from below, and then the drums go where the water goes.

JUNO They won't.

She goes across with Teo and looks down into the lower hold with a torch. The drums stand in the dark, stenciled SX-9, weeping their green film into a bilge that is rising a finger's breadth at a time.

Close-up: the lower hold bilge, black water climbing the frames an inch at a time, the nearest blue drum's seam already wet

Juno watches the water climb and holds her face still.

END INSERT

She comes back onto the tug's deck and looks at the lanterns bobbing in a line on the flats, and at the water she cannot pump, and at the hour she has.

JUNO Teo. Voss's flooding holds the bow up. What if we flood her more?

He is quiet. The wind lifts a loose line and drops it.

TEO More weight aft, more bow rise. The forepeak takes less over the seam and the bulkhead's got less head on it. But she'll sit down in the mud and strain her bottom. If she can't float she'll break her back. That's a ship that won't come off these flats in one piece.

JUNO And the drums?

TEO The more her bow comes up, the higher the drums sit. They'd be dry. You're not asking me to flood her so I can save the pumps.

JUNO I'm asking you to flood her so we don't need them. We've been trying to hold the sea out and we're losing because we haven't got enough pump. So we put the sea in her where we want it and lift the hold above it.

TEO It's insane. It might work.

He rubs his face with the flat of his hand.

TEO It'll lose you the ship.

JUNO She's not my ship.

TEO She might have been. If you'd saved her off the reef instead of beaching her, she'd have been your ship and the award with her.

JUNO I've been thinking it since the reef. Get Voss. Tell her to flood it all, fore and aft.

Meridian Dawn, dawn

Ambience: a great slow settling, water taking a hull

The dawn comes up gray behind the channel, and the freighter sits down in the mud as the light comes. Her tanks swallow the water and swallow it, her stern settles until the well deck is at the waterline, and her bow rises until the sprung seam stands clear of the sea.

On screen: Ines Voss (at the tank valves, hands on the wheel, drenched), Juno (on the tug's deck, watching), Teo (at the rail with a torch on the lower hold)

Picture: The Meridian Dawn settling stern-first into the west flats at dawn, her bow lifting clear of the surge, the Ninth Hour alongside her quarter, the sandbag wall rising behind on the flats

The forepeak water comes down over the stumps of the pumps and lies there and does not rise. The bulkhead takes what is left and groans and holds.

TEO She's dry. She'll stay dry. The drums are high and the ship's down and the mud's holding her.

JUNO Say the rest.

TEO She'll not float off this tide or the next. But she's not going to break.

The tide turns at seven and does not come back.

Kestrel Reach, morning

Ambience: gulls on wet sand, a slow tide going out

The surge is going out and the morning is gray and cold. The wall runs low along the north flats, sandbag on sandbag, the whole length of it. People lie along it asleep in their oilskins. The boatyard man sits against a bag with his hands raw and his eyes shut.

On screen: Juno (wet to the knees), Pell (notebook in hand), the well man (gauges), the council woman (clipboard)

PELL Test it.

The well man draws from the north well, the south well, the well by the school, and runs his tests on the grass with the wind trying to take his paper. Juno stands with her hands at her sides and waits.

PELL Fresh. All three. Not a salt grain in one of them that wasn't there before the surge. The lens held and the flats held.

He puts his hand out and Juno takes it. The well man puts his hand out too, and the council woman, and along the wall the sleepers wake and look at one another.

THE WELL MAN Fresh. The water's fresh.

The word goes along the wall, fresh, fresh, and someone begins to laugh, and then the laughing spreads until it is everywhere at once.

Iron Gannet, morning

Ambience: a boat coming alongside, wind off the flats

The yellow tug lies hove to off the channel, her stack cooling. Rusk comes over his own rail from the boat and stands on his bridge wing with his papers under his arm and the flats behind him, the wall low along the north lip, the beached Dawn down in the mud.

On screen: Doran Rusk (oilskin buttoned, papers under arm, jaw set), his mate (at his shoulder, silent)

RUSK Get the radio. Reach Harbor. I'll file it from here and be gone before the fog lifts.

MATE You'll not go ashore and say it to the harbor master's face?

RUSK I've said what I came to say on that wall once already in my head, and it doesn't get better said twice.

He looks out at the wall and the people along it and does not look pleased.

RUSK Log it plain. The Gannet stood by through the night at the harbormaster's direction and rendered all assistance asked of her and no more. I take no part in the beaching. In my judgment she was put aground in a manner inconsistent with the law of salvage, and any claim arising from it is not mine.

MATE And the complaint about the interference?

RUSK Off the file. There's no prize in this for anyone. I'd only be spending my own money on lawyers to prove it.

He tucks the papers under his arm and turns for the ladder.

RUSK And put it down that the town's water's fresh. I'm glad of it. Say that too.

MATE Aye.

The Gannet's stack goes black, and she goes out the estuary mouth into the fog and does not come back.

Ninth Hour, midday

Ambience: a hand pump working, a slow swell under the hull

The old tug lies alongside the Dawn's quarter, her fenders crushed flat, her paint scored, her stack black. She lists to port where she took the knock. Teo is in the engine room with the pump in pieces on the deck plates and his hands black to the elbow. Juno stands at the pipe.

On screen: Juno (at the pipe, coat still wet), Teo (below, looking up through the hatch)

JUNO Talk to me.

TEO She's a mess. Cracked head, burnt winding, bent coupling. She'll want a slip and a fortnight and a thousand dollars of parts. Bilge pump and hand pump and that's all that turns.

JUNO Then that's what turns.

TEO Juno. You know the court's not going to pay us for this.

JUNO I know it.

TEO Do you?

His voice comes up the pipe warm and flat and a little sad.

TEO There's a fourth payment due Monday and a bank that wants what a bank wants, and we've got no prize and no award and a tug that won't make a tow till she's fixed and no money to fix her. That's not a hard sum. And there's another thing. Pell's on the flats yet, and he's had his notebook out since the light came, walking that wall and writing in it. Whatever he's writing, he's not writing a salvage report.

JUNO He's writing a town.

TEO He's writing something. I saw the way he looked at the well man's paper.

JUNO Then we'll hear it from him and not from the wall.

TEO And the boat?

JUNO We'll do it the way we do everything. Badly, and last.

Sound: a real laugh out of the engine room, tired and surprised

TEO Go and get some sleep. There's nothing more the old girl can do today and nothing more you can do either.

JUNO Teo.

TEO The town's water's fresh. Whatever happens Thursday, that's true and nobody can take it off you.

Ninth Hour, evening

Ambience: water slapping a hull, gulls going home

Juno stands at the rail and looks out at the Dawn lying down in the flats with her drums dry in her flooded hold, the wall behind her, the channel mouth open and gray. The ebb is pulling the last of the surge out with it.

On screen: Juno (alone at the rail, ship's watch in her hand)

Close-up: the brass ship's watch on its chain, the back worn smooth, a name and a year scratched small into it

She turns the watch over twice and puts it back against her ribs.

The sun goes down behind the ridge. The flats go gray, then brown, then dark, and the water coming up the estuary mouth is clean and fresh, and the salt line goes out with the ebb.

Juno looks at the lights of Kestrel Reach, one by one, all up the hill.

JUNO Thursday, then.

She goes below and lies down in her bunk in her wet coat and sleeps, and the tide rocks the old tug under her, and out past the wall the beached freighter sits quiet in the mud.

Sound: a bell, small and clear, from somewhere ashore

In the dark, someone in the town is ringing a bell.
