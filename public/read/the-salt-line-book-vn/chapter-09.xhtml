Kestrel Reach

Kestrel Reach, morning

Ambience: wind off the harbor, a loose shutter knocking, gulls

The notice on the meeting house door flaps at one corner. The paper has gone damp and crimped. The harbor is gray, the tide is out, and the flats beyond the quay smell of weed and rain.

On screen: Juno (collar up, reading the notice), Teo (two cups in his hands, red cap shoved back)

Close-up: a paper notice tacked to the door, its pencilled lines gone soft in the damp

Town meeting. Item before the council. All welcome.

JUNO That's the whole of it. A meeting, and not one word about what for.

TEO Thirty-one years behind that desk, and that's the notice he writes when he doesn't want to be asked on the street.

JUNO Then I'll ask him in the room.

TEO You'll be at the pump till six. I know you.

JUNO I'll be at the pump till six. Then I'll be in the third row.

She walks away down the wharf. He follows her. The wind takes the notice and lays it flat again.

Ninth Hour, afternoon

Ambience: a hand pump clicking, a diesel cold and silent

The engine room is cold. The big engine sits stripped, its head off and a rag stuffed in the open cylinder. Juno is buried to the elbow in the bilge, her good coat hung on a pipe and gone dark with grease.

On screen: Juno (arms in the bilge, hair in her eyes), Teo (in the hatchway, two cups)

TEO A spanner and a bad mood won't turn her.

JUNO I know.

TEO Then why are you down in there?

JUNO Because she's my boat.

He sets a cup on the rail for her, drinks the other himself, and lets the argument go. That is his way of saying the rest.

TEO The meeting's at six. You'll want to wash.

JUNO I'll want to fix this first. Then I'll want to wash.

Kestrel Reach, evening

Ambience: rain on a tin roof, benches scraping, a hall settling

The meeting house is fuller than it was for the charges. People stand along the walls and fill the doorway, and the cold comes in behind them. The lamps are lit early. The children are at the back, and this time they make noise, and nobody tells them not to.

On screen: Juno (clean coat, third row, seated), Fen (work coat across her knees, gray brows drawn down), Teo (at the back wall, arms folded), Pell (at the front, book open)

Fen has saved the seat. Her hands are folded, and she carries the courthouse smell of dust and paper and long waiting.

JUNO You didn't have to come up.

FEN I wanted to see what a town does.

JUNO What does it do?

FEN I'll tell you when it's done.

Pell calls the meeting to order and looks past his book at the benches the way he always does.

PELL Two items. I'll take the small one first, and I'll say it quick. I've been saying it slow to myself for a month and I've got no better at it.

He shuts the book.

PELL I'm giving up the harbormaster's post at the end of the month. My grandfather dug this town's first well. My father ran the launch. I've stood behind that desk thirty-one years, and I mean to go home and dig in my garden and watch somebody else do it.

A murmur runs along the benches, softer than surprise, a town hearing what it knew was coming.

PELL Now. The other item.

He turns the page.

PELL A bank in Bellmouth holds a mortgage on the salvage tug Ninth Hour. Four payments are out, there's a date in December, and the bank will seize her and sell her. The court's ruled, the prize is gone, and there's no man in this room can pay eleven thousand four hundred dollars out of a fish catch.

He raises his head.

PELL But this is not a man in this room. This is a town.

On screen: the crowd (still, then stirring), the boatyard man (on his feet before Pell sits)

PELL Your council has been in touch with the bank, and the bank has agreed a price. The council won't tell you how it was struck, and it won't tell you what the fish house and the boatyard and the man from the well have put in already, because every one of them asked me to keep quiet. What the council asks is that the town make up the rest. Not a gift to a captain. A purchase, for the town. A harbor without a tug is a harbor that watches and waits, and we've watched and waited long enough.

BOATYARD MAN I'm for it. I knew her father, and I let a quarrel stand nine years past the point where a man should let it stand, and I'm sorry for it in front of the whole town.

He sits down hard.

On screen: the fish house woman (tin in her hand, standing), the schoolteacher (coffee can with the lid cut open), a woman near the door (standing, unknown face), Gorse (hat in his hands, on his feet)

The fish house women read their numbers off the tin one after another. The schoolteacher brings the children's collection, eleven dollars and forty cents in a coffee can.

WOMAN NEAR DOOR I filled a sandbag in the dark and never learned whose idea it was till the paper printed it. I've three hundred dollars and a boy in my yard who can splice.

Then Gorse stands, the chandler from the head of the wharf who keeps half the harbor's ledgers in his back room, and the room quiets, because he does not speak at meetings.

GORSE I'll be the one. Somebody has to be. I've got no quarrel with the captain, and that's true. But I've a mortgage of my own at the same bank, and a bank that forgives a debt for a town is a bank that knows a town will pay what a boat won't. I'm not saying no. I'm saying what it costs.

He turns his hat over.

GORSE If we buy her, I want it in the books where the money came from and where it went. Read out at the meeting after. And I want the boat worked, not kept. A town tug that sits at the pier is a monument, and I can't sell chandlery to a monument.

WOMAN NEAR DOOR That's fair.

BOATYARD MAN It's fair, and it's a hard word on a good night, but it's fair.

PELL Read out at the meeting after. And the boat worked. Both in the minutes.

Gorse puts his hat on.

GORSE Then I'm for it.

Pell puts it to the room, and the vote is not close.

Kestrel Reach, evening

Ambience: rain on a tin roof, a hall settling, someone crying quietly at the back

Pell writes the count in his book, closes it, and looks straight down the benches.

On screen: Pell (book shut, hand flat on it), Juno (on her feet before she knows it), Fen (hand on Juno's arm a moment), Teo (back wall, one corner of his mouth turned up)

PELL The council calls for the money Monday and pays the bank Tuesday. The boat's the town's before the week's out. Come to the office Thursday of next week and sign for her. We'd have you sign in front of everybody, only you'd hate it, and I've known you twenty years and I won't do that to you.

Juno is standing. Her voice comes out rough, and she lets it.

JUNO I'll sign. I don't know what to say, so here's the plain thing. I came into the Bellmouth that morning with a letter in my coat that said I was four payments from nothing. I put the letter away and did the work. I'd have done it knowing it cost me the boat and the prize and my name at the bank, because the drums don't care about any of that and neither does the water.

She looks round at the wet coats and the children and the coffee can.

JUNO A good many of you stood on a wall in the dark and filled bags beside me and never asked what for. That's the thing. That's all of it. And Gorse is right. She'll work. I won't have her kept.

She sits. Fen's hand rests on her arm a moment and comes away. The meeting moves on to other business, and for the rest of the evening nobody looks at her again, and the town could not have done her a kinder turn.

Reach Harbor, morning

Ambience: a bell above a door, a radio hissing, a tide board ticking

The office smells of paper and old varnish. The window shows the harbor full of rain. The bank's man is polite and quick and never once mentions the court ruling, and his pen scratches and stops.

On screen: Juno (good coat, signing), Pell (looking on, hands behind his back), Teo (by the door, cap in his hands), the bank's man (polite, brisk)

Close-up: a deed of sale on the desk, the town's name in the purchaser's line and the vessel's name written in full in the hand of a clerk

The Ninth Hour, of Kestrel Reach, sold as she lies, to the town.

The last paper is blotted. The bank's man shakes her hand.

BANK'S MAN The bank wishes you well.

Juno takes that to mean the bank has got what it wanted and would now like to forget her.

Pell puts the papers in the safe, shuts it, and rests his hand on the door a moment.

PELL There. She's a town boat now. A town boat wants an engineer, and I hear there's a good one going spare and asking for parts, a thousand dollars' worth.

Teo crosses the room. He looks at the papers, then at Juno, and the wryness has left his face.

TEO I'll stay. Where else would I go? A man can't walk off a thing he's held together this long. It'd fall down the minute his back was turned.

He puts his cap on.

TEO But I'll want the slip for a fortnight and the parts, and it's understood I fix the engine and not the politics.

PELL Understood.

TEO And the boat's name stays on her.

JUNO Her name stays. The town can own her. Her name stays.

Teo nods once and goes out. The bell above the door rings and settles.

Kestrel Reach, afternoon

Ambience: wind off the flats, water lipping at stone, gulls

The pier is cold and the light is thin. The tide comes in over the flats, lipped at the sandbags, and draws back. Out past the wall the Dawn lies in the mud with her bow lifted, and gulls walk her decks like they own them.

On screen: Ines Voss (gray shore coat, hands in pockets, smaller than she was on her own deck), Fen (at her side, work coat on), Juno (coming down the pier)

VOSS They want me back at the courthouse Monday. The company's lawyers have found a way to ask the same question twice and call it new.

JUNO How long?

VOSS It will take a while. My lawyer says there's a chance I'll do time. He says everyone says that and it's usually a mood. This time I think maybe not.

JUNO You ran a ship onto a rock on purpose. There's a law for that.

VOSS There is.

She looks out at her ship.

VOSS I went out with the surveyor Tuesday. The back's not broken. She's set in the mud to her bilges and she'll lie there till they cut her up. The lower hold's dry. He put his hand on the drums and they were dry as a church, and the wall behind her has not moved an inch.

JUNO And the slick?

VOSS The boom's still round her and it's stayed empty. Whatever wept stayed in the steel. The estuary's had nothing off her but rust.

Voss's mouth moves, not quite a smile.

VOSS The deep tanks held. That's what saved her. A ship that floods even and settles in soft mud will lie there a good while. She'll come to pieces slow.

JUNO And the barge?

VOSS The Lysander. Her master's sworn he came alongside in the dark and took nothing off her and was paid to keep quiet, and he named the man who paid him, and that man's named the next one up. Fen's given a statement too.

Fen keeps still at her own name.

VOSS The mate dogged the engine-room door from inside the night we grounded, so nobody would go down and find the tanks flooded early. Barrow hosed out number three because the drums had been staged in it. The ship unloaded nothing. There was nothing to unload. The lime manifest was paper, and the drums were the whole of her cargo, and now they're evidence in a shed at Bellmouth with a guard on it.

JUNO I'm glad of that. I've wanted to know.

Voss reaches down to a canvas bag at her feet.

VOSS I've a thing for you. Not a gift. I'm in no position to give gifts. It's a thing I'd like you to keep, because I don't want to see it go to a scrapyard, and because you're the one who knows what it was for.

She lifts out the Dawn's bell. Brass gone green with salt, the ship's name cut into it in a band of raised letters, a short piece of lanyard still knotted at the crown.

JUNO Why me? I beached your ship. I flooded her. She'll never float again.

VOSS You kept her drums out of the water and you kept the town's wells fresh. The bell was the thing I heard when they hauled her off the rock and I thought she'd go under us, and she didn't.

Her eyes stay steady.

VOSS That's what was saved, Halloway. Not the ship. Ring it when a ship comes in safe. I'd like to think of that.

Juno turns the bell over once and tucks it under her arm.

JUNO I'll ring it. It'll want a bracket.

VOSS It's got a crown. You'll find a bracket.

Fen and Voss go back up the pier together. Juno watches them thread between the fish crates and the bollards, then carries the bell along the wharf.

Reach Harbor, morning

Ambience: a window rattling in the wind, a radio left low, gulls

Fog sits on the water outside the office window. The tide board ticks. The desk is bare where Pell's files have been carried out, and a clock on the wall has stopped at a quarter past four, and nobody has bothered about it.

On screen: Juno (coat on, standing behind the desk), Pell (flat cap, list in his hand, coat buttoned for the weather)

PELL That's the job.

He hands her four pages in his own hand.

The pilot boat wants her injector cleaned. Don't let the mechanic tell you it's the pump.

JUNO That's it?

PELL That's the job. The rest you'll learn. You've been reading the channel forty years without being paid for it. Now you'll be paid, and they'll expect you to be right.

He pulls his cap down.

JUNO You left the clock stopped.

PELL It stopped the year before and nobody bothered. Why should I?

He goes out. The door swings and settles. Juno hangs her father's watch where the clock had been, and sits down behind the desk, and looks at the water through the window. Then she goes down to the launch and cleans the pilot boat's injector herself.

The Bellmouth, morning

Ambience: foghorn buoy moaning, water breaking on the reef, a coaster's engine turning slow

Fog sits on the strait, cold enough to make the eyes water, and the reef shows as a streak of white that opens and closes. The launch noses out of the harbor with the pilot aboard, and a coaster comes out of the gray, her cranes down and her deck loaded with pale bales.

On screen: Juno (climbing the coaster's ladder, coat under her arm), the Heron's master (big, weathered, one glance over his shoulder that says what he thinks of a pilot sent out in a boat with cracked paint)

He watches her come up and does not offer his hand.

MASTER You're the pilot.

JUNO I'm the pilot. Heron, bound for Kestrel Reach. Put her on that heading and I'll take her in.

MASTER On that heading she goes on the reef.

JUNO She does. That's why you'll ease her a point to starboard and hold her there till I say.

He stays where he is. She gives him the channel regardless, turn by turn, buoy by buoy, in the flat voice she keeps for tugs and weather, and by the narrows he is doing exactly what she says and no longer looking at her at all.

On screen: Juno (hands in her coat, eyes forward), the Heron's master (watching the water now, grip easy on the spokes)

JUNO There's a set to the current past the reef no chart marks true. It runs off the shoulder and it'll want you to the westward. Let it. Then give me a quarter point back when I say now.

MASTER Now?

JUNO Now.

The Heron answers. The reef slides by to starboard, a streak of white in the fog, and the flats open beyond it, gray where the tide has left them.

Picture: Juno standing in the Heron's wheelhouse as the coaster comes down the Bellmouth channel past the white line of the reef, the beached Meridian Dawn lying in the flats off her bow, the town's roofscape gray beyond

The Heron passes the beached Dawn with her bow lifted and her bell gone and gulls ashore on her hatches, past the sandbag wall, into the estuary.

MASTER Fish pier or south wharf?

JUNO South wharf. The fish house women are working this morning. You'll foul their lines on the pier.

He grins, the first grin of the day, and eases her over.

Juno puts one hand on the rail of a ship she does not own and looks back up the channel. Under the Heron's stern the salt line lies thin across the mouth, moving, going out.

Sound: a bell ringing, faint across the water

Faint across the flats, where somebody has found a bracket and hung it, the Dawn's bell is being rung for a ship come in safe.

On screen: the Heron's master (glancing aft, eyebrows up), Juno (not turning)

MASTER What's that bell?

JUNO That's the Meridian Dawn. She's had a good morning.

She keeps her hand on the rail and watches the line go out.
