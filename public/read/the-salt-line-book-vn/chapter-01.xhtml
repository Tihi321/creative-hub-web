Four Payments

Ninth Hour, early morning

Ambience: foghorn moaning far off, water slapping the hull

The galley is small and warm, worn wood and brass gone green at the edges. Fog presses a gray face to the porthole. A coffee pot sits cold on the burner, and under it, the corner of a white envelope.

On screen: Juno (oilskin over sweater, hair still damp from sleep, reading)

Juno draws the letter from under the pot and reads it standing up.

Close-up: a bank letter, folded twice, the letterhead of a Bellmouth bank above three short paragraphs

Four payments in arrears. Eleven thousand, four hundred and some dollars. The vessel Ninth Hour will be seized in forty days.

Sound: paper folding

She folds it small and puts it inside her coat, flat against her ribs.

TEO (off-screen) You found it, then.

On screen: Teo (entering, wiping hands on a rag, red cap shoved back)

TEO I put it under the pot so it wouldn't be the first thing you saw. It saw you anyway.

JUNO It's the note. Four payments on the note. That's all I'll say of it.

TEO Since last month it grew two thousand five hundred dollars.

JUNO Last month was last month.

Teo braces his hands on the table and looks at the fog.

TEO Give me wire and a prayer and I'll keep this boat to the day they cut her up. But a bank's got lawyers. Lawyers don't listen to a man with a wrench.

JUNO Nobody's asking you to argue with them. Get the third injector seated. Then we work.

She takes one swallow of coffee, sets the cup down half-full, and none of it is said aloud.

TEO You're not going to drink that.

JUNO I'm going to get dressed.

Ninth Hour, morning

Ambience: diesel idling rough, a beat missing

The engine room is hot and cramped, pipes sweating, an old diesel ticking over with a cough in its throat. Yellow light, oil-stained gray everywhere. A tin box of spares rusts through at one corner.

On screen: Teo (coveralls to the elbow, one cheek frowned tight), Juno (in the hatchway, holding a lamp)

TEO Third injector in three months, and that was the last one in the tin.

JUNO Will it hold?

TEO One night in port, that's what she wants. A slip and a thousand dollars of parts.

JUNO Then make tonight hold.

He cuts wire from an old crab pot that lives by the bench and threads it through the collar where the injector seats.

TEO Wire off a crab pot. Zinc over steel. Your father used baling wire and spite. Spite is what we're short of.

JUNO We've got plenty of spite.

TEO You've got plenty. I'd like some back. Hold the lamp steady.

The lamp sways with the hull and she straightens it. He sets the collar, pulls the wire taut, tests it with a thumbnail.

TEO Give her throttle.

Sound: diesel coughing, then a hard clean rhythm

Juno opens the throttle and the old engine catches and settles into a beat she can feel through her boots.

TEO (grinning) Wire and a prayer. She pulls true from here to the reef if she has to.

JUNO Don't spend the prayer all at once. We've got a barge to move.

The Bellmouth, late morning

Ambience: foghorn buoy low and mournful, water running fast

Fog lies thick over a narrow channel of dark green water running fast between two low, brown-grassed headlands. Whitecaps show in the throat of the strait. The tug and her laden barge move through it slowly.

On screen: Juno (at the wheel, hands easy), Teo (in the door, papers in hand)

TEO Big flat-hulled thing, that Meridian Dawn. I saw her in the harbor in the summer. Paint peeling off her bows like old skin. Halyard Shipping keeps her.

JUNO Halyard's due the east dock today with lime. Boring cargo, boring day. I'll take boring.

Teo lifts his head and turns it, sniffing.

TEO You smell that?

JUNO (slowly) Salt. Coming in on the flood.

Sound: radio crackle

PELL (on radio) Ninth Hour, Ninth Hour, this is Reach Harbor. Pell.

JUNO Reach Harbor, Ninth Hour. Go ahead.

PELL (on radio) You outbound through the strait?

JUNO Tow of spoil for the east dock. The dredge owes me four hundred dollars.

PELL (on radio) Child, I've known you to turn down four thousand. Listen to me now. The autumn surge is up early and it's not gentle. Came in the mouth this morning pushing two knots against the ebb. Flood tonight and it'll stay through morning. That's forty hours of bad water. Forty, Juno.

JUNO I'm hearing you.

PELL (on radio) If it keeps up, the salt line's going to kiss the estuary mouth. Seawater's heavy. It slides in under the fresh like a thief and it doesn't leave with the tide. My wells draw off the lens, and the lens is all this town drinks between here and the ridge.

JUNO I know what's under Kestrel Reach, Pell.

PELL (on radio) My grandfather dug the first well. I won't hand it to my granddaughter gone to salt. Forty hours. You write it down.

JUNO Will do. If the surge meets a wreck, I'll charge double.

PELL (on radio) Your father's mouth. God rest him. Reach Harbor out.

Teo clips the barge papers to the board and watches her set the mic down.

TEO You didn't tell him.

JUNO Tell him what?

TEO About the letter.

JUNO He's got a town to keep. Not my debts.

TEO You carry it in your pocket like a shell you found. All right. So long as I'm the one who knows.

JUNO You're the one who knows.

She gives the old diesel a touch more throttle and the tug steadies. The fog thins at the mouth and the reef shows as a line of white that comes and goes.

Teo climbs to the ladder.

TEO I'll be below. Let me know if anything interesting washes by.

The Bellmouth, midday

Ambience: fast water breaking on shoal, gulls

The channel is narrow as a jug's neck here, running hard with the flood. Pale fog stands off to the westward. The reef's white line shows and hides. No sound but the water.

On screen: Juno (turned half to the radio, frowning)

Sound: static, then a flat, formal voice

VOSS (on radio) —Meridian Dawn, Meridian Dawn to any vessel. We are aground on the Bellmouth reef. Position follows.

The static eats the numbers. The voice returns, steady as a doctor reading a chart.

VOSS (on radio) We are on the reef. Requesting assistance. We are the freighter Meridian Dawn.

On screen: Teo (up the ladder fast, red cap crooked)

TEO That's the Halyard boat. That's a freighter on the Bellmouth and your surge coming tonight and we're dragging a barge of dredge muck.

JUNO Find the charts.

He stays put; the charts are already under her hand, in the drawer where they have always been. She pulls them out one-handed and puts the wheel over a quarter turn.

TEO (quietly) Juno. The barge.

JUNO The dock'll take it floating. Nobody loses a load of mud.

Sound: radio click

JUNO East dock, East dock, this is Ninth Hour. I'm leaving your spoil barge tied and floating at the north buoy. There's a freighter hard aground on the Bellmouth. You want your barge, send a man for it. And you tell your office the four hundred's off my contract, not theirs to keep. I'll answer for the drag. Log it.

DOCK VOICE (on radio, tired) You're leaving a loaded barge at a buoy?

JUNO I'm leaving her tied and floating and I'll answer for it. Reach is going to hear about a lot tonight.

She hangs the mic. Her hand wants to shake and she keeps it still.

TEO There. Now it costs you something and I believe you. Say the next part right. Not the bank. Say it because it's true.

JUNO A freighter on that reef with the surge coming will break her back and empty her guts into the strait. Whatever she carries runs into our estuary and then into every cup in Kestrel Reach. I want her off that rock before the salt line comes in. If the court pays me, good. If it doesn't, I'll still sleep. That's the truth and there's no better one. Now stoke her.

TEO Then that's what you get.

He is gone down the ladder before she picks up the mic.

JUNO Reach Harbor, Ninth Hour. We're taking the spoil off contract and running for the reef. Meridian Dawn's aground.

PELL (on radio) God and his angels. Juno, there's a reef and a surge and you're one tug.

JUNO I've got the tug. I've got the reef. I've got Teo. I claim her, Pell. I want it on the log before anyone else gets there and makes it a race.

PELL (on radio) I'll log it. High-order salvage's a fool's wage tonight. If the surge meets that freighter you'll be working surf in the dark.

JUNO I know it.

PELL (on radio) Think about your father before you go out there. Not what he'd do. What he'd say if you came home wrong.

JUNO He'd say I came home.

PELL (on radio) That's what I'm afraid of. Reach Harbor out.

Juno clicks over. Static, and under it, waiting, that flat and formal voice.

JUNO Meridian Dawn, Meridian Dawn, this is salvage tug Ninth Hour. We're inbound from the south through the Bellmouth. One tug, one crew. Hold your people together. We're coming.

A long space. The static breathes.

VOSS (on radio) Understood, Ninth Hour. We'd about given up on being seen.

JUNO We see you.

She hangs the mic and puts the wheel over and drives her father's tug out of the channel, into the fog, with the letter folded against her ribs and the tide running under her, cold and fast and rising.

On screen: Teo (at the hatch, looking up at her)

TEO What did she sound like?

JUNO Calm. Too calm for a woman on a reef.

TEO That's what worries me. Calm water's water that's been somewhere.

Picture: Juno at the Ninth Hour's wheel, bow turning into the gray fog off the reef, Teo braced in the engine-room hatch below her

She holds the wheel with both hands and keeps her eyes on the fog.
