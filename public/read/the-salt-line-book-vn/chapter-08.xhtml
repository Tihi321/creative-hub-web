Charges

Kestrel Reach, morning

Ambience: wind off the harbor, gulls, a boat knocking at a pier

The wharf steps are wet and the light is flat and gray. The fish house behind them smells of ice and diesel. Boats work at their moorings, and the water in the harbor is brown with the last of the flood.

On screen: Juno (good coat, wind in her hair, reading), Pell (green jacket, hands behind his back)

Pell holds out the printed page rather than hand it over.

PELL A thing like this ought to be handed to you. Not read off a screen in a room by yourself.

Juno takes it and reads it with her thumb holding the wind down.

Close-up: a county notice on stiff paper, a company name at the head, then a list of charges in small print, and below the company's name four names of men

Illegal dumping of listed hazardous waste. Sixty drums. False manifest. Conspiracy to conceal. Four names below the company's, and she knows two of them.

JUNO Voss.

PELL Fully cooperated. Her statement's the spine of the whole thing, they tell me. She's been at the courthouse since Tuesday, giving it over and over, and she does not flinch from it. Her mate's done the same.

JUNO And the others?

PELL Witnesses. Fen's told them everything she knows twice. The boy's told them about the beating. Barrow's lawyered up and gone quiet, which is his right.

JUNO The drums are the case.

PELL The drums are the case. Your engineer's sample went to the court chemist sealed and witnessed, and it's SX-9 to the last decimal and no lime in it anywhere. They've got a barge that came alongside her in the dark. They've got the manifest that names the reef and doesn't name the barge. It's going to hold.

Juno reads the four names again and finds she feels nothing much for them.

JUNO Good.

PELL And it's going to take you into that courtroom with it. You found it and opened it and beached it, and they'll want you to say so in your own words.

JUNO Then I'll say it.

PELL Thursday. Wear the good coat.

Bellmouth courthouse, afternoon

Ambience: a radiator knocking, paper turning, a pen scratching

A low brick room with a green ceiling and tall windows that lay gray shapes of light on the floor. Benches, a raised bench, a brass bell on the clerk's desk. The fog outside is a blank wall.

On screen: Juno (good coat, ship's watch at her throat, hands flat on her knees), the examiner (narrow, silver-haired, unhurried), the company's lawyer (heavy, papers stacked before him)

The witness chair is hard and too tall, and Juno keeps both feet flat on the floor and decides not to think about it.

EXAMINER In your own words, captain. From the mayday.

JUNO The mayday came in the fog. I had a barge on tow and four hundred dollars riding on it. I cut the barge loose at the north buoy and ran for the reef.

EXAMINER And when you came alongside?

JUNO Her boats were swung out and empty. Her bridge was dark. I hailed her three times and got no face at the rail. I put my hook over her on the belief she was abandoned.

EXAMINER Then you found her crew.

JUNO Three of them in the forepeak. Their stories didn't agree and they'd not name their master. The logbook's last pages were torn out. The half-entry that was left I've got by heart.

EXAMINER Say it.

JUNO Course altered west. At master's order, no consul.

EXAMINER And the lower hold?

JUNO A panel bolted and hasped and painted over, and a smell round the edge of it that was not lime. My engineer cut it. Sixty drums behind it, and half of them weeping.

EXAMINER Describe the film.

JUNO The color of a frog's back. Thin as oil. It moved on the bilge water when the ship worked, and the smell of it sat in the back of your throat and stayed.

EXAMINER When you understood what was in that hold, what did you do?

JUNO I put my line on her bow and took her into the estuary.

EXAMINER You did not tow her out to sea.

JUNO No.

EXAMINER What did you understand would follow from that choice?

JUNO That I'd lose the claim. A ship beached by the hand that claimed her is not a ship saved from the sea. I knew it on the reef. I knew the law would call her a ruin I'd made myself.

LAWYER Objection. The witness characterises the law to suit herself.

JUDGE (off-screen) Overruled.

EXAMINER You knew the law would look at you that way. Why, then?

JUNO Because the drums don't float when the sea takes them. They sink, and what runs out of them comes up the estuary with the flood and down into the lens, and the lens is the only water this town has. I had an hour and one decision. I'd rather live in it with the wells fresh.

EXAMINER Would you do it again?

JUNO Yes.

She hears the small dry scratch of the pen and does not look at the lawyer.

Bellmouth courthouse, afternoon

Ambience: the radiator knocking, benches creaking

The company's lawyer stands with his papers and takes his time. In the back row Pell shifts forward, and Juno gives her head the smallest shake, and he sits back.

On screen: the company's lawyer (on his feet, three pages), Juno (hands still flat on her knees), Pell (back row, jaw set)

LAWYER You lodged your claim before you knew the state of her holds.

JUNO I did.

LAWYER You lodged it knowing the vessel was not abandoned.

JUNO I lodged it on a deck with no master on it and no answer to my hail. When I learned better, I put it in the log myself.

LAWYER And your tug was in arrears at the time. Four payments.

JUNO Four payments. The bank had forty days on me.

LAWYER You had a reason to want a prize.

JUNO I had a reason to want anything that paid the note. I'd have claimed a rusty bucket. That's the truth of it, and the truth of it doesn't change what was in that hold or what I did with it.

The lawyer studies his papers and asks nothing more.

Kestrel Reach, morning

Ambience: wind off the harbor, a pump ticking somewhere

The fish house steps again, colder now. The harbor is gray and empty of boats. Two gulls fight over something on the quay and give it up.

On screen: Juno (good coat, ruling in her hand), Pell (beside her, hands deep in his pockets)

Close-up: a printed ruling, folded and unfolded and read more than once, the finding set out in close lines with two phrases underlined

The court finds the vessel grounded by her master's deliberate act. The services were voluntary and of great merit and averted a grave danger to life, property, and the public water supply. On the law, no salvage award is due, no property having been preserved within the meaning of the articles.

Juno reads it a fourth time. It has not changed.

JUNO No award.

PELL Not one dollar.

JUNO The paragraph about the water. Does it pay at the bank?

PELL No, child. It doesn't.

JUNO Then it's worth what a paragraph is worth.

She folds it and puts it in her coat, and goes down the wharf without a word to a soul on the way.

Ninth Hour, morning

Ambience: water slapping the pilings, a diesel breathing and failing

The cabin is gray through the salt on the glass. The stove has no fire in it. The bank's first letter lies flat on the chart table with the creases gone soft from handling, and beside it Teo's pencil sums on the back of an old tide table.

On screen: Juno (coat still on, letter in front of her), Teo (back against the doorframe, arms folded, black to the elbows)

TEO The boat's not the bank's yet.

JUNO Don't. Not that. We've said it seven years and it was true every year but this one, and this year it sounds like hope and it isn't.

He comes and looks down at the sums and puts a black finger on the column she has added twice.

TEO There's a fifth payment now. A fee for the second notice. Twenty-two dollars and a half. Smallest thing in the whole business and I've stared at it all morning like it was the one that sank us.

JUNO Twenty-two dollars of fee and the whole rest still owed. It's a stone in a landslide.

TEO We've nine hundred and forty in the world. A cracked head, a burnt winding, and a ruling that says what you did wasn't salvage. I've done it every way there is. There's nothing in it.

JUNO I know there's nothing in it.

She folds the letter along its old crease, tears it in four, and puts the pieces in the cold stove.

TEO (quiet) Tomorrow, then.

JUNO Tomorrow we light the stove.

TEO And today?

JUNO Today you put the number one pump back together and I put my coat on a hook.

Reach Harbor, morning

Ambience: a radio hissing, the tide board ticking, a kettle not yet boiling

The office smells of paper and old varnish. The window shows the harbor full of rain. Pell's desk is a landscape of files. He opens a folder, holds up a sheet, and reads a pencilled word off the top of it aloud.

On screen: Pell (a sheet in his hand, reading glasses on), Juno (wet from the street, standing), Doran Rusk (seen only as a name on a page)

PELL Withdrawn.

He lays the paper down flat.

PELL He filed it before he stood on the wall, and the clerk says he paid his fees and took his papers and told them he was done with the Bellmouth. The Gannet's gone out the estuary. A man doesn't withdraw and sail off unless he means it.

JUNO Then it's done.

PELL It'll sit in the county register all the same. Some clerk in an office who never saw the wall will read it and ask why a man withdrew and there'll be no good answer. A grudge doesn't have to be carried by the man who made it. It just waits in a file.

JUNO Let it wait. The worst thing in that file is a company that put drums on a reef, and they've got lawyers.

Pell turns the sheet over twice and sets it down.

PELL He said a true thing on the wall. That he'd have towed them to sea because it paid. He didn't have to say it.

JUNO He said it because it was true, and because he wanted it in my head the way it was in his. He was right about what he'd do. He was wrong about what I'd do. I don't need to hear him say so to be done with him.

Pell puts the sheet away in a drawer and shuts it and takes off his glasses.

PELL Town meeting Thursday. The notice is on the door. It says nothing about salvage or Rusk or your tug, because I wrote it and I know what to leave out of a notice.

He looks at her a moment.

PELL You'll be there.

JUNO I'll be there. I've been at every one.

Kestrel Reach, evening

Ambience: rain on a tin roof, benches scraping, a crowd settling

The meeting house is full to the back wall with people in wet coats. The lamps are lit early. Rain runs down the tall windows and the room smells of wet wool and seaweed and coffee.

On screen: Pell (at the front, book in his hand, open), Juno (near the door, hands in her lap), the boatyard man (oilskins, near the aisle), the council woman (on the platform, pen ready)

Pell does not read from the book.

PELL I'll not go over the ruling. You all know the shape of it. The court says she wasn't a salvor, and the law says a salvor's what you have to be to get paid, and there's men in Bellmouth who'll tell you that adds up to her doing wrong.

He puts the book down.

PELL It doesn't add up to that, and I'll say what I saw. I saw that woman take a ship onto the flats in a rising sea to put her drums above the water and her wells above the salt. I saw her lose the prize and not say a word about it while she did it. I watched her flood the ship she'd come to save, because it was the only way under heaven to keep the drums dry and the lens fresh.

He straightens.

PELL I stood on that wall and read the gauges. The lens held. It held because of what she decided in the dark with no sleep and nothing in it for her. This harbor has told the world for a hundred years that a town is a thing you keep. That's what she did. Kestrel Reach keeps her. I move the council say so.

On screen: the council woman (writing), the crowd (hands going up), Juno (near the door, not moving)

The council says so, and the vote is not close.

PELL The boatyard.

On screen: the boatyard man (standing, red to the ears, looking at the floor)

BOATYARD MAN I sandbagged beside her all night. I saw her at the wall before the light. Boatyard'll stand her a haul-out and a slip at cost, whenever she wants it.

He sits down again before anyone can thank him.

On screen: the woman from the fish house (standing, tin in her hand), the well man (on the aisle, hat on his knee)

FISH HOUSE WOMAN The fish house takes a subscription. Name and sum in the tin.

WELL MAN I'll have the school test its water every Friday and write it in a book, so no one's in doubt again.

Juno sits with her hands under her coat where no one can see them shake, and does not stand up, and does not speak.

Kestrel Reach, night

Ambience: rain easing, water lapping a pier, a lamp humming

The street is wet and the lights lie in the puddles. At the end of the wharf the fish pier lamp is lit and lays a yellow road on the water, and the road breaks and makes itself again. Out past the flats, the Dawn lies in the mud.

On screen: Juno (collar up, walking), Pell (beside her, cap down against the rain)

PELL A subscription's a fine thing on a Thursday night. It's the Tuesday morning I'll be watching.

JUNO It'll be what it is. Whatever comes Tuesday, you did a thing tonight that wasn't owed to me.

PELL It was owed. I've watched salvage come and go forty years. I've seen men paid for less than you did and better men paid nothing, and the law's never once come out where the water was. So a town said its say tonight and wrote it down. Something comes of it, or something doesn't. That's how it goes in a harbor.

They come to the end of the wharf. The Ninth Hour lies alongside the fish pier, scored and black-stacked and listing to port, and the engine room glows yellow through her portholes.

PELL There's a thing I've been meaning to ask and I'll ask it plain, because we're standing in the cold and neither of us has the patience for the long way. If the council were to ask the bank what it would take in cash to make them whole and let the boat go—

He stops himself.

PELL I've said more than I meant to. There's an item coming up at the next meeting and it's not for me to tell you what's in it. Be there.

Juno does not look away from the water. She learned long ago not to build a house on a man's half-sentence.

On screen: Teo (at the rail, coiling a line, then looking up)

JUNO I'll be there. I've been at every one.

TEO She'll want a slip before she's good for anything. Whatever else you're about, harbor master, put that in your book.

PELL It's in the book. It's been in the book since the flats.

Teo goes below and the glow of the engine room moves along the portholes.

Picture: Juno and Pell at the end of the fish pier at night, the wet street lights behind them, the old scored tug lit yellow from inside where Teo works below, and out past the flats the black outline of the beached Meridian Dawn against the gray

Juno stands in the cold and watches the lit portholes of her father's tug.

JUNO Tuesday, then.

Pell pulls his cap down and goes up the street to his supper. She stays until the lamp on the fish pier goes out, and the water goes dark, and the only light left is the one in the old tug's belly.
