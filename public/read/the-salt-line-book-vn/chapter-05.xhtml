The Drums

Meridian Dawn, evening

Ambience: water working against the hull, wind along the rail

The fog sits low and gray overhead. The foredeck is slick. Aft, the Iron Gannet's winch turns and turns, and her wire hangs slack. Voss stands at the break of the foredeck, coat buttoned to the throat, hands at her sides.

On screen: Ines Voss (still as a bollard), Juno (stopping a pace off), Teo (at the deckhouse corner, sample crate under his arm)

JUNO Sixty drums. Half of them weeping. The stencil says SX-9. Your bilge is lifting that film and putting it over your rail.

Voss says nothing.

JUNO Now you tell me what's in my hold. Or I take it off you in front of your crew and a harbormaster.

VOSS It's not your hold.

JUNO It's my salvage.

VOSS Then salvage the ship. Leave what's not the ship alone.

JUNO Can't. It's wet, and it goes up-channel if she tears open. You knew that when I found the panel.

JUNO I've been offered money to sink my own claim. I've been given until nightfall. I've had three lies from three of your people. I'll not stand here and watch a master watch her own ship poison a town's water and won't say why. What are the drums?

Voss's eyes go to the open hatch. It is the first thing she has looked at directly all evening.

VOSS They're not lime, they're—

Her jaw sets. Something behind her face closes like a fist.

VOSS I'll not say. Not to you and not to the court.

JUNO You'll not say.

VOSS I'll tell you this much. The drums are not the ship and not the cargo and not salvage. Take my ship off this reef and you take them with her, and you'll answer for that to people who won't ask your name first.

JUNO Better for who, captain?

Voss gives her nothing.

Sound: a winch paying out wire, then the crack of a messenger line hitting steel

Across the water the Gannet's men are running. The messenger line slaps the Dawn's stern deck. The wheelhouse radio cracks.

RUSK (on radio) Salvage tug Ninth Hour, this is the Iron Gannet. My line goes on that wreck's stern in ten minutes. Have your people clear.

JUNO (into handset) Iron Gannet, Ninth Hour. Keep your line off this ship. I'm her salvor of record.

RUSK (on radio) You're welcome to it in a courtroom in a year. What'll matter is where she was when the water came. I'm putting her out past the shelf where the bad in her can sink deep and quiet.

JUNO You've never once put your head down that hatch.

RUSK (on radio) Ten minutes, captain. Iron Gannet out.

Sound: radio click

On screen: Juno (handset in her fist), Teo (in the door, gloves on)

TEO He thinks it's lime and a scuttling job.

JUNO He'll know in ten minutes, or we're all in it with him. I'm not cutting his line and I've not the horses to out-pull the Gannet.

TEO Then what?

JUNO My line goes on her bow. I take her south into the estuary and beach her on the west flats where there's mud to hold her. Let him tow her stern to sea after that.

TEO South channel's narrow. She touches going over, she goes sideways, and the drums go in.

JUNO Then she doesn't touch. Seal the lower hold with the board in the partition. Set the portable pump with the discharge led back into her own deep tanks.

TEO Partition's not a fit. There's a gap where the tank line runs through. Two fingers wide.

JUNO Then block the gap. With whatever holds water and isn't us. Move, engineer.

Meridian Dawn, evening

Ambience: a heavy diesel throttling up, water breaking white

The braid comes over the rail and slaps the deck, monkey's fist and all. Juno has the axe half out of the rack before her hands know what they want.

On screen: Teo (turning the monkey's fist over in his glove), Juno (hand on the axe handle)

TEO You're taking it.

JUNO I'm taking it and I'll give it back to him point first. Lead his braid back to my winch. He wants to pull. I'll have him pull where I point.

TEO That's either the cleverest thing you've said all day or the last.

He loops the line over his shoulder and goes forward.

JUNO Bow bollard. Winch. Go.

Meridian Dawn, evening

Ambience: a winch pressure easing, wind snapping a coat

Juno comes to the wheelhouse door and stops with her hand on the latch. Behind the salt-grayed glass the chart table lies under its green cloth, and the wheel shows brass bright where hands have worn it.

On screen: Ines Voss (in the doorway as if it belonged to someone else), Juno (opening the door)

JUNO Come in. Shut the door.

Voss comes in and shuts it. The reef hums up through the plates. She stands at the forward glass with her hand flat on it and will not sit and will not turn.

JUNO Say your piece. Say it plain, before that wire is over my bow and I've no more time to ask.

VOSS (voice gone plain and worn) I ran her on the reef.

Juno says nothing.

VOSS On purpose. I had my boat out to sound the rock three weeks before we sailed. I brought her west at one in the morning with a north lamp behind me and no one to fetch it. She struck at ten minutes past the hour and she held. There's no fog in this. There's me.

JUNO Your mate?

VOSS In it with me. He's ashore now with the rest. The owners don't know. They think the reef took me in the fog.

JUNO Why.

VOSS Halyard Shipping. Thirteen years on the north run and never lost a deckhand. In the spring they put drums aboard for the lower run. Agricultural waste, they said. Then a man came aboard with a rubber apron on him and put his hand on a drum like a man on a sick beast.

JUNO What did he say?

VOSS Keep them off the heat. Don't let the seams go wet. If one weeps, put it over the side in deep water and don't log it.

JUNO So you looked.

VOSS I took a sample the way you took one. My brother-in-law runs a bench in the city. SX-9. A degreaser. The kind of thing a plant makes and can't get rid of at home, so it goes in a ship and the ship opens her sea-cocks past the shelf and the deep has it and the manifest says lime and everybody sleeps.

JUNO And you reported it.

VOSS I wrote it out and gave it to the coast office, and in three weeks my brother-in-law was out of a job and had a letter from a lawyer. Then Halyard's man came aboard and said the company was pleased with me.

Her voice holds level.

VOSS Then they told me the Dawn was for sale once the last run was made, and would I take her out one more time. I knew what the last run was. Sixty drums and a manifest that said lime, and the plan was the shelf and the sea-cocks and no ship to come home. Either way, no drums.

JUNO And you came west.

VOSS If she went aground with the drums in her, they'd be found, and a drum found on a shore has to be dealt with where there are courts and cameras and no deep water to hide it. I meant it to be found by a tow with a nose for a lie. And it was.

Juno takes the half-entry out of her coat and holds it up.

JUNO Course altered west. At master's order, no consul.

VOSS I wrote it and tore out the page before it so one day someone would know it wasn't the fog that did it.

JUNO Why this coast? Why my estuary?

VOSS Because a narrow strait makes the news and a port wall doesn't. Because I knew the rock would hold her. And because I chose your town for what it would cost other people. I'll not dress it.

Juno folds the paper away.

JUNO The drums don't go to sea. They don't go into the deep and they don't go up my estuary. I'm taking this ship onto the west flats, where a court can come and take them off her in daylight. You'll take the wheel, or I'll take her off this rock with you tied to the rail.

VOSS You'd do it.

JUNO I'd do it.

VOSS Then I'll take the wheel. And you'll have my word the bulkhead holds to the bar. There's no lying about a bulkhead. It holds or it doesn't.

JUNO That's the first thing you've said I've trusted all evening.

Meridian Dawn, evening

Ambience: a winch turning, water breaking white

Rusk stands forward with two of his crew, hands behind his back, studying the reef like a builder studying a bad foundation. The braid lies on the bow where Teo has made it fast.

On screen: Doran Rusk (boots apart, coat buttoned to the chin), Juno (coming up the deck), Ines Voss (at the break of the foredeck)

RUSK You've got my line on your bow and a freighter groaning under you and you've not moved her an inch. I've been patient.

JUNO You've been ten minutes.

RUSK The company's man's been on the phone since six. They want her off this reef and out past the shelf by morning, and I'm the man with the horses.

He puts his arm out at the water beyond the bar.

RUSK That ship's got something bad in her and bad wants deep water. We put her over past the shelf and what's in her stops being anybody's problem.

JUNO No tow on this ship out past the shelf.

RUSK A court. What court hears a broke tug with a bank letter in her drawer?

JUNO The sample doesn't miss payments. And I'd have you hear what's in that hold from her own mouth.

Rusk looks at Voss. Voss looks back at him, steady.

RUSK Then say it to me, master. What's in the hold?

VOSS Sixty drums of SX-9. My owners loaded them as agricultural lime and told me to open the sea-cocks past the shelf and not log it.

RUSK You're telling me you took a contract of lime.

VOSS I'm telling you the manifest is a lie and I knew it. I ran my own ship onto that reef so the drums would be found where there are courts instead of trenches.

Rusk looks at the drums' hatch and at the braid on his bow.

RUSK Then if I tow her out, I'm not scuttling a wreck. I'm drowning evidence.

JUNO You are.

RUSK I don't like being made the last to know.

JUNO Then don't be. Take your wire off this bow and stand by. Choose it, Rusk. You've ten minutes' worth of choosing left and we've less.

RUSK They'll pull my contract.

JUNO Then they'll pull it, and you'll have the harbormaster's log to show why.

Sound: a horn, low and long, off the estuary mouth

Every head on the foredeck comes round. A pilot boat comes out of the Bellmouth channel with white at her stem, and the town's small boats strung out behind her.

On screen: Pell (in the boat's bow, bullhorn raised, boots wet)

PELL Ahoy the Dawn. Ahoy all. This is the harbormaster of Kestrel Reach. I'm coming aboard. There'll be no tow put on this vessel and no line cut on her until I've had my say. I'll be aboard in ten minutes.

Rusk looks at the town's boats coming on, and then at Juno.

JUNO Ten minutes.

She turns her back on him and goes to the rail to meet the boat.

Meridian Dawn, night

Ambience: a heavy diesel under load, water breaking against a bow

Dark has come down flat and gray. The estuary mouth opens ahead, the bar low and pale, the town's roofs beyond it. Rusk's braid has been taken off and run as a spring to the Ninth Hour's winch. Pell comes up the ladder, notebook out, pencil wet.

On screen: Pell (jacket soaked, notebook open), Juno (turning from the glass), Ines Voss (at the wheel), Teo (in the wheelhouse door, wet to the elbows)

PELL Claim's lodged. Sample's in my satchel, sealed, tape written in your engineer's hand and mine. I'll put the hour down.

JUNO Put down the sample came off drum one, lower hold, and the master identified the code.

PELL I'll put down it came off a drum and the master said SX-9. The rest the court can ask her.

PELL Tide's making on the bar. She'll be over it or not inside the half hour.

JUNO Then tell the town's boats to stand clear of the channel mouth.

PELL I'll tell them. And Juno. The town's already at the mouth with sandbags. If the drums go in, sandbags won't hold it.

JUNO Then they won't go in.

PELL Your father would have said the same thing at the same hour and been just as wrong or just as right. Bring her in.

He goes. Voss puts the wheel over, a quarter turn, slow.

VOSS She's coming round. The reef's letting her go.

JUNO Tell me when she's clear.

VOSS Line's taking the strain.

TEO Engine's pulling. Listen to her.

Under everything, the old diesel takes the weight and holds it.

JUNO Take her slow. Hold her head to the swell and let the bosun work.

Picture: Meridian Dawn coming off the Bellmouth reef at night under the Ninth Hour's tow, her bow swinging south toward the estuary mouth, the pilot boat and the town's small boats standing off the bar, her decks alive with men working a mat over the shoulder

The white water goes out from her bow in a slow ring, and she floats, and she answers her wheel.

VOSS She's off the rock.

JUNO Say that again.

VOSS She's off the rock.

On screen: Juno (taking up the handset), Ines Voss (not looking from the glass), Teo (grinning despite himself)

JUNO Iron Gannet, Ninth Hour. She's off the rock and she's under my tow and she's going into the estuary to be beached on the west flats. Keep your wire off her.

RUSK (on radio) You've not heard the last of this, captain.

JUNO Neither have you.

She puts the set down. Ahead the estuary mouth opens, gray water and the long low bar. The freighter's bow comes round to the channel slow as a held breath.

JUNO Take her over slow. Not a touch. Not one.

VOSS Not one.

The town's lights wait beyond the bar, small and yellow and very close.
