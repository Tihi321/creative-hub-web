The Reef

The Bellmouth, afternoon

Ambience: fast water breaking on the reef, wind through freighter rigging

Fog sits thick on the strait. The reef shows as a line of white teeth that open and close. The Meridian Dawn lies across it sidelong, bow lifted and hung on the reef's shoulder, stern swinging in deep water. Paint flakes off her in pale scabs and rust weeps down her flank. Her deck cants to starboard, just enough to be wrong.

On screen: Juno (at the wheel, eyes narrowed), Teo (at the rail, head cocked)

Juno eases the throttle to bare steerage and creeps up on the white water.

TEO There she is. Lying on it like a drunk on a curb.

JUNO Drunks roll off curbs.

TEO This one might. No slick, no bubbles at her waterline. She's not holed bad.

JUNO Yet.

TEO Yet. The surge comes in and works her, it'll open her like a can.

Juno puts the wheel over and brings the tug in along the freighter's starboard side, wide of the reef.

TEO I'll take the fenders.

He swings them over by their lines, one by one, and the two hulls come together with a soft heavy knock. The old tug shudders up through the wheel and into Juno's wrists.

JUNO Meridian Dawn, Ninth Hour alongside. Put a ladder over.

No face shows over the freighter's rail. The fog moves across her derricks. Aft, a line slaps a mast.

JUNO Say again. Freighter Meridian Dawn, this is the tug alongside. Show me a face.

On screen: Teo (looking up, rag hanging from his hand), Juno (stepping to the door)

TEO Juno. The bridge windows are dark.

JUNO Both of them. And the boats are swung out and empty.

TEO Deck's bare. Could be they're below with a radio that's died.

JUNO Could be. We'll go and find out.

She hauls herself up the boathook line and comes over the rail.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: iron grinding on rock, low and slow

The deck is wet and cold and it moves underfoot, a slow grind of iron against rock that comes up through the soles. The bow is hung high, so the deck slopes uphill toward the forepeak. Fog thins here. The anchor windlass shows through it, and a winch drum shining with old grease.

On screen: Juno (crouched, listening), Teo (coming over the rail behind her)

Sound: a small board moved, then a hush

Juno goes still. Forward, behind the windlass, a steel door is set in the deckhouse. It stands not quite shut, a black line of gap along its edge, the width of a breath.

TEO (low) You hear that?

JUNO I heard it.

TEO Somebody's in there, and they don't want to be found.

JUNO Then let's not sound like the thing they're afraid of.

She walks forward and stops short of the door and lets the silence run.

JUNO Meridian Dawn, tug alongside. Don't open that door onto a scared man. I haven't got a gun, and my engineer doesn't like them. Come out where I can see you.

The door stays shut. Then it opens, just enough, and three faces look out in a stack, one above the other.

On screen: Barrow (old, wiry, bald, weathered as a fence post), Fen (work coat, gray brows, staring at the planks), Davy (split lip, one eye bruised, gone blank)

Barrow comes out first and plants himself in front of the other two on the sloped deck.

BARROW Who are you?

JUNO Juno Halloway. Salvage tug Ninth Hour. I heard your mayday, and I've come to help if you'll let me.

She tips her chin at Fen.

JUNO Are you hurt? Anyone below?

FEN No. Not hurt.

JUNO Then come out of there. What's your name?

BARROW Barrow. Bosun. Or what's left of one.

JUNO Bosun. Where's your crew?

BARROW Gone.

FEN Ashore.

Davy says nothing. The two words hang in the air like birds flown the wrong way past each other.

JUNO Gone. Ashore. Which is it?

BARROW It was dark.

JUNO Dark or not, you know your own crew. Where's the master?

The three of them look at one another. Nobody is weighing what is true. They are weighing what to say.

BARROW Can't say.

JUNO You can't say where your master is.

BARROW That's right.

JUNO Her name's Voss. I heard her on the radio last night. She asked for help. Is she aboard now?

BARROW We can't say.

Davy turns a little, as if to hide the split lip and the bruise. Fen looks away from him, too fast.

JUNO You can't say. You were asleep when the ship struck. You don't know where your people went. You were hiding in the forepeak when a ship that asked for help heard nothing back.

Nobody speaks. Overhead, a gull complains at the fog.

JUNO There's a tide coming and a surge behind it, and your freighter's on a reef. I've a tug alongside and an engineer who can keep her afloat, if you'll tell me the truth. If a lie's what you've got, I'll take it. But I won't like it, and I'll remember it when the harbor master writes down who said what.

BARROW We were asleep. There was a noise. The ship went over. There was no one about, so we went in the forepeak to wait. That's all.

JUNO That's all.

BARROW That's all.

Teo comes over the rail with the pump gear on his shoulder and a coil of hose on the other.

On screen: Teo (hose over his arm, looking at the three of them), Juno (watching Teo)

TEO Nobody's telling you anything.

JUNO They were asleep.

TEO Everyone's always asleep. Give me an hour. I'll sound this hull and tell you what the reef has done to her.

JUNO Do it. I'll take her log.

TEO A master doesn't leave her ship aground and go quiet. Not with a radio working and a tug coming. She said hold on. And now she's not here and these three are.

JUNO Hull first. People second.

TEO Hull first.

He goes aft with the hose dragging behind him.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: fog against glass, a radio humming on emergency batteries

The wheelhouse smells of cold coffee and burned wiring, and under that something faint she cannot name. The windows are fogged. An empty mug has rolled across the chart table and left a brown ring on the chart. The radio console glows dim.

On screen: Juno (alone, coat dark with spray)

She stands over the chart and studies a penciled course laid hard and straight from the channel into the reef's north shoulder. A rule drawn across a page. A ship that lost her way would have wandered.

Close-up: the Meridian Dawn's logbook, open on its rack, a wad of pages torn out close to the binding and ragged paper tongues left behind

The last surviving page ends in a half-line: Course altered west. At master's order, no consul—

Juno turns the book over. The torn stubs hang down, still white and raw. Nobody tears ten pages clean with bare hands, and nobody does it long before today.

JUNO At master's order.

Teo comes up the ladder with his head down, his face the color of old paper under the soot.

On screen: Teo (grimy, soot to the wrist), Juno (logbook under her arm)

TEO Hull's sound. No breach wide enough to matter. Her bow's sitting on the rock like a man leaning on a fence, but the water's not coming in. I've got a suction in the bilge.

JUNO Then that's a salvage.

TEO That's a salvage. Juno, there's a thing.

JUNO What thing?

TEO There's no cargo. I opened the number two hatch. A freighter this size, bound for the east dock with lime, and her holds are empty as a church on Monday.

JUNO Empty.

TEO Swept clean. Boot marks in the dust where a load's come out. Whatever she carried came off in a hurry, and not at the east dock, because the east dock's not on the Bellmouth reef.

JUNO So she unloaded before she grounded. Or grounded to unload.

TEO And then the floor was scrubbed and the book was torn, and the bosun slept through all of it.

Juno looks at the torn edge of the logbook in her hand.

JUNO Can't say where the master is. Everything went dark. Asleep.

TEO Sleeping people don't hide in the forepeak.

JUNO No. They don't.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: a radio humming on emergency batteries

Juno brings the mic up and sets the channel. The radio finds Kestrel Reach, far off through the fog.

On screen: Juno (mic in hand, logbook under her arm)

JUNO Reach Harbor, Ninth Hour. I'm aboard the grounded freighter Meridian Dawn. I want it logged.

PELL (on radio) Logged how?

JUNO Salvage. First salvor on scene. I came alongside believing her abandoned. Deck bare when I hailed, bridge dark, boats swung out and empty. I put my hook over her rail on that belief, and I want that belief in the log.

A pause on the channel, and the sound of a man setting down a thing he knows will be read again.

PELL (on radio) Fog on the strait, deck empty, boats swung out, no answer to hail. Written. And then?

JUNO Then I found three of her crew in the forepeak. The bosun and two hands. Not one of them will name her master.

PELL (on radio) Hear me now, and hear me once. A salvor who boards a crewed ship needs the master's say. Every dollar of your claim will lean on that boarding. If it comes out that you knew men were aboard before your hook touched her rail, you'll be arguing from the mud.

JUNO I didn't know it. I know it now.

PELL (on radio) Then keep the two facts apart. Put down what you saw from your wheelhouse and put the crew in behind it. I'll log the fog and the boats and the hour you came alongside. And you'll write everything down yourself, the times and the names. Maritime court will read it.

JUNO I'll write it.

PELL (on radio) And Juno. The surge is still running. My tide board shows it arriving at the flood tonight and staying through morning. Forty hours of bad water, and it wants to push up the estuary on the first of them.

JUNO Tonight.

PELL (on radio) Tonight. So if you mean to save that freighter, do it before the salt line comes in. Once it's in, the only thing she's worth is what the court says she's worth. And the court won't care about Kestrel Reach. It won't care about your four payments either.

Sound: radio click

The channel falls quiet.

On screen: Teo (on the foredeck below, coiling hose), Barrow (at the rail with Fen and Davy, the three of them close together)

Juno stands in the dark wheelhouse. Through the fogged glass she can see Teo on the foredeck, and the three sailors by the rail like people sorry to be there, and beyond them the rocks showing white where the tide works the swells, and the gray rising water.

She puts the logbook inside her coat, against the bank letter, and goes down to the deck.

JUNO Teo. How long to get a tow line on her stern and pull her off that reef?

Picture: Juno on the Meridian Dawn's canted foredeck, the three silent crew behind her at the rail and Teo before her with the hose, and the reef breaking white all around the stranded hull

TEO Tonight? With the surge coming? Depends what she does when the tide lifts her, and I won't know that till it does.

JUNO Then we'll know it together.

TEO And if the master's not aboard to give the word?

Juno looks back at Barrow, who does not look at her.

JUNO Then we'll have to find out what a bosun's word is worth.
