Empty Holds

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: water slapping the hull, a low iron grinding on rock

Fog has come down through the open hatch and lies flat on the floor plates. The hold is empty, its steel walls a cold gray-blue. The deck slopes gently uphill toward the forepeak.

On screen: Juno (at the foot of the ladder, torch held low), Teo (coming down behind her, boots ringing)

Juno turns a slow circle with the beam. The plates are clean. Not new-clean. Scrubbed-clean, with a mop's memory in the corners and a watery shine where the last of it dried.

JUNO Teo. Look at that paint.

He crouches at the base of the wall and runs his thumb along a long scuff at knee height.

TEO Dragged. All the way out. A load that sat and settled doesn't leave marks like that.

JUNO No. That's a load that got pulled. One thing after another, and all of it to one end.

TEO The stern ramp's down. I saw it from the top. Lowered and left down.

JUNO Then that's how a thing came off her in a hurry.

Juno walks aft. Her boots echo and the echoes come back off the far bulkhead so she sounds like a woman walking toward herself.

TEO Plates are dry. Ship aground on a reef and her holds dry as a barn floor.

JUNO That surprises me too.

Close-up: a wide steel gate set into the after bulkhead, standing open, flat gray sea lying beyond it just below the floor plates, without a ripple

The sea lies calm in the end of the hold, still as water in a cellar.

TEO That water's not coming in through the ramp.

He crouches, puts his hand in it, smells it, then licks the back of his knuckle and makes a face.

TEO Fresh. Spray, or her own washing-water. The tide's not in her.

JUNO The tide's not in her, her holds are empty, her ramp is down, and her crew were asleep. That's four things in one morning.

TEO There's a fifth.

On screen: Teo (pointing at the floor plates), Juno (turning her beam that way)

TEO Boot marks. In the dust. The dust's the only honest thing on this ship.

The steel wears a fine gray film. In the film are traces: prints circling the ramp, a scuff where small shoes once stood still beside a pair of large boots.

JUNO A woman's been down here. And she stood and talked to a big one, and then they went.

TEO You're reading dust like a tea leaf.

JUNO I'm reading it the way a salvage court will read it.

Sound: a low grinding groan running through the plates

The deck shudders. The open ramp-water shivers, then goes still.

TEO (checking his watch) Tide's slackening toward the ebb. Two hours to turn, and the surge is behind it.

JUNO Then we haven't got a morning. We've got hours.

TEO I'll get the suction down here. You go and talk to your three sleeping beauties.

He hauls his hose past her, paying it out over the plates, and she climbs for the ladder.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: water breaking white on the reef, wind through the rigging

The three crew stand bunched at the forepeak rail. Beyond them the reef shows white and hides, shows white and hides. The fog is thinning to the west.

On screen: Barrow (arms folded, bald head bare to the fog), Fen (collar up, eyes on the reef), Davy (winding a rag around a winch drum), Juno (approaching)

BARROW Captain.

JUNO Bosun. I've been down your holds. Number two's empty, swept, and her stern ramp is down in the water.

Barrow says nothing.

JUNO There's dragged paint on the walls and a wet drag-mark in the dust that says a load came off her and went over the side in a hurry. The water in that hold is fresh.

Fen turns her head very slightly. Davy winds his rag, and winds it, and has run out of winch to wind.

JUNO You were asleep when the ship struck. That's what you told me, and I wrote it down. But that load didn't come off this hold while you slept in the forepeak. Someone would have heard it.

No answer.

JUNO Where was the Dawn bound?

BARROW East dock.

JUNO Her holds are empty. Her manifest will say lime, and it will be a lie, and I'll have written that you told me lime.

BARROW East dock.

JUNO When did the cargo come off?

BARROW Don't know.

JUNO Did you help unload her?

BARROW Don't know.

Fen breathes out through her nose, a small flat sound, the kind a person makes when something has gone past what they can stand.

FEN Barrow. For God's sake.

BARROW (not turning his head) Fen. Don't.

JUNO Fen. Yesterday you said ashore. When he said gone. Which of your people went ashore?

FEN I said it. It's still true.

JUNO Who went ashore?

FEN Ask me something I can answer.

JUNO Can you say where your master is?

DAVY (to the winch) She's aboard.

On screen: Davy (rag stopped in his hands), Barrow (turning, face gone hard), Fen (going still), Juno (watching them all)

The silence goes all the way around the foredeck and comes back.

BARROW You'll be quiet now, Davy.

DAVY She's aboard. She's been aboard the whole—

Barrow takes one step. Not far. Only the shift of a big lined body between the young man and Juno. Davy stops talking.

JUNO Master's aboard, then. That's a start. Where aboard?

Barrow's jaw sets. He says nothing, and the nothing is a wall, and he looks at her over the top of it and waits to be asked again.

JUNO All right. Then I'll ask the thing I came to ask. If I put a line on your stern and pull, does she come off whole, or do I tear her open and drown you?

Fen's hands come out of her pockets and grip the rail, and the knuckles go white.

BARROW That's a question for the master.

JUNO Where is she?

No answer. The fog moves between them. Juno understands that this silence is not the same as the last one. The bosun does not know where his master is standing, or does not want to know.

JUNO Then I'll find her myself.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: a deep hollow hum, water dripping somewhere

A narrow shaft with a ladder, so tight her shoulders touch both sides. Below, a low dark hold, pipes bundled tight to the walls, and a smell that is neither bilge nor fuel.

On screen: Juno (climbing down, torch up), Teo (crouching by the after bulkhead, beam on a plate of steel)

JUNO Teo. What's that smell?

TEO Don't know. Sweet, isn't it. Sweet and sharp both. Come and look at this.

Juno comes down. Her torch runs across a square of steel set flush into the wall. It has hinges. A hasp. A row of bolt heads around its edge, and on the bolt heads the paint is not the same paint as the wall beside it.

TEO That's not a bulkhead. That's a door somebody put in front of something and painted over so it wouldn't look like a door.

JUNO Provision room?

TEO Provision room's aft of that. I checked. Provision rooms don't get hasped and bolted and painted.

He puts his hand flat on the panel and leans close to the seam.

TEO And this is where the smell's coming from. Round the edge of it. Same smell as the bilge in number two.

Juno puts her nose near the seam and breathes in. Sweet and sharp and wrong. A smell that would get into a man's coat and stay there.

JUNO There's one proper home for a smell like that on a working ship. It's labeled and sealed and logged.

TEO It doesn't live behind a painted door in a lower hold.

JUNO Can you get it open?

TEO I can burn those bolts with the torch. Half an hour.

Juno looks at the bolted door and the thread of wrong smell coming round its edge. The thing that was a salvage claim in her chest goes cold and settles into a harder shape, a thing with edges.

JUNO Hold off on the torch. Find me her papers instead. Manifest, deck log, every scrap in her desk.

TEO You want the record before you want the proof.

JUNO I want both. The record first. Proof can wait for the jar. I'll not open that panel until I know what the court will say I was looking at.

TEO And if the record says nothing?

JUNO Then the smell will have to do the talking.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: water breaking white on the reef, wind through the rigging

The fog has folded over the forepeak and left the young man alone by the winch. He winds the rag and unwinds it and does not run.

On screen: Davy (split lip, one eye bruised, hands not steady), Juno (a long stride off, hands at her sides)

JUNO Your lip. The reef do that?

DAVY Fell.

JUNO On what?

DAVY On the deck.

JUNO Deck's flat as a plate. You'd bruise your chin, not your mouth.

He winds the rag. His hands are shaking, and he is trying to wind the shake out of them.

JUNO I'm not the law and I won't take you to anyone. But Fen won't say and Barrow won't say, and I've got a tide coming and a bolted door in your lower hold. I've got to know whether the master's holding that door shut or wants it open. Tell me which, and nothing more, and I'll be content.

Davy stops winding. He looks up at the fog, and at the forepeak, and his young face makes a decision and is afraid of it.

DAVY (low) We were not asleep.

Juno says nothing. She waits.

DAVY The bosun went down to the deep tanks with a hose the day before the grounding. He came back wet to the shoulders. And then the master brought her west, and the bosun had us in the forepeak before she struck, and he said—

He stops.

JUNO Go on.

DAVY He said we were asleep. He said it before she struck. He said it in the forepeak while the ship was still running.

JUNO Where's the master now?

DAVY I don't know. She come out of her cabin after and went forward, and she's not come back, and the bosun don't want her found.

He looks at her straight for the first time. There is no lie in it.

DAVY I've said more than I meant to. I'd be glad if you'd not tell him it was me.

JUNO I won't tell him. Now put that rag down and go and find my engineer in the lower hold. Tell him I said to hold the torch until I'm there.

Meridian Dawn, afternoon

Ambience: a rising wind, the grind of iron on rock

Teo comes up the ladder two rungs at a time and finds her at the rail with her hand on the cold steel, looking at the water.

On screen: Teo (soot to the wrist, a rust smear on his cheek), Juno (turning)

TEO Juno. Before we do anything else, there's a thing I owe you.

JUNO Say it.

TEO I got a look at number three hold. Starboard side, behind the engine casing. Empty too, and swept, but not swept the same. That one's been hosed. Scrubbed and left wet, and it's not rain.

JUNO Washed out late.

TEO Hours ago. Not days. In thirty years of salvage I've seen a hold scrubbed for a few reasons. Weevils. Fever. A cargo that leaked and they want it forgot.

He wipes his face and leaves a smear of rust and soot across his cheek.

TEO These people aren't merchants. If I drag her off and she opens on the reef with whatever's behind that panel, it's not a salvage. It's a spill. A spill in this strait runs up the estuary, and the town drinks from the lens.

JUNO I know it.

TEO Then find the master and put it to her straight. I'll hold the torch. I'll not open that door on my own judgment.

JUNO Keep the suction in number two. Get a sample jar and your gloves and wait for me. If it's the thing I think it is, I want it seen and sealed before this ship comes off that rock and the sea gets a vote.

TEO Aye, captain.

Meridian Dawn, late afternoon

Ambience: white water working the reef, foghorn far off

The fog is a gray room with no walls. At the windlass stands a tall woman in a heavy gray coat and a ship's apron, cap pulled low, square to the white water. She does not turn when Juno's boots come up the sloped deck.

On screen: Ines Voss (cap down, hands at her sides, face set), Juno (stopping three strides behind her)

JUNO Captain Voss.

Voss says nothing.

JUNO My engineer says your hull's sound. He says with two lines and knowing what your bottom did when it struck, he could haul you off before the surge comes in tonight.

VOSS Tonight.

JUNO But there's a bolted door in your lower hold with a chemical smell coming round it. I'm not pulling this ship off this reef until I know what's behind it. So I'll ask you plain. Will you help me save your ship, and will you tell me what I'm standing over?

The fog goes through the both of them. The tide clucks at the freighter's flank.

VOSS Not yet.

JUNO Not yet is not a no.

VOSS It's not a no.

Her voice is low and steady and it costs her something to keep it so.

VOSS You've got a claim, and a court, and a town up the estuary. You'll get your answer when I've thought it through.

JUNO The tide won't wait for your thinking.

VOSS No. It won't.

She gives Juno nothing else. The white water shows and hides. Juno spits over the side and leaves her standing.

Meridian Dawn, dusk

Ambience: a hollow hum, a torch hissing

The last bolt turns. The door swings wide on its hinges, and the smell comes out all at once.

On screen: Teo (gloves on, jar in hand), Juno (sleeve to her mouth, torch in the doorway)

TEO That's the last of them.

Juno holds the torch in.

Picture: Juno and Teo at the bolted panel in the Meridian Dawn's lower hold, a steel compartment packed with blue drums, one drum leaking a bright green slick into the coaming below

Drums. Row on row, low and wide, stacked tight in a compartment no bigger than a larder. Every one painted the same dull blue and stenciled with a black code. The nearest has paint gone black and bubbled and creeping down its side. A slick lies in the coaming beneath, thin and bright, threaded with green and yellow.

JUNO Count them.

Teo counts, lips moving. His face does the sum and does not like it.

TEO Three tiers. Four across the bottom. Sixty. Give or take one that's behind.

JUNO Sixty.

TEO And one leaking, and the rest thinking about it.

Close-up: a steel drum seam with a bright bead of solvent swelling and running down the blue paint, the stencil code SX-9 half eaten away by corrosion

The black bead comes down the drum slow and bright.

JUNO Get your jar. Gloves on, jar sealed, the time on the lid. Then get me her papers. Manifest, deck log, everything in her desk.

TEO Sealing it now.

JUNO And Teo. Not a word of this to the three of them, not yet. We know now what they've been afraid of, and a frightened man does a fool thing.

TEO What are they afraid of?

JUNO Being caught with it. Or what the owner does to the man who lets it be caught. I don't know which, and it wants knowing before I put a line on this ship.

TEO So what do we do?

JUNO We keep this panel shut and in the log as sealed and in her master's keeping until I've had my say with her. The finding-out is the salvage now, whether the court rules it or not.

TEO That's not what the court pays for.

JUNO I know what it pays for.

Teo seals the jar, writes on the lid, and sets it upright in the crate. Out past the bow the white water is beginning to move, slow and heavy, as the tide turns under them.

TEO The surge won't be long now. When she comes in over that reef, this ship starts to work.

JUNO Then she comes off before it does.

TEO If the master tells us where the water went.

JUNO She will.

Sound: a radio squawk from the wheelhouse above, then a voice, careless and loud

RUSK (on radio) Meridian Dawn, Meridian Dawn, this is the salvage tug Iron Gannet. We hold the Halyard contract on your vessel. We are inbound from the south. Any man aboard who wants a tow that floats, stand by.

On screen: Juno (head coming up), Teo (jar in both hands, staring at the deckhead)

TEO That's not the Gannet's voice I know. That's Rusk's.

JUNO It's Rusk's.

TEO How far off is he?

JUNO Close enough that he's calling on my channel.

She looks at the sealed jar in his hands, and at the drums beyond the open panel, and the torch-beam throws all their blue backs into a long shining row.

JUNO Shut it. Shut it and bar it and don't let him near this hold.

TEO And if he boards anyway?

JUNO Then he boards a ship already claimed, and I'll be the one standing in his way.
