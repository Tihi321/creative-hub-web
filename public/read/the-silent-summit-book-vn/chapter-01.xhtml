The Unnamed Peak

Ilvar Range, the road above the glacier

Ambience: a cold wind over bare rock, and a jeep engine coughing its last

The jeep sits dead on the track, dust settling around it. Below, a brown moraine runs down to the white edge of a glacier. Above, a grey peak climbs into a flat cloud.

On screen: Ilse (boots planted, listening through her soles), Teodor (climbing out, knees cracking), Priya (city-pale, new gear), Hesk (wide shoulders, rope neatly coiled)

The engine coughs, sputters, dies. In the quiet Ilse gets out and lays her fingers on the warm hood.

ILSE Out, all of you. Road ends here. Camp is half a kilometre on foot.

TEODOR So the road's finished. You felt it go soft?

ILSE I felt the mountain. Moving all morning. Small things. It settles in the afternoon heat.

TEODOR That's what I told you. And that shoulder. That shoulder's no good.

Priya drags her rucksack by one strap and stands a little apart, one hand pressed over her jacket pocket.

TEODOR Ilvar's last unclimbed face. Six expeditions tried it and took the hint.

PRIYA That's it? The one in the photograph?

ILSE That's it. Cloud's wrong today. Should be blowing east. It's leaning south.

PRIYA How can you possibly know that?

ILSE Pressure changed an hour ago. I felt it in my ears. It'll clear by evening.

Priya frowns at her, then takes out a small notebook, looks at it, and puts it away without writing anything.

The page stays blank. Ilse notices. She says nothing.

HESK You're Marchetti. I read about your Karsten Wall. I heard the radio calls. I heard you need a translator to lead a team.

TEODOR She's standing right here, Hesk. Say it to her.

HESK I am saying it to her.

ILSE You want to know how a deaf woman leads a mountain.

HESK I want to know how you hear a falling serac.

ILSE I don't hear it. I feel it before it falls. Keep up. The porters are already at camp.

Base Camp, flags snapping over orange tents

Ambience: canvas flapping, a stove hissing, prayer flags cracking in the wind

The tents sit on a rock shelf above the glacier's edge. Lantern light glows in the mess tent. Nima and Dawa wait outside it.

On screen: Ilse (pack still on, scanning the camp), Nima (weathered, hands ready), Dawa (broad, already grinning)

Nima takes Ilse's hand in both of his. Dawa laughs before anyone has said anything worth laughing at.

DAWA You walked fast. Nima said you would. He said the leader walks fast, the team follows, nobody complains.

NIMA (signing slowly) The mountain is quiet today. Too quiet. It is waiting.

ILSE (signing back) It can wait.

DAWA He likes you already. Says you have good feet. Good feet beat good ears. Ears lie. Feet don't.

TEODOR (arriving, dropping his pack) I complain. Put that in the record.

Base Camp, inside the mess tent

Ambience: a lantern creaking on its hook, wind pressing the canvas

The map fills a folding table, weighted at the corners with mugs. A red line climbs the western flank. The lantern swings, and the light moves over the paper like water.

On screen: Ilse (finger on the summit), Teodor (arms folded), Priya (hands clasped, eyes on the map), Hesk (at the flap, arms crossed), Nima (seated, quiet), Dawa (straightening a mug)

ILSE Summit, day six, if the weather holds. The icefall is the only gate the mountain has. We go through it in the dark, before the sun loosens the seracs. Nobody talks in the icefall. You watch my rope and my hand.

PRIYA And the bell?

ILSE Below the summit, in a cave of ice the old accounts describe. A bronze bell, about fifteen kilos. History says a guide named Karel Vasek rang it all night in 1908.

PRIYA (voice catching) That's the account in every book.

ILSE The bell is worth nothing but its story. You'll get your photographs. Then we move fast, because the window does not wait for history.

Close-up: a photograph laid on the map, showing a bell hanging from an iron bar and a wooden case frozen to a cave wall

The dated photograph shows a bronze bell hanging from an iron bar, and beside it a wooden case with iron corners. Someone has written 1908 on the back in faded pencil.

HESK Let me say the thing nobody's saying. Six expeditions failed. Two people died of altitude. And now we go up with a leader who can't hear a radio?

ILSE I have a light. Red, amber, green. Teodor calls the weather from below and I read the light. On the rope we use tugs. Two, stop. Three, climb. One long pull, danger, anchor now.

HESK And if you can't feel the rope because your hands are cold?

ILSE My hands are never that cold. Ask your questions here, not at six thousand metres.

HESK Then here's one. A week's pay. Before we leave the icefall, you'll call a crevasse that isn't there. Because the ground lies, and you trust your feet.

TEODOR That's enough.

ILSE One week's pay. Agreed. And a second stake. If I call a real one before you hear it, you wear my rope and you don't argue the signals for the rest of the climb.

HESK Deal.

They don't shake. Nima watches Hesk the way a hawk watches weather. Dawa quietly sets a mug back on a corner of the map.

Base Camp, at the edge of the tents

Ambience: a stove ticking down, wind combing the moraine

The summit cloud has gone from grey to gold and is thinning to rose. Cold comes up out of the rock.

On screen: Hesk (crouched, hand flat on a slab), Ilse (crouched beside him)

Ilse finds him alone, watching the summit. She crouches and lays one palm on the rock. He crouches too, mocking it.

Sound: a low deep grinding, felt more than heard

HESK What is that?

ILSE The icefall is moving. Not fast. The glacier is a river that never stops. When the seracs shift, it goes through the rock. You hear it with your ears when it's close. I feel it with my feet when it's far.

HESK It's stronger here.

ILSE That stone sits on a thin crust over a hollow. It carries the vibration farther. In the morning we check everything, together. And Hesk. The bet was a good way to ask your question. Next time, just ask.

Ilvar Range, on the glacier above the moraine

Ambience: wind over hard snow, the faint squeak of boots

Blue light before sunrise. The glacier runs white and smooth toward a jumble of ice towers. The roped line moves out from the moraine.

On screen: Ilse (second on the rope, scanning the snow), Nima (breaking trail), Hesk (three back, watching her), Teodor (heavy and even), Priya (at the tail, nervous), Dawa (behind her)

TEODOR (close to her ear) What?

ILSE The hollow one. Under there. Three metres.

She sets her bare palm on the snow. The surface feels thin and drumlike, a resonance rising through her hand.

ILSE Nima. Probe.

Sound: a probe striking ice, then dropping into water

The probe goes down through the crust, then falls the length of the man's arm. Nima pulls it back, the tip black and wet.

NIMA Crevasse. Open under the bridge. We cross there. Solid.

They cross one at a time on the anchored rope. On the far side, the rope goes taut toward Hesk, then slack, then two tugs, then three. Climb. They climb.

TEODOR The boy's quiet. Quiet's a good start.

ILSE He hasn't paid anybody anything yet. He still thinks I'm lucky.

Ilse tugs the rope three times, and the whole line moves on up into the cold.

Ilvar Range, at the foot of the icefall

Ambience: wind turning through blue ice, a rope creaking under load

Below the icefall, the team checks harnesses in the grey light. Ilse raises her fist.

On screen: Ilse (brow furrowed, hand on a pillar of ice), Nima (tense, listening with his whole body), Hesk (watching her back), Priya (white-faced), Teodor (braced)

Halfway up the step, Ilse stops dead. She lays her fingers on the blue face of ice.

ILSE (to herself) It's moving. Ice against ice. Faster now. I don't like the shape of it.

She tugs the rope once, long and slow. Four anchors bite into the snow. Hesk pulls once, a question. She doesn't answer.

Sound: a crack like a door slamming somewhere inside the mountain

The pillar leans. She looks up, then back down the line, and points up and to the right. Her fist goes up. Hold.

Picture: Ilse Marchetti stands at the head of the rope on the icefall with her fist raised, while a house-sized pillar of blue-green ice breaks away above the line at the Ilvar Range

The serac comes down in a wall of blue-green, slow, then all at once, and strikes the snow to their left.

Sound: a concussive thump and a hissing cloud of ice dust

Debris sweeps over the track they walked ten minutes before. Ilse keeps her fist up until the ice settles.

ILSE Climb.

PRIYA That could have killed us. We didn't hear anything.

TEODOR You don't hear a serac fall until it's fallen. Even with ears. It's the dust that kills you. It comes faster than the ice.

NIMA (signing fast) The rattle above us is not finished. More loose ice on the right of the step.

ILSE (signing back) We take the left line and we take it slow.

HESK You felt that before it fell. In your feet. Everyone felt it after. But you felt it before. I owe you a week's pay.

ILSE You owe me a rope to wear. And no arguing the signals.

TEODOR And to stand there and watch us all keep our heads. That's what a team is.
