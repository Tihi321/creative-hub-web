Descent

Ilvar Range, first light

Ambience: water boiling, a low wind like breathing

Grey light sits soft over the stove and the flattened tents. The storm has worn itself out. No mark shows in the snow.

On screen: Ilse (stepping out, hood down), Dawa (at the stove, whistling), Nima (up the ridge with a rope), Teodor (in the litter, foot packed in snow), Priya (case on her knees), the rescue man (coiling line)

Ilse walks one slow circle of the camp. Her boots find snow and nothing else. No tremor, no creep.

ILSE (to herself) The ground has stopped moving. Best reading of the week.

DAWA (grinning) Water is hot. Drink it before it freezes again. That is my medical advice.

TEODOR I have decided to be cheerful. I want everyone to note how well it is going. For an ankle sprain on a high-altitude expedition, immediate care often involves careful management of the injury alongside altitude considerations.

ILSE Note taken. How is the foot?

TEODOR The foot is not invited to this conversation.

PRIYA Ilse. Look east.

The far wall has come out of the cloud, black and clean, and the whole icefall with it, a wreck of frozen blocks glittering in the low sun.

NIMA (coming down, signing) The line is set. All the way to the second terrace.

Ilvar Range, mid morning

Ambience: wind combing dry snow

In daylight the glacier is a different animal. The light lies across it and shows the sags, the long blue seams, the hollows where a man would go in to the waist.

On screen: Ilse (one hand on the line, watching her feet), Nima (leading, probe out), Teodor (carried on the litter, eyes shut), Priya (case strapped high, grey-faced), Hesk (bar in fist, shoulders raw), the doctor (thin, at Teodor's side), Dawa (rear, stove on his back)

Ilse walks with one hand on the line and her feet still awake. Strange to have the mountain outside her again, plain as a field, when she carried it all night inside her hands.

PRIYA (stopping, turning, wiping her face) Sorry. I keep looking at it. I did not expect to see any of it again.

ILSE Look now. You may not pass this way again.

PRIYA I am crying in a tired way. I know. The danger is over and nobody has told my body.

HESK Nobody has told mine either. My shoulders are filing a complaint.

Ilvar Range, the first flat place below the terrace

Ambience: wind combing dry snow

The doctor kneels by the litter, works Teodor's boot off, and studies the ankle without a word. The others stand and watch his face.

On screen: the doctor (kneeling, face like a folded map), Teodor (upright, jaw set), Ilse (watching the doctor's mouth), Hesk, Priya, Nima (standing off)

THE DOCTOR (slow and flat) Swelling. Bruise the colour of a storm. A small crack maybe in a bone. Not broken.

THE DOCTOR He does not walk on it again today.

TEODOR I have walked on it all night.

THE DOCTOR Then you have done enough walking on it for one man. The strap stays on. He rides.

TEODOR I will not argue with the litter.

ILSE (to herself) Then it hurts more than he will say.

Teodor settles back, closes his eyes, and lifts one hand in the two-fingered wave that means he is all right and she is not to fuss. Ilse lays her palm on his shoulder and feels the shake in him under the coat.

Ilvar Range, the top of the icefall

Ambience: wind through broken blocks, water moving somewhere below

Coming up it was a climb. Going down it is a ruin. The old track is gone under a grey rubble of blocks frozen together, which looks solid and is not.

On screen: Nima (pointing east under the wall), Ilse (still, palms open), Hesk (at the cradle), Priya (hands on the case), Dawa (anchors ready), the rescue man (rope over his chest)

They stand at the top with the wall hanging over them in great soft ledges, and nobody speaks.

NIMA East. Under the wall. That is how I came up.

ILSE One at a time. The bell on the litter. The litter on the fixed line.

DAWA Nobody walks beside the bell. That is the rule today. The party moves carefully across the ice, following established ropes and anchors to navigate around any large blocks of ice in the rubble field.

ILSE Nobody walks beside the bell.

Ilvar Range, inside the ruined icefall

Ambience: water moving somewhere below

The litter comes down the fixed line past the bend where the chute runs black under the grey. Each time it settles at an anchor the bronze gives a small dense thump into the ground.

On screen: Ilse (at the bend, feet flat), the rescue man (above on the line), Dawa (on the anchor), Hesk (below, taking the ropes), Priya (waiting above with the case), Nima (at the next anchor)

Ilse stands at the corner and feels the litter come to her through her soles as much as through the ropes. Fifteen kilos of bronze, gone into the mountain a hundred and twelve years ago, coming down now by the hands of people who do not own it.

ILSE (counting under her breath) One. Settle. Two. Settle. Hold.

The ice answers and holds. Hesk takes the next length and does not speak and does not take his eyes off the cradle.

Sound: a small dense thump of bronze on ice

Priya comes down the fixed line behind it and says nothing for the whole of the fall. At the bottom she sets the case on clean snow and looks back up at the ruin they have come through.

PRIYA I want to do it properly.

ILSE Say the rest of it.

PRIYA There is a man in the valley who has been sending me letters about the bell for a year. I have answered none of them. He has my name and my silence and his own newspaper.

PRIYA The archive first, before the paper. I want to stand in the east hall and hand the book over with my own name on the wrong end of it. Not the way I meant to. The proper way.

ILSE The paper will not be kind.

PRIYA I have had enough of kind. Kind is what my mother gave me instead of the truth.

Ilvar Range, the last terrace above the glacier

The sun is full. The glacier runs pale and safe down to the moraine, and camp shows as small orange roofs. Dawa sets out the stove. Tea comes round in a tin cup that is now everyone's.

On screen: Ilse (sat on the ground), Hesk (back to the wall, hands hanging), Teodor (in the litter, cheerful and grey), Priya (budgeting nothing, at ease), Dawa (stove), Nima (quiet)

Ilse sits down without first feeling the ground through her hands. That is the newest thing of the whole week.

TEODOR I am still noting the cheerfulness. It is going badly.

ILSE You are grey to the chin.

TEODOR And cheerful underneath.

Hesk gets up. He comes over, puts a folded paper into her hand, and stands there. It is the pay slip, signed across the back.

HESK The bet. It is yours. Put it in the school.

ILSE A week's pay is nothing.

HESK It is a start. And I am going to be the first one through the door.

HESK (showing his palm) You will teach me the one thing I still cannot do. I will be some trouble.

ILSE You will be some trouble.

She puts the paper in her pocket. Hesk goes back to the wall and sits down, and that is the whole of the apology, made with his hands this time.

Base Camp, the moraine in full sun

Ambience: canvas snapping, voices, a lamp hissing

They have been seen from the moraine an hour ago. The whole camp is out: porters, the cook, the flags, the mess door flapping. People are running.

On screen: Ilse (walking in, hat in hand), Teodor (carried in on the litter), Priya (case in her arms), Hesk (at the cradle), Nima and Dawa (either side of the bell), the doctor (lamp already lit), the sponsor's man (round, soft-faced, clipboard), the rescue man (at the litter head)

Teodor is carried into the mess tent and put on a stretcher. The doctor comes and finds Ilse in the sun before she has finished her tea.

THE DOCTOR The foot will heal. Off it weeks, on it by winter.

ILSE Teodor is not lucky. Teodor is careful, and he was carrying a bell.

The sponsor's man arrives at a walk that wants to be a run. He stands over the bell with his mouth open. The cook comes out, puts his palm on the bronze, and keeps it there.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN This is it. This is the bell from the cave. The whole story. How did you get it down?

ILSE By hand.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN Miss Rask. The office told me. About your family. About the guide. It came up from the valley before I did. I have not known what to do with it.

PRIYA Write it. The whole of it. The cook rang the bell and the guide lay under a rock and the record gave the guide the credit.

PRIYA I am his great-great-granddaughter. I came up here to burn a page and I did not. Say that too.

Close-up: the pay slip, signed across the back in a hard young hand

The slip is creased and grey at the edges, and the signature is large and careful, as though he had practised it.

PRIYA There is a burnt place on the first page the size of a man's glove. Beside it is a note in my own hand that says how it came there. Show it to your newspaper man. Nobody has to be flattered and nobody has to be spared.

The sponsor's man makes a small sound in his throat and turns his clipboard over as if he has forgotten what it is for. He does not write anything down.

Base Camp, the edge of the shelf

Ambience: canvas snapping, voices, a lamp hissing

The evening light is low and gold. The moraine runs out to the shelf edge, and below it the glacier goes down slow and quiet toward the valley.

On screen: Ilse (alone, boots on old rock), Hesk (at the bell, hands behind his back), Nima (at the stove with Dawa), Priya (in the mess doorway, burnt page in hand)

Ilse walks the camp and feels it under her feet. The moraine is solid. Her feet tell her nothing at all, and nothing at all is the sweetest reading of the week.

She counts them, as she counted them going up when the counting was a thing she would not do out loud. Nima and Dawa at the stove, both whole. Teodor asleep with his foot up. Priya reading the burnt page aloud to nobody, getting it right.

Hesk stands at the bell looking at it the way a man looks at a thing that has beaten him and that he has come to love.

ILSE (to herself) Eleven walked up. Eleven are on the shelf. The mountain has taken a week, an ankle, and a good deal of our pride.

Base Camp, outside the radio tent

Ambience: canvas snapping, a radio hissing

The sponsor's man comes out of the radio tent at a trot with a flimsy in his hand. The sky at the west edge has gone flat and yellow. The flags on the mess tent turn like things looking for a direction.

On screen: the sponsor's man (flimsy in hand, grave), Ilse (cup going down), Nima (arriving), Dawa (behind him), Priya (in the doorway), Hesk (by the bell)

THE SPONSOR'S MAN From the valley. The forecast desk.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN Storm in from the west. They say four days. They say hold the camp. But the valley is already white, and the barometer dropped faster than the model.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN There is a man in the city who has looked at the same figures. He says two days, and he is usually right. The road out is under snow as far as the bridge.

Ilse goes past him to the edge of the shelf and stands on the old rock and takes her boots' reading.

ILSE (to herself) The glacier is not quiet. There is a pressure under the nothing. A long low shifting. The ground gone very slightly heavy, the way a slope feels a moment before it decides.

She comes back to the group.

ILSE Two days. The valley is right and the desk has been wrong about the same weather all week.

ILSE We go for the bridge at first light. The bell, the book, the boy with the bad foot. If the bridge is gone, we go over the moraine the hard way and we go on foot.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN On foot, with a litter, in a storm?

ILSE On foot with a litter, or we are still on this mountain when it shuts.

She goes to the mess tent door. Teodor is awake on the stretcher and does not ask what has happened, because he can read her as well as she reads ground.

TEODOR That quick, then.

ILSE That quick. Can you travel?

TEODOR (with enormous dignity) I can be carried. It is a different skill, and I have been practising it all morning.

Base Camp, the shelf at dusk

Ambience: wind rising, tent fabric straining

The yellow has gone out of the west and the light is going with it. Porters fold tents in a hurry. The sponsor's man stands in the middle of it holding his flimsy and no notion what to do with it.

On screen: Ilse (turning to Hesk), Hesk (jaw set, looking west), the rescue man (hauling stakes), Priya (case to her chest)

Hesk is already looking at the yellow edge of the sky. She does not have to tell him anything.

HESK The stakes go with us. The tent, the rope, the stove, the shovel. I will see to it tonight.

HESK Tell me what you want me to feel for, on the road, in the dark. You taught me the floor and the anchor. Teach me the weather.

Ilse turns his burned palm upward and lays her own over it.

ILSE The wind comes from the west before the snow. Put your hand on the ground and feel the change in it. When the ice goes from quiet to listening, come and tell me.

ILSE Ring the bell if the radio dies. You know the four now.

HESK I know the four now.

He closes his fingers over hers and lets go. Ilse walks out to the edge of the shelf once more and stands with her back to the camp and the whole white length of the glacier going down toward the valley.

ILSE (to herself) The glass is falling. The mountain is not letting us go after all.
