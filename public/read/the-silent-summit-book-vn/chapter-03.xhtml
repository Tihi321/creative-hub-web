Tremor

Ilvar Range, an hour before dawn

Ambience: wind combing dry snow, thin and soundless

The tent glows a dull orange from within, then darkens as the lamp is hooded. Four figures come out into the blue grey, roped and ready. Above them the slope is a pale presence in the dark.

On screen: Ilse (hood up, listening through her boots), Teodor (heavy pack, axe out), Hesk (watchful, close to the rope), Priya (tail of the line, pack hugged to her chest)

Ilse stands a moment with her weight on both feet and feels the mountain through the soles of her boots. Something thin and high went through her shoulder blade an hour before the alarm. It has not come again.

ILSE The mountain spoke in the night. Once. It will not say it twice. We move now.

TEODOR The slope is still there, then.

ILSE It is still there. That is why we cross it before the sun touches it.

She counts them on the rope with her hand, four times over. Then she turns and leads up into the dark.

Ilvar Range, camp at the foot of the névé slope

A small dome tent shakes in a flat, cold wind. Nima sits outside it with his head in his hands. Dawa crouches beside him, a stove hissing between them.

On screen: Nima (grey-faced, one hand pressed to his temple), Dawa (steady, worried), Ilse (arriving, kneeling), Teodor (behind her), Hesk (waiting on the rope), Priya (hanging back)

Ilse kneels in front of Nima so he can see her hands. She signs slowly. He watches her fingers, and signs back.

NIMA (signing) My head is full of loud water. I cannot trust my feet on the icefall.

ILSE (signing) Then you are right to go down. I will see you at the col. Keep the radio warm.

She lays her hand flat on his shoulder. He grips it once, hard, and lets go.

DAWA I take him down by the way we came, while the snow is set. We wait at the col with the radio and the spare fuel. You come down and we go home.

TEODOR Take the spare canisters. We can cut our fuel if we must.

DAWA We have enough. Walk careful, all of you. The mountain is talking and it does not like to repeat itself.

Dawa shoulders both their loads. The two of them go down the first slope, slow and sure, and the ridge takes them from sight.

ILSE (to herself) Turn around, Marchetti.

She turns around.

Ilvar Range, the long snow face above six thousand metres

Ambience: a pressing cold, no wind at all

The face runs up white and featureless into a grey sky. The snow is so dry it gives no sound under the boots. No one speaks.

On screen: Ilse (leading, spaced), Teodor (second, even), Hesk (third, head down), Priya (last, breath clouding)

The cold no longer bites. It presses. The four of them go quiet together, unprompted. Priya's breathing grows loud and ragged in the thin air.

TEODOR (mouthing it, close to Ilse's ear) How is the air?

ILSE Like a hand on the chest. Everyone feels it. Tell them to breathe slow and not to fight it.

He turns and passes it down the line. Priya nods and makes herself breathe slower. Hesk does not look up.

Ilvar Range, the traverse beneath the hanging slope

Ambience: a deep, waiting quiet

A white wind-packed slope leans over the track, loaded above a soft grey band. The crest above is close enough to touch. Ilse halts the line and sets her palm to the snow.

On screen: Ilse (palm flat on the snow, brow furrowed), Hesk (second, waiting), Teodor (braced), Priya (rope snug, eyes up)

The surface rings a little under her weight, a thin crust with something soft beneath it. She does not like it, and she says so.

ILSE It is loaded above a soft band. We cross fast and spaced. Nobody stops under the crest. Not for a bootlace, not for a view.

TEODOR And if it moves?

ILSE It starts under the feet before it moves. I will tell you. Hesk, you are second. Priya, keep the rope snug and your eyes up.

HESK What does it feel like? Before it moves.

ILSE Like a deep slow rising in the boots. You will not miss it. Go.

They go. Halfway across, the ground speaks.

Ilvar Range, mid-crossing

Ambience: a deep rising hum through the snow, then a roar

The snow begins to tremble underfoot. High above, the slab creaks against its bed.

On screen: Ilse (axe driving to the head, face set), Hesk (three metres off the line, under the crest), Teodor (looking back at Ilse), Priya (gripping the rope)

Ilse drives her axe to the head, drops her weight, and strikes the snow with her palm. Three hard beats. The ground, the ground, the ground.

TEODOR The beats! That is her. That is the ground talking!

She signs at Hesk with a face he cannot mistake. Down. Anchor now. Hesk has climbed toward a jut of rock out from under the crest. He sees her hands and does not come down.

ILSE (shouting into the wind) Hesk! Down! Down now!

Teodor drives his axe in, puts his weight on the hump, and hauls the rope tight. Hesk moves because Teodor moves. Priya moves because the rope pulls her.

Sound: a crack like a door slamming inside the mountain

Picture: Ilse Marchetti is face down and anchored on the traverse as the hanging slab breaks away above the line in a wall of white, Hesk Vandry and Teodor Brann pinned to the hump and Priya Rask dragged low on the rope

It opens along a crack a hundred metres wide. The snow lifts as though something under it has drawn breath. Then the whole slab comes off and comes down.

White fills the world. The rope jerks. She takes the pull through her shoulders and her heels and lies with her cheek on her arm, counting. One. Two. Three. The hump holds. The river of white pours past below them and falls away into the bowl.

Sound: the long subsiding roar, then total silence

The roar goes on, and then it stops. The silence that follows is so complete she carries it on her back.

She tugs once. The answer comes back up the line. One, two, three. She digs them out and checks them with her hands, because her voice cannot reach Teodor's deaf side in that wind.

TEODOR I am whole. A cut on the cheek. Nothing.

PRIYA (spitting snow, shaking) It came over us. I felt it go past. It was so fast.

Teodor has a red line down his cheek. Thirty metres wide of them, the slab has buried the traverse completely.

ILSE Everybody up. Hands and feet and faces. Hesk, Priya's face. Teodor, my back.

Ilvar Range, the far side of the slide

Ambience: snow dust settling, a raw wind

They stand upright in the settling dust. The slope above is scarred white and raw. The buried track is a smooth grey bank behind them.

On screen: Ilse (calm, dust in her hair), Hesk (pale, voice high), Teodor (hand to his cut cheek), Priya (still shaking)

Hesk turns on her. His voice has gone high and raw.

HESK Reckless. That is what that was. One metre more and it would have taken us. You saw nothing. You called it on a feeling in your feet.

ILSE (quietly) Quiet. Quiet is the one thing that can reach you now. Listen to me.

HESK I am listening.

ILSE The crossing began eleven minutes ago. It takes fifty to be clear of the hanging slope. I called it at the end of the soft snow, where I could call it. You were off the rope, under the crest, and you would not come down until Teodor hauled you. That is the recklessness on this hump. It is not mine.

HESK I could not feel it. The way you do.

ILSE That is not the failing. The failing is that you did not come down when I told you to come down.

TEODOR Listen to me, boy. I have climbed with this woman nineteen years. I have seen her call weather the forecasters missed, and ice that would have killed us all. I have never once seen her guess.

HESK Teodor —

TEODOR She pinned you to the hump and you asked her twice before you moved. Take your accusation down that rope quietly, or I will tie you off and leave you for the mountain.

Hesk looks at his boots. The wind moves the dust between them.

HESK I understand.

They climb off the hump and up the far side of the slide, spaced and quiet. Priya comes up last, and Ilse waits for her and checks the rope at her waist with her hands.

The Ice Cave, the shelf below the final wall

Ambience: wind turning on a high terrace

A broad white terrace runs back from a great hanging sérac. On its far side the snow lies against a bank of black rock. In the rock is a dark mouth that is not quite a cave and not quite a shadow.

On screen: Ilse (slowing, scanning the rock), Teodor (pointing), Hesk (head down), Priya (hands on her pack)

TEODOR That is the cave. We are where the grid puts it.

They cross in the flat hard light. Ilse stops them a little way from the rock and has Priya come up beside her.

ILSE You have carried that pack through the storm and past the slide. What is in it?

PRIYA Archive materials. Protective cases, handling gloves, a small cutter, the document boards. The archive made me promise to do it correctly.

ILSE A cutter.

PRIYA For the boards. If the book is there it must not be handled bare. That is the archive rule.

Priya turns away. Ilse waits. Priya stands very still with her hands on the pack.

PRIYA It is for the page. The first page. I told myself I came here to correct the record. I have begun to think I came here to bury it. That is all I can say on a shelf in the light.

ILSE I will hold you to the rest of it when there is time to hear it.

Priya nods once and picks up the pack. They come to the mouth together and look.

The Ice Cave, the sealed mouth on the shelf

Ambience: a still cold, wind blowing fine dry snow

The mouth goes back a little way and then stops, filled by a plug of ice grown into it over a century. It is blue at the heart and white at the edges.

On screen: Ilse (both palms on the plug), Teodor (lamp high), Hesk (axe in belt), Priya (bag and boards)

Ilse sets both palms on the plug and holds them there until the others quiet. The cold burns rather than freezes.

Through the burn comes the deep low pulse of the mountain. Under the pulse is a small stillness, an emptiness where the cave goes back.

And in that space something shifts that is not the mountain. A vibration all its own, finer than any the rock makes. Like a wire drawn very tight, struck once, left to ring.

ILSE (to herself) The bell is behind it. I felt the room. And I felt something ringing in it. Small. Old.

She stands back.

ILSE We cut it. It will take all of us in turns, and the window narrows while we cut. Hesk, first shift beside me. Teodor, the lamp and the snow. Priya, the bag and the boards. There is no hour to rest in. We go now.

Hesk pulls his axe from his belt and goes to the seam.

The Ice Cave, cutting the plug

Ambience: axe blows ringing, fine snow hissing into the mouth

The blue heart of the plug meets the rock at a thin seam. Chips of hard grey ice ring on the shelf and skitter away. The sky above the far wall is going grey at the north edge.

On screen: Ilse (working the seam), Hesk (cutting beside her), Teodor (lamp high), Priya (holding the bag)

The plug does not shatter the way summer ice shatters. It comes away in hard grey chips, and every blow tells Ilse how much thickness still stands between her and the dark.

Hesk cuts beside her and for a long stretch says nothing. Then, between strokes, with his eyes on the seam, he speaks.

HESK I could not feel it. On the hump. I have put my hand on the ground a dozen times since and felt nothing at all.

ILSE Most people feel nothing. That is not a failing. It is why we rope up and drill the signals. You moved when Teodor pulled. That is all the mountain asked of you.

HESK It is not what I said to you.

ILSE No. It is what you are doing now. That is worth more to me than the other.

A plate of ice comes away and rings on the shelf. Hesk works on, quieter. Teodor takes the second shift and Hesk the third.

Priya holds the lamp and the bag and takes no turn. Each time the wind blows fine snow into the mouth, she clears it and goes back to watching the seam, and her hands stay hooked in the strap of her pack.

The Ice Cave, the hole through the plug

Ambience: wind fallen, a thread of air moving out of the ice

The hole is barely wide enough for a person to look through. Cold air comes out of it, old and still. The lamp throws a small hard circle into the dark beyond.

On screen: Ilse (face to the hole), Teodor (beside her), Hesk (behind, waiting), Priya (lamp in hand)

It smells faintly sweet, and of iron. Ilse puts her face to the hole and cold air comes out against her skin.

ILSE There is a room. Low ceiling. The air has not moved in a hundred years.

TEODOR And the bell?

ILSE I can feel the ring of it through the rock. It is still inside. Patience. We open it in the morning when we can see what we are doing.

They pitch the tent on the shelf close under the rock. Teodor stands watch on the north ridge and watches the cloud there, and it is coming down.

TEODOR Cloud on the north ridge. Moving fast. We have tonight, perhaps.

ILSE Then we sleep in turns and we are through that hole at first light.

Priya sets her pack inside the tent, sits with it against her knees, and does not lie down. Hesk lies down and is asleep in a minute. Teodor takes the first watch. Ilse takes the second, and sits with her back to the rock, and feels the small, fine ringing go on and on behind the ice.

ILSE (to herself) Whatever you are ringing for. We are almost there.
