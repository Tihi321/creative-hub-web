The Bell and the Book

The Ice Cave, the shelf before dawn

Ambience: wind turning on a high terrace, fine dry snow hissing

The shelf lies grey and cold under a sky going pale at the east. The tent glows faintly. The hole through the plug is a dark mouth in the rock.

On screen: Ilse (hood up, axe in hand), Teodor (lamp high), Hesk (at the hole, eager), Priya (pale, holding her pack)

Ilse comes last out of the tent and stands by the hole a moment, feeling the rock through her boots.

ILSE It is quiet. Too quiet. The wind has gone round to the north.

TEODOR Then we go quickly.

They go through the hole one at a time, Priya lit from below by the lamp and climbing like a woman wading into cold water.

ILSE Keep your head down. The ceiling is low.

PRIYA I am through. Ilse. Ilse, come and see this.

The Ice Cave, inside the room

The room is small and blue. A low ceiling comes down over them, and the walls curve away into darkness the lamp cannot reach. The floor is black ice, smooth and clean. In the middle the bell hangs from an iron bar across the mouth.

On screen: Ilse (stopped, staring up), Priya (lamp in hand, trembling), Teodor (behind her), Hesk (frozen at the hole)

The bell's mouth is turned down. The crown is bright with a thin skin of frost where the bronze has not gone green. The bar has taken its weight since 1908.

Ilse walks to it and lays her hand flat on its side. The bronze meets her palm with a cold like water.

ILSE (to herself) Nothing. No hum. Not a wire. Just cold.

TEODOR Small thing, isn't it. For all that.

ILSE Heavier than it looks. That is the way with bells.

Behind the bell, against the far wall, a wooden case stands half swallowed by the ice, its lid shut, its lock rusted hard. On the top lies a parcel wrapped in oilskin.

PRIYA (small and flat) It is his. The case is his. It has the expedition's mark burned into the lid. The three crosses and the ring.

TEODOR Open it. Gently.

The Ice Cave, the case

The lamp is set down on the ice. Hesk works the small axe at the frozen lid. Priya kneels close.

On screen: Hesk (cutting, careful), Priya (kneeling, hands clasped), Ilse (watching), Teodor (lamp)

The ice comes away in grey chips. Hesk sets the oilskin parcel aside, clears the lock, and Priya works the rust with a bar until it gives.

Sound: a rusty lock snapping open

The lid comes up. Inside, packed in black wool, lies a second bundle in oilskin hardened to a shell. Three empty bottles. A tin of biscuit. A coil of rope gone to powder at her touch.

Close-up: the ledger laid open on the ice, its cloth binding black and swollen, a burnt bite at the corner of the first page

The first page is written in a careful, level hand: the book of Anders Weiss, cook to the expedition of 1908, who cannot hear and who keeps his account in writing, that the truth may be here when the storm is done.

ILSE Read it. Out loud.

PRIYA The evening of the eleventh of September the mountain came down upon the camp.

The Ice Cave, the reading

The lamp throws a long shadow across the black ice. Priya reads with both hands flat on the book, in the flat, steady voice she uses in the archive.

On screen: Priya (reading, white-knuckled), Ilse (still), Teodor (against the wall), Hesk (sitting on his heels)

PRIYA The master Vasek was struck in the tent and took harm upon his shoulder and his face, and did not wake. There were seven living and the storm upon us, and no way down and no voice that could carry.

PRIYA So I took the bell kept for the signal. I hung it on the iron bar and I rang it through the night. The men came to the sound by threes and by twos. Six lived. One did not.

PRIYA I rang until my arms were dead, and then I rang with my feet upon the rope, and the ringing went into the ice, and the ice carried it down the mountain like a road.

Ilse puts her hand back on the bronze. This time something comes, from the ice under her boots, from the whole shelf and peak, the slow deep pulse of the mountain, and inside it a small struck-wire memory.

ILSE He rang with his feet. He knew the ice would carry it. He spoke to the mountain, not to the air.

PRIYA And this. And I will set down here that the master Vasek was a good man and a brave one, and did not know what happened in those hours. But the account that walks down the mountain will be written by men who were not here. So this is my book, and it is the truth, and I leave it in the place of the bell.

TEODOR A hundred and eighteen years, waiting in the dark.

Priya's face has gone loose and bright and close to breaking. Her hands shake on the book.

ILSE Hesk. Put the book back and close the case. We photograph everything now, in place.

Priya does not move. Her thumb has found the corner of the first page, and the dry paper lifts.

The Ice Cave, the burning

The lamp flame stands up straight on the ice. Priya holds the book in both hands and looks at the fire.

On screen: Priya (face breaking, thumb on the page), Ilse (stepping forward), Hesk (rising), Teodor (pushing off the wall)

PRIYA I cannot. It is not a correction, it is a confession. My family is the shame. He was the cook they blamed, and I have spent my life in the hall that hangs his disgrace on the wall.

ILSE Priya. Step back from the lamp.

PRIYA I was going to bury the truth. I told myself I would say the book was too damaged to read.

She puts the corner of the page into the flame. It catches at once, orange rippling up the leaf, smoke white and thin.

Sound: a dry papery flare, then a small wet hiss

Picture: Hesk Vandry seizes Priya Rask's wrist in the blue cave and crushes the burning page between his glove and palm, a thumb-wide scorch smoking on the leather, Ilse Marchetti and Teodor Brann close behind

Hesk crosses the ice in two strides. He does not grab the book. He takes her wrist and pinches the burning corner flat and holds it until no glow is left.

A bite has gone out of the corner, brown-edged and ragged, the size of a thumbnail. The smoke hangs in the roof in a thin white breath.

PRIYA I am sorry. I nearly burned his name out of the world.

ILSE (steady) Nearly. The rest is in the case.

TEODOR A book that survived a fire is a better witness than one that did not.

HESK (quiet, looking at his palm) Get the case, Priya. You are the archivist. Pack it like you were taught. I will hold the light.

Priya kneels and does it. Her hands have steadied. She wraps the book in oilskin and wool, closes the lid, and locks it with the bar, slowly, without a word.

The Ice Cave, the cave photographed

Priya works with the camera from her pack, precise and slow, and writes each exposure in a small book.

On screen: Priya (focused, professional), Ilse (holding the lamp), Hesk (waiting), Teodor (at the wall)

She photographs the bell where it hangs, the crown letters, the iron bar, the mark of the rope on it. She photographs the case, its lock and mark. She photographs the first page open with its burnt corner black against the ice.

TEODOR The bell, then. Three of us and me holding.

Teodor takes the crown while the others ease it off the iron. It weighs more than fifteen kilos. When they lay it in the rope cradle Ilse has built, mouth down, the cave seems emptier.

ILSE It does not sing.

TEODOR Give it something to say and it will.

They carry the cradle through the hole. In the white flat afternoon light the bronze takes the sun and holds it, and for a moment it glows as if it were warm.

The Ice Cave, the shelf at noon

Ambience: wind over a high terrace, thin and rising

The radio sits on the rock outside the tent. Teodor holds the antenna wire high. The bell lies in its cradle against the rock.

On screen: Ilse (at the radio), Teodor (antenna up), Hesk (by the cradle), Priya (case against her chest)

Sound: a short hiss of static, then a thin, distant voice

The valley answers after a hiss. A man's voice, thin with distance and surprise, asks who is on the channel.

ILSE (into the radio) Base camp, this is Marchetti on the shelf. All four alive. We have the bell and the book. The book is whole but for one scorched corner, scorched today and by our own hand.

ILSE The cook is the man. Anders Weiss rang the bell. His own hand wrote it, and his account is with us.

The speaker is quiet a moment. Then the man speaks again, slowly, to someone behind him and then to her.

THE VOICE Say again, Marchetti. Say again the cook.

ILSE His name was Anders Weiss. He was deaf and he was not the man they blamed. He saved six. He wrote it down. We are coming down.

Sound: a clean burst of shouting through the speaker

The valley makes a noise like a room full of hands. Teodor's jaw is tight. He nods at her once.

THE VOICE Marchetti, the forecast desk holds four days. But I do not trust it. There is cloud from the north the models have not placed, and my barometer has fallen nine millibars since noon. If it comes early you will not get four days. Call me every four hours.

ILSE Four days by the forecast. The mountain has not read the forecast.

She turns the dial down and wraps the radio in its case.

TEODOR I have never liked a radio that agrees with me. Look at the ridge. The far wall. The cloud is down on the north.

The north ridge has gone grey and soft. The Ridge of Echoes has disappeared into a low whiteness that is not snow but cloud, and the wind is coming with it.

ILSE (to herself) The mountain says two. Maybe one.

ILSE We leave at first light. We go down through the icefall in the dark, and we carry the bell, and we do not stop until the col.

PRIYA The book is in its case. The bell is in its cradle. I am ready, Ilse. All of me, this time.

The cloud comes down the north wall and takes the ridge, and the shelf goes grey, and the four of them stand in the lee of the rock with the bell between them and the descent still to come.
