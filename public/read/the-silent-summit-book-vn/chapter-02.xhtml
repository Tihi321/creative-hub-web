The White Pulse

Ilvar Range, the high bowl below the summit shoulder

Ambience: a low, pressing silence, then wind rising

The high bowl lies white and open under a pale sun. Cloud gathers on the summit far above, like a lid closing. The snow is smooth and undented.

On screen: Ilse (walking point, head tilted), Teodor (behind her, squinting up), Hesk (second on the rope, watchful), Priya (thin-lipped), Nima (scanning the sky), Dawa (steady)

Ilse stops. She turns her face up and presses two fingers under her jaw, then against her cheekbone.

ILSE The pressure is turning. It presses on my face like a hand.

TEODOR How long?

ILSE An hour. Maybe less. We halt at the névé slope and dig.

HESK Dig what?

ILSE A hole to live in.

The light dulls. The cloud comes down off the summit in a silent wall, and the world goes white from the top down.

Ilvar Range, the foot of a névé slope

Ambience: wind driving snow, shovels biting

The drift lies deep and stable in the lee of the slope. Six figures bend to the snow, swinging axes and shovels. The trench fills behind their heels as fast as they clear it.

On screen: Ilse (digging, hood iced), Teodor (shouting into the wind), Priya (wet gloves, clumsy), Hesk (swinging hard), Nima (left flank), Dawa (right flank)

TEODOR (shouting) Cut! Cut there!

His word reaches Ilse only as the shape of his mouth and a shudder through the snow. She nods and drives her axe in.

Priya's gloves go stiff and useless. Ilse pulls a spare pair of mitts from her jacket and pushes them into her hands.

ILSE Swap. Keep digging.

PRIYA I'm fine.

ILSE You're not. Swap.

Priya swaps and digs again, saying nothing. Nima and Dawa fall into an easy rhythm on the two flanks, the rhythm of men who have dug a hundred shelters.

Hesk swings his shovel like a man proving something to the snow. The snow takes no notice.

ILSE Roof. Smooth it. Doorway there.

They smooth the roof, cut a doorway, and plug the wind with a bag of gear and a spare rope. Inside, six people breathing warm the air fast. It smells of wet wool, hot metal and fear.

Ilvar Range, inside the snow cave

Ambience: a constant low seething beyond the walls

The chamber is small, its roof curved and glistening. A stove glows at the centre. Beyond a metre of snow the storm roars.

On screen: Ilse (seated nearest the roof, listening), Nima (at the door, face to the wind), Dawa (dozing upright), Priya (counting canisters), Hesk (back to the drift), Teodor (still against the wall)

Ilse sets her palm flat on the ice floor. The storm's pressure comes up through her seat bones and into her spine.

ILSE (signing to Nima) The drift is holding. The roof will last.

NIMA (signing back) For now.

Priya counts the fuel canisters aloud. Twice. The same answer both times.

PRIYA Four full, two half. That is what we have.

TEODOR Then we have two days. Maybe three.

Dawa falls asleep sitting up, chin on his chest. Nima shifts his legs without waking him so his shoulder comes to rest against the wall of snow.

Near evening the storm tears a corner off the roof. White pours in.

Sound: a tearing hiss of driven snow

ILSE Breach! Pack it!

Priya holds the headlamp. Ilse and Teodor pack the gap. Hesk passes blocks of cut snow like a man feeding a fire. Nobody speaks; there is no point.

The second morning repeats the first. Same roar, same pressure on the roof, same grey light leaking through the plugged door.

Ilvar Range, second afternoon

Ambience: the seething pressing harder, snow settling above

The roof sags a little in the middle. Everyone's eyes drift up to it, then away. The stove puts out a thin, patient flame.

On screen: Hesk (flicking cards, restless), Nima (teaching the game), Dawa (offended dignity), Priya (watching), Teodor (flat and still), Ilse (palms on the floor)

NIMA Two cards short. The game still works. Mostly.

HESK You are cheating.

DAWA I do not cheat. I am simply better.

HESK You are carrying a lucky stone. That's the only explanation.

DAWA (with immense dignity) I carry no stone.

The dignity is so total that everyone laughs. The laughter makes the shelter feel smaller and warmer than it is. Then it runs out, and the day goes on.

The drift settles with a long soft creak. Ilse signs to Nima. They rise and pack the roof with three more blocks. It holds. They sit again.

TEODOR (flat) I do not like this one.

PRIYA You have been in storms worse than this.

TEODOR I have been in storms where I could not see my own glove. That is not what I do not like. Tell her what the mountain is doing.

ILSE Under the storm there is another sound. A grinding, high up above the shelf. It started yesterday. It is getting louder. That is not the storm. That is the slope above us moving on its own bed.

HESK Will it come down?

ILSE Maybe. Not while the snow falls like this. When the snow stops, it will move, or it will not. There is nothing to be done in this snow. So we sit, and we wait, and we do not spend ourselves.

PRIYA You always know, don't you. What the mountain is doing.

ILSE Not always. Often. I would know what you are carrying. Not the cases. The other thing.

Priya goes still. Her hands find her pack and hold it against her chest.

Ilvar Range, the confession

Ambience: the storm low and constant

The stove has burned down to coals. The faces in the red light are close together and very still.

On screen: Priya (hands clenched on her pack), Ilse (watching her), Teodor (turned, listening), Hesk (sitting up), Nima (quiet by the door), Dawa (awake now)

PRIYA I should have told you before we left.

TEODOR Told us what?

PRIYA The money. The funding. It is not the archive's money. Not mostly. The archive put in a little for equipment and permits. The rest is mine. My savings, and a loan against my flat.

HESK You are paying for this? All of it?

PRIYA Two years of research. Four months of grant letters that all came back no. Nobody funds a correction. Nobody funds a plaque being wrong. So I did.

TEODOR That is a great deal of money for a plaque.

PRIYA (flat and precise) It is not for the plaque. It is for the plaque to come down. And for what goes in its place. The truth fits like a key in a lock, and the lock has been shut for a hundred and eighteen years. I am the only one with the key.

PRIYA Nobody funds a correction. So I sold my quiet and my savings and I paid. And I did not tell you, because once it was said you would look at me the way you are looking at me now.

ILSE It does not change the mountain. A rope does not care who paid for it. The bell does not care.

Priya puts her face in her hands. Teodor reaches across the gear and lays his big glove on her shoulder.

TEODOR That is the trouble with a lie. You tell it because you fear the people you tell it to. Then you find they are all right, and the lie is the only thing that was not.

HESK (slowly, chewing the words) You should have trusted us with it.

Nobody answers him. He sets his cup down and does not pick it up again.

ILSE The bell is a bronze bell in a cave. What does it have to do with your money?

PRIYA Everything. The man on the plaque, Karel Vasek, rang the bell all night, every book says so. It is not true. A cook rang it. A deaf cook, named Anders Weiss, hiring on because a deaf man did not complain about the noise of the wind at night.

Ilse does not move. Her palm stays flat on the ice.

PRIYA He rang it in four strikes and a pause, from midnight until dawn, and the six men who lived lived because of it. Vasek lay in a tent with a broken shoulder and a frozen face and never rang a thing. And when it was over, the credit went to the famous guide, and the cook went down in the record as the man who rang too late.

TEODOR And the record stayed wrong.

PRIYA For a hundred and eighteen years. My great-great-grandfather died in a poorhouse in a valley town with no plaque, no portrait, no name on any wall. My family carried it. We did not speak of him. We spoke of Vasek, because the world did, and it was easier to be ashamed of the cook quietly.

ILSE So you climb for a cook.

PRIYA I climb to give him back his night. That is what all of it is for. The money, the cave, the bell. The book in the cave is his. I know it. And I mean to bring it home and read it out in the east hall under the painting, and make them take the painting down.

The stove ticks. The storm presses on the roof. Nobody says anything for a while.

ILSE Then we bring it out whole. All of it. Finish your tea. Tomorrow we see what the mountain has done.

Ilvar Range, outside the shelter at first light

Ambience: a silence so complete it rings

The storm has broken all at once and extravagantly. The sky is hard pale blue. The snow squeaks under boots, and every sound carries too far.

On screen: Ilse (kneeling, palms on the crust), Hesk (watching her closely), Teodor (standing with her), Nima (pale, one hand to his head), Dawa (beside him), Priya (blinking in the light)

Ilse goes a little way from the shelter, kneels, and lays both palms on the wind-scoured crust.

ILSE The grinding is still there. High up above the shelf. It did not stop in the storm, and it did not come down. It is waiting on its bed.

TEODOR The slope above the shelf. The one we have to go under.

ILSE Near enough.

TEODOR Then we go up fast and light, and we do not linger. And we watch the ground.

Nima signs slowly, and Teodor speaks for the rest.

TEODOR His head filled with a loud water in the night. He cannot trust his balance on the icefall. He will take Dawa and go down by the way we came, this morning, while the snow is set. He will wait for us at the col with the radio and the spare fuel.

ILSE (signing) You are right to go. I will see you at the col.

Dawa shoulders both their loads without being asked. Ilse watches them down the first slope until the ridge takes them. The rope behind her loses two of its voices. She makes herself turn around.

Ilvar Range, the snow bridge on the upper route

Ambience: a faint trickle of snow into a void

The route turns across a narrow white arc of snow spanning a crevasse. From the edge it looks solid. From the edge it always looks solid.

On screen: Ilse (alone at the bridge edge, kneeling), Hesk (second on the rope, behind), Teodor (braced), Priya (tail of the rope, waiting)

Ilse holds up her fist. The stop signal travels down the rope and back up again, clean and quick.

She walks forward alone and presses both palms flat on the bridge. She waits.

ILSE (to herself) Hollow. And under the hollow, a grinding. The middle is a skin.

She walks her hands along the arc. At the centre they stop. Through her palms comes a faint gravelly trickle, snow shifting down into the dark.

She takes her hands away and walks back to the line.

HESK What is it?

ILSE The bridge is bad. The middle is a skin. Not much left under it.

TEODOR Can we cross?

ILSE Roped, spaced, crawling, belayed from both sides. Or we go around. There is rock on the far side and a step. We go around.

TEODOR It is a bridge on the only easy route up the mountain. Every party for a hundred years has crossed something like it.

ILSE Every party for a hundred years has been one storm away from a burial. We go around.

Hesk steps up to the bridge edge himself. He lays one hand on the snow, the way he has watched her do it, and holds it there.

HESK (quietly) I feel nothing.

ILSE Not with the hand. With the ear in the bone. Take your glove off and press harder.

He pulls his glove off, presses his bare palm to the snow, and frowns. Then his whole face changes.

HESK It is humming. Like a drum with nobody playing it.

ILSE Now you know what a bad one feels like. Now you wear my rope to the step and you do not argue the signals.

HESK I paid the week's pay in my head already. I will pay it in money at the bottom.

Ilvar Range, the rock step above the bridge

Ambience: wind over cold stone, crampons scraping

A rib of black rock rises above the crevasse. The climbing is hard and cold. Hesk leads the last pitch, light and quick.

On screen: Hesk (leading, finding holds), Ilse (belaying from below), Teodor (watching), Priya (last, breath clouding)

Ilse belays from below and watches his feet find the holds. He climbs more carefully than she has seen him climb before.

He comes down onto safe snow and tugs the rope three times, steady. Then a fourth.

ILSE (to herself) The fourth tug means nothing. I think he knows it.

The fourth tug travels up the line to her hands. She pulls twice in answer. He grins, embarrassed, and turns away.

Priya comes across last and slowly, and stands with her hands on her knees.

PRIYA That cost us time. An hour and a half, at least.

ILSE It cost us the margin. We go anyway.

Beyond the step the ground flattens beneath a hanging slope, and the light is going.

Ilvar Range, camp beneath the hanging slope

Ambience: wind combing the snow, a stove hissing in a tent

The tent is pitched in the lee of a rock buttress. Beyond it the slope rises steep and pale in the last light. Ilse stands a little apart with her palms on the snow.

On screen: Ilse (alone, hands on snow), Teodor (coming to her), Hesk, Priya (inside the tent, dim)

She feels the grinding, no more than a stone's throw up the slope, moving against its bed in small steps, the way a door moves before it opens.

Teodor comes and stands beside her. She keeps her hands where they are for a while. Then she takes them away.

TEODOR Tomorrow we cross under it.

ILSE In the dark, before the sun loads it. Fast. We do not stop under the crest, not for the view, not for the rope, not for anybody's bootlace. And we tell them tonight.

They tell them in the tent, over the stove. Four faces take it.

HESK The bridge. Going around cost us the margin, didn't it.

ILSE It did. We are going anyway. The crossing is made in the dark.

Priya asks nothing. Her hands lie folded on her pack, and her eyes stay on Ilse's face.

ILSE Ask it.

PRIYA The cutter. In my pack. The small one. I told you it was for the document boards. It is for the page. The first page. If it is there.

HESK (sitting up) You mean to cut it out.

PRIYA I mean to take his name out of it. With the cutter if the fire was too slow. So that at least nobody reads it and knows him for a liar. I have known since the city that I would.

Her voice cracks.

PRIYA Then the storm came, and you put your hand on my shoulder, and I could not say it in the snow cave with all of you looking. I am saying it now. Before the slope. In case there is no after.

ILSE You will bring it out whole. Both of us will. And the bell. And the page. That is the promise of the dark crossing.

PRIYA I do not trust myself to keep it.

ILSE Then I will keep it for you until you can.

Ilse puts out her hand. Priya takes it, briefly, and lets go.

Ilvar Range, crossing under the crest in the dark

There is no moon. The lantern is hooded. The slope above is a presence carried in the legs more than the hands.

On screen: Ilse (leading, spaced), Teodor (behind her), Hesk (after him), Priya (tail of the rope, gripping her pack)

They go at four, fast and spaced, no stops. Halfway across, the ground under Ilse's right boot gives a small, terrible sigh.

She drops onto both palms without being told. The whole slope waits. She holds. It holds. She gets up and goes on.

Past the crest the ground smooths and opens into the long white shelf. She does not let herself look back until her boots are on flat, hard snow. Then she counts the rope four times over. Every one of them.

NIMA (voice over the col radio, thin) Ilse. The barometer is falling. Two days, maybe less. Do not linger up there.

ILSE (into the radio) Understood. Two days.

The Ice Cave, the shelf at first light

Ambience: wind over a high terrace, thin and cold

A broad white terrace runs back from a great hanging sérac. On its far side, snow lies against a bank of black rock with a dark mouth in it that is not quite a cave and not quite a shadow.

On screen: Ilse (palms on the cold rock), Teodor (beside her), Hesk (at the pack, restless), Priya (holding her pack, staring at the mouth)

Ilse gives the rock beside the mouth a long time. The mountain's deep pulse is in it, slow and heavy. Under the pulse, faint and fine, something is ringing like a wire strung tight.

ILSE Listen. Not with the ears. Put your hands here.

Hesk kneels and presses both bare palms to the rock. He is still for a while.

HESK There is something in it. Something small. Like a bell that has already rung and not stopped.

ILSE The mouth is plugged with a hundred winters of ice. We cut it at first light, all four of us in turns.

HESK And the slope above us?

ILSE Is not going to wait for another evening. A long cut at the plug is a long time under it. We take the morning to read what we can from outside, and we cut. If the mountain moves again, we go down without it. The window is the window.

Priya stands at the mouth with her hands on the ice, and says nothing, and will not look away from it.
