Ridge of Echoes

The Ice Cave, the shelf before dawn

Ambience: wind turning on a high terrace, fine dry snow hissing

The tent comes down in the grey. The bell waits in its rope cradle against the rock. Four packs sit ready, and one of them is still open.

On screen: Ilse (hood up, restless), Teodor (steady, watching her), Hesk (repacking fast), Priya (case between her boots, hands flat on the lid)

Ilse stands with her palm on the shelf rock. The wind turns over in the upper bowl and she feels it turn. The forecast gave four days. Her feet give her two.

ILSE The difference gets counted in hours we do not have. We go at first light.

TEODOR Sit down and eat something. You are no good to anyone hungry.

ILSE I am not hungry.

TEODOR The bell comes down in the cradle, middle of the line. We go at the pace of the slowest, and the slowest is the bell.

Priya has sat all night with her hands on the case and has not slept. Hesk repacks, and repacks again, and leaves half of it scattered on the rock.

HESK (tight) I am ready. I am ready.

ILSE Then pick it all up and be ready.

Ilvar Range, the first terrace below the shelf

Ambience: a hiss of spindrift over hard snow

The icefall pours down in steps, a river caught mid-fall. The team moves roped, the cradle between them. The sky above the far wall wears a flat iron look.

On screen: Ilse (front left of the cradle), Teodor (front right), Hesk (rear, head turning), Priya (rear, case strapped high)

They make the first hour the good hour. Dry rock, hard snow, the old track still holding under the new. Every fifty metres Ilse calls the line tight, and the ice remembers where they cut it.

Ilse lays her hand on the bell. Through the bronze and the cradle she feels the terrace, and the ice under it, and the ice is moving.

ILSE (quiet) The ground is on the move. Slow. Something above has not decided yet.

TEODOR Then we do not stand still to argue with it.

Hesk has stopped with the rear rope slack and his head turned back up the way they came. He comes on, and holds his tongue until the second terrace.

Ilvar Range, the second terrace

Ambience: wind lifting, spindrift running low

The cloud below has gone from white to grey and is climbing the mountain instead of falling. Hesk stands at the rear of the cradle with his bare palm flat on the bronze.

On screen: Hesk (working a sum he dislikes), Ilse (watching his mouth), Teodor (listening), Priya (hands on the cradle edge)

HESK We should leave it.

ILSE Say it again.

HESK Fifteen kilos slung between us, and it is going to cost us. I can feel the weight. Slowly is how people die on mountains. You told me that yourself.

HESK We can cache it. Mark the spot, come back in the spring. It is a bell. It has waited a hundred years.

ILSE That is the best argument you have made since the road. Look down.

She points into the icefall. The cloud has swallowed the next terrace and is still climbing.

ILSE In an hour that is a whiteout. Then we go by rope and by voice, and a voice carries nowhere in a whiteout. But you can follow a bell. It was rung through a night to bring six men over ice like this. It already knows the way.

HESK So the bell is a radio, and the radio is dead.

ILSE The radio is not dead yet.

HESK It will be. Then we are four people in a whiteout carrying a bell, when we could be four people running.

ILSE Four people who can find each other beat four who cannot. We take the bell.

Hesk holds her eyes, and the snow goes between them. At last he takes up the rear of the cradle.

HESK Your call. It is your rope.

That is not the end of the argument. It is only the pause.

Ilvar Range, the slope above the third terrace

Ambience: snow settling, wind rising

New snow has filled the old scar and laid a soft white apron over it. Ilse puts her full weight on every step.

On screen: Ilse (probing with each boot), Teodor (behind, rope snug), Hesk (ahead at the cradle), Priya (careful, case high)

Teodor goes into a hole. Not a crevasse. A hollow under the new snow where a boulder has shifted and left a gap.

Sound: a crunch, then a body going over sideways

His boot punches through to the knee, his pack swings, and he goes down with the cradle rope still in his fist. Ilse sits hard on the rope. Hesk is on him before she has finished moving.

On screen: Teodor (in the snow, holding the ankle, breathing through his teeth)

TEODOR Well. That's the day buggered.

Ilse cuts the bootlace and works the boot off. The ankle has swollen to the colour of an old bruise. She feels it front and back with her thumbs.

ILSE (to herself) Bones are whole. I am almost sure the bones are whole.

ILSE Stand. Put your weight on it.

He stands, puts his weight on it, and goes straight down with a sound like a sigh.

PRIYA (steady) That is not a sprain you walk off. A bad ankle and the man rides.

TEODOR Nobody rides. I have no litter and we are not building one. I can hop.

ILSE You do not hop five hundred metres of icefall.

TEODOR Do not put a rope on me and hang me off your shoulder. You need your shoulder for the bell.

Ilse wraps the ankle tight with the long bandage, pulls the boot on, and laces until the leather creaks.

ILSE You walk in the middle of the line now. Between Hesk and Priya. One tug for a good step. Two for a bad. Three and we stop.

TEODOR That is the slowest pace on the mountain.

ILSE That is the pace. Now walk.

Ilvar Range, the long slope above the icefall

Ambience: wind rising to a roar with no direction

The cloud climbs the mountain and meets them. The far wall goes, then the icefall, then the ridge. The world folds into a grey room the size of a rope.

On screen: Ilse (shouting, losing words), Teodor (grey with pain, walking), Hesk (turning, hearing nothing), Priya (case up out of her pack)

Ilse stops them, turns them, and crowds them down around the bell. Her feet feel the wind through the ground, a hard high hum, and beneath it the deep irregular counting of the mountain.

She lays her hands on the bronze. Through it comes the slide of new snow over old slab, running down the grade toward the drop.

ILSE (shouting into Hesk's ear) The snow is moving under the cradle.

Hesk shakes his head and raises his hand in the sign she taught him. Nothing here. Then he shuts one eye and makes a second sign, slow and clear. And on this side the storm is too loud to hear.

Priya pulls off a glove and lays her bare hand on the wooden case. Her face changes. She grabs Hesk's arm and puts his hand where hers had been, and he needs no words.

PRIYA The ground under the case is still. The case is on the old ice of the terrace. The bell is on the moving snow.

Sound: a burst of static, four seconds of a voice, then a flat hiss

The radio cracks against Ilse's shoulder. She works the dial and gets one more click, and then nothing. She straps it away and marks the promise broken.

ILSE (to herself) When we miss the fourth hour, they will know. Their knowing will not reach us.

She gathers them into a huddle and uses her hands on their shoulders, slow and wide. Radio dead. The bell goes on the ridge. Follow the ring.

TEODOR Above the icefall?

ILSE They cannot find us in this. They can find the bell. Four hours and the cloud lifts. The icefall will be a mess below. Now.

HESK We go up on the ridge and we cannot come down the icefall at all. We will be above it with no way down.

ILSE Yes.

HESK And if we stay here we are on a slope that is already moving.

ILSE Yes.

He holds her gaze. Then he makes the flat decision sign with his hand, takes the front of the cradle, and looks back at her.

ILSE (to herself) He has stopped arguing. He has started climbing.

Ilvar Range, the shoulder below the Ridge of Echoes

Ambience: a roar with no direction in it, snow going nowhere

They go back up the ground they have just come down. Four on a rope with a bell between them and nothing past the end of the rope.

On screen: Ilse (sighting the rope, blinded), Teodor (hauling the bad leg, weeping once), Hesk (breaking trail), Priya (rear, case high)

Teodor goes up on the good leg and brings the bad one after and sobs once between his teeth and goes on. Twice the snow surges under Ilse's boots. Once the whole slope lets go somewhere below in the grey, and she neither hears it nor learns where it went.

It takes an hour and a half to gain what twenty minutes would give in clear air. Then they come over the last of the slope onto the shoulder.

The Ridge of Echoes lies along the top of the icefall like a spine. Black ice, packed snow, no cover. The wind comes over it with nothing in the way.

The Ridge of Echoes, in the lee of the great slab

Ambience: wind over bare rock, snow driven flat

A great flat slab of rock stands out of the ridge. They crouch in its lee with their backs to the stone and their knees together. No world past arm's length.

On screen: Ilse (driving a piton), Teodor (sat, ankle out), Hesk (tying off the cradle), Priya (case in her lap, hood gone)

Ilse runs the line to a piton she drives with four blows and ties Teodor off, then Hesk, then Priya. Then she gets the bell out of the cradle.

A spine of rock stands up out of the snow at the high point. She finds a crack wide enough for the crown, lays the bronze in, and wedges it with two flat stones.

She drives a piton above it and ties the crown so it cannot walk away. Then she takes the bar from the cradle's lashing and strikes the bell.

No sound comes through the air. Her bare hand lies on the rock, and the rock takes the sound and gives it to her, down the ridge and into the frozen mountain in a long slow wave.

She strikes again and sets the rhythm she learned in the cave. Four slow beats and a rest. Four and a rest. Then she puts the bar into Teodor's hands and lays his palm on the rock until he nods.

TEODOR I feel it. Go and rest. I have got it.

The hours that follow are not hours a person counts. They take the bar in turns and keep the bell ringing, and the world stays folded in grey.

The Ridge of Echoes, in a lull

Ambience: wind falling a little, then filling again

The grey has darkened. Priya sits with her back to the slab and the case in her lap. Her hood is down and her hair is stiff with rime.

On screen: Priya (flat, far away), Hesk (crouched beside her), Ilse (at the bell), Teodor (against the rock)

PRIYA I lied to all of you.

HESK I know. You tried to burn the page.

PRIYA That is not the lie. I told Ilse I was a woman who had found some papers. I told base camp the archive funded the equipment. It put in a little. The most of it was me.

PRIYA My savings and a loan against my flat and a bad arrangement with a man in the city who calls himself a banker. If it failed I would have gone home to nothing.

PRIYA I thought that was why I was doing it. So that at the end I could say I paid for the truth and it was expensive and I was brave. That is a lie too. I was doing it so I would not have to read the truth about my own family.

HESK The guide. Karel Vasek. He was the one with the painting.

PRIYA He was the one with the painting. The east hall has him with the bell-rope and his mouth open, singing, coming down out of the sky to save them. He was in a tent with his shoulder broken and his face broken and could not say what day it was.

PRIYA Every plaque in my country says his name. And my great-great-grandfather's name is the cook's shame. My mother whispered it. I was not allowed to ask. She taught me to be ashamed of him.

HESK He rang it all night. With no way up and no way down. Why ring at all, when no one could hear and he could not hear the answer?

PRIYA Because it was the only thing he could do.

Hesk looks down the ridge to where Ilse stands at the bell with the bar in her hands. He gets up and goes along the rope.

On screen: Hesk (beside her, bare hand on the rock), Ilse (bar raised)

HESK I see it now. You are not calling the mountain. You are asking it where it is going. Weiss did the same. He put the bell in the ice and asked it to carry his voice where he could not.

ILSE Yes.

HESK Your bet is unpaid.

ILSE Let it stay unpaid. It was a stupid bet.

Ilse strikes the bell, and they both feel it go down the ridge into the dark, and Hesk gives her the nod he gave her in the icefall.

The Ridge of Echoes, deep night

Ambience: a roar with no direction, nothing past the rope

There is no night and no day. Only the ridge and the bell and the four of them. The grey has gone to moving blackness.

On screen: Ilse (in the lee, palm on rock), Teodor (bar in hand), Hesk (crouched by the slab), Priya (hands between her knees)

Teodor rings first and his hands are good. Ilse lies in the lee where the rock carries the strokes and corrects the rhythm with her palm when it drifts. Then Hesk rings, then Priya, then Ilse again.

PRIYA (slow with cold) They might not come. Even if they hear. No one can climb in this.

ILSE The valley keeps a clock even when we do not. They will have missed us by now. They will be at the foot of the icefall and they will hear nothing and they will stop and wonder.

ILSE Then one of them will feel the ground go small and tight under his boots. He will put his hand down. He will walk up the mountain to the place it is coming from. The rescue is not a thing you hear. It is a thing you follow.

HESK We could try the descent. The ridge runs down the east side. If we roped the bell down the east face, slowly, four at a time, we might make the bottom before the worst of the cold.

HESK If we stay here we freeze. Asking, not arguing. Might is better than sitting.

ILSE Then feel it yourself. Come and put your hand where mine was.

Hesk goes to the edge, lays his bare hand on the ice through the snow, and stays there a while.

ILSE Loose and grainy. The whole face is sliding against itself. Four hundred feet of a sieve on a face we cannot see. We would be down the first pitch and it would take us, and the bell would be under it.

HESK Then we stay.

He makes the flat decision sign and goes back to the slab and sits down.

PRIYA Have you ever been in one that did not end?

Ilse lets the question sit. She thinks of a sentry line set too late on another mountain, and a partner who did not come home. Then she makes herself look at Priya.

ILSE I have been in one when it ended wrong. The mountain does not tell you which kind it is until the end. So you keep ringing.

She strikes the bell. Four and a rest. The rock takes it and she feels it go down the ridge and out into the mountain, and somewhere far below, the mountain carries it on.

TEODOR (from the slab) Ilse. If it comes down to it. The ringing. You keep it going, you hear me. Not me. You.

ILSE Nobody is leaving anybody on this ridge. Ring your set.

He rings his set. Four and a rest. The wind takes the sound and the ground keeps it, and the night settles over the Ridge of Echoes with no way down and no radio and four people keeping a sound alive in the ground.
