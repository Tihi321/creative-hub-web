The Silent Signal

The Ridge of Echoes, deep night

Ambience: a roar with no direction, nothing past the rope

The lee of the great slab is black. Four shapes sit close, capped with rime, and the bell stands in its crack with the bar through the crown.

On screen: Ilse (back to the slab, palm on the rock), Teodor (sat beside her, bad foot out), Hesk (at the head of the tent, shovel across his knees), Priya (hands round her knees)

Ilse counts the dark by the strokes. Four strikes, then a rest. Each set sends her hand a little deeper into the rock.

ILSE (to herself) This rock has been struck a thousand times. It has never once rung.

Through her palm she reads where the ridge stands solid and where it rings hollow. Where the ice meets the stone. Where the stone has broken away and hangs.

PRIYA Do you feel them yet?

ILSE I feel the mountain. It does not answer quickly. Keep the pot going and wake us to drink.

Priya works the stove. Hesk rises without being told and goes out to check the anchor, and Ilse follows him through the floor with her hand.

The Ridge of Echoes, later in the dark

Ambience: a roar with no direction, nothing past the rope

The grey has gone to moving blackness. Ilse walks the small circle of the packs and lays her palm on everything that will take it.

On screen: Ilse (walking the circle), Teodor (flat, eyes open), Priya (pot steady), Hesk (at the door)

The wind is dying. The roar has left it, and what remains is a hiss and a tugging. The storm is spending itself.

At the east edge of the ridge her hand finds the loose ice face has stopped sliding and firmed again, the way a face does when the wind that worked it rests.

ILSE (to herself) The east is quiet. Quiet is not safe. Quiet is only quiet.

She lays her palm on the bronze still wedged in its crack and strikes it once, hard. Then she waits, counting.

ILSE Hesk. Come down. Put your hands where mine are and tell me what you feel.

Hesk goes to his knees, pulls off a glove, and lays both hands flat on the snow and the rock beneath it.

HESK One. A pause. One. That is not ours. Too fast. Too eager.

ILSE Iron on iron. Down in the icefall.

HESK Nobody has any business down there.

ILSE Nobody sensible. Fetch the bar.

She strikes once more, and the answer comes back up through the ice inside a count of ten. Slow, the way cold carries a thing. And human.

The Ridge of Echoes, at the bell

Ambience: wind thinning to a hiss and a tug

Ilse goes down the line, shouting into ears and setting her hands on shoulders, her signs wide.

On screen: Ilse (face close, signs wide), Priya (nodding), Teodor (listening), Hesk (bar in fist, waiting)

ILSE They are below. They hear us. We keep ringing.

She stops at Hesk, and puts her face close to his so he can read her mouth.

ILSE Steady. Slow it down. Do not let them run to us.

HESK They are coming to a sound in a whiteout. A man runs, he comes over a cornice, and then we are five bells short.

ILSE That is exactly it. Ring slow. Four strikes, then rest. Let them walk to us one step at a time.

He nods, and the nod has no argument in it at all, only the readiness of a man handed a thing to do.

HESK Four and a rest. I have got it. Go and be warm.

The Ridge of Echoes, the long hour

Ambience: a hiss and a tug, gaps between

The little circle of the slab has a stove going and four bodies close. Nobody is allowed to lie still.

On screen: Ilse (walking the circle), Priya (bar in hands, counting), Teodor (against Priya's knee), Hesk (rope end, eyes up the ridge)

Ilse puts the bar into Priya's hands and makes her take the rhythm. She sets Teodor where they can keep each other's warmth. She sets Hesk at the rope end.

ILSE Now we work. That is the whole of the work. Four cold people busy enough not to lie down.

PRIYA One, two, three, four. Rest. One, two, three, four.

TEODOR You are a beat ahead of the mountain.

PRIYA I am cold.

TEODOR Then ring slow. Cold is a reason, not an excuse.

Ilse walks the circle a third time. The answer comes through the rock, not Priya's, then nearer, and she lets the counting go and counts only answers.

The strokes come up the ridge one step at a time now, careful. A man who trusts his footing because he can feel it and cannot see it. The ringing stops. The feet come on.

A shape walks out of the white into the small clear air around the slab. Ilse is on her feet before she has decided to be.

The Ridge of Echoes, Nima arrives

Ambience: wind thinning, the hiss going out of it

Nima is thin and dark and haggard, his eyebrows furred with frost. He comes up the last step with his head down and his shoulders in, and stops.

On screen: Nima (frost in his brows, shoulders in), Ilse (upright, staring), Teodor (half raised), Hesk (rope end, spun round), Priya (bar in hands)

He stands still and looks at the four of them and at the bell. Then he makes the sign Ilse taught him. I have been listening and at last I am here.

ILSE (signing back) You came up.

NIMA I came up.

TEODOR Your head. It was full of loud water at the col.

NIMA Dawa is below at the terrace with the line. Two more behind me from the rescue party, with the doctor. Slow.

NIMA I lay down at the col and the ground went small and tight under me. I said to Dawa, the mountain is ringing like struck iron. So I got up and I came.

ILSE The ringing is better than the lying down.

NIMA Yes. Come. We go before the wind comes round again.

The Ridge of Echoes, the descent begins

Ambience: wind thinning to a pull

The cradle is cut up out of the rock and the bell set back into it. Four men take the ropes. Two rescue men come up with spars and a doctor who goes straight to Teodor.

On screen: Ilse (roping up), Nima (probe in hand), Teodor (sitting down, not arguing), the doctor (thin, face like a folded map), the rescue man (coil across his chest), Hesk (at the cradle)

The doctor takes one look at the ankle and speaks to Nima, and Teodor sits down at once and lets himself be laced and strapped, and does not argue.

ILSE (to herself) Teodor arguing was the mountain. Teodor sitting still is the road home.

THE DOCTOR (slow, flat) The strap stays on and he rides. No weight on it. Not one step.

TEODOR I have walked a good deal further on a worse foot.

THE DOCTOR Not this evening.

They strap him and lift him and set him on the litter, and Ilse sees the case strapped on her own back, because she is not going to hand it to anyone on a face where a man could go over.

Ilvar Range, the ridge above the icefall

Ambience: wind thinning, a pull with gaps

The ridge is worse than they left it. New snow has filled the hollows and made a soft lie of the ground, and the ice beneath is black and hard.

On screen: Nima (leading, probe tapping), Ilse (second, rope in hand), Hesk (cradle), Priya (running the litter line), the rescue man (beside Teodor)

Nima goes first with a probe and a long step and a short one, tapping ahead like a blind man in his own house. Ilse goes second with her feet reading the ice through her soles.

ILSE The hollows are lying. Nobody trusts a flat white place tonight.

NIMA Then we step where I step and we do not step pretty.

They bring the party down the way they brought them up. By feeling.

Ilvar Range, the edge of the icefall

Ambience: a hiss of wind through broken blocks

The whiteout thins and does not lift. The icefall walks out of the grey, broken and tumbled, with no track left in it at all. The track they cut coming up is gone.

On screen: Ilse (at the edge, palm to the ground), Nima (beside her), Hesk (cradle ropes), Priya (litter line), the rescue man (waiting)

Ilse stands at the edge and feels the icefall through the ground. It is a new thing. A heap of fallen blocks with water moving underneath.

ILSE Not the old line.

NIMA The new one. East, under the wall.

ILSE The east face was sliding.

NIMA The sliding is done. I felt it coming down. It went quiet.

Ilse lays her palm flat and holds it there. Quiet, and the quiet has the set of a thing that has finished moving.

ILSE East. But the bell comes last. If that face goes, it goes without us on it.

HESK And if it goes with the men halfway down it?

ILSE Then they have a rope and a voice and a way back. The bell has none of those. It goes last.

Hesk looks at her a moment, then takes the word without argument and sets the cradle down.

Ilvar Range, inside the changed icefall

Ambience: a hiss of wind through broken blocks

Two hours to make a terrace they crossed in a quarter of an hour. Every step is work. The bell comes down in the cradle with four men at the ropes.

On screen: Ilse (case on her back, feet reading), Nima (finding the way), Hesk (front of the cradle), Priya (litter line, hands clenched), the rescue man (point, axe out)

The rescue men take the points. Nima finds the way. The blocks are slotted and loose and a man's boot can go to the ankle between them.

NIMA Here. Then left. Then the long step and do not look down.

ILSE Priya. Snug the line. The litter goes where I go.

PRIYA The litter goes where you go.

Halfway down the second terrace the wind stops altogether. The silence that comes into the icefall has weight to it, and Ilse stands in it and lets herself breathe.

Ilvar Range, the second terrace of the icefall

The icefall has gone quiet. A fallen block of ice stands in the middle of the terrace. Priya sits down on it with the case in her lap and does not get up.

On screen: Ilse (coming down the step to her), Priya (grey with cold, crying), Hesk (stopped with the cradle), the rescue man (waiting), Nima (above, watching)

Ilse comes down the step and puts her hand under Priya's arm.

ILSE Up. We are not stopping on the terrace.

PRIYA No. I mean, yes. But.

The crying has come on her all at once. It has the shape of a thing held under and come up. The tears are freezing as they come.

PRIYA I have to say it before we get down. Before base camp. Before any of them.

ILSE You told me on the mountain.

PRIYA Not the whole of it. I planned it, Ilse. Before we left the city I planned it. I brought a cutter and I had planned to take the book and to burn what did not suit me and call the rest the truth.

PRIYA I financed the whole of it so I could come up here and make my great-great-grandfather the hero of it.

ILSE Look at me.

PRIYA I would have done it. If it had not been for the boy with his glove. Tell me what the record has to call it.

Ilse crouches down on the ice so that her face is level with Priya's. Her feet on the terrace tell her nothing is moving. Her hand on the case tells her it is steady and cold.

ILSE The record calls it what you just did. You tried to burn a page and then you read it out loud to four people. You meant to bury the truth and then you carried it down a whiteout.

PRIYA That is a shameful record.

ILSE It is a true one. There is a man in your family who rang a bell all night and could not hear it answered. There is a man in your family who wanted to take his page out of the book and did not.

ILSE The book goes to the archive entire. I will see it published whole, and I will write the preface, and I will not be kind. Do you understand me? It will not be kind and it will be told.

PRIYA Then I will be the one to tell it.

Hesk comes down the step with the bar in his fist. He has heard none of it and guessed most of it. He sets his hand flat on the block beside her.

HESK (flat) The case goes with her and nobody touches it. That is my part. The story is not mine.

HESK Get up, Priya. You will have years afterwards to be ashamed in. Do not do it on a terrace.

Priya wipes her face with her mitten and makes a small business of it, and then stands straight.

PRIYA Hand me the rope.

Ilvar Range, the last terrace below the icefall

The blocks thin to snow. The icefall is behind them. Below, the glacier runs pale and flat to the moraine, and the camp's tents show as small orange roofs.

On screen: Ilse (at the bell), Nima (lowering the bar to her), Hesk (beside him), Priya (litter line, upright), the rescue man (holding the cradle ropes)

At the last terrace the wind is gone and the night is clear above. The bell comes down onto hard snow and stands.

NIMA The line below is set.

ILSE Then we ring once more and we let them know we are here and coming down.

Ilse sets her feet flat on the snow, and the tone comes up through the ice into her boot soles and up the bones of her legs, a clean round note that goes out into the dark.

Sound: a long bronze note and silence after

She waits. The answer comes up through the ice, three beats and a pause and three beats, faint and steady, from the tents below.

ILSE Four beats. Slow. Let them know.

Hesk takes the bar and rings it. Ilse stands with her palm on the bronze and lets it go, and the whole mountain carries it down.

HESK (quietly) They heard it all night. The whole line below. We were not ringing for nothing.

ILSE There is no such thing as ringing for nothing. Come on. We have a road and it is downhill, and I intend to walk all of it.
