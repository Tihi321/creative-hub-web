The Archive

The Archive, morning

Ambience: the city talking through the pavement, buses and footsteps and a machine humming far below

Grey stone. Built to look as though nothing inside it could ever be wrong. Reporters crowd the steps in wet coats, cameras up, breath smoking.

On screen: Ilse (boots planted, palm flat on the entrance stone), Priya (case in her arms, chin lifted), Teodor (on a stick, cheerful, bad foot in a strapped boot), Hesk (behind them, coat buttoned wrong)

Ilse presses her palm to the stone. The machines in the belly of the building turn, and the turning comes up through her soles, a fine steady hum. Like standing on the skin of a sleeping animal.

A reporter leans in with a recorder. He asks, slowly and loudly, whether she needs help up the steps.

ILSE I can feel the building. I can hear your question. Neither of those is the trouble.

Priya goes up ahead of them with the case. The reporters make a lane, the way a river makes a lane around a stone.

HESK (muttering) There are more of them than there were of us on the whole mountain.

The Archive, the east hall

Ambience: a hundred people holding still, chairs creaking, a tall room breathing

The hall is full to the doors. At the far end hangs the painting, two men high: a strong man with a rope and his mouth open wide as if singing, and at his feet a small dark figure with a tray of cups. The bell stands on a plinth beneath it. The logbook lies open in a glass case, the burned page in its own small frame and its own small light.

On screen: Ilse (stopped in the doorway, hand on the frame), Priya (walking to the plinth), Teodor (limping to a chair), Hesk (staying by the door), the thin archivist (front row, glasses on a chain)

The floorboards lean. A hundred people, all leaning the same way. A boat leans like that when everyone aboard has gone to one rail.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST Miss Marchetti. We have kept you a seat at the front. The committee will hear us after.

ILSE Then let us give them something to hear.

The Archive, in front of the painting

Ambience: the tall room breathing, a hundred chairs still

Priya sets both hands on the edge of the plinth and looks up at the open mouth in the painting.

On screen: Priya (pale, steady), Ilse (seated, watching the floor), Teodor (foot on a second chair), Hesk (by the door, arms folded), the thin archivist (glasses in hand)

PRIYA For a hundred and twelve years, this painting has told you that a guide named Karel Vasek rang the bell in the storm of the eleventh of September, nineteen hundred and eight.

PRIYA I am Anders Weiss's great-great-granddaughter, and I am here to tell you that the painting is wrong.

Nobody moves. The hall has the pressure of held breath.

PRIYA The man who rang the bell was the cook. The cook was deaf. He rang in four strikes and a pause, from midnight until dawn, and six men lived. The guide lay unconscious under a rock and did not wake until it was over.

PRIYA This is his ledger. He wrote down every hour of that night, and every name, and how he rang.

She lifts the logbook and the framed page together so the lights catch them.

PRIYA The first page was burned by me. In the ice cave, on the mountain, with the flame I carried up there for the purpose. I meant to finish the job and burn the rest. Hesk Vandry crushed it out with his glove, and the hole is the size of that glove, and both of those things are the record now.

PRIYA I intended to come home with a book that agreed with me. I intended to bury him again. I want that written down with the rest of it.

She sets the logbook down and puts her hand flat on the glass.

PRIYA The shame is not that my great-great-grandfather was a cook. The shame is that this building hung his picture and called him a coward and did not look for the truth for a hundred and twelve years. I ask the archive to change the wall.

Nobody claps. The holding goes on so long Ilse feels it as pressure in the boards. Then a woman in the front row stands, and another, and the clapping comes unevenly, like hail.

Near the back a heavyset man in a work coat is on his feet, shouting that a cook cannot have rung a bell he could not hear. Ilse feels his stamping through the floor the way she felt the slab move on the hanging slope.

ILSE (to Teodor, quietly) Anger through the floor, I trust. The rest I do not.

TEODOR The rest is a wet room and a warm coat. Let it run.

The Archive, the committee room

Ambience: rain on a window, a pen not writing

Five of them behind a long table under a wet window. Two are from the newspaper. The sponsor's man sits with his clipboard on his knees.

On screen: Priya (at the end of the table, logbook in front of her), Ilse (seated), Teodor (foot on a second chair), the thin archivist (at the table's head), the young clerk (ink on his cuff), the sponsor's man (clipboard, pen hovering)

The young clerk lays a letter on the table and smooths it flat with two fingers, as though it might otherwise get away.

THE YOUNG CLERK Delivered a week ago. A nephew of a nephew of Karel Vasek writes that the bell was Vasek's property, carried up by Vasek's men, and that we hold it without title. He asks us to determine ownership before the bell is exhibited.

THE YOUNG CLERK There is a second letter from the newspaper. Their man wants the claim heard in public, and a statement.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN That is what we shall write down. Whose bell is it?

PRIYA Vasek's men carried it up. A cook rang it. A nephew of a nephew is welcome to the account and not welcome to the bell.

The five look at one another. The young clerk lifts his pen and does not write with it. Ilse feels the hesitation go round the table through the wood, a rope going soft when everyone on it has stopped pulling.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST Then say this, and write it exactly. The archive has examined the claim. The bell was raised by an expedition and given to the people of this city, and any man who believes he owns it may put his belief before a court, at his own cost, and the archive will meet him there.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST That is the statement. Print it under whatever headline you like. And tell your man at the back of the hall that we have heard him shout.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST A bell rung by a deaf man is a bell rung by a man who could feel it and not hear it. That is the whole of what this building has failed to understand for a hundred and twelve years.

THE SPONSOR'S MAN (writing fast) I have that. I have all of it.

ILSE The bell came off the mountain in the hands of a deaf guide, a cook's great-great-granddaughter, and a boy who bet against them both. It can sit here while the lawyers enjoy themselves. It is not going far.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST Miss Marchetti. You led the expedition. You are deaf. And the boy who bet against you is enrolling in this school I keep hearing about.

ILSE He is the first one through the door. He says he will say he was wrong every year until they are sick of him.

The thin archivist holds Ilse a while over her glasses. Then she turns to the table and sets her hand flat on the wet wood.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST Then I move we put the cook's name on the wall before the lawyers put the bell in a cupboard. This archive has told a wrong story for a hundred and twelve years, and I shall enjoy writing the correction.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST And I should like to hear about the school. Education is our business here. If we have been telling children the wrong story, we should be the ones to tell them the right one, and tell them who carried it down.

That is how it begins. A thin old woman, a sentence, a wet window. Before the committee rises, it has become a grant.

The Archive, the east hall

Ambience: a soft quiet, a coat dripping, chairs turning

The hall has thinned and not emptied. The painting is still up. The bell stands on its plinth with the light running down its shoulder.

On screen: Hesk (coat wet through, moving to the plinth), Ilse (in the aisle, turning), Priya (by the glass case), the thin archivist (front row), Teodor (chair, foot out)

Hesk comes in from the street with his coat dripping and stands at the plinth. The hall goes quiet in a soft way, the way a room goes quiet for a boy who is about to do something difficult.

HESK I said I would say this at the school. Then I thought, the trouble with a quiet apology is that the ones who need to hear it are usually not in the room.

HESK I bet a week's pay that a deaf guide would call a crevasse that was not there. I did it on the first morning, in front of the whole expedition.

HESK I said it because I did not want to believe someone could feel the mountain in a way I never would, and because a bet was easier than being frightened.

He looks at Ilse across the hall. She does not move, and she does not help him.

HESK She called the real crevasse on the glacier. The serac in the icefall. The slab on the hanging slope. The hollow under the new snow that took Teodor Brann's ankle. The east face loose, when I was all for going down it with four men and a bell.

HESK I was wrong every time. I told her so on the mountain, and I am telling the city so in this building, so it will go in the record with the rest.

Close-up: a folded pay slip, signed across the back in a hard young hand, laid on the plinth beside the bell

The slip is creased and grey at the edges. The signature is large and careful, as though he practised it.

HESK My week's pay. It is the first money in the school. I am the first pupil, and I will say this every year on the first day until they are tired of me.

A woman near the front says something to her neighbour. The neighbour shakes her head and smiles at the boy by the bell with his wet coat and his folded paper.

ILSE (quietly, to Priya) He paid it in money at the bottom, as he said he would.

PRIYA He paid it twice. The mountain took the first one.

the valley town, morning

Ambience: bread rising below, a shutter creaking, boots on bare boards

A long low room with a stove, a coil of rope on a peg, and bread smell coming up through the floor all morning. Four pupils stand in a line.

On screen: Ilse (at the window, chalk in hand), Teodor (stool by the window, stick against the wall, bad foot out), Hesk (rope in fist, alert), the two women (older, watchful, new boots), the boy of sixteen (long wrists, trying not to look eager)

Four pupils. That is all the money there is. One of them is Hesk, two were told all their lives that a deaf guide was a kindness and not a trade, and one came because his parents think he is here to learn rope work.

TEODOR I am not teaching you this because I am fit. I am teaching you this because I am not.

TEODOR If a man cannot climb for a season, he can still teach what he knows. A school that only teaches when the teacher is fit is a school with no teachers in it half the year.

He puts Hesk on a bight of rope and makes him tie it until his fingers know it better than his brain. He does not talk while he ties.

TEODOR The first way was a guess. I do not teach guesses. Tie it again.

THE BOY OF SIXTEEN Master Brann. Is it true you came down the mountain carried?

TEODOR It is true. And every man on that ridge carried something, and the heaviest thing was a bell. Now tie it.

ILSE Put the rope down. Coats on. We are going up the screes before the light.

the valley town, before dawn

Ambience: wind over loose stone, a thin hiss, a town waking far below

The screes run grey and steep above the roofs. The town lies below in one lamp and another. Five people stand in a line with their eyes shut and their boots on the ground.

On screen: Ilse (at the end of the line, hands loose), Hesk (eyes shut, frowning), the two women (cold, determined), the boy of sixteen (mouth open, breathing)

Ilse walks the line and sets a hand on each shoulder. Then she stands back.

ILSE Boots on the ground. Eyes shut. Do not listen.

THE BOY OF SIXTEEN What am I listening for?

ILSE You are not listening. You are feeling. The stones tell you how they lie. Put the whole of your foot down and wait.

They stand in the cold for an hour. Nobody feels anything, and nobody admits it. The wind moves over the scree and the loose stones chatter somewhere below.

HESK (suddenly, voice carefully flat) The wind is coming from the east. The stones are ringing on that side. I can feel them ring.

ILSE Good. That is the wind on the stones. Now feel the difference when it is a man.

She walks down the slope behind them, and they feel her come before they turn round. The boy of sixteen laughs out loud, once, and puts his hand over his mouth.

ILSE (to herself) A boy in a snow cave with his palm on the ice, asking whether the mountain was always talking. It is always talking. The trick is to be still enough to hear.

the valley town, afternoon

Ambience: bread cooling below, a stove ticking, chalk on wood

The rope hangs on its peg. Chalk numbers cover one wall. Priya sets a folder on the table and keeps her hands on it.

On screen: Priya (folder under her hands), Ilse (chalk still in hers), Teodor (stool, boot strapped), Hesk (at the window, listening), the boy of sixteen (sweeping)

PRIYA Three places a year. Tuition, bed and board, boots and a coat. Two from the valley and one from anywhere.

PRIYA It is not enough to be rich and it is too much to be nothing, and the interest on my flat will pay for it if I am careful and do not eat in restaurants.

PRIYA I want it called the Weiss Scholarship. If the archive wishes to put my name to the money, it may, in small letters, at the bottom, where the lawyers find it and the visitors do not.

ILSE Your name will not be small. It is in the preface. I wrote it.

PRIYA I have read the draft. You called me a coward and a fool, and then you said I brought the book down off the mountain and gave it to the men who would hate me for it.

PRIYA A woman who does that has paid for the bell twice, once in money and once in shame. I have not been called a fool in print before. It is a great deal better than being pitied.

ILSE Then the big letters are his, and the small letters are yours, and mine are at the bottom saying I am deaf and led by touch. That is the whole of the order.

TEODOR And my name is on nothing at all, which suits me. I have a stool and a stick and a class of guesses to correct.

The Archive, winter evening

Ambience: the building humming to itself, an open door letting in the cold

The hall is empty of visitors. A blank wall where the painting hung. On the boards below it, the bell on its plinth, the logbook in its case, the burned page in its frame. A small card in the middle of the blank wall.

On screen: Ilse (middle of the hall, palm to the floor), Priya (under the blank wall, hands folded), Hesk (at the bell, bar in hand), Teodor (chair, foot out, stick against it), the thin archivist (glasses off)

Close-up: a small card on a blank gallery wall

The card says: In preparation — Portrait of Anders Weiss, Cook, 1908. Beneath it a smaller card says the painting previously on this wall may be studied in the east annexe, under glass, with a label containing the word wrong eleven times.

HESK I counted them. It is the finest thing this building has ever done.

THE THIN ARCHIVIST I wrote it. I did not write one of the elevens by accident.

TEODOR I have climbed with this woman nineteen years and she never once told me what she was going to do before she did it. Now she has a preface that says the whole of it in plain words.

TEODOR I did not recognise her in it. She is much better company in the preface than she is at four in the morning on a snow slope.

The thin archivist laughs until she has to take her glasses off. Priya does not cry and is not grey. She looks at the word wrong on the card as though she has walked there from very far away.

PRIYA Ring it.

HESK Here? In the hall?

THE THIN ARCHIVIST We are a library. If a bell cannot be rung in a library, where may it be rung?

Ilse walks to the middle of the hall and sets her palm to the floor. The building is quiet. The city hums through the stones, indifferent to the whole business of the hall.

She feels the bell on its plinth, a small dense weight above the boards, and the people standing round her, and the winter cold coming in at the open door.

ILSE Ring it.

Hesk swings the bar once and the bell speaks. Ilse does not hear it. She feels it come through the plinth and the old boards and up into her boots, a great round tone rolling out into the stone.

Picture: Hesk Vandry swings the bell's iron bar in the empty east hall of The Archive while Ilse Marchetti stands on the old boards in the middle of the hall with her palm flat and her feet apart, and Priya Rask and Teodor Brann turn their faces toward the sound

It rang in a cave in an ice cliff in nineteen hundred and eight and brought six men home. It rang on the Ridge of Echoes in a whiteout and brought four.

It rings now among wet streets and people going home to supper, and one of the people standing in it is a deaf woman from the valley with the whole of the bell in her bones.

ILSE (to herself) Somebody somewhere is feeling this and does not yet know what it is.

ILSE Again.

He rings it again. The tone goes through her and through the hall and out, and she thinks of a school above a bakery and a boy who bet against her and was the first through its door.

ILSE Again. One more, and then we will go and eat.

The third note rolls out under the door and down the steps into the winter air. Hesk sets the bar down. Priya puts her hand on the glass case. Nobody in the hall says anything at all.
