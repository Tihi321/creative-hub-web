The Three Keepers

Salon, a few minutes past one in the morning

Ambience: wind against tall windows

Madame Vérac stops applauding. She folds her bare hands in her lap, and for a moment the smile is gone entirely, like a lamp somebody forgot to light.

On screen: Madame Vérac (bare hands, no smile), Inès (steady), Pichon (stunned, reaching for his notebook)

MADAME VÉRAC (neutral) Very good, my dear. Very tidy. You would have made a fine auctioneer.

PICHON (surprised) Madame, you confess?

MADAME VÉRAC I confess that Mademoiselle Varga is correct. It is not the same thing, but it will do for your paperwork.

She turns back her left cuff. Strapped to her wrist is a plain silver watch with no hands at all.

Close-up: A plain silver wristwatch on Madame Vérac's wrist, with a clean white face and no hands.

DEC (worried) Oh, I hate that watch. I hate that I know what that watch means.

MADAME VÉRAC (happy) Gaspard thought the Ninth Key was his to sell. Nothing of ours is ever sold, Monsieur Hartley. It is only lent.

INÈS Where is it?

MADAME VÉRAC Gone home. It went up the belfry of the old chapel by the Seine before his tisane was cold. Three keepers guard it now.

DEC Three keepers. Lovely. Are they friendly keepers?

On screen: Madame Vérac (smiling again), Inès (narrowed eyes), Dec (uneasy), Pichon (standing, handcuffs out)

MADAME VÉRAC (happy) Each keeper of the belfry takes one of two vows, my dear. The Struck Hour or the Stopped Hour. The first never lies. The second never tells the truth.

INÈS Which keeper took which?

MADAME VÉRAC That would be telling. And I am, as of this moment, no longer telling anything.

She holds out her wrists to Pichon as if offering him a lot at a very good price.

PICHON (tired) Madame Vérac, you are under arrest. Properly. Not in principle.

MADAME VÉRAC How refreshing.

Salon, half past one

The officers have taken Madame Vérac to the car. The Baron is demanding a taxi. Rémy is sitting on the lectern step, eating one of his own tarts very slowly.

On screen: Pichon (buttoning his raincoat), Dec (pacing), Inès (winding her mustard scarf)

PICHON I will send a car to this chapel in the morning, when I have a warrant, a judge and a croissant.

DEC In the morning it'll be in Switzerland. Or in a lake. Or inside another dead man.

INÈS We go now.

PICHON (angry) You go nowhere. You are witnesses. Witnesses sit.

INÈS Then arrest us, Commissaire. Properly. Not in principle.

Pichon looks at her for a long moment. Then he looks at the forty dead clocks. He takes a roll of canvas out of his coat and puts it in Dec's hands.

PICHON (grumbling) I never gave you these. I am going to the car to argue with a judge. If you fall off a tower, I will be very annoyed.

DEC (happy) The cutlery. Commissaire, I could kiss you.

PICHON Then I would arrest you again. Go.

Belfry stairs, two o'clock in the morning

Ambience: wind moaning through arrow slits, water lapping far below

A narrow stone staircase winds up inside the chapel tower. Through the arrow slits, the lights of the Seine slide past, very small and very far down.

On screen: Dec (holding a lantern, cheerful), Inès (one hand on the wall, pale)

DEC Two hundred and twelve steps. I counted. I'm a good person to climb with, because I narrate.

INÈS (scared) Please do not narrate.

DEC You're afraid of heights.

INÈS I am afraid of the bottom. The heights are fine. It is the distance to the bottom.

DEC (kind) Look at my back, then. My back is very boring. People have said so.

Sound: a heavy wooden door creaking open above them

Lantern light spills down the stairs, and with it the smell of pigeons, cold bronze and candle wax.

Belfry, a quarter past two

Ambience: wind through wooden louvres, pigeons shifting on beams, a faint hum from the bells

Three great bronze bells hang from oak beams, the great bell in the middle. Moonlight comes through the louvres in pale stripes. Ropes drop through holes in the floor.

Picture: Brother Anselm, Brother Tobias and Sister Odile stand in a row before the three great bronze bells in the moonlit belfry, lanterns at their feet, watching Dec and Inès climb in through the trapdoor.

DEC (worried) Evening. Morning. Hello. We're not here to steal anything.

INÈS We are here to take something back. It is different.

On screen: Brother Anselm (stooped, iron key ring), Brother Tobias (round face, sandals, grinning), Sister Odile (black veil, handless watch on a cord)

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) Welcome, welcome! We love visitors. We have a great many visitors at two in the morning.

SISTER ODILE (neutral) You are very welcome here.

BROTHER ANSELM (thinking) You are not welcome. But you are here, so you should know the rule of this tower.

DEC Rule. Good. Rules are great. I love a rule.

The old keeper speaks slowly and far too loudly, as if the bells were still ringing in his ears.

Clue: In this belfry, Brother Anselm explains, every keeper sworn to the Struck Hour always tells the truth (a knight, Dec mutters), and every keeper sworn to the Stopped Hour always lies (a knave). Which of the three is a knight and which is a knave?

INÈS (thinking) So each of you is one or the other. And you will not say which.

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) Oh, we would never hide a thing like that.

DEC He's hiding it.

On screen: Inès (notebook out), Dec (lantern raised), Sister Odile (cold, still)

INÈS Then tell me about each other. That much you can do.

Sister Odile does not blink. She touches the handless watch at her throat.

Clue: "Brother Anselm and I are both sworn to the Struck Hour," Sister Odile says. "We are both knights."

On screen: Inès (writing), Dec (lantern raised), Brother Tobias (bouncing on his bare toes)

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) My turn! I hate going next.

Clue: "Sister Odile is a knight, sworn to the Struck Hour," Brother Tobias says, beaming at her.

On screen: Inès (writing), Dec (lantern raised), Brother Anselm (leaning on a beam, tired)

BROTHER ANSELM (sad) And I say this, though it costs me nothing to say it.

Clue: "Brother Tobias is a knave," Brother Anselm says. "He is sworn to the Stopped Hour."

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) How very kind of you, Brother.

DEC Right. Right. So somebody's lying, and possibly several somebodies, and the floor is made of very old wood.

On screen: Brother Anselm (pointing up), Brother Tobias (pointing at the trapdoor), Sister Odile (pointing at the louvres)

INÈS The Ninth Key. Where is it?

BROTHER ANSELM Behind the great bell. On the beam, in the dark.

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) Down in the crypt, under the third stone. Easy to find. No traps at all.

SISTER ODILE (neutral) It was thrown into the river an hour ago.

DEC (worried) Three keepers. Three directions. One of them I'd follow, and the other two would get me killed.

INÈS Then we work out who is a knight and who is a knave before you put a foot anywhere.

DEC You've got the restoring face again.

INÈS (thinking) Quiet. I am removing the varnish.

The bells hum softly above them. Pigeons shuffle on the beams. Three keepers wait, smiling or not smiling, in the moonlight.

Puzzle: The Three Keepers

  • In the belfry, the keepers sworn to the Struck Hour always tell the truth (knights) and the keepers sworn to the Stopped Hour always lie (knaves). Who is a knight and who is a knave?
  • Sister Odile says: "Brother Anselm and I are both knights."
  • Brother Tobias says: "Sister Odile is a knight."
  • Brother Anselm says: "Brother Tobias is a knave."

INÈS (determined) Brother Anselm is the knight. Brother Tobias and Sister Odile are both knaves.

Sister Odile's hand closes on her handless watch. Brother Tobias stops smiling for the first time all night.
