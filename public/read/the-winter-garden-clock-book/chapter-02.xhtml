Who Held the Clock

Salon, half past eleven

Ambience: wind against tall windows, an officer's pen scratching

The forty clocks still say nine. Snow has started in the courtyard. The gold chairs have been pushed into crooked rows.

On screen: Pichon (finishing his second tart, napkin in collar), Inès (arms folded), Dec (cuffed to a gold chair, sulking)

INÈS Commissaire. How many clocks are in this room?

PICHON (thinking) I have not counted them. I am a policeman, not a cuckoo.

INÈS Forty. All stopped on the same second. Open one.

Pichon sighs, wipes his fingers on the napkin, and opens the glass of a walnut bracket clock. Inès points without touching.

INÈS (thinking) A brass pin through the pendulum rod, on a spring. Set hours ago, and the hands set true with it. At nine it drops, and the clock dies.

DEC (excited) A timed pawl! That's lovely work. That's somebody with patience and small hands and a lot of time alone with these clocks.

INÈS Forty pins, Commissaire. Set this afternoon, while the lots were being hung. Dec arrived with me at eight.

PICHON (annoyed) So the cutlery cannot stop forty clocks.

DEC The cutlery can do a lot of things. Not that.

Pichon looks at the walnut clock for a long time. Then he takes a small key from his pocket and unlocks the cuff.

PICHON You are free, Monsieur Hartley. Provisionally. The tools stay in my coat, close to my heart and far from yours.

DEC (happy) That's the nicest thing you've ever said to me.

PICHON Do not get used to it. Go and stand somewhere I can see you.

Library, a quarter to midnight

Ambience: a slow mechanical whirring from the glass cases

Green banker's lamps make pools of light between tall oak shelves. In the glass cases, brass automata wait: a writing boy at a tiny desk, a singing bird in a gilded cage.

On screen: Baron Ostrander (in a leather armchair, cane across his knees), Dec (peering into a glass case), Inès (standing, notebook out)

BARON OSTRANDER (sad) Look at him. The writing boy. Jaquet-style, eighteenth century, forty cams. He writes four lines of verse in perfect copperplate.

DEC And Moreau wouldn't sell him.

BARON OSTRANDER (angry) He stole him from me in nineteen ninety-six, he would not sell him to me after, he would not sell him tonight, and now he will not sell him ever, because he is dead, which is very selfish.

INÈS You hated him.

BARON OSTRANDER What?

INÈS (louder) You hated him.

BARON OSTRANDER I heard you the first time. I was hoping you would withdraw it. Yes. I hated him for thirty years. It kept me young.

Dec taps the glass. Inside, the writing boy's pen is stopped mid-stroke, a single ink line on the card in front of him.

DEC (thinking) He's stopped too. Same pin, I'd bet. Somebody went round every clockwork thing in the house.

INÈS Baron, at nine, where were you?

BARON OSTRANDER (happy) Somewhere I could be seen. Somewhere I was not in the winter garden, as your clever little cane theory already proves.

INÈS That is not an answer.

BARON OSTRANDER It is the only one I give to restorers.

He turns his good ear firmly towards the bookshelves. The interview is over.

Kitchen, midnight

Ambience: a black range ticking as it cools, a tap dripping

The kitchen smells of lemons and cold butter. The small window by the back door is misted from inside, with feathers of frost at its corners.

On screen: Dec (crouched by the window), Inès (behind him), Pichon (in the doorway, eating a cold potato)

DEC There's a bit of the mist wiped clear. Shoulder height on a short person. Chest height on me.

PICHON You are not a measuring stick, Monsieur Hartley.

DEC I am tonight. Who was outside this window at nine?

PICHON (reading notebook, potato in the other hand) The chauffeur of the Baron. He was smoking in the courtyard. He swears he saw one figure in the kitchen at nine, through the glass. Black and white. Then the mist came back.

INÈS (thinking) Black and white.

DEC That's half of Paris.

INÈS In this house tonight, it is two people. A black suit with white gloves. A white jacket with a black waistcoat.

PICHON The chauffeur is short-sighted, and he was looking at his cigarette. He could not say which.

Clue: So the chauffeur's black-and-white figure means only this: either Madame Vérac or Rémy Castel was in the kitchen at nine.

On screen: Dec (thinking), Inès (thinking), Pichon (chewing), Rémy Castel (coming down the stairs, gloomy)

RÉMY CASTEL (worried) Somebody is eating my potatoes.

PICHON The police are eating your potatoes, Monsieur Castel. It is a sign of respect.

INÈS Rémy. Monsieur Moreau owed you three years of wages.

RÉMY CASTEL (sad) Three years, four months. I wrote it on the inside of the flour cupboard. He laughed when he saw it.

DEC That's a motive. Sorry. That's a horrible thing to say to a man in an apron.

RÉMY CASTEL It is a motive. I know. I did not kill him. Flour, sugar, butter, one egg.

INÈS (kind) Rémy, who set the clocks for the sale this afternoon?

RÉMY CASTEL (thinking) The auction house. Madame's people. They were in every room for hours with their little boxes and their white gloves.

Winter garden, half past midnight

Ambience: a fountain trickling, wind against glass panes

The body has been taken away. Only the bench, the lanterns and the cold orchids remain. The gravel still holds the shape of where people stood.

On screen: Inès (kneeling by an orchid pot), Dec (holding a lantern)

DEC You've stopped breathing. You do that when a painting's lying to you.

INÈS Light. Lower.

Something white is pushed deep behind a pot of orchids, under the leaves.

Evidence: A white cotton auctioneer's glove, its fingertips smeared gold with fresh brass polish.

DEC (surprised) That's a glove. That's one of hers. Or one of the auction people's.

INÈS Brass polish. Fresh. Somebody handled brass tonight with this hand.

DEC The whole house is brass. There's a brass carriage clock on the lot list, with a lovely little lever lock, which I did not touch.

On screen: Inès (standing, glove held in a handkerchief), Dec (lantern), Madame Vérac (at the glass door, smiling, gloves gone)

MADAME VÉRAC (happy) My dear, are you collecting my laundry?

INÈS Is it yours?

MADAME VÉRAC We wear thirty pairs a night. One is always lost. Usually in a canapé.

DEC There's polish on it. Brass polish.

MADAME VÉRAC (happy) Of course there is. I carry the lots to the lectern myself. Everyone knows that. Ask anyone.

Clue: "Of course I had the brass carriage clock tonight, my dear," Madame Vérac says. "Lot twelve. It was in my hands at nine."

INÈS (thinking) At nine.

MADAME VÉRAC At nine I was being an auctioneer. It is what they pay me for.

DEC And the Ninth Key? What do they pay you for that?

For one second her smile stays exactly where it is and her eyes do not.

MADAME VÉRAC (happy) Ten per cent, my dear. Like everything else. Excuse me, the commissaire wants my statement.

She goes. The glass door sighs shut behind her.

On screen: Inès (still), Dec (lantern, uneasy)

DEC Everybody in this house had a reason. The Baron hated him. Rémy wasn't paid. She gets ten per cent of a relic the Brotherhood says doesn't exist.

INÈS Three people. Three things in hands: the curtain cord, the brass carriage clock, the poisoned absinthe. Three rooms: the library, the kitchen, the winter garden.

DEC And one of each, at nine. Rémy said the clock or the cord. Now she says the clock.

INÈS Rémy said one of two. He never said which. One clock, one pair of hands, at nine.

DEC And one of each, then.

INÈS (determined) And one of them ended here.

Salon, one o'clock in the morning

Pichon has gathered everyone among the dead clocks. The Baron sits with his cane across his knees. Rémy stands by the door, twisting his apron. Madame Vérac smiles from the front row.

On screen: Pichon (standing, napkin finally removed), Inès (in front of the covered portrait), Dec (beside her)

PICHON (tired) Mademoiselle Varga. You asked for everyone. Here is everyone. It is one in the morning and my blanquette is a memory.

INÈS Thank you, Commissaire.

DEC (quietly) Are you sure?

INÈS I am sure of what we found. Now I put it in order.

She looks once along the row: the Baron, Rémy, Madame Vérac. The clocks say nine behind them.

Puzzle: Death in the Winter Garden

  • Either the curtain cord or the poisoned absinthe was in the kitchen.
  • Baron Ostrander was not in the winter garden.
  • Rémy Castel had either the brass carriage clock or the curtain cord.
  • Either Madame Vérac or Rémy Castel was in the kitchen.
  • Madame Vérac had the brass carriage clock.

INÈS (determined) Madame Vérac. With the brass carriage clock. In the winter garden.

The salon is silent. Then, slowly, Madame Vérac begins to applaud with her bare hands.
