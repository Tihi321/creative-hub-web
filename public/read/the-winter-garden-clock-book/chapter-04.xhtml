Behind the Great Bell

Belfry, half past two in the morning

Ambience: wind through wooden louvres, pigeons shifting on beams, a faint hum from the bells

The stripes of moonlight have moved a little across the floor. Nobody else has.

On screen: Brother Anselm (calm, leaning on a beam), Brother Tobias (not smiling), Sister Odile (cold, one hand on her watch)

BROTHER ANSELM (happy) Well reasoned, young woman. I have kept this tower for forty years, and nobody has ever worked it out on the first night.

BROTHER TOBIAS (angry) She got it completely wrong. Hopeless. Truly hopeless.

DEC (thinking) That's the most encouraging insult I've ever had, and I've been to Galway weddings.

INÈS Behind the great bell. On the beam.

BROTHER ANSELM On the beam, in the dark, where the clapper cannot reach. You will need long arms.

Dec looks at his own arms with an expression of deep personal pride.

DEC (happy) I have the longest arms in the Republic of Ireland. It's on my passport.

INÈS It is not on your passport.

DEC It should be.

Belfry, twenty to three

On screen: Dec (on tiptoe on a crossbeam, reaching), Inès (holding the lantern up, gripping a rope), Brother Anselm (watching)

The great bell hangs in the middle, wider than a café table and old as the river. Behind it, the oak beam vanishes into black.

INÈS (scared) Dec. Do not look down.

DEC I wasn't going to. Now I'm going to. Why would you say that?

INÈS Do not.

DEC (thinking) There's a box. Little iron box, nailed to the beam. Lock on it. Of course there's a lock on it.

He unrolls the cutlery one-handed, balancing against the cold curve of the bell.

DEC (happy) Six-lever, old French, rust on the second lever. Hello, my darling. You're going to be very easy and I'm going to be very gentle.

Sound: a small rusty lock clicking open

Evidence: The Ninth Key: a long black iron key, warm to the touch, with a bow shaped like a clock face that has the nine at the top.

DEC (surprised) It's warm. Why is it warm? Iron doesn't do warm. Iron does cold, and occasionally tetanus.

INÈS Bring it down. Now. Slowly.

On screen: Dec (clutching the key), Inès (alarmed), Brother Tobias (grabbing a bell rope), Sister Odile (grabbing another)

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) Do take your time! Nobody here wants that key back at all.

SISTER ODILE (neutral) We would never ring the bells with you up there.

INÈS (scared) Dec, they are going to ring the bells.

DEC (scared) I gathered that from them saying they weren't!

Sound: a great bronze bell swinging and striking, deafening

Belfry, a quarter to three

Ambience: three great bells clanging, the whole tower shaking

The great bell swings out of the dark like a bronze wall. The beam bucks under Dec's feet. Pigeons explode upwards through the louvres.

Picture: Dec hangs from a bell rope above the open trapdoor of the shaking belfry, the Ninth Key in his teeth, while Inès leans out over the drop with both hands reaching for his jacket.

DEC (scared) Mmf! Mmmf mf mff!

INÈS (scared) I cannot understand you with a key in your mouth!

DEC Mmf mmf!

INÈS (angry) A fene egye meg. Give me your hand.

She does not look at the drop. She looks at his jacket collar, the way she looks at one square inch of a painting, and nothing else in the world.

She grabs it. She pulls. Dec swings in over the boards and lands on her and on the lantern and on a very annoyed pigeon.

On screen: Dec (flat on the floor, key in hand, breathing hard), Inès (sitting up, shaking, glasses crooked)

DEC You looked down.

INÈS I looked at your collar.

DEC You pulled me in. You. Over a hole. In a tower.

INÈS (angry) Say nothing else about heights tonight, or I will put you back.

DEC (happy) Understood. Completely understood. I love you. In a colleague way. A professional, collegial sort of way.

INÈS Get up.

On screen: Dec (getting up), Inès (straightening her glasses), Brother Tobias (backing to the stairs), Sister Odile (backing to the stairs)

Brother Tobias and Sister Odile are edging toward the trapdoor, the ropes still swinging behind them.

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) We are definitely staying right here.

SISTER ODILE (neutral) And nobody is coming up those stairs.

Sound: heavy footsteps on stone, a man wheezing

Belfry, three o'clock

Ambience: the bells humming down into silence, wind through louvres

On screen: Pichon (red-faced, out of breath, half a baguette in hand), Brother Tobias (caught), Sister Odile (caught), Dec (key held up), Inès (calm)

A round head appears in the trapdoor, then a raincoat, then half a baguette, then the rest of Commissaire Pichon, who sits down on the boards and does not move for some time.

PICHON (tired) Two hundred. And twelve. Steps.

DEC I counted too!

PICHON (angry) Nobody asked you to count, Monsieur Hartley. I heard the bells from the quai. Half of Paris heard the bells. The other half is calling me.

INÈS These two rang them, Commissaire. To shake him off the beam.

PICHON Is that so, Brother? Sister?

BROTHER TOBIAS (happy) We have never touched a bell rope in our lives.

SISTER ODILE (neutral) We are only humble keepers, and we wish you well.

PICHON (thinking) I have been a policeman for thirty-one years. I know a lie when it is that cheerful.

He produces a pair of handcuffs and, after a moment's thought, a second pair. Then he breaks the baguette in half and gives the bigger half to Brother Anselm.

BROTHER ANSELM (happy) Thank you, Commissaire. I have not eaten since vespers.

PICHON You are also coming down, Brother. As a witness. A fed witness.

On screen: Pichon (holding out his hand), Dec (holding the key behind his back), Inès (watching both of them)

PICHON The key, Monsieur Hartley.

DEC (worried) Right. About that. It's warm. I don't think it should go in an evidence bag. I think it should go somewhere with a fire blanket.

INÈS Commissaire. Give us until morning. You can have it at eight with a croissant.

Pichon looks at the key, at Dec, at the stairs he will have to go down.

PICHON (tired) At eight. With two croissants. And if it is in Switzerland at five past, you are both going to prison in principle and in practice.

Atelier, dawn

Ambience: rain on a skylight, the hum of a small heater

Grey dawn comes through the slanted skylight. Half-cleaned saints and shepherds watch from the easels. Jars of pigment glow on the worktable under the desk lamp.

On screen: Inès (glasses pushed up, magnifier in hand), Dec (sitting on a stool, cup of tea, exhausted)

DEC Eight hours ago I was eating a lemon tart in a nice house. That's the last uncomplicated thing that happened to me.

INÈS Look at the shaft.

She turns the key under the lamp. Along its black iron shaft runs a hairline seam no thicker than a brushstroke.

DEC (thinking) That's not a key. Well, it is a key. But it's also a case. That's a key that's a box that's a key.

INÈS Open it. Gently. You said you would be gentle.

DEC I was talking to a lock. Locks like to hear it.

He sets a pick into the seam and turns. The shaft splits along its length like a seed pod, and something small drops onto the worktable with a click.

Close-up: A tiny silver clock face no bigger than a coin, with a single hand, pointing not at a number but at a word engraved around the rim in old French letters.

DEC (surprised) One hand. A clock with one hand. Pointing at, what is that?

INÈS (thinking) It is not a number. It is a word. Old French. Very old.

She leans in with the magnifier. Her lips move. Then she stops moving at all.

DEC (worried) Inès. You've got a new face. I don't know that face.

INÈS (scared) It says "Gardien". Keeper.

DEC Keeper of what?

Inès slowly turns the tiny clock over. On the back, scratched by hand, are three more words, and the hand on the front ticks forward once, all by itself.

INÈS Of the Last Hour.

Outside, somewhere over the roofs of the Marais, a church clock begins to strike, and it does not stop at eight.
