Rosin and Sequins

Green room, Saturday, night

Ambience: rain on the skylight, a clock ticking

The green room smells of wet umbrellas now. The champagne bucket has been pushed into a corner, with a constable's card on it that says DO NOT TOUCH.

Kranjc sits on the very edge of the velvet sofa, as if the sofa might be catching.

On screen: Kranjc (charcoal suit, grey kid gloves folded on his knee, polite), Nada (standing, hands behind her back), Luka (by the mirrors, notebook ready)

NADA Tell me where you were during the act, Doctor.

KRANJC (soft) Above it. In the fly gallery. Bruno liked me to watch his breathing from up there, when he came out of the cabinet. One sees the chest move very clearly from above.

NADA And you climbed the ladder to get there.

KRANJC There is no other way, Inspector. Unless one is a sandbag.

LUKA (to himself) Pale gloves on the rungs...

KRANJC (turning his head slightly) The young man has something to say.

LUKA Only a question, Doctor. Have you ever been up the fly ladder before tonight?

KRANJC Many times.

Luka goes to the costume rack and comes back with a pair of white cotton stage gloves. He holds one up. The palm is streaked a dull grey-gold.

LUKA (fast) I climbed it, ten minutes ago, with these on. The flymen rub rosin on every rung so their hands don't slip. It's sticky and it's powdery at the same time, it gets into everything. Look. Six rungs and my palms are gold.

Evidence: a white stage glove with grey-gold rosin dust on the palm, from six rungs of the fly ladder

NADA Your gloves, please, Doctor. Hold them up to the light.

Kranjc does not move for a moment. Then he holds up both hands, palms out, like a man surrendering very politely.

The grey kid is spotless. Not a grain on it.

KRANJC (smoothly) I keep a spare pair in my bag. I changed them.

LUKA May I see the other pair, then? The ones with the rosin?

KRANJC I threw them away.

NADA (dry) Where?

KRANJC I do not recall. The evening has been distressing.

Nada walks to the door and holds it open.

NADA Then come with me to the ladder, Doctor. Climb two rungs. Only two. I will hold your bag.

Stage, Saturday, night

The stage is bare now, the curtain open on the dark, empty house. A single ghost light stands on its pole beside the brass trapdoor.

The fly ladder runs straight up the back wall into black, iron rung after iron rung.

On screen: Kranjc (one gloved hand on the ladder, very pale, not moving), Nada (holding his bag, patient), Luka (looking up into the dark)

NADA Two rungs.

Kranjc lifts one foot to the first rung. His glasses flash as he looks up. He puts the foot down again.

KRANJC (quietly, tight) I do not care for heights. I never have. My father kept me off the hayloft for it.

NADA And yet you watched from the fly gallery every night this month.

KRANJC I may have... misremembered. I watched from the wings tonight. Near the ladder.

LUKA (writing) The wings. Near the ladder. Not up it.

NADA (to Luka, low) Spotless gloves, no spare pair to show me, and a man who cannot climb two rungs. Say it plainly.

Clue: Luka says it plainly: there is no rosin on his gloves and he cannot climb two rungs, so Doctor Kranjc was not in the fly gallery.

Kranjc takes his bag back from Nada. His hands are steady again.

KRANJC I am not on trial for my fear of ladders, Inspector.

NADA No, Doctor. Not for that.

Stage, Saturday, night

A swish of sequins comes out of the wings. Mira walks into the ghost light still in her white gown, her veil pushed back, a shawl over her shoulders that does nothing for the cold.

On screen: Mira (white sequined gown, veil pushed back, red curls coming loose, arms crossed, defiant), Nada (calm), Luka (staring at Mira's gloves)

MIRA The constable says I'm next. Go on, then. I've been sawn in half every night since August, I can take a few questions.

NADA Where do you go, Signorina Valenti, when the Bride vanishes?

MIRA (with a short laugh) You want me to give away the trick? Now? He's dead, and he'd still sue me.

NADA He would have to stand in line.

Mira looks at the trapdoor. Her face changes for a second, then hardens again.

MIRA Up. There's a panel at the back of the cabinet and a little lift, and I come out at the top of the ladder. I climb the last part. In this dress. Every night.

LUKA (pointing, gently) Your gloves, Signorina. The palms.

Mira looks down. Her white satin gloves are streaked grey-gold, the fingers stiff with it.

MIRA (sighing) Rosin. Of course. It never comes off. I owe the wardrobe mistress four pairs.

NADA So you were in the fly gallery when he dropped.

MIRA I'm always in the fly gallery when he drops. That's the act.

Fly gallery, Saturday, night

Ambience: wind in the high roof, ropes creaking

High above the stage the iron catwalk hums under their feet. Ropes stand in rows on the pin rail like the strings of a huge harp.

Below them, painted skies hang in the dark. One brass stage weight lies on its side by the rail, out of its row.

On screen: Mira (holding the rail, looking down), Nada (careful on the narrow iron), Luka (crouched by the brass weight)

LUKA This one's out of place. They're all lined up on the hooks, except this one. It's heavy. It's got your rosin on it, Signorina. Little white satin prints.

Mira is quiet for a long breath. Then she lets go of the rail and folds her arms.

MIRA (flat) Fine. Yes. I picked it up. I was going to drop it on his cabinet when he came back to take his bow. Smash his precious silver stars. Ruin the act in front of all Trieste.

NADA Why?

MIRA Four months, Inspector. Four months without wages. He paid the doves before he paid me. He said a bride should be grateful for the veil.

NADA And did you drop it?

MIRA (softer) No. I stood here holding it with both hands like an idiot. And then he didn't come back up, and I put it down, and I've been shaking ever since.

LUKA What about the trick rope? Orsini's escape rope. The one he's tied up with in the second half. It's gone from his trunk in the wings.

MIRA (snapping) And where would I put a rope, in this?

She holds her arms out. The gown fits her like a glove from neck to knee, the seams small and tight.

MIRA The wardrobe mistress sews me into it at seven. She cuts me out at eleven. There isn't room in here for a hairpin, let alone a rope. Ask her.

Luka looks at the tiny stitches along her side. He nods slowly.

LUKA She's sewn in. No pocket, no sleeve, nowhere for it. She couldn't have carried it up that ladder or down it.

Clue: Mira is sewn into a gown with no room for a hairpin, so Mira did not have the trick rope.

NADA (counting softly) A laced bottle. A brass weight. A missing rope. Three people, three places, three things.

She looks down through the gap in the catwalk, at the small square of brass far below on the stage.

NADA Luka. Read me everything we have. Every line, in order.

Stage, Saturday, night

The company has gathered at the edge of the ghost light, as if it were a campfire. Livia sits on a prop throne. Mira stands with her arms folded. Kranjc waits apart, in the shadow of the wings.

On screen: Nada (centre stage, by the trapdoor), Livia (on the prop throne, smoking), Mira (arms folded, tired), Kranjc (in the shadows, calm), Luka (notebook open)

NADA One of you went down to the trap room tonight and waited for him. One of you had the champagne. One of you had the weight. And one of you had his rope.

LIVIA (sharp) Darling, you make it sound like a parlour game.

NADA It is a very old one. Who, with what, and where.

Puzzle: Death Under the Stage

  • Either Mira or Doctor Kranjc was in the fly gallery.
  • Whoever had the laced champagne was not in the trap room.
  • Signora Brandolin had the laced champagne.
  • Doctor Kranjc was not in the fly gallery.
  • Mira did not have the trick rope.

NADA (quiet, certain) Signora Brandolin was in the green room with her laced champagne. Mira was in the fly gallery with the brass stage weight. And you, Doctor Kranjc, were in the trap room, with Orsini's trick rope.

Every head turns to the shadow of the wings. Kranjc steps out of it, unhurried.

KRANJC (soft) That is a very tidy story, Inspector. Does it have a reason in it?

MIRA (suddenly) It does. Bruno had a little black book. He kept it in his cape. He used to tap it and smile at people. He tapped it at you all week, Doctor.

Luka kneels by the folded cape the constables brought up from below. He finds a slit in the scarlet lining and draws out a small black notebook.

Close-up: a small black notebook, open at a page of neat columns of names and dates

LUKA (reading) "Kranjc. Morphine. Twenty-two prescriptions, signed with the names of three dead patients. Tell the board on Monday." It's underlined twice.

NADA He was going to ruin you on Monday, Doctor. So he did not get to Monday.

For the first time, Kranjc smiles properly. It is not a nice smile.

KRANJC Inspector. You found him behind a padlocked door. I have never had a key to the trap room in my life. Search me. Search my bag. Search my house.

LIVIA (slowly) He's right. The keys are the night crew's. Nobody else touches them. Not even me.

NADA Then someone opened the door for you.

Far off, over the harbour, the bells of the city begin to strike. One. Two. Three.

Sound: distant church bells striking midnight

MIRA (pale) Oh, no. It's midnight. Closing night.

LUKA What happens at midnight?

LIVIA (with a tired laugh) The Lanterna game, darling. And now you'll never get a straight answer until dawn.
