The Vanishing Bride

Auditorium, Saturday, late evening

Ambience: a full theatre murmuring, an orchestra tuning

The Teatro Lanterna glows like the inside of a jewellery box. Red velvet, gold leaf curling off the balconies, a chandelier that has seen better decades.

Every seat is full. It is the last night of the season, and Trieste has come to say goodbye.

On screen: Nada (grey wool coat over a plum theatre dress, spectacles, calm), Luka (tweed cap pushed back, notebook open, delighted)

LUKA (whispering fast) Third row, Inspector. Third row! I can see the brass on the trapdoor. I can see the scuffs on it.

NADA (dry) You can see a great many things, Luka. Try watching one of them.

LUKA I'm writing it all down. In case he tells us how it's done.

NADA He won't. Magicians never do. That is the whole of their profession.

The lights drop. The orchestra stops mid-squeak. One spotlight falls on the centre of the stage.

Sound: a drum roll

On screen: Orsini (tailcoat, scarlet-lined cape, waxed moustache, beaming), Mira (white sequined bridal gown, lace veil, red curls, smiling stiffly)

ORSINI (booming) Ladies and gentlemen of Trieste! For one last night, I give you... the Vanishing Bride!

Mira steps into a tall black cabinet painted with silver stars. Orsini swirls his cape across the door.

ORSINI (booming) One. Two. Three!

He throws the door open. The cabinet is empty. Not a sequin left.

The audience gasps, then roars.

LUKA (scribbling) Gone. She's gone. Where does she go? Inspector, where does she go?

NADA (counting under her breath) Four seconds behind the cape. Enough for a panel. Up, I think, not down.

ORSINI (booming) And now, my friends, the groom follows his bride!

Orsini stands on the brass trapdoor. He bows. He smiles at the third row as if he knows Nada personally.

Sound: a heavy wooden clunk

The trapdoor drops. Orsini drops with it, cape and all. The spotlight swings to the back of the stalls, to the aisle where he is meant to appear.

On screen: Nada (watching the aisle, still), Luka (craning his neck, grinning)

LUKA Here he comes. Any second. He comes up behind the last row, everyone says so.

The spotlight waits on the empty aisle. The audience waits with it.

Ten seconds. Twenty. Somebody laughs nervously. The orchestra plays the same three bars again.

NADA (quiet) That is forty seconds, Luka.

LUKA (smile fading) Maybe he's... building suspense?

NADA (standing up) Orsini has never built suspense in his life. He builds applause. Come on.

Trap room, Saturday, night

Ambience: water dripping, ropes creaking on pulleys

Under the stage the air is cold and smells of damp wood and old paint. A single bulb swings above a forest of counterweights and propped-up scenery.

Above, the square of the trapdoor shows a thin line of light. Feet are running somewhere overhead.

On screen: Nada (spectacles catching the bulb, grim), Luka (pale, notebook clutched to his chest)

Picture: Nada kneels beside Orsini, who lies still on the boards of the trap room, his scarlet-lined cape spread around him, as Luka holds up the swinging bulb.

LUKA (shaky) Inspector. Is he...

NADA (quiet) He is. I am sorry, Luka. Don't touch anything.

She closes Orsini's eyes with two careful fingers. She does not look at him long. She looks at the room.

NADA (counting) One staircase. One door, padlocked from this side. One trapdoor, which he came through. No window.

LUKA (writing, voice steadier) The body was found in the trap room. Under the stage, by the counterweights. Nine minutes past ten. I'm writing nine past ten.

NADA Good. Write that the padlock is closed, too. And that his cape is not torn.

LUKA Not torn. Right. There's rope burn on the beam up there, but that's old. Everything down here has rope burn.

Nada stands and brushes her coat. Her face is calm, but she takes off her spectacles and cleans them twice.

NADA Somebody was waiting for him down here, Luka. In the dark, while two thousand people clapped.

Green room, Saturday, night

The green room is warm and shabby, all mirrors and bulbs and feather boas hanging like sleeping birds. A silver bucket of ice sits on a little table with a bottle of champagne in it.

Three glasses stand beside it, poured and waiting for a toast that will not come.

On screen: Nada (thoughtful), Luka (sniffing at a glass, frowning)

LUKA Inspector, smell this one. No, don't drink it. Smell it.

NADA (leaning in) Sweet. And something underneath. A chemist's shop.

LUKA Like my grandmother's cough syrup. The one that made her sleep through Easter.

Evidence: Orsini's full champagne glass, with a sweet, medicinal smell

NADA The glass with the star painted on the stem. Whose is that?

LUKA (reading a card propped against it) "For the Great Orsini. Ti voglio bene. L."

A door swings open. Perfume and cigarette smoke arrive before the woman does.

On screen: Livia (black lace, ropes of pearls, long cigarette holder, regal and shaken), Nada (polite), Luka (standing up straight)

LIVIA (grand) Darling, they tell me the police are in my green room. I did not know we sold tickets to the police.

NADA Inspector Ferluga, Signora Brandolin. I was in your third row.

LIVIA (sinking onto the sofa) Then you saw. You saw him go down and not come up. Thirty years I have run this house, and nobody has ever not come up.

NADA Signora. The champagne in Orsini's glass. What is in it?

Livia looks at the glass for a long time. She taps ash that is not there from her holder.

LIVIA (quietly) Laudanum. A little. Only a little, darling, I am not a monster.

LUKA (whispering) Grandmother's cough syrup.

LIVIA He was leaving me. He signed with a house in Vienna. The train is at seven tomorrow morning. If he slept through it, he would have to stay, and do one more month, and I would not go bankrupt by Christmas.

Clue: Signora Brandolin had the laced champagne: she says so herself, the bottle was hers, and she poured it.

NADA You laced it here?

LIVIA Here. I carried that bottle up from my office and I put it in that bucket and I have not taken it out since. Ask anyone. Nobody else touched it.

Luka crouches by the bucket. He lifts the bottle by its neck, slowly, and holds it up to the light.

LUKA Inspector. It drips. The ice cracked the label and there's a little chip in the glass, right here at the bottom. Drip. Drip. See? It leaves a spot every time.

NADA Where does it leave spots?

LUKA (following the floor) Here. Here. A ring on the table. Two by the sofa. A trail to the door of her office and back. That's all.

Trap room, Saturday, night

Nada and Luka come back down the narrow wooden staircase. Luka holds a lamp low over every step.

On screen: Luka (on his knees on the stairs, lamp close to the wood), Nada (standing above him, patient)

LUKA Dust. Dust. A cigarette end, very old. A nail. No drips. Not one, Inspector. Not on the stairs, not by the counterweights, not near him.

NADA The bottle leaves a spot wherever it goes.

LUKA Wherever it goes. Like a snail.

Clue: The leaking bottle marks every place it has been, and there is not a drop on the trap room stairs: whoever had the laced champagne was not in the trap room.

NADA (nodding slowly) So our sleeping draught is a small sin, and a separate one. Good. I like my sins in separate boxes.

LUKA Signora Brandolin wanted him asleep, not... this.

NADA Signora Brandolin wanted him on stage for another month. A dead magician sells no tickets.

Green room, Saturday, night

Livia has lit a real cigarette now. Her hand shakes a little, and she does not hide it.

On screen: Livia (smoking, pearls twisted around her fingers, tired), Nada (sitting opposite her, notebook borrowed from Luka)

NADA Who else was backstage during the act, Signora?

LIVIA The girl, of course. Mira. She has to be, she is the Bride. And his doctor, Kranjc. He comes every night now. Bruno said it was his heart. Bruno said a great many things.

NADA And during the vanishing? Where were you?

LIVIA Here, darling, waiting with my bottle like a fool. But I looked out once, through the wings. I wanted to see him take his last bow.

She points the cigarette holder up, towards the dark above the stage.

LIVIA Somebody was on the fly ladder. Climbing, right when the Bride vanished. I saw only the hands on the rungs. Pale gloves. White, or pale grey, in that light. I could not tell you which.

LUKA (writing) Mira wears white satin gloves. In the act.

LIVIA And Kranjc never takes his off. Grey kid. He says hands carry disease.

Clue: Whoever climbed the ladder wore pale gloves, and only two people backstage wear them: either Mira or Doctor Kranjc was in the fly gallery.

NADA (to herself, counting) One bottle. One ladder. One trapdoor. One locked door. Four things, and only one of them killed him.

Stage door, Saturday, night

Ambience: rain on cobbles, a ship's horn far off

The harbour street behind the theatre shines with rain. Posters of the Great Orsini peel off the wall, his painted moustache running in the wet.

A police wagon has pulled up. Its lamps make two yellow circles on the stones.

On screen: Nada (collar up, watching the street), Luka (cap dripping, shivering)

LUKA The constables are coming. Do we let anyone leave?

NADA Nobody leaves until dawn. The company scatters at seven, with the Vienna train and everything else. We have until then.

Sound: footsteps on wet cobbles, slow and even

A thin man comes out of the rain under a black umbrella. He carries a doctor's bag. He does not hurry.

On screen: Kranjc (charcoal suit, wire-rimmed glasses, grey kid gloves, black bag, perfectly calm), Nada (watchful), Luka (wary)

KRANJC (soft, polite) Inspector. I am told my patient has died. I am Doctor Kranjc. I attended him.

NADA You attended him tonight, Doctor?

KRANJC Every night this month. A weak heart, and a strong opinion of himself. The two often go together.

He folds his umbrella neatly and shakes it once. His gloves do not get wet. He seems to have arranged that too.

KRANJC I will sign the certificate, of course. It will save everyone a great deal of trouble. Heart failure, under the strain of performance. Very sad.

LUKA (blurting) He didn't die of his heart. Someone...

NADA (raising one hand) Luka.

KRANJC (smiling faintly) The young man is upset. That is natural. Shall we go inside, Inspector? I have my pen.

NADA (calm) Keep your pen in your bag for now, Doctor. Tonight I will decide what goes on the certificate.

Kranjc bows, very slightly. Behind his glasses, his eyes do not change at all.
