Bows to an Empty House

Stage, Sunday, before dawn

Ambience: rain easing off, gulls far away

The ghost light has burned all night. Under it the company has sat in a loose circle on crates and prop chairs, coats over their knees, nobody quite asleep.

High above the stage, the small dusty windows of the fly tower have turned from black to the colour of dishwater.

On screen: Nada (spectacles pushed up, wide awake), Luka (cap over his eyes, notebook on his chest), Teodor (sitting on an upturned crate, key ring in both hands)

LUKA (lifting his cap) Is it dawn yet?

NADA Not yet. Seven minutes. I have been counting the gulls.

LUKA You can't count gulls, Inspector.

NADA I have counted nineteen. They are very regular.

Teodor turns the iron key ring over and over. One key on it is newer than the others, bright brass among the rust.

Evidence: the bright brass trap room key on Teodor's iron key ring, newer than the rest

NADA (quietly) Signor Marušić. You do not have to wait for the sun to tell me why.

TEODOR (gruff, not looking up) Nothing to tell. That doctor never held a single paper of mine.

Pina, on the next crate, closes her prompt book slowly over one finger.

TEODOR (gruff) Never lent me a lira. Never came round every Friday for it, either. Never said he'd tear the lot up.

LUKA (sitting up, whispering) Inspector. He's a Mask. Every word's the wrong way round.

NADA (softly) I know, Luka. Listen to it the right way round.

TEODOR (to his hands) And he never asked for one turn of a key. Just one. For a minute. Never said nobody would get hurt.

For a moment nobody speaks. Zlatko reaches over with one huge hand and rests it on the old man's shoulder.

ZLATKO (slow) He did say it, Teodor. I heard him at the stage door on Thursday. You believed him. That is all.

TEODOR (gruff, eyes wet) I'm not sorry. Not one bit.

PINA (quietly, exact) He is. He cried in the paint shop at half past ten. I wrote down the time.

Stage, Sunday, dawn

A thin bar of grey light falls through the fly tower windows and across the boards, all the way to the brass trapdoor.

Somewhere outside, a ship's bell rings the hour.

Sound: a ship's bell ringing far off

LIVIA (standing, pulling her lace around her) Sun's up, darlings. The game is over.

On screen: Livia (black lace, pearls, tired and kind), Teodor (standing slowly, untying his apron), Pina (paper mask in hand), Zlatko (paper mask in hand)

One by one the three of them drop their paper masks into the old iron stove in the wings. Pina first, then Zlatko, then Teodor.

Teodor unties his leather apron. He folds it once and lays it on the crate, with the key ring on top of it.

Picture: Teodor lays his folded leather apron and his iron key ring on a crate in the grey dawn light on the stage, while Nada watches and the paper masks burn in the stove behind him.

He looks at the keys for a long time, and then he looks at Nada, and he nods once. He does not say a word.

NADA (gently) Thank you, Signor Marušić. That is enough.

Stage door, Sunday, dawn

Ambience: water dripping from gutters, carts on cobbles

The rain has stopped. The harbour street shines like a wet mirror. The posters of the Great Orsini hang in grey ribbons off the wall.

A police wagon stands by the stage door, its horse breathing clouds into the cold.

On screen: Kranjc (charcoal suit creased, grey kid gloves, black bag held by a constable, calm), Nada (collar up), Luka (camera on its strap)

KRANJC (soft, polite) I trust you will tell the board, Inspector. They do so like to be told things on a Monday.

NADA I will tell them on Sunday, Doctor. I would not want you to wait.

KRANJC You know, Bruno was a terrible man. He would have ruined three families with that little book. Mine was only the first.

NADA (dry) Then you might have come to me. I am in the third row most Saturdays.

For a moment Kranjc's face does something it has not done all night. It looks tired.

KRANJC I do not care for heights, Inspector. Or for the police. Or, it seems, for being right.

The constable opens the wagon door. Kranjc steps up into it neatly, as if into a train carriage. He does not look back.

Sound: a wagon door slamming, wheels on cobbles

LUKA (watching the wagon go) He never once took his gloves off.

NADA No. Some people never do, Luka. That is the whole of their profession.

Auditorium, Sunday, morning

Ambience: an empty theatre, the soft creak of old wood

The house is empty. Every red velvet seat is folded up, two thousand of them, like sleeping bats. Morning light comes in through a high window and turns the dust to gold.

The chandelier is dark. The gold leaf on the balconies is peeling, the way it always has.

On screen: Nada (sitting in the third row, coat on her knees), Luka (beside her, camera in his lap)

LUKA Why are we still here? It's over.

NADA Because Signora Brandolin asked us to stay. And because I have never seen this house empty. Look at it.

Luka looks. For once he does not write anything down.

On the stage, the curtain slowly rises. Behind it, the company stands in a line along the footlights. Nobody is in costume except Mira, who still cannot get out of her gown.

On screen: Livia (centre, pearls, chin high), Mira (white sequined gown, veil pushed back, red curls loose), Pina (prompt book under her arm), Zlatko (striped jersey, cap in his hands), Teodor (no apron, grey shirt, a constable at the wings behind him)

LIVIA (grand, to the empty house) Ladies and gentlemen. Tonight, our friend Bruno Orsini made his last vanishing.

MIRA (quietly) He was a dreadful man. He owed me four months.

LIVIA He owed everybody something, darling. And now we owe him a bow.

Mira swallows. She steps to the edge of the stage, beside the brass trapdoor, and takes Livia's hand.

LIVIA You'll be paid, Mira. Every lira. I'll sell the chandelier if I have to.

MIRA (a small, crooked smile) Don't sell the chandelier. Sell the cabinet. I never want to see a silver star again.

ZLATKO (slow) Places, everyone.

PINA (reading softly from the prompt book) "The company bows. The house is silent. Curtain."

Picture: Livia, Mira, Pina, Zlatko and Teodor bow together along the footlights of the Teatro Lanterna to an empty red-and-gold house, while Nada and Luka sit alone in the third row.

The five of them bow, low and slow, to two thousand empty seats.

In the third row, Nada puts her hands together, once, twice, three times. After a moment, Luka claps too.

The sound is very small in that big gold room. It is enough.

Stage door, Sunday, morning

Ambience: gulls, the harbour waking up

The harbour street is busy now. Fishwives call to each other. The seven o'clock train to Vienna whistles somewhere across the city, leaving without its magician.

Sound: a distant train whistle

On screen: Nada (coat buttoned, spectacles clean), Luka (cap pushed back, notebook closed)

LUKA Inspector. Can I ask you something?

NADA You will anyway.

LUKA When Signora Brandolin says the game is very old. Older than the roof. Do you think it's true? Or do you think she made it up, tonight, for us?

Nada thinks about it as they walk. She counts the steps without meaning to. Eleven. Twelve.

NADA (dry) She is an actress, Luka, and she owns a theatre that is losing money. I think it is the oldest game in the world.

LUKA (grinning) That's not an answer.

NADA No. It is a much better thing. It is breakfast. Come on. You are buying.

They walk off down the shining street, side by side, into the morning.
