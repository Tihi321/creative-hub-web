Apprenticeship

The training hall, Saturday morning

Ambience: a clock ticking, radiators knocking, the rustle of paper

Long table, mismatched chairs, photographs of the old gasworks hanging crooked. Daylight through tall windows.

On screen: The inspector from the licensing office (charcoal suit, green folder open), The woman who taught cable theory (plum cardigan, chalk on her cuff), The man from the substation (dark utility suit, grey moustache), Dell (clean shirt, shaved, crutch against his chair), Sol (notebook on her knee, behind him)

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE This is a review of a license. It is not a trial of a night.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE Mr. Marchetti. The city has heard several numbers on the air. I would like one of them to be true.

DELL Four breakers on the survey. Two off it. Six in all.

DELL The sixth is the section link on the district wall. That's the one I'm sorry for.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE And the two off it?

DELL The island switch in the pumping house. I put it in young and proud. I didn't want a thing that saved a block sitting in an office for a year.

DELL I knew it was wrong when I put my hand to it. Every year after.

DELL The wall link went in when the survey went to the wrong address and the courtyard flooded.

DELL I should have made the drawing. A wire off the drawing is a wire the next storm can catch a man on.

THE MAN FROM THE SUBSTATION Aye.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE The code is not sentimental about that. A licensed man who installs unrecorded apparatus has broken the terms of his license.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE In a serious case he can lose the license and the pension it carries.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE Miss Vieira. The board will hear you now.

Sol stands. She opens the notebook.

SOL I'm Sol Vieira. I've been Dell Marchetti's apprentice since I was fifteen. I've written a word and I'm going to read it, because I want it exact.

SOL In the surge I put my hand to six boxes. The waterworks breaker, with the vault flooded and the fault cooking the water. I swam under it.

SOL The market hub, where the lug had been cut away. I rebuilt it with a busbar and a turnbuckle.

SOL The depot's third, where the tags were wrong and the drawing upside down, and I wrote the correction in this book in my own hand.

SOL The pumping house island switch, hot and ticking, off the drawing. I turned it off and bound it and put it back.

SOL The wall link, with a chain of neighbours passing tools down a ladder, and the old woman holding the tin lid over the fuse.

SOL Six boxes. Every one is written here with the time and the fault and the fix and my name against it.

SOL If there's a seventh in this city, it isn't mine. I'll find it before the next storm and write it down.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE Your mentor's note in your chapter four was smudged. You wrote your own. Why did you not do as the note said?

On screen: Sol (steady, notebook open), Dell (looking at his folded hands), The woman who taught cable theory (leaning in)

SOL Because the note was wrong. A man drew that room at twenty from memory, got it upside down, and never went back to check.

SOL A man who draws carefully can still draw a room from the wrong side, and then the drawing lies to the next person who reads it.

SOL His care wasn't at fault. The paper was. So I read the room instead.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE And the switches off the paper. What is your opinion?

SOL A city's wires and a city's people are not two different circuits. You can't survey one and leave the other dark.

SOL That's Dell's word and it's mine now, because I've been in the water. The four on the survey would not have held it alone.

SOL But a wire off the paper is still a wire that can catch the next man. The man from Kirk Row is right, and both are true at once.

SOL A board that can only hold one of them at a time will get a district killed. That's my word.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE The board will deliberate. The record will show the apprentice spoke without notes.

SOL I had notes. That's a notebook. It's a different thing.

The woman with the chalk on her cuff puts her hand over her mouth.

The training hall, Saturday afternoon

Ambience: the clock ticking, a chair scraping, one window propped open on the yard

The same room. The table cleared of everything but the folder. Sun in a bar across the floor.

On screen: The inspector from the licensing office (standing, folder in both hands), Sol (seated), Dell (hand on his stick), The man from the substation (still, watching the window)

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE The board finds that Mr. Marchetti installed two items of electrical apparatus off the survey and off the record, in breach of the terms of his license.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE He is reprimanded in the strongest terms the code permits, and the reprimand is entered against his license.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE The unrecorded apparatus will be surveyed, drawn and recorded at the city's expense. Mr. Marchetti will not do the drawing. A man does not draw his own fault.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE The apprentice Vieira will do the drawing under supervision, because her notebook shows she can.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE That is the board's sentence on the paper.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE The board has also considered the terms of his license as they apply to an electrician past the age of retirement.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE A reprimand is a reprimand and not a revocation. The license stands. The pension the license carries stands.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE His word at this review was true. His drawings before the fault were careful.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE It finds this city's lights were held by his care, and not in spite of it. That is the board's sentence on the man.

DELL (thin) Thank you.

DELL I'd like it minuted that I'm sorry for the paper and not for the switch. And I'll not draw a thing that saves a block and hides in a wall again.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE It's minuted.

SOL May I put it in the book, sir? The sentence. In my own hand.

THE INSPECTOR FROM THE LICENSING OFFICE It's the record. Put it where the board can read it at the next survey.

SOL Then it goes in the front.

Close-up: The front page of Sol's notebook, the board's sentence copied out in a square schoolgirl hand, space left beneath it for the inspector's signature.

END INSERT

Sol writes it under the sentences she wrote at fifteen and does not stop to read the old ones.

The training hall, Saturday afternoon

Ambience: boots on gravel, gulls, a low radio somewhere across the yard

A small yard behind the hall, a low stone wall hot in the sun.

On screen: Ife (boots off, feet in the sun, sleeves rolled), Sol (out the back door, notebook hugged to her chest)

IFE My boots are off and it's Saturday. Sit down. You look like a person who's been read at.

SOL They kept the license. They reprimanded him and kept the license and the pension.

IFE Then he'll be all right.

IFE The gearbox note was the bearings.

IFE The man with the wrench found a set at the depot, in the second tram car. The tram's mine, so it's legal, and he fitted them by feel this morning.

IFE Motor's dry and the sump's filled. If the starter takes it she'll run before dark.

SOL She'll take it.

IFE You don't know that.

SOL I know the sound of that gearbox now. I know a bearing fitted by a man who's done it forty years will hold.

SOL I'll be there when you put the starter to it. If it doesn't take, we find it together.

Ife picks at a callus on her palm. She does not look at Sol.

IFE The barge is my mother's block's barge and it's going to be a barge again by tonight. I've got the salvage and a claim in writing with a foreman's signature on it.

IFE What I haven't got is a person who can hear a bearing shim before it's shimmed.

IFE I'm not asking for charity. I'm asking for terms. A partnership.

IFE Winter work and summer work. You come when the water's up, I come when it's dry.

On screen: Ife (hand out flat, jaw set), Sol (turning to face her)

IFE And the hall grievance is behind us, and I'll say so when the hall sits. What do you say?

SOL Partnership. In writing.

SOL And if you shout at me when I'm right about the gearbox, I shout back. A partnership that only goes one way is a partnership with a dodgy bearing in it.

IFE Shout all you like. Just be right.

They shake hands flat, hard, and neither lets go first.

The loading arch, Sunday morning

Ambience: water lapping under stone, a fan turning, a radio's papery hiss

Grey dawn on the arch stone. A little table with a lamp on it, and the lamp is lit, because the district is lit.

On screen: Ozzy (headset, hoodie, ragged and happy), Dell (chair beside the table, paper in front of him), Sol (cap in her hands), Ife (watching from the arch stone)

OZZY Port Alaric. It's Sunday morning and the sun's coming up on the arch.

OZZY On the table is an indenture, a pen, a retired man who was reprimanded yesterday and kept his license, and beside him an apprentice who was in six boxes in one night.

OZZY Mr. Marchetti. Say your word.

DELL Her father wired his first lamp with me. He was a better hand than me and he's been gone eleven years.

DELL When the surge took the control room I sent her out with my book and my bag. A man in a chair is no use in a flood.

DELL She read the waterworks with her hands under a live fault. She rebuilt a hub the looters had cut.

DELL She put in the depot's third when my drawing lied to her, and wrote the correction herself.

DELL She went down a wall in the rain for a switch that was my fault. And yesterday she told a board what a city is.

DELL Forty-one years I've had a hand in this trade. This is the best hand I ever put to it. I sign her out today as a full electrician.

DELL And I'll say the other thing the city's owed since the night of the surge.

DELL I put two switches in a wall and did not draw them. I was ashamed and I stayed ashamed and it got worse for the staying.

DELL I'm sorry for the paper. That's my word and the whole of it, and I'll not say it again.

He signs. The pen scratches. He pushes the paper across.

DELL Sign your own name. Nobody signs it for you.

SOL How much do I owe for the stamp?

DELL Nothing. It came out of the toolbag. That's the last joke you get at my expense.

Sol takes the pen. It hardly shakes at all.

DELL Read it out, girl. The city wants to hear what it says.

SOL No. The city wants to hear it run.

SOL Ozzy. Aim the microphone at the wall.

On screen: Ozzy (aiming the microphone at the stone), Sol (palm flat on the arch stone where the cable comes through, cap under her arm)

Sound: a low even hum of load, and behind it a motor, a fan, a lift, the small far choir of a district

Sol lays her palm flat on the stone where the cable comes through.

SOL That's the wall. That's the district feeder carrying load.

SOL That's the hospital, and the lifts, and the blood fridge, and Barrow Row, and the market's lower lamps.

SOL It's been through the flood and it's dry and it's true. That's my paper signed, and that's the sound of the city it's signed to.

OZZY That's a good sound to end on.

The loading arch, Sunday morning

Ambience: a motor idling, the fine rush of a carrier wave

On screen: Ozzy (headset pushed back, wiping his glasses), Sol (beside the table), Dell (paper folded in his breast pocket), Ife (still on the arch stone)

OZZY Port Alaric. The surge is down and the streets are drying and the hospital district is lit.

OZZY There are streets that will stay dark tonight and tomorrow. I've read you the list and I'll read it every evening until there's no list.

OZZY There's a review that sat yesterday, a man who took his medicine, a partnership at the depot yard, an old woman with a complaint on file, and a socket to make safe on Kirk Row.

OZZY This station will keep saying both truths as long as it has a mast. It has a mast, because there's a man with a coil of clean wire and a boy with a tool roll who've promised to keep it up.

OZZY If you hear nothing from us for a day, it's because I'm asleep. If you hear nothing for two, come and knock.

OZZY This is Ozzy Kwan, and this is Port Alaric coming back up.

Sound: the carrier clicking off into silence, then water under the arch

DELL Come on. The gearbox is dry and the day's not done.

The stone bridge at the depot yard, Sunday evening

Ambience: a starter grinding, then an engine catching clean, a crowd's long sound

The yard has drained to cobbles. The barge lies at the stone bridge with the shed lamps burning over her. A crowd stands along the yard wall.

On screen: Ife (hand on the tiller, jaw set), Sol (down by the engine casing, ear to the hatch), The man with the tobacco tin (wrench over his shoulder, on the bridge), The sister's boy (on the wall, both hands on the coping)

Sound: a starter turning slow and wet, a cough, a catch

The engine turns slow and wet and coughs and catches. The note comes up out of the dead iron clean and true.

Ife lets it run a minute. She pulls the throttle back and cuts it.

IFE It's got a bearing in it from the second tram car.

IFE It's got a coil of clean wire from the man with the wrench, and a shim from the shop, and oil from the depot's store.

IFE It's not a new engine. It's the same old engine with a new heart out of the same scraps. And it'll run.

IFE Terms are in writing. It's a partnership.

The yard makes a sound that is not applause and is better.

THE SISTER'S BOY I fitted the shim! I'm the shop!

THE MAN WITH THE TOBACCO TIN You're the shop.

The lock keeper's lane, dusk

Ambience: low water muttering round a stoop, a kettle somewhere above

The lane has drained to a wet channel. Chalk on the door at number eleven, and the dead service pipe and dead cable still dead in the wall.

On screen: Sol (boots wet, cap in hand, notebook in her coat)

Close-up: A chalked note on the door at number eleven in a child's block letters: WATER DONE. DOOR STICKS. NO POWER IN BACK ROOM.

END INSERT

Sol takes the pencil from behind her ear and marks the corner of the note with a small cross, for the survey.

The bridge with the chain, dusk

Ambience: iron ringing slow and shallow in low water

The arch stands grey and wet. The chain hangs down into shallow water and drags and rings.

On screen: Sol (on the bridge, hands on the parapet)

It is only a chain now, and not a channel. She walks on.

Market District, dusk

Ambience: lamps humming over the lower street, a shutter being pulled down a long way off

The market's lower streets are lit and the upper ones are dark, and the line between them runs down the middle of the square.

On screen: The old man in the vending machine (dry now, coat buttoned, wrench in his good hand, under the lamp), Sol (coming along the high stone)

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE You came down.

SOL I said I'd come down for it. I'm giving it back to the man it belongs to. That's a different thing and you know it.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE It's a different thing.

He puts the wrench in his coat. His face does a thing she has not seen it do before, which is to be pleased.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE The hall's lit. My daughter's got her shutter open and a customer. God keep the ones who held the light.

SOL Keep the ones who lent the wire. Your line's on the survey.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Then it'll be drawn by a girl who comes down.

Hospital District, dusk

Ambience: a transformer humming steady, water gone to a trickle in the guttering

The courtyard is dry. Down the canal face the rusted ladder stands above low water. The district wall is lit along its top.

On screen: Sol (on the wall, palm flat on the stone by the link box)

The transformer hums the note it hummed all night, and there is no tick in it. Sol keeps her hand there a while.

Port Alaric, dusk

Ambience: wind coming up the delta, distant lamps buzzing, water going down

Above the Grid Control Room, still full of water, the flat tar roof, and the whole drying city below, lit in patches and dark in patches.

On screen: Sol (cap off, notebook open on the parapet, pencil in hand)

Picture: Sol stands alone on the roof of the Grid Control Room at dusk, notebook open on the parapet, the drying city below her lit in patches and dark in patches, and under everything a low steady hum of load.

Close-up: The last page of Sol's notebook in a square schoolgirl hand: Sunday evening. Six boxes, all holding. Survey Monday. Draw the wall, draw the pumping house, draw the lock keeper's lane, the lot of it, where the next man can read it. The partnership's in writing. The license stands and the pension stands and the man's sorry for the paper. The city's up. Not everywhere. Enough.

END INSERT

Sol shuts the notebook and puts it in her coat.

Under her boots, through the stone and the iron, the whole city hums, true and steady and full of load.

SOL Six boxes holding. Four on the survey, two off it, and one circuit.

She puts her cap on and goes down the stairs into the drying streets, and the hum goes with her.
