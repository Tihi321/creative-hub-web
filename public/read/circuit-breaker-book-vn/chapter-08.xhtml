The Chain

Hospital District, night

Ambience: the flood running over the lower rungs, the vault ticking

The searchlight holds hard on the rusted rungs. Sol's boots find the stone of the parapet and she stands dripping, the line still round her waist.

On screen: Sol (soaked to the ribs, bag short on her back, hands hanging), Ife (at the top of the ladder, line coiled over one shoulder)

IFE You're up. You're up, and the tea's gone cold, and I don't care.

SOL The grille was seized. The lever was jammed. It's done.

IFE I heard it done. I was on the end of the line while it was done.

Hospital District, night

Ambience: rain softening, water lapping the parapet stone

The parapet is full of people. The old woman stays with her lamp. The sister's boy sets his canvas roll on the coping. The woman in the window does not put down her light.

On screen: The old man in the vending machine (coat over one arm, soaked, stooped), Sol (turning to him)

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE You kept your eyes on the switch. There's no shame in that. It was the wrench I asked for.

SOL I kept my eyes on the switch. I'll come down for the wrench when the market's lit.

He holds out her coat. It has been dry in his one good arm the whole time, and now it is sodden, and she puts it on and does not mind.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Then I'll hold it again, when you come.

Hospital District, night

Ambience: the searchlight's motor idling, a folder's plastic sleeve creaking

On screen: The municipal engineer (folder in his hands, helmet off, hair flat to his head), Sol (in the sodden coat, standing straight), The old woman at the culvert bridge (lantern raised, at the coping)

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER Miss Vieira. I want to say it while everyone's here, because the record's going to say it and I don't want the record and me to be two different men.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER The link is reset. The vault is stable. The district is lit.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER The record will show that the link was unrecorded, and that the pumping-house island switch was unrecorded, and that both were installed by a licensed electrician of this city on his own initiative and never submitted for survey.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER That is a code matter and it's not a small one.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER The record will also show that they held the wards of a hospital through a second high tide. I'll be submitting both in the same report.

SOL What does that mean for Dell?

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER Miss Vieira—

SOL No. What does it mean for him. Not for the record. For him.

SOL He's a week off his retirement and he's been thirty years on this city's plant and it saved a hospital tonight. I'd sooner the city heard it from you than read it off a form.

The engineer looks at her a long moment.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER His license will be reviewed. That's not mine to decide and it's not mine to withhold, and I'd be lying if I said otherwise.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER A review is a process. It has a hearing and a panel and a man is allowed to speak at it.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER He's already done the hardest part of that tonight, which is to say it himself, with his name on it, before anyone asked him to.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER But the review will also have the district's word, if the district cares to send it.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE Then the district will have its say. I've got a complaint already drafted in my head. It's got a culvert in it and a kettle.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER An unpermitted installation and an undrafted complaint. The record will be a lively one.

Hospital District, night

Ambience: a handheld hissing low, water slapping the courtyard wall

On screen: Dell (not seen, voice only over the handheld), The municipal engineer (looking at the handheld), Ife (line coiled over her shoulder, jaw set)

DELL (over the handheld) I'll take the review. I'll sit in the chair and be civil and I'll not say the law was wrong.

DELL (over the handheld) But you'll put one line in your report for me, Engineer. A city's wires and a city's people are not two different circuits.

DELL (over the handheld) You can't survey one and leave the other dark. That's the whole of it.

THE MUNICIPAL ENGINEER That line's going in the report.

IFE The tram. The water's going down on the yard. I have been up all night and I am going to need a person who can read a motor.

SOL Read the room, not the page.

Ife's mouth twists, and it is not quite a smile.

IFE The barge engine's full of the yard. I'll come to the wall for you and I'll come to the depot. That's working. We'll talk terms in the boat.

SOL We'll talk terms in the boat.

Hospital District, night

Ambience: a works motor turning over, rain soft on the courtyard

The rain has eased. The floodlights are on, and along the far reach the market's lamps come on in ones and twos as the feeders find their feet.

On screen: Sol (in the boat, bag against her knee), Ife (stepping down beside her), The sister's boy (on the coping, sitting beside his canvas roll), The woman on the roof (lamp still up in her window)

Sol's hand finds the bag strap by habit and does not find the wrench that should be looped through it. She tilts her head back and listens for the far reach.

Sound: the soft knock of a skiff under a low bridge, far off

SOL One more thing before the wall. There's a man in a soaked cardigan and no shoes with a daughter in the market hall, and he's owed a wrench back.

IFE Then come back for it.

SOL When the market's lit. I said I'd come down for it myself.

The boat goes out of the courtyard and round the corner and up the flooded street toward the loading arch.

OZZY (over the handheld) The district held. Write it on your stairs. The district held.

Sol sits in the bottom of the works boat with her hands in her coat and the sound of the city coming back up through the hull into her boot soles, street by street, note by note, the way the grid does when she puts her hand on the stone.

SOL (quiet) It's not all of it.

IFE What?

SOL Nothing. It's enough to hear.
