The Fourth Breaker

The stone bridge at the depot yard, night

Ambience: water hurrying through the culvert, a kettle whistling up on the bridge

The barge sits tied at the bridge, shed lamps burning over her. Up on the wet stone, a warm square of doorway, and the sister's boy comes down with a canvas roll under his arm.

On screen: Sol (bag slung, wading to the step), Ife (line over her shoulder, pole in hand), The sister's boy (torch up, boots too big)

SOL You've got the wire.

THE SISTER'S BOY Coil and a battery and a torch with a bad switch and I brought the pliers too.

IFE Then we go now, before the second one comes down the reach.

THE SISTER'S BOY I'll walk you to the depot wall.

Drowned Tram Depot, night

Ambience: the culvert muttering, water lapping the wall

The corner of the depot wall, black water beyond it. Shed lamps glow behind them; ahead, nothing but the reach running away.

On screen: The sister's boy (stopped, torch held two-handed), Sol (up to her chest), Ife (ahead, sounding with the pole)

THE SISTER'S BOY That's as far as me. I don't go in the black water.

SOL Nobody's asking you to.

THE SISTER'S BOY Here. The wire. And the battery's good for an hour if you keep it dry.

He puts them in her hand, then scrambles up onto the wall and stands there with the torch.

THE SISTER'S BOY I'll hold it on you. Until you're out of reach.

IFE Then hold it well.

THE SISTER'S BOY I'm holding it!

Market District, night

Ambience: water pulling hard at the legs, a low hum with no edges to it

The outfall reach runs cold and purposeful, coming down out of the drowned streets and turning at the market's edge toward the sea. The fishmonger's lamp is out. The fishmonger's window is out.

On screen: Sol (bag held high on her shoulder, wading to the chest), Ife (pole ahead, line coiled at her waist)

Sound: a small breath of a torch clicking off behind them

IFE (calling back) Four. Four and a half. Three, there's a step. Mind the step.

SOL I have the step.

They wade. Ife calls the numbers and Sol counts them and listens.

SOL Stop. Stand still a moment.

IFE Standing.

SOL The market's dark again. You hear that?

IFE I hear the water and I hear you.

SOL The hub's holding. I can feel it holding, the same as when I put it back. But the lamps are out.

IFE You said the hub lit them. We saw them come on. The whole hall.

SOL It did. And the old man was going to signal with his lamp. The hub's still holding. Somebody's cut the feed off it again since.

SOL That's not a fault. A fault rings. That's a hand.

IFE Then talk to them. You had a word for the depot.

SOL I asked on the air. The air's the one place a looter won't answer.

The vending machines stand in their row with all four doors open. The fourth is empty. A folded coat lies on the shelf.

On screen: Sol (hand on the empty shelf), Ife (torch on the machines)

SOL He's moved. He said he'd shout from the hall door and signal with his lamp. He didn't signal.

IFE Then he's dead or he's hiding.

SOL He's not dead. A man who strips copper in a flood doesn't die easy. And he had a line to the woman across the way. He'd have gone to her before he'd have gone into the water.

Sol stands still and closes her eyes.

Sound: a small regular knocking, coming and going under the stone

SOL There's someone under the hall. The arches are full. There's a knocking in the water. Leave the pole. This way.

Market District, night

Ambience: water slapping under stone steps, an old man's coughing

Under the hall's stone steps, an air pocket the size of a cupboard. The old man is half in the water, shirt caught on a broken paling, his good arm holding a boat hook up out of it.

On screen: The old man in the vending machine (grey, soaked, hook up), Sol (hauling at the paling), Ife (in the water, hands out)

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Mind the chest. The chest is all right. It's the shirt that's the trouble.

SOL Hold on the hook. We have you.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE I'm holding. I've been holding a while.

They get the shirt loosed and the old man up the steps and his breath back out of him.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Hub's still good.

SOL I know it's good. Tell me the rest.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE I went to the hall door with my lamp, the way I said. The hub was holding and the lamps were bright and I had the lamp up and I was shouting the news across the way to Mrs. Okafor.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Then a man came round the corner in a skiff. A wrench in his hand and a remote in his pocket. He took the market off at the remote like a man turning off a light in his own house.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE The hub never moved. He cut the feed above it. I shouted at him and he looked at me and put his hand on the remote again. I knew he meant to hold her dark until he'd got what he came for.

IFE So you went after him. In the water. In the dark.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE I am an old fool. And my wrench went in the water and that's the last of it.

SOL What did he want?

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Copper. What do they ever want. The hall's main comes down the arch and it's fat as your arm. Half an hour and no one to see him, and this market's dark for a month.

On screen: Sol (looking at Ife), Ife (looking back, jaw set)

IFE We can't stop for him. Say it, and I'll say it with you.

SOL We can't stop for him. But we can mark him.

Sol goes down the steps with the boy's wire, ties a loop round the fat main where it comes out of the arch, and wedges a scrap of broken paling in the bracket.

SOL He'll feel that catch and know he's not alone in his own dark. It's not fixing it. It's marking it.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Marking it is not the market lit. And my wrench is in the water and I'll want it back.

SOL You'll have it back. I'll come down for it myself when the market's lit. That's a promise.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE And your coat. I'll hold your coat dry. You'll want it when you come down.

SOL Keep it folded. I won't be long.

The pumping house under the Silt Market footbridge, night

Ambience: a low steady note with a bad harmonic over it

The footbridge rises out of the dark at the far end of the reach, a low arch of wet stone, the outfall running under it. A rusted ladder climbs the arch's face. Above it, a black opening, an iron grille, a brass plate.

On screen: Sol (hand on the ladder, bag over her shoulder), Ife (line round the arch footing, braced in the current)

Close-up: A brass plate under old paint: PUMPING HOUSE. Below it a date scratched by hand, weathered almost away.

END INSERT

SOL There it is.

IFE Four rungs under the water. The rest is lace.

Sound: a thin papery hiss from the handheld radio

OZZY —say that again. Say the part about the schematic.

DELL There's no schematic. The fourth is not on the grid's drawings and never was. It's a manual island switch. I put it in myself under the market outfall, years back.

DELL The section link the company drew was on the wrong side of the outfall pumps. I put a switch where a switch needed to be, and I didn't put it on paper.

DELL A switch that isn't on paper is a switch a man can't be caught for. It's on the arch and it's on my hand and it's on the air now. That's the whole of the story.

OZZY Sol Vieira. Are you hearing this.

SOL I'm hearing it.

OZZY Then answer me straight. An unpermitted switch. Unrecorded. Off every drawing the crews carry. Is that what I just put on the air.

OZZY A man has to climb a wet ladder to a box no inspector has seen and turn it. And if it's wrong, we lose the hospital line. Am I hearing it right.

SOL You're hearing it right.

OZZY Then tell me why I shouldn't hold this back till morning.

SOL Because the emergency line is a battery of hearts and it doesn't run forever. And if you hold it back, the city finds out anyway, and it finds out as a secret.

SOL Tell it now. Tell it whole. It's a lawbreaker's switch. I'll not argue that. It's a lawbreaker's switch and it's a right switch and the hospital's on the far end of it.

OZZY I'll put it on. All of it. And Sol, there will be consequences, and they will come before morning.

SOL Let them.

The pumping house under the Silt Market footbridge, night

Ambience: water rising past the stone, the radio carrying voices

The water comes up the arch while the radio talks, and the city talks back.

On screen: Sol (arm through the ladder, water to her waist), Ife (holding the line taut, face unreadable)

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE (over the radio) I'm at the depot and I've had my hands in the yard's water since before the second surge.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE (over the radio) You want to know about Dell Marchetti's switches, ask the block behind this bridge. The law didn't light a lamp tonight. Dell did.

SOL That's her.

IFE That's her.

THE MAN WITH THE TOBACCO TIN (over the radio) I'm on the Silt Market footbridge. I've watched the water come over that ladder since the second surge.

THE MAN WITH THE TOBACCO TIN (over the radio) I'm not going to say the old man's wrong. I'm going to say I don't know that he's right.

THE MAN WITH THE TOBACCO TIN (over the radio) I've my children on the far side of the market and no light on the far side of the market, and I'm told to take my boat to a switch that isn't drawn on any paper because a man put it in himself and didn't say. I don't know him. Why would I trust it?

On screen: Sol (looking up at the grille, then at Ife in the water)

SOL Because you don't have to trust him. You have to trust the switch.

SOL If it's the wrong switch, the wrong switch shows itself. It'll ring wrong and run hot and come back dark, and I'll tell you on the air before I put my hands in.

SOL My name's Sol Vieira and I'm Dell Marchetti's apprentice and I'm going up the ladder.

THE MAN WITH THE TOBACCO TIN (over the radio) I'll hold the light.

SOL Then hold it on the ladder and not on me.

Ife pays the spare line round the arch footing, pulls it taut, and braces her chest against the stone.

IFE Hear me once, then.

IFE Your Dell put a switch in a wall and never told a soul, and the hospital's riding on it tonight, and my mother's block is riding on it. I don't like riding on a lawbreaker's secret. I don't like it at all.

IFE But I'd like my mother's block in the dark a deal less. So go up. Every one of us on this water is standing on a man's secret tonight.

SOL If it's wrong, the first thing you'll hear is me telling you. Not the radio. Me.

IFE I'll be listening for you and not the radio. Go.

The pumping house under the Silt Market footbridge, night

Ambience: water dripping off boots into dark water below, a small ticking behind iron

The rusted ladder climbs the wet stone face. Rust comes off it in flakes. Behind the grille, black, and out of the black a low note and a bad harmonic and a small ticking like a clock with a bent hand.

On screen: Sol (boot on the sill, two fingers through the grille, face to the iron), Ife (below, hands white on the line)

Picture: Sol clings to the rusted ladder halfway up the black arch face above the flooded reach, the torch light of a stranger on the footbridge holding her in a hard white beam while the water pulls at her boots below.

SOL I'm up and I can hear the switch. It's hot. Been hot a long while. Ticking like a kettle left on.

She gets a glove off and works two fingers through the grille. The hot brass finds her knuckles and the tick comes through it and up her arm.

SOL It's seized. The lever's set in its own scale and it won't come.

SOL Off first, Dell said. Off first, and I can't get it off from here. Two fingers through a grille won't turn a link that's been hot since the first surge.

IFE Then come down.

SOL Not yet. I'm reading it again.

Sound: the tick quickening, a hot creak of brass

SOL It's not just seized, Ife. It's loaded. The housing's swelled against the bracket and the whole link is sitting hot in its own scale, and one pair of hands on a wet ladder won't free it and bind it and put it back.

SOL That's three things with two hands, and the third one is the hospital.

IFE Then you can't do it. Say it plain, so the city hears it plain.

On screen: Sol (both hands on the grille, forehead almost to the iron)

SOL Ozzy. Put me on.

OZZY (over the radio) You're on.

SOL The fourth is found. It's here and it's hot and it's seized, and I can't finish it off this ladder tonight with what I've got. It needs a dry hand and a clean tool and more than one pair of arms, and it needs doing right.

SOL So I'm not turning it blind. A switch turned wrong is a hospital dark. I'll come back with what it needs and I'll do it where the city can see my hands.

OZZY (over the radio) The city hears you, Sol Vieira. The fourth is found and not finished.

OZZY (over the radio) Then hear this. The second high water is in the reach and it's coming up. Anyone on the water below the market footbridge, get to a wall.

DELL (over the radio, low and slow) Sol. You did right. A switch that's seized is a switch that's telling you something. Listen to it.

SOL I'm listening. I'm coming down.

She gets her boot off the sill and goes down the rusted rungs, and the water has climbed two of them while she was up.

On screen: Ife (both hands out, line taut), Sol (dropping the last rungs into her arms)

IFE I've got you.

SOL The arch is going under. The whole pumping house is going under before the second tide's done.

IFE Then it goes under with the switch still hot and my motors still sitting, and the market still dark, and you still standing.

SOL Three and a half breakers. That's what I've got.

IFE Then we take the half and we come back for the rest. That's the bargain, and it's a worse one than I named, and I'm still standing in it.

SOL Then hold the line while I get my feet, and we go and get what the fourth needs before the tide does.

The reach runs faster than it did, and the second high tide is in it now, and somewhere far off a works boat's motor turns over and comes on.
