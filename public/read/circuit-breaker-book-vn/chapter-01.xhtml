The Sound of the Grid

Grid Control Room, night

Ambience: low transformer hum, rain hammering glass, the click of the annunciator

A high concrete hall the size of a small church. Green status lamps glow along two walls of black annunciator panels; one emergency bulb swings overhead. Brown water stands ankle-deep across the floor and is rising.

On screen: Sol (navy coverall, sleeves rolled, pencil behind her ear, hand flat on the panel), Dell (brown canvas jacket, cap pushed back, eyes closed, in the squeaking chair)

DELL Read it to me.

SOL Zone four. The waterworks line. It's not tripping, it's whistling.

DELL Whistling.

SOL High, up under the vault. Like a kettle that hasn't decided yet.

DELL (eyes opening) Then it's decided.

Dell crosses to the wall map under plastic. Rain slaps the windows. Sol follows, one hand trailing the steel.

Close-up: Dell's hand-drawn grid map, pipes in three colors of pen, the north arrow crooked, labels in private shorthand

DELL How high's the mark along the market now?

SOL Two steps up the ramp.

DELL Two.

SOL I grew up on those curbs. The water's standing over them.

DELL Bottom of the hour. Read me the whole board.

Sol walks the annunciator, pressing her palm to the warm metal, thumb stopping her other ear.

Sound: a deep, muffled phut through the floor

Sol's feet go apart before she decides to move.

DELL Talk to me, child.

SOL Zones three and four just lost a phase. The whistle stopped. The vault's flooded.

DELL Don't guess.

SOL I'm not guessing, I'm reading. Hear that chatter? That's the hospital feeder trying to reclose into a fault.

DELL It'll keep trying.

SOL Until it melts.

On screen: Sol (clipped, sharp-eyed), Dell (on his feet, hands hovering over a toolbag and a warped notebook)

Dell sets a stiff canvas toolbag and a swollen notebook on the desk. His hands hesitate.

DELL Radio. Ozzy's frequency. Now.

SOL Substations have phones.

DELL The phones are dead. The lines run along the canal, and the canal is in the street.

Sol spins the dial. Static, then a warm rapid voice.

On screen: Sol (listening, dial under her fingers), Dell (watching the rain)

OZZY Water over the barriers at Blackgate. I say again, water over the barriers at Blackgate. This is not the tide. Move to your upstairs floors. Do not go down.

SOL He's saying the barriers are gone.

DELL A hundred years old, built for a different river.

Dell puts the notebook into her hands and closes her fingers around it.

DELL You know what a breaker does. You read a line by ear better than I ever did. That's not a kindness, it's a fact.

SOL Four breakers?

DELL Four that matter tonight. They trip, they get reset by hand, because the automatic's gone.

Sol opens the cover. Old ink, brown at the edges, and her own pencil in the margins.

Close-up: A notebook margin in square schoolgirl letters: CHECK THIS. ASK ABOUT THIS. An arrow beside the words: bottom line looks wrong

SOL I wrote this when I was sixteen.

DELL Three of them are in the book. The fourth is off every map there is, and I'm the reason.

On screen: Sol (book half-open, staring at him), Dell (not meeting her eyes)

SOL Then tell me now.

DELL Not on a roof in a storm. When the three are back, ask me where it lives. Ask me on the air, so Ozzy hears it. I've kept a thing too long.

SOL You expect me to find a breaker that isn't on paper.

DELL I expect you to be the part that isn't written.

Sound: a close, heavy phut, then the long brown-out of the lights

Bank after bank of annunciator goes dark. Only the little red exit eyes remain. Water lies across the landing like tarnished steel, and climbs.

DELL Roof. Leave it. All of it. Just the bag.

SOL Your coat's on the hook.

DELL Leave it.

They climb the back stairs, water lipping at their heels. Dell takes them slowly, one hand on the rail. Halfway, he stops and puts his hand out sideways.

On screen: Sol (bag knocking her hip, breathing hard), Dell (hand on the rail, grey with effort)

DELL I'd like to have finished the boat.

SOL You'll finish it.

DELL I'd like to have finished it before tonight. That's not the same thing.

Port Alaric, the roof above the Grid Control Room, night

Ambience: rain like gravel, wind, water sucking through the flooded floors below

The flat tar roof in the storm. The whole dark delta spreads below, canals and streets gone to one black moving thing.

On screen: Sol (soaked, hat gone, bag over her shoulder), Dell (collar up, leaning into the wind)

Below, the long bright string of the market district plunges end to end. Then the waterworks and the low neighborhoods, block by block. Then the tram depot. The hospital flickers twice and holds.

Picture: Sol and Dell stand on the tar roof in the rain, the whole of Port Alaric gone dark below them but for one lit hospital at its center.

SOL The hospital held.

DELL That's the emergency line. Wards, lifts, blood fridge, the lights in the operating rooms. That's what we're buying time for.

SOL How long?

Dell gives no answer. That is the answer.

Sol looks down at the black water where Canal Street should be.

SOL A city's a circuit. Everything that makes it a city is a current running through copper in the dark.

DELL Somebody's got to close it.

Sol pulls a handheld radio from the bag. Static, then the warm voice, thin now, with rain on it.

On screen: Sol (radio at her ear, hands unsteady), Dell (watching her)

OZZY This is Ozzy Kwan in the tower on Perch Street, and I am going to stay with you. Power is down in the market and the waterworks and most of the west side. The hospital is on its emergency line.

OZZY That line is a battery of hearts, and it does not run forever. If anyone knows a soul who can get to the grid, tell them to find Ozzy Kwan. I'll be here.

SOL Where's the first one?

DELL The waterworks. Where the canal bends. You'll have to swim to the vault, more likely than wade.

SOL Then I'll swim.

DELL Read it by sound and touch. The water's already made the book a liar.

Sound: a flat transformer boom rolling down the river

Far off a crowd makes a long low sound, and the rain takes it.

On screen: Sol (turning back, close to angry), Dell (small against the dark)

SOL You had a week left.

DELL A week, and a chair that doesn't squeak, and a boat I meant to finish. The storm can't read a calendar.

Sol stops at the roof door.

DELL When the three are back, ask me where the fourth lives. On the air.

DELL My book's a map, and the water's made the map a liar. Three in the book. One off it. One circuit.

SOL I'll ask you on the air.

DELL Go on, then.

Sol goes down into the cold, busy water. The notebook stays dry in her coat. The dead things in the bag wait to be alive.

OZZY I'll be here.

The rain comes down on the dark city, and the city listens.
