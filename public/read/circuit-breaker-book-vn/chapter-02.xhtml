The Barge at Canal Street

Canal Street Mooring, night

Ambience: steady rain hiss, low water churn, a distant generator

Water sits waist-high over the kerb. The café awning has torn loose and swings on one bolt; the locksmith's window is a black hole. A flat family barge rides low at the corner, one warm lamp burning behind its wheelhouse.

On screen: Sol (soaked, bag over her shoulder, hands lifted and open), Ife (rain hat, boat-hook across her body, standing in the lamp's gold circle)

SOL It's me.

IFE I can see it's you. That's why I said far enough.

SOL The hospital's on its emergency line. You can hear it if—

IFE (flat) I'm asking what you want. Not what's happening.

Sol plants her feet in the current.

SOL I need a boat. Yours.

IFE Course you do. Go ask somebody who likes you.

SOL Nobody likes me. I'm not here to be liked. It's four breakers by hand, or the hospital's line doesn't hold past morning.

Ife turns the boat-hook a quarter turn, like a screw.

IFE That's Dell's bag.

SOL He gave it to me.

IFE Says a lot about Dell.

IFE You had a board in the training hall last spring. The one with the burnt neutral. You told the assessor I left the tester in it.

SOL I remember a tester and a burn.

IFE I was there till two in the morning fixing what you broke. I got written up. Careless. You got promising.

On screen: Sol (still, jaw tight), Ife (rain running off the hat brim, eyes hard)

SOL The assessor's write-up doesn't hold a hospital. It doesn't hold lights on.

SOL Your family's on Barrow Row. That's the hospital district.

IFE Don't.

SOL Barrow Row runs off the hospital's transformers. When the emergency line goes, the flats go dark. Your mother's stair. The lifts. Your gran's machine.

Ife looks off down the black street.

IFE (half to herself) Four flights and a landing and a light that's been gone since I was twelve.

IFE Don't answer that.

She steps into the wheelhouse. A drawer. The radio registers with a hiss.

On screen: Sol (listening, thumb to one ear), Ife (coil of line in her hand, lamp turned up)

Sound: the radio burr of Ozzy's voice, close

OZZY First casualty reports. On the east side, at the Silt Market, a roofer, Tomas Ilyich, seventy-one, fell from an upper window reaching for a neighbour's roof. Neighbours have him. They say he is alive, and they say he needs a doctor.

OZZY And listen—the hospital district is still lit. Still lit. That is not the same as safe.

Ife throws the line. Sol catches it against her chest, gets the loop over the bollard, hauls.

IFE All right. Here's what it costs, and I'll say it once.

IFE The tram's down in the depot with the water over her. My engine's an old diesel on its last summer. That tram carries the motors to make her new.

IFE So. You get me to the depot, I get first claim on the tram's salvage. I pilot, you navigate. Say yes or climb back in the water.

Sol turns it over once, like a fitting, to see the thread.

SOL The tram's not one of the four. It's not on the map.

IFE I know it's not on the map. That's the whole point of it.

SOL Yes. First claim on the tram's salvage is yours. You pilot, I navigate.

IFE Say it with the terms in it.

SOL You pilot. I navigate. The tram's salvage is your first claim. And whatever's left of the night, we split.

They shake once, hard and brief.

IFE And after that, we're even on the board, and I never hear the word promising again.

SOL Take it. I'll say it on the radio.

IFE Not on the radio.

On screen: Sol (frowning), Ife (jaw set)

IFE A thing said on a radio belongs to everybody. This one's mine. Say it to Dell and to the hall.

SOL Then it's yours. To Dell and the hall.

IFE Fine.

Ife casts off in two pulls and lets the loop pay through her hands. The engine comes up out of its mutter into a heavy knocking note.

IFE Read me the way, then. I can't see a street sign from here, and the signs that are left are under the water.

Sol goes up to the bow, Dell's notebook dry inside her coat.

SOL The waterworks is downriver, round the bend where the canal turns. But the straight run through the market is wrong tonight.

SOL Shut up a second.

She closes one ear with her thumb and turns the other to the dark.

Sound: a faint high stutter under the rain, steady as a mosquito at a window

SOL Hear it? A feeder arcing in the water at the market. Old pipe run, modern cable pulled through it, nobody drew where. Anyone wading there tonight, the current won't ask.

SOL So we go round the back of the lock keeper's lane. Deeper water, but cold water. And the bridge with the chain will tell us the true channel.

Ife comes out of the wheelhouse to look at her.

IFE Say that again, slower. And put your hand on the gunwale while you say it, so I know you're not making it up.

Sol puts her hand on the gunwale and says it again.

IFE And the chain?

SOL Hung under the arch on the keystone, and the keystone on the old pilings. High water, it drags and rings. When it rings, it rings off the true channel.

IFE How do you know that?

SOL I don't. Dell knows it, and now I've got Dell's book.

She touches the cover once through her coat.

On screen: Ife (looking at the coat, something shifting behind the grudge), Sol (watching her)

IFE Keep that dry. Both of them.

The lock keeper's lane, night

Ambience: rain on window glass, water slapping narrow walls, the engine's knocking

The lane narrows to four paces, houses leaning almost to meet overhead. Lamps burn in upstairs windows and lay gold threads on the black water. Ahead, a stone arch rises with a chain hung beneath its keystone.

On screen: Sol (at the bow with the lamp cone falling ahead), Ife (at the wheel, hat brim dripping)

SOL Past the locksmith's. Now the lane.

IFE It's a living room. It's somebody's living room and we're driving through it.

SOL Stoop at eleven, railings on the left. Then the dead service pipe.

IFE I see the railings.

The barge nudges a wall and grinds along it. Above, a curtain moves.

On screen: Ife (eyes up at the windows), Sol (hand out, counting under her breath)

IFE Someone's watching us out of their upstairs like we're the weather.

SOL We are the weather.

IFE There. The chain. I hear it.

Sound: iron ringing low and slow in moving water

Picture: Sol and Ife on the barge's deck in the lamplight, the barge passing under the stone arch with the chain hanging and ringing above them, rain silver in the lamp cone.

SOL That's the true channel.

IFE Then hold on to something.

The barge slides under the arch and the ringing passes overhead and falls behind. Ahead the lane opens into a long straight reach.

On screen: Ife (relaxing her grip on the wheel by one finger), Sol (breathing out)

IFE Straight from here?

SOL Straight from here. The waterworks at the end of it.

IFE Then get up here where I can see you.

Sol climbs to the wheelhouse. Ife keeps her eyes forward.

IFE One thing. You said you'd say it to Dell and the hall. What if Dell won't hear it? Man's retiring in a week and he's off his map tonight, by the sound of you.

SOL He'll hear it.

IFE That's not an answer, that's a hope.

SOL He gave me his book and his bag and told me to ask him the hard questions on the air. A man who does that will hear a word in a room.

IFE Huh.

IFE Keep that radio up. I want to know what the man says next.

Ozzy's voice comes out of the little set, thin but steady.

OZZY Still lit. Write it down. Say it over your stairs. The hospital is still lit.

The barge pushes a slow white wrinkle ahead of it, and the wrinkle goes out into the black and is gone.
