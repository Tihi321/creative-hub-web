The First Breaker

The lock keeper's lane, night

Ambience: rain dripping from eaves, water slapping narrow walls, the engine's slow knock

The lane narrows until the houses lean across the water. The rain comes off the eaves in a thin drip instead of a fall. Ahead, a wet stone arch with weeds hanging off it.

On screen: Sol (at the bow, knee braced on the gunwale, eyes half shut), Ife (at the wheel, chin up, reading the dark)

SOL Houses on your left are nine and eleven. Stoop at eleven. Iron railings.

IFE Mind them yourself.

SOL I'm minding them. Ease left.

The bow swings a hand's width. Iron scrapes wood, complains, and is gone.

SOL Pipe on the right. Hundred-year-old service run. Hear it? Not a hum. A leak. On nothing.

IFE Meaning?

SOL Meaning it's dead. It can't hurt us. Leave it alone.

SOL There's a cable in that wall three feet up. Dead too. Cut at the junction where the lane bends. Somebody did that right.

IFE Somebody did that sometime. Kick the stern off that stoop.

The bridge with the chain, night

Ambience: iron ringing low and slow in moving water

The lane opens out. A wet stone arch stands against the black with a heavy chain hung beneath its keystone, dragging in the current. Rain beads on the weeds.

On screen: Sol (head turned, listening hard), Ife (hands light on the wheel)

Sound: a struck iron note, low and irregular, coming and going with the push of the water

SOL There. Hear it?

IFE I hear it.

SOL When it rings, it rings off the true channel. Best sound all night.

IFE Then hold something.

The barge comes in on a patient quarter turn. The chain goes by on the left, ringing.

Beyond the arch the water opens into a long straight reach. Low at the end of it, huddled and dark, the waterworks.

SOL (quiet) That's it.

On screen: Sol (standing, bag lifted clear), Ife (throttling down, watching the reach)

SOL Smell that? Wet stone. Old iron. Under it, a solder iron left on.

IFE That's the fault.

SOL That's the fault. Dock us anywhere. Water's over the loading steps, but there's a platform inside the arch.

IFE Ram it slow?

SOL Ram it slow.

The Waterworks, night

Ambience: a small high electric stutter in the water, the barge's idle

The brick pumping station hunches over a flooded yard. Inside the loading arch, black water lies chest-deep and still. Out in the middle of it something warm breathes off the surface.

On screen: Sol (chest-deep, bag held over her head, palm flat on the wall), Ife (on the barge's nose, coil of line over one shoulder)

SOL (to herself) Not a hum. A stutter. Mosquito at a window.

Sol walks her palm down the cold wall. Below her hand the cold has a shape. Out in the middle of the floor, a wrong bathwater warmth.

SOL Fault's on the vault floor. East side, under the second bay. Live in the water, and the water carries it round like a rumor up a stairwell.

IFE And where we're standing?

SOL Quiet. Let me check quiet.

Close-up: A small brass-faced tester in Sol's wet hand, its needle lying flat at nothing

The needle sits at nothing. She moves the lead a yard. Nothing. A yard toward the warm place, and the needle kicks once, a nervous twitch.

END INSERT

SOL Two yards from here it bites. Past that it's the grave.

IFE So we don't go past that.

SOL No. I go down under it and come up under the vault hatch and do it by touch.

On screen: Ife (life vest dripping, face gone still), Sol (testing the ladder's top rung with two fingers)

IFE And if you don't come up?

SOL Then don't come in after me. Keep the engine running and the radio on.

IFE Sol.

SOL If I don't come up, you take the barge to Dell at the grid. You tell him the first one's done, which it will be. Then you get the hospital the rest of the way.

SOL And you take the tram's salvage and you don't think about it twice.

IFE (flat) If you don't come up, I'm not going to the tram depot for salvage. I'm going to Barrow Row for my mother.

IFE So come up.

Sol puts the tester away and takes a breath and goes down.

Under the water it is a low roar and then a beat of pressure. Cold at the top, colder at the bottom.

Picture: Sol, deep under the black water of the flooded vault, one hand hauling along a riveted metal lip toward a hatch, a faint warm cloud glowing ahead of her cheek.

Sol counts the pressure beats to keep herself slow. One. Two. The heat comes before she expects it, a warm cloud against her face, tasting of a battery's mouth.

She goes still and lets the current carry her around it instead of through it.

Her fingers find the hatch handle. The handle is hot. She does not let it stop her. Her thumb crosses the housing and finds the breaker body: cast, old, worn, the lever up.

The lever is up because it tripped. All she has to do is pull it down. It fights her.

Sol braces her shoulder on the housing and drags the lever an inch, another inch, silt grating in the mechanism.

The lever goes home with a clack she feels through her jaw rather than hears through the water.

The current changes. The warm cloud goes cool. The stutter above her stops as if a door opened and let the rumor out.

Sound: a great whooping breath breaking the surface, then coughing

On screen: Sol (face in her own wet sleeve, hanging off the arch ledge), Ife (at the edge, staring)

SOL Done. First one's done.

Sound: pipes filling with a rising note, pumps turning somewhere in the dark

The maintenance lamp over the arch was dead. Now it glows a sickly orange, and stays on.

IFE You're alive.

SOL Terms of the job.

On screen: Sol (hauling herself up, legs hanging, radio dripping), Ife (crouched at the arch, hands out to steady her)

Sol shakes the handheld out. It comes back on with a burr in it.

OZZY —repeat, the hospital district is holding. Backup generators have come in. The wards are steady, the lifts are running, and the blood fridge is holding cold.

OZZY I want you to hear that cold. Cold blood is a good sign. It means the line has bought time.

OZZY And whoever in the waterworks district just got the pumps back up, the lamps are coming on in the low streets. If that was you, thank you.

OZZY But a lit lamp is not a dry street. Stay up.

Sol sits on the ledge with her boots in the water and lets the small thing in her chest go quiet.

IFE Say it like a checklist.

SOL Waterworks done.

The Waterworks, night

Ambience: pumps singing in the wall, water lapping at the loading ring

The barge drifts at the loading ring, riding easy. The little orange lamp keeps the arch company. The reach runs away into the dark, and down it to the right there is a roof.

On screen: Sol (torch in hand, legs still wet, sitting in the bow), Ife (at the gunwale, eyes narrowed at the reach)

Sol puts the torch on the reach. A signpost. A mailbox under water. A lamp green with weed.

Then, further right, the roof. There is a person on the roof.

On screen: The woman on the roof (raincoat too big, child on her hip, hauling at a crate), Sol (up, torch steady), Ife (already moving)

A woman in an oversized raincoat pulls at a supply crate on wet slate. The crate sits in the gutter, the water lapping its corner, and she is not winning.

IFE Don't. I know that voice. I've been that voice. That's a woman doing sums.

SOL There's a chimney stack left of her.

IFE Then she's got a line and we've got a crate. You're learning, promising.

SOL Don't.

IFE Too late. Can you throw?

Ambience: rain hissing on slate, the barge's idle

Sol gets her feet under her in the bow. Twenty yards of black water to the roof. The coil is old and stiff and does not want to go.

She loops half the line in the bottom of the bow, takes the rest in her left hand, puts her right hand through it, and lets the coil's own bulk carry it.

The line goes up and out, comes down across the gutter, and goes into the water a yard short.

THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF (thin across the water) Again! The chimney's behind me—

Sol hauls hand over fist.

Sound: a coil of line hissing out over black water

The second throw goes high and wide. The woman gets a hand to it, then both, and wraps it twice.

On screen: The woman on the roof (line biting in her fists, child clamped in one arm), Ife (shouting, hands cupped)

IFE Tie it to the chimney! Not the pot, the stack itself! Two rounds and a knot you can't argue with!

SOL The stack holds. Haul when she ties!

The woman works with her whole body. Sol takes two turns round the bollard and lets Ife have the engine and pay it out slow.

Sound: wet slate shrieking under a dragged crate

The line comes up out of the water, stands straight and singing, and the crate slides over the ridge and stops against the chimney.

On screen: The woman on the roof (sitting down hard, child in her lap, rocking), Sol (shouting across), Ife (turning away at the wheel)

THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF What's your name!

Ife's jaw works. She does not answer.

SOL Ife Aduba! Barge Aduba, out of Canal Street!

SOL Tell your block a roofer fell in the market, and the market needs a doctor!

THE WOMAN ON THE ROOF Aduba!

She points up at the sky, then hard at the dark where Barrow Row lies. Then she sits down with her child and her dry crate.

On screen: Ife (voice perfectly even, hand on the throttle), Sol (settling beside her, bag on her hip)

Ife puts the engine in gear. The reach opens ahead.

IFE That's forty feet of my good line gone. I want it back before the night's out.

SOL You will get it back.

SOL She pointed. Over toward the market.

IFE I saw.

SOL I think she's got a doctor.

IFE Then let's get the market's breaker and leave the doctor to it.

IFE Two down and two to go. Tell me the way.

Ambience: the engine's steady knock, a wet radio burr

On screen: Sol (palm flat on the cool gunwale, eyes closed), Ife (throttle up, watching the reach)

SOL The reach runs straight to the market. The market's a square with streets off it like spokes. The hub of the wheel is where the second breaker lives.

SOL We'll want the winch for the housing, and the bolt set won't want to turn.

IFE Keep your hand on that rail while you say it.

Sol keeps her hand there. The barge goes on down the reach into the dark.

OZZY The hospital district is still lit. Say it over your stairs. Still lit.

SOL Waterworks done. Market next. Depot after.

Sol closes her eyes and feels the river go under the hull, and puts her hand on the bag of dead things.

SOL Then the fourth one. The one that's off the map.
