The Third Breaker

Drowned Tram Depot, night

Ambience: wet iron, dead oil, a hum deep and half a note flat

A wide black yard ringed by a high brick wall. Half a dozen tram cars stand in the water to their windows. The shed's roof sags at the middle.

On screen: Sol (in the bow, torch low, head tilted), Ife (at the wheel, one hand on the pole, one on the throttle)

SOL Under the shed. Hear that?

IFE I hear the engine. I hear my heart. Nothing else.

SOL Like a fridge giving up. Half a note flat.

IFE That's her.

SOL The tram?

IFE My engine. Sitting in the water where I can see her and can't have her.

Ife brings the barge in slow.

IFE Sol. Look at the shed.

SOL The whole yard's filling from under. A spring's broken through the floor and it's running in.

IFE Then we do your breaker quick.

SOL We do your breaker right.

Sol opens the notebook on her knee.

SOL Shed or the office block at the end. We find it, we put it back, then the fourth, and the whole depot's yours. All four. That was the bargain.

On screen: Ife (mouth going flat, jaw working), Sol (notebook open, pencil behind her ear)

IFE You think I've forgotten the terms. I named them.

SOL I'm not cutting them loose.

IFE I'm asking you to make the water stop coming up over my motors while you hunt your switch. There's a difference, and I know the difference, and it's eating me.

IFE My mother's block is across the yard from that lot. If the yard's a lake by morning there'll be nothing left to claim.

SOL Then we're quick and right and this one goes first. And when all four are back, I'll stand in this water and help you cut them loose myself. You have my word.

IFE I'll hold you to the word. And I'll be eyeing my motors the whole time.

The depot office, night

Ambience: water lapping at a wall, the barge's idle knocking

A two-storey brick box at the end of the yard, its door under the water. The barge lies alongside where the fire escape's bottom steps sink and vanish.

On screen: Sol (leg over the rail, toolbag over her shoulder), Ife (hand on the pole, eyes on the trams)

SOL Tie off and stay with her.

IFE If you don't come up I'm coming in after you. Not for my motors. For you. You're my crew now, and that's a worse bargain than the one I named.

SOL You'll come up. I'll make a noise.

SOL If the engine goes while I'm under, don't follow it down. A hot engine in cold water cracks. She's been running rough and hot since the lane this afternoon.

IFE She's an old diesel that's been on her last summer since the spring. I know what I've got under that hatch.

IFE Go on. Move.

The water in the office reaches Sol's chest. The steel door gives. The vault beyond runs dry to the walls: two pillars of switch-gear, older than the grid's.

On screen: Sol (chest-deep in the doorway, torch up, breathing hard)

Sol reads the plates and her breath stops.

Close-up: Six grey plates on the left pillar, paper tags wired on in a square hand: DEPOT IN. DEPOT OUT. DEPOT IN. A word smudged past reading. OFF—DO NOT, with the pencil stopped there. The sixth plate bare. Dell's drawing is a wheel with six spokes: in at the top, out at the bottom, mind the two that look alike. In the room the in-line sits at the bottom. Where the drawing says it goes, a plate sits with no tag at all, and the wire behind it hums.

END INSERT

IFE (from the stairwell) How are you doing?

SOL Fine. Give me a minute.

IFE Water's up a step. Give me one minute.

Sol presses her knuckles to the pillar and feels the hum come up through the leather and into the bones of her hand.

On screen: Sol (knuckles on cold iron, eyes shut), Ife (out of sight below, voice climbing)

SOL (to herself) The drawing doesn't match the room. Read the room, then.

SOL The long note's low, by the bottom plate. That's the depot's supply off the trunk.

SOL The one with no tag rings low and long with a bad harmonic, and the housing under it runs warm. It's loose in its housing. It didn't trip because the trip's downstream of the bad connection.

IFE Then put it back.

SOL It's a make-and-break, Ife. It's on. I can bind a loose housing, but I can't work a switch that's on and hot.

IFE Then turn it off.

SOL Off means the shed's pumps go off, and the water's coming up faster.

IFE Sol. The water's over the outside step. Two minutes and it's in your stairwell.

Sol takes the reading again: the pillar going warm and cool and warm under her knuckles.

SOL (quiet) Write what you did when you couldn't read it.

SOL Ife. I turn the bad switch off and the in-line with it, strap the housing, and put them back in the right order. If it goes wrong, get up the top window onto the shed roof. The shed roof is dry. That's your way out.

IFE That's a long way to go for a switch.

SOL It's the only way to go. Stand back.

She puts her weight on the lever and it comes down. The hum dies and leaves a silence that rings. She binds the loose housing with the strap and the turnbuckle, and it goes tight in her wrists. Then the in-line down, and the bad switch back up, and the hum returns even.

On screen: Sol (hand flat on the plate, listening to her own breathing)

SOL Warm, cool, cooler, cool. It held.

Sol writes in the margin of the tram page.

Close-up: A notebook margin in a square schoolgirl hand: turned off, bound, back on, cool. Under it, smaller: Dell's drawing is upside down. Read the room, not the page.

END INSERT

The torch still burns, and the torch is not what has gone dark. Outside, a black drop, and then the echo of an arc opening upstream and staying open.

On screen: Sol (looking up from the page, pencil still), Ife (on the stairwell landing, staring out)

IFE The shed's gone. The yard's gone dark.

Sound: the flat slam of an arc opening and staying open

SOL The in-line's holding. Somebody upstream's put a fault through.

Drowned Tram Depot, night

Ambience: water pushing at the hull, the shed's pumps silent

Sol comes up into the barge's lantern with the toolbag over her head. The shed's long row of lamps is dead.

On screen: Sol (wet to the chest, dripping), Ife (pole up, holding the barge off the wall)

SOL Something upstream opened and took the depot with it. My switch is right and there's no power to it and the water's coming up.

IFE The shed's dark and my motors are in the dark. Answer me what we do.

SOL You keep the barge off the wall. I listen for the depot trunk. If the trunk's alive the shed can have it back, and if the depot comes back the pumps come back.

IFE And if it doesn't?

SOL Then we've got a barge with an old engine and a city with a worse one, and we do the fourth in the dark.

SOL If the shed stays dark I lose you a night's work, and I know what a night's work costs. All four, then your claim. I'll make it right. But not now.

Ife puts the pole down, pulls the throttle back, and holds the barge in the middle of the yard with a face like a closed door.

Sound: tin ringing under boots like a drum

On screen: Sol (flat on the shed's tin roof, ear pressed to it, radio in her other hand), Ife (mid-yard, arms folded, not looking up)

Sol lays her ear to the tin. The long even note runs under the floor and is alive. Then a smaller sound: a thin high ringing off the trams' wet shells, starting somewhere in the third tram.

Sol gets the radio up and turns the knob.

OZZY —still with us. Two breakers done. Two left. The second high water is coming behind the river.

OZZY The depot quarter has gone dark and I have no word from it. If you are there, say a word into any radio you have.

SOL Ozzy. This is Sol. Dell's apprentice. The depot's dark on a bad arc upstream and the shed pumps are down and the water's in my vault and the breaker's reset and it's not enough.

SOL I need the depot trunk back. Whoever's got a hand on a remote upstream, put it back now, and if you're a looter with a wrench, put it back now, and I'll not ask your name.

OZZY Held. She's held the breaker and she needs the feed. Hear her.

OZZY (small, close) Sol Vieira. Say another word when you've got it. I'll be listening.

The dark holds. Nobody puts the trunk back. Sol draws the six plates as they actually stand, and writes what she did to each.

The yard's wall runs off to a low stone bridge over the culvert, and a shape stands there with a lantern and a bucket coming up dripping.

On screen: The old woman at the culvert bridge (lantern at her feet, two buckets), Sol (calling from the tin)

SOL You on the bridge. What are you doing?

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE Bailing. The culvert's backing up and the yard's the low part and my sister's in the block the other side of it.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE If the yard goes she's got a wet floor and no way up. And you?

SOL Electrician. I reset the depot's breaker and it's dark anyway and the pumps are dead.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE Then you'll want the yard's water down before you want the shed's. If the yard comes down, the shed empties, and maybe your pumps can breathe.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE I've a second line and bucket under the bridge. Fetch them yourself. Get off that roof before it puts you in the water.

The stone bridge at the depot yard, night

Ambience: rope and dripping water, the count kept low

A low stone bridge where the old road crosses the culvert. The barge lies against its wet stone with a line round the bollard.

On screen: The old woman at the culvert bridge (cape and rubber boots, lantern hung on the bollard), Ife (flat, hands on the rope), Sol (bucket in hand)

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE Fill and haul and tip. There's a stone step by the bridge. Don't stand in the yard. The yard's got the culvert in it.

IFE I've got an engine and no time.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE An engine that runs is a better pump than an engine that's hot and dead in a shed. Come on.

They bail. Two lines over the side and two buckets coming up. The yard goes down an inch and then two, and the shed's floor shows black and wet and no worse.

Then the light comes on.

Sound: old bearings shrieking, then settling

Ambience: the shed's pumps running, the barge's engine coughing and stopping

The shed's lamps light one and two and the whole row. And the barge's engine coughs, and stops.

On screen: Ife (hand on the throttle, not moving), Sol (at the hatch with the torch), The old woman at the culvert bridge (on the bridge edge, lantern raised)

SOL Intake. The wake put a wave over the gunwale and it went in at the air. She's flooded.

Sol gets the hatch up. Hot oil and river water come out at her.

SOL But that's the half of it. She was running rough and hot before we ever came into this yard. She'd have shrugged off that wave in the spring. She flooded because she was finished, and tonight found the wave that would prove it.

IFE I know what she was.

IFE I heard her all afternoon, running hotter than she had any right to, and I told myself it was the water and the load and the dark.

IFE She was gone the minute the wave came over. That's none of your doing and all of mine for asking her for one more night.

IFE Three hours of bailing and I can't even get the barge over to my own motors, and the motors are safe and lit and the barge is the thing that's broken.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE You'll not get her going in the middle of the yard with no light on the water. Tie her to the bridge.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE And I'll get my sister's boy down with his tools, and my sister's got a fire and a pot of something. She's in the block the other side of the bridge.

SOL We can't stop. The fourth breaker's off the map and the second high water's coming.

THE OLD WOMAN AT THE CULVERT BRIDGE Then you can't stop with a dead engine either. Come alongside. You'll take a cup and a dry minute and go on. A dry minute is half a mile, out here.

Ife poles them in, and the old woman has a line round the bollard before anyone can ask.

Sound: footsteps on wet stone, a boy's voice

On screen: The sister's boy (torch up, rain jacket too big, tool roll under one arm), The old woman's sister (up on the bridge, waving, apron on)

THE OLD WOMAN'S SISTER Up here! I've got the kettle on! Bring them up!

Sol sits down on the engine hatch hard.

Close-up: A notebook page on top of the rest: the six plates drawn as they actually stand, in a square schoolgirl hand, each with one line of what was done to it.

END INSERT

Sol gets the radio up and presses the key.

On screen: Sol (radio at her mouth, notebook on her knee), Ife (at the gunwale, arms folded, listening)

SOL Ozzy. Sol. The depot's lit. The third breaker's done. Three done, one left.

SOL And I'm calling in my promise to Dell, and I want you to hear it and put it on, because he made me promise to ask it on the air so you'd hear.

SOL Dell. The fourth breaker's off your map. You told me on the roof you were the reason. So I'm asking. Where is it, and what did you do?

Sound: a small papery hiss from the handheld

DELL Sol Vieira.

DELL I'm at the loading arch and my leg's gone out from under me and I'm not coming down, so hear it on the air the way I said.

DELL The fourth is in the pumping house under the Silt Market footbridge, the low arch where the old storm outfall comes in.

DELL It's off my map because I drew that map when I was twenty and I drew it from the wrong side, and I found out at forty and never fixed it, because a map you've drawn wrong once makes a man ashamed to look at it.

DELL There's a page in the back with a wheel on it. That's the pumping house and it's upside down. The fourth is the section link between the outfall pumps and the hospital feeder.

DELL It's a make-and-break and it's had water on it since the first surge. Turn it off before you put your hands in. Off first, then bind it, then put it back. Say it back to me.

SOL Off first. Then bind it. Then back.

DELL Then go, and go now, because the second high water's closer than Ozzy knows.

DELL That's my question answered, Ozzy Kwan. Put it on the air and let the city hear what an old man was too proud to draw.

OZZY You heard him. Three done and one left, and it is the one that holds the hospital line, and the man who drew the map has told the city where it is.

OZZY The Silt Market footbridge, the low arch, the outfall. Whoever is out there with a boat, hear that and go.

Sol closes the notebook and looks at Ife across the dead engine.

On screen: Sol (hatch cover down, notebook under her arm), Ife (looking at her motors under the shed lamps, then back)

SOL The outfall's two reaches back the way we came. The engine won't turn and the barge is heavy. The barge is done and the fourth isn't, and I can't carry a boat on my back.

IFE Then we carry what we can.

IFE The boy's tools, my line, your torch and your book.

IFE And if the engine can be made to cough by morning it'll cough, and if it can't, my motors sit and wait for the water to go down like everything else in this city.

IFE All four, you said. And you'll help me cut them loose after. I'll be standing in the water when you do.

SOL I know you will.

SOL Bridge, then. And the fourth breaker, and Dell's map read the right way up at last.

THE SISTER'S BOY I've got pliers and two drivers and a torch with a bad switch and a bit of wire. Which do you want?

IFE All of it. Bring all of it.
