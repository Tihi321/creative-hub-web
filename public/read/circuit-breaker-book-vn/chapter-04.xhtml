The Second Breaker

Market District, night

Ambience: water lapping against shutters, a distant engine knock, rain easing to a fine hiss

The reach narrows. Flat black water sits between the tops of drowned stalls, a fence that has given up. One lamp burns steady in an upper window over the fishmonger's, and it is the only light in the square.

On screen: Sol (in the bow, torch in hand, wet to the shoulders), Ife (at the wheel, eyes forward, jaw set)

SOL Left. There's a car under us on the right. Take it left.

IFE Taking it left.

The barge noses past the drowned car into the square. Sol keeps the torch low and calls the way a foot at a time.

SOL Somebody left that lamp burning for us. That's a landmark.

IFE Or somebody's letting the oil down slow in a cup.

IFE The hub's in the middle. Where?

Sol opens the notebook under the torch. The market page shows the wheel and its spokes and a little square at the hub, with Dell's old shorthand beside it.

Close-up: A notebook page, water-stained at one corner: a hand-drawn wheel of streets around a small neat square, and beside it in brown ink: THE MARKET HUB, SECOND ON, LEVER'S A HARD PULL, THE WHOLE SQUARE WON'T HOLD UNDER THE WINCH, MIND THE CONTACT — and a brown smudge where a wet cup sat years back.

Sol runs her thumb along the smudge and gets the word back: CONTACT.

SOL Mind the contact. That's the word.

IFE Meaning what?

SOL Meaning the housing's live somewhere it shouldn't be. Good to know before I put my hands in.

She closes the book.

SOL Take me to the middle.

Market District, night

Ambience: water lapping against shutters, the barge's idle knocked down to nothing

A cast-iron cap stands a hand's width out of the black, a pillar rising under it. Sol lies on her belly in the bow and puts her ear to the iron.

On screen: Sol (flat on the bow, ear pressed to iron, torch laid aside), Ife (crouched by the winch, watching her)

SOL It's ringing. Like a bell with a crack in it.

SOL That cap's not bolted. It's been got at.

IFE Looters.

SOL People. There's a whole dark city out there and copper buys things.

Sol lifts the cap. It comes up with the housing riding with it, loosened, half out, up wrong. The cast body has a gouged hollow where a bolt and its iron have gone. A copper lug hangs out like a tongue, burnt black at the tip.

On screen: Sol (kneeling over the housing, torch between her knees), Ife (crouched opposite, frowning)

IFE Tell me what I'm looking at.

SOL Somebody had it half out with a wrench. Had that lug off and back on wrong. It heated and it burnt, the water got it, and the fault tripped it.

Sol draws her finger above the gouge without touching it.

SOL And they took the copper. A storm doesn't cut copper. That's a hacksaw, and it went out of this square this evening with somebody in a boat.

IFE So we haven't just got a broken hub. We've got a hub half pulled apart by a thief.

SOL By somebody in a bad night. There's copper at this hour in four machines under the fishmonger's awning. Come on.

Market District, night

Ambience: a thin trickle of water inside a metal cabinet

The four vending machines stand in a row under the fishmonger's awning, half-submerged. The fishmonger's little lamps are dark. Sol works the first lock with a bar, and it comes open in a minute.

On screen: Sol (elbow-deep in a coin box, torch in her teeth), Ife (holding the torch steady, watching the black water along the shopfronts)

SOL (around the torch) A lock built to keep out a boy with a coin isn't a lock built to keep out an electrician with a bar.

The coin box holds wet vouchers. The little copper chute comes out in her hand with three feet of harness to it.

SOL Dead as a spoon. Second machine's already bare. Third one.

The third machine gives up a chute, two brackets and a strip of busbar long as her forearm.

SOL That'll do. That'll do nicely.

She reaches into the fourth machine and her hand stops. A man's hands come up slow and easy out of the dark inside it. He is old and wet through, holding a stub of a wrench.

On screen: The old man in the vending machine (bald pink scalp, soaked cardigan, hands raised, wrench in one fist), Sol (bar lowered, torch off his eyes)

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Don't put that light in my eyes.

SOL Sorry. I'm not here for you.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Same as you, by the look. The market's been stripped since it went dark. I'm not stripping it. I've a lamp up in a window and a line to a woman across the way and a daughter in the market hall up to her knees.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE I'm looking for a length to make a hook and pull a shutter open.

SOL Take what you need. Leave me the coin chutes.

He looks at the copper in her hand and the wet housing with the gouge in its side, and puts it together in one breath.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Market hub. You're the ones putting the hub back. Then hear this.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE There's a wire under the fishmonger's front, down the gutter, come off the awning when the feeder let go. You can't see it. The water's black there and it's got a slick so it lies still.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE I've been telling every boat to keep off the gutter.

On screen: Sol (gone still, small), Ife (staring at the black strip of water along the shopfront)

Sol gets down into the shallows with the tester and puts the lead in the water a yard from the wall. The needle kicks. She steps back and puts it in again, and the needle holds high and steady.

SOL (clipped) Live. It's live in the gutter and it's been live a while. The gutter runs the whole front of the square.

SOL You've been pulling that shutter with a hook. Have you been in that gutter to do it?

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Only my arm.

SOL Then your arm got lucky and I'm glad. Keep it out now, and the hook too.

SOL Tell the woman across the way. Tell the hall. The gutter bites, the hub's being put back, keep to the high stone.

The old man nods once, straight down, like a man agreeing to a thing he means to hold to.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Give me your line and I'll get my daughter and shout it from the hall door.

Sol has no line for him. Ife has a line and a winch and a temper about both. Sol gives him the old stiff coil out of the bag.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE That's a poor line.

SOL It's the line we've got. Tie it right.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE Tie it right, she says.

He goes into the shallows with the copper and the wrench held up over the water, and stops without turning.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE The lamp in my window. When you've got the hub back, put your boat in the light and I'll know it's done and I'll shout it where it'll be heard.

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE That's what a lamp is for. It's for the next one coming.

On screen: Sol (watching him go, mouth tight), Ife (hands going to the winch before she has decided anything)

He goes into the shadow with his arm up out of the gutter. Sol climbs back aboard with her hands going a pint a minute.

IFE A man with a skiff and a remote has been cutting feeds. Ozzy said it two hours back and I thought it was a story for the radio.

SOL It wasn't a story. That lug was cut, not burnt off. And this housing didn't loosen itself.

IFE Then when we've got this hub back, that man on the water will know we've seen him. You want to mark it.

SOL I want it marked.

Market District, night

Ambience: the turnbuckle creaking, water slapping the hull

The copper lies on the deck, and the deck has become a shop: a dead housing, a busbar, a boat hook, two of them kneeling either side with the torch between their knees.

On screen: Sol (kneeling, hands black with grease), Ife (opposite, holding the busbar flat)

SOL The bolt's gone and there's no thread. So I'm not putting a bolt in it. I'm going to strap it.

SOL Busbar across the burnt iron and down over the tongue, bound off clean and tight. If it's loose it heats, and if it heats it goes, and we lose the square and the road up to the hospital with it.

IFE How do we make it tight?

SOL Not the winch. That'd have the strap off and the barge over the top of us.

Sol is up for the boat hook and the turnbuckle off the third machine.

SOL The turnbuckle and the pole for a lever and me.

She sets her palm flat on the cold iron of the dead side and leaves it there until she is sure of the cold.

SOL Power's off this side. Now the bind.

The turnbuckle goes round the busbar and round the tongue. She gets the boat hook in as a lever and pulls, and the busbar lays flat against the burnt iron and the tongue comes down under it. She turns it a half more, and the pole bows, and she feels it in her wrists. She stops when her wrists say stop.

SOL That's the whole of my testing, a wrist.

She puts the tester on the busbar and the tongue. The needle goes over and sits firm and does not shiver.

SOL Continuity. It's rough work and Dell would have done it neater. A strap and a turnbuckle, and it'll run warm. But it's tight.

IFE Then the lever's down in the black.

SOL And the cap's been got at and the pillar's loosened. I go over the side.

On screen: Ife (up on the gunwale, rope in hand), Sol (at the rail, breaths deep)

IFE You go under and I hold the line and watch the gutter. Nothing else.

SOL Nothing else.

She goes over the side.

Picture: Sol, deep under the black water of the market square, one hand finding the twist in the loosened iron pillar and following it down like a stair rail, a faint warm glow coming off the bound housing beside her.

The market's water is a moving square with a river pushing through it from three sides, nothing like the still roofed water of the works. She goes down the pillar with her eyes shut, and the cap rocks under her hand and knocks against her ear. The lever is up, because the fault tripped it, and that is a small good thing.

She pulls. It comes down an inch and jams, and the square rocks the pillar and the pillar rocks the lever.

Her chest is going. She sets her feet against the gouged body and gets both hands to the lever and hangs her whole weight off it, and the lever goes down and does not go home.

The stutter comes up all around her, high and wrong. The strap. She has switched it on inside the vault and the strap is running hot where she bound it, and the heat is coming through the iron into the water, and she has her hands on the thing that is heating it.

She gets her hand off and finds the turnbuckle, and it is warm, and she turns it. A quarter and it turns easy. A quarter more and it fights her. She turns it anyway, one last notch.

The stutter in the water changes and goes low and even and quiet, and the thing in her chest lets go.

She comes up under the barge's stern and gets her hand to the boat hook.

On screen: Sol (hauled over the gunwale, a heap of wet), Ife (pulling her in, looking past her)

The lamp over the fishmonger's is still burning. Down the row the fishmonger's little lamps come on, one, two. Then the market hall's own high windows stand up out of the dark, lit and yellow and full of people.

Sound: several voices shouting from the hall door, echoing across the water

On screen: The old man in the vending machine (at the hall door, arm up, a woman beside him), Sol (sitting against the dead housing), Ife (staring at the lit hall)

THE OLD MAN IN THE VENDING MACHINE (thin across the water) Hub's in! Lamps are on in the hall! Keep off the gutter!

The shout goes up and down the high stone, not one voice now but several.

IFE Will you look at that.

IFE (voice cracking) I said it was not my business, saving other people's crates of an evening. Will you look at that.

SOL Market hub. Second one's done.

Market District, night

Ambience: the barge's engine running rough, water moving faster against the hull

The boat sits in the old man's lamp light a moment, the whole square yellow around it. Sol gets the notebook out to put the hub down.

On screen: Sol (notebook open on her knee, pencil in hand), Ife (at the wheel, listening to the radio)

SOL I went down on a guess and a wrist, and the guess held.

She writes in the margin, her hands shaking now that it is allowed, and she makes the letters anyway.

Close-up: A notebook margin in a square schoolgirl hand: market hub reset, strap and turnbuckle, ran warm but tight. Under it, smaller: read the contact word before the hands go in, not after.

END INSERT

OZZY —write it on your stairs. The surge has not begun to fall.

On screen: Sol (pencil still), Ife (head coming round)

OZZY It is rising faster than the tide table said. The hundred-year mark the engineers painted on the low walls is not the mark any more. The water is over it.

OZZY The barrier holds its line, but the river above it is still coming down on us. The second high water will be worse than the first. If you are in the low streets and you are dry, get up two floors and take your neighbours' old people up with you.

OZZY And I will say the thing I have been saving. There are four breakers in this city that must be put back by hand. One is in the waterworks, which is lit and pumping this hour. The next is in the market, and I can see the market hall's windows from where I sit. Two are done and two are left.

OZZY Whoever is out there doing this, hear the water line. The hospital district's line has held through the first high water. It will not hold through the second at the rate we are losing time. So finish it before the second high tide. I do not know if you can hear me. I am told you can. Finish it.

The voice goes on to the small news, the streets and the names. Sol sits with the pencil in her hand and writes nothing for a while.

IFE The second high tide.

SOL Two breakers left, a barge that runs hot, a line gone to a roof, and the whole city rising. Quick won't do now.

SOL We've got to be quick and right, and the right one's the one that scares me.

IFE The one off the map.

SOL Dell said it was off his map and he was the reason. He made me promise to ask him on the air so Ozzy heard.

Sol looks at the little wet radio on the gunwale.

SOL A man who draws like that doesn't leave a breaker out because he forgot it. He leaves it out because he couldn't draw it where anyone could read it.

IFE There's one page in this book he wrote and then put a wet cup down on. He was sorry for that word before either of us was born.

She gives the notebook back.

IFE Ask your question from the depot. I want to hear the answer with the terms in it.

Sol takes the throttle and Ife the pole.

On screen: Sol (throttle forward, jaw set), Ife (pole in the water, reading the dark ahead)

The barge goes out of the market under the little lamps coming on down the row. The old man at the hall door waves with his arm up, and the lamp over the fishmonger's burns behind them and is still burning when they turn the corner into the dark.

The water ahead stands higher on the walls than it did an hour ago, and moves past the hull faster than it has any right to, coming down from the river.
