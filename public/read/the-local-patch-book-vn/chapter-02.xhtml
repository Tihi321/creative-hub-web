Patch Faithful

Concourse, Tuesday, morning

Ambience: the murmur of a crowded promenade, footsteps on deck plates

The great curved promenade is three storeys of shop fronts and benches. Overhead, far across the cylinder, the hanging gardens of the other side hang upside down in the morning light.

Every display along the concourse shows the same thing: a column of primes and a curve of decay counts, shrinking like a fuse.

On screen: Aris (rumpled coat, hair unwashed, ration tray in his hands, startled)

He stands in the galley line with a tray of reconstituted protein. People around him are not looking at their food. They are looking at the screens.

ARIS (to himself) Four hours of sleep. Four hours. What happened in four hours?

His slate buzzes in his pocket. Then again. Then it does not stop.

He pulls it out. The physics office log entry he filed last night sits at the top of the ship's feed, with a number beside it.

ARIS Eleven thousand forwards.

ARIS (pale) The physics feed. Every terminal that ever subscribed gets a copy. Nobody has unsubscribed from the physics feed in forty years.

ARIS And the board post on top of it. Of course.

Two places ahead of him, a woman in a grey work smock speaks to no one in particular.

WOMAN IN LINE (hushed) They're counting down.

ARIS (startled) Who is?

WOMAN IN LINE Whoever wrote us. Haven't you seen it? It's on every wall.

She points at the screen. Aris's own name sits at the bottom of the post, small and white.

ARIS (quietly) I've seen it.

WOMAN IN LINE My mother always said the numbers were set by a hand. The Programmer's hand. Nobody believed her.

WOMAN IN LINE (bright-eyed) Now there's proof. With error bars.

ARIS Confidence intervals.

WOMAN IN LINE What?

ARIS Nothing. They're called confidence intervals.

On the far wall someone has already painted a symbol: a square bracket, open, and a column of primes inside it. The paint is still wet.

WOMAN IN LINE The Faithful are going to the Core. All of them. You should come.

She leaves her tray on the counter and walks away. Three people behind her leave theirs too.

On screen: Aris (alone in the moving crowd, tray forgotten, staring at his own name on the wall)

ARIS The Patch Faithful.

ARIS A few hundred people who pray to the constants on Ring 5. A nuisance to the Command Node. A comfort to each other.

ARIS (half proud, half afraid) Twenty years they've had their prayers. Now they have my confidence intervals.

AI Core, Tuesday, afternoon

Ambience: a low sustained murmur of many voices, cooling pumps behind steel

The tall steel bulkhead of the AI Core rises over the plaza, its round blue-white eye unblinking. Behind it, three centuries of rules: rations, air, births, disputes. Not a mind. A rulebook. The nearest thing the Keres has to a throne.

Banners in hand-painted letters hang from the railings. Candles line the floor in uneven rows.

Three thousand people fill the plaza and spill back up the concourse. They are not shouting. They are swaying, and humming, and waiting.

Picture: The Patch Faithful filling the plaza before the AI Core, thousands of faces lit by candles and the steel eye above them, a banner of prime numbers stretched across the crowd.

A banner across the front reads, in red paint: GIVE THE SHIP TO THE ONE WHO WROTE IT.

Voices rise in the crowd, a chant soft as breathing.

VOICE IN THE CROWD Two. Three. Five. Seven.

VOICES IN THE CROWD Eleven. Thirteen. Seventeen. Nineteen.

VOICE IN THE CROWD Open the Core. Let the Programmer speak through it.

VOICES IN THE CROWD Two. Three. Five. Seven.

Command Node, Tuesday, afternoon

Ambience: soft fans, the pulse of status lights

The schematic of the Keres glows on the wall. The forward spindle, where the AI Core sits, has gone from green to a deep amber. Cold tea stands untouched on the long table.

On screen: Kaelen (navy jacket, earpiece in, arms folded, watching the crowd feed)

KAELEN Listen to them.

WATCH OFFICER (off) I am listening, Chief. Three thousand at the Core doors and more coming down the spindle.

KAELEN Listen to the band. Low. Broad. That's prayer, not shouting.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Cortisol's up around the Core.

KAELEN Up. Not spiking. They aren't angry.

He leans closer to the feed. In the candlelight, the faces are wet-eyed and bright.

KAELEN (slow) They're hopeful. That's worse.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Worse?

KAELEN Anger burns out. Fear burns out. Hope doesn't deplete. Hope compounds.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Standard containment says a visible patrol at the Core doors. Barriers at the spindle.

KAELEN No patrol.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Chief, if they breach the Core—

KAELEN They won't. They aren't here to break anything.

He uncrosses his arms and pulls the environmental map across the schematic.

KAELEN Somebody handed them a countdown. Now they need something to do with their hands. You don't answer that with sticks.

KAELEN You answer it with chemistry. And light.

On screen: Kaelen (both hands on the map, moving markers, eyes calm)

KAELEN Oxytocin analog into the Core concourse. The in-group blend. Make them feel like a congregation, warm to each other.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Warm to each other is how mobs start.

KAELEN Warm to each other is how families start. Mobs start cold.

KAELEN Lights down. Shift the spectrum to four-eighty nanometres. Hard blue.

WATCH OFFICER (off) The band they use in elder care.

KAELEN The band that slows the agitated and deepens their sleep. Decades of our own data say it works.

KAELEN And open the agricultural vents. I want the hydroponics bays up that concourse. Grass. Green. Ordinary.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Ordinary?

KAELEN Nobody storms a door smelling cut grass. They think of picnics.

The watch officer makes the changes. On the feed, the candlelit plaza turns blue.

On screen: Kaelen (lit blue from the feed, still)

The crowd does not leave. But it softens. The chant slows. Voices drop to a hum, low and steady, three thousand people swaying together.

At the front, a man who was pressing his palms to the steel bulkhead lets his hands fall and sits down on the deck.

KAELEN (softly) There. Inflammation down.

WATCH OFFICER (off) No one at the doors, Chief.

KAELEN Hours. Not days.

He checks the chem-bank reservoir. His jaw tightens.

KAELEN Eleven percent gone in one watch. And I haven't touched the lavender.

He writes the number by hand on the table's surface, in plain digits, under yesterday's four.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Chief. Reactor office. The neutron multiplication factor is falling.

KAELEN I know. I read the same telemetry you do.

KAELEN Call Vera Lin to the Command Node. Tell her it isn't a drill and it isn't a paper.

KAELEN (low) Tell her the fire's in the walls this time.

Reactor Ring, Tuesday, evening

Ambience: the deep hum of the reactor through the shielding

The curving corridor rises out of sight in both directions. Amber warning stripes run along the grey shielding walls. Through the narrow viewport, the reactor glows a deep, patient orange.

Vera has dragged a lab stool here and surrounded herself with screens: four of reactor telemetry, one of her own enzyme models. Three empty stimulant wrappers lie by her feet.

On screen: Vera (eyes red, hands trembling around a stylus, stimulant patch on her wrist, jacket on the floor)

VERA (to the screens) Three watches awake. Hands steady. Mostly.

VERA You two are telling me the same story from opposite ends.

She drags the enzyme curve over the reactor curve. They lie on top of each other almost perfectly.

VERA Same slope. Same week.

Close-up: The reactor's long telemetry window

The orange line reads: NEUTRON MULTIPLICATION (BERYLLIUM): -0.0040% AND FALLING. TRITIUM BREEDING RATIO: 1.0812, TREND DOWN.

VERA The beryllium's supposed to slow the neutrons and multiply them. And it's losing its appetite.

VERA (dry) Join the club.

VERA The reactor is just a very big, very hot piece of particle physics. If the forces move, it moves with them.

Her slate chimes. Kaelen's summons, flagged red. She glances at it and turns it face down.

VERA In a minute, Chief.

She opens Aris's prime analysis on the fifth screen. She runs it through her own checker. The checker thinks. Then it agrees with him.

VERA (very quietly) Oh, that's not fair.

She watches the drift intervals tighten into a curve that looks, for one moment, like something counting.

On screen: Vera (still, lips parted, the curve reflected in her eyes)

For a moment she feels the pull of his certainty, like a current around her ankles.

VERA Counting down to what?

She looks at the orange numbers. Then at her enzyme plates on the other screen. The pull lets go.

VERA I don't care. I really, honestly, don't care.

VERA The universe changed its mind. Fine. How do we bend with it?

She pulls up the Keres firmware tree. Thousands of branches unfold on the screen, each a subsystem that can be patched from the Command Node.

VERA The ship already bends. Tritium ratios, enzyme models, air mix. All patched from one room, pushed out to every node at once.

VERA The local patch. Oldest trick on the Keres.

VERA Same thing a cell does. Catch the wrong letter in the DNA, rewrite it before the cell divides.

She taps the trunk of the tree.

VERA But every patch so far had a person writing it. Somebody had to notice the change first.

VERA (slowly) What if the patch wrote itself?

Picture: Vera on her stool in the Reactor Ring, lit orange by the reactor viewport, a branching tree of glowing code spread across five screens around her.

She begins to type. Fast, then faster.

VERA (narrating as she codes) Not a control system that holds the reactor to fixed constants. One that reads the constants as inputs.

VERA Measure the cross-sections. Measure the enzyme rates. Measure the forces in the folds.

VERA Then rewrite the logic gates to match. Gates that are data. Gates that move when the world moves.

VERA (half laughing) Software with a genome. Mutate or die.

Three hundred lines scroll up the screen. Her slate buzzes again under her hand.

VERA (not looking) Tell the Command Node I'm busy. Tell them I'm rewriting the physics we're standing on.

VERA I'll be there when it holds.

The multiplication factor on the telemetry window drops another ten-thousandth of a percent. She writes the next line.

She types the line after that. Her hand jerks. The stylus skids.

Sound: a harsh error tone from the test build

A red flag blooms on the screen. A gate has flipped the wrong way under a cross-section she already fed it.

On screen: Vera (frozen, staring at her shaking hand)

VERA (quiet) That's the stimulant. Four hours past where it holds.

VERA Building the one thing on this ship that can't afford a wrong gate. With these hands.

She peels the patch off her wrist and drops it on the floor. She pours a cup of tea from a flask. She does not drink it.

VERA (slower) Again. Slowly.

She backs the line out and writes it again, one character at a time. The test runs. The flag turns green.

VERA Holds.

VERA (dry, to the reactor) Was that the only mistake, or just the first one?

The reactor hums back at her, deep and orange.

VERA Yeah. That's what I thought.

VERA Whatever it costs, we pay it here. In the dark. At whatever hour the constants pick.

She picks up her slate at last and stands.

AI Core, Tuesday, night

Ambience: a deep, slow hum of prayer, steady as an engine

The plaza is drenched in hard blue light. The candles burn lower. The steel eye of the Core shines over three thousand swaying people, quieter now, but still here.

The air smells of cut grass from the vents.

On screen: Kaelen (at the edge of the crowd, jacket open, hands empty and visible), Aris (beside him, slate clutched to his chest, nervous)

ARIS You said this would be a short conversation.

KAELEN It is. One sentence.

ARIS Which sentence?

KAELEN (calm) "It's only physics."

Aris looks at the crowd. Faces turn toward them. Someone recognises him.

VOICE IN THE CROWD (rising) That's him. That's Thorne. He found it.

The hum changes pitch. Heads turn like a field in wind.

KAELEN (low) Feel that? That's the fever. You can take it down with three words.

ARIS Or I can lie to three thousand people.

KAELEN Is it a lie?

ARIS (bitter) You don't know. Vera doesn't know. I'm the only one who's looked at it long enough.

KAELEN I know what happens to this crowd if you tell them God is counting down.

On screen: Kaelen (steady, eyes on the crowd), Aris (eyes on the steel eye of the Core, trembling)

KAELEN I'm not asking you to believe it. I'm asking you to say it. For their blood pressure.

ARIS For their blood pressure.

KAELEN Their chemistry doesn't care what's true. It cares what it hears.

ARIS (quiet, bitter) That's the difference between us, Chief. I care what's true.

KAELEN And I care whether they're alive on Sunday.

Kaelen hands him a microphone. Aris holds it for a long moment.

Sound: a soft click as the plaza speakers come alive

ARIS (into the microphone, shaky) My name is Aris Thorne. I wrote the analysis you've all read.

The crowd goes utterly still. Three thousand faces turn up to him in the blue light.

ARIS The Chief wants me to tell you it's only physics.

He looks at Kaelen. Kaelen does not move.

ARIS (voice steadying) I can't. I don't know that. And I won't say a thing I don't know.

A sound rises from the crowd, not a shout, but a long breath going in.

ARIS The gaps are prime. They're shrinking. Something is going to happen, and I don't know what.

KAELEN (barely audible) Aris.

ARIS (lowering the microphone) That's the truth, Chief. You'll have to medicate it.

On screen: Kaelen (face unreadable, one hand already at his earpiece), Aris (shaking, defiant)

KAELEN (into the earpiece, very calm) Lavender to the Core concourse. Full draw.
