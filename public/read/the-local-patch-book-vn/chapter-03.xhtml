Polymorphic Firmware

Bio-Integration Lab, Wednesday, morning

Ambience: incubator fans, and under them a slow pulse coming up through the deck

The long lab is half dark, its overhead lights dimmed to save power. The incubators glow green. Vera sits cross-legged on the floor between the benches, three screens angled around her.

Her palms are flat on the deck plating. Through it comes the beat of the cooling loops, steady for thirty years. This morning it limps.

On screen: Vera (on the floor, sleeves rolled, stained hands flat on the deck, eyes closed, listening)

VERA (to the floor) I hear you. You're limping.

VERA Thump, thump. Thump... thump. Like a heart that's missing a step.

She opens her eyes and looks at the nearest screen.

VERA Tritium breeding ratio. Yesterday one point zero eight one. This morning one point zero seven nine. And steepening.

VERA Breed more fuel than you burn. That's the only rule the plant has. And we're sliding toward one.

VERA At one we break even. Below one we start eating the fuel we have left.

She pulls the cross-section data. A line of orange numbers wobbles across the screen.

VERA Lithium-6 capture. The chance a passing neutron gets caught. Parts per million yesterday. Tens of parts per million today.

VERA (dry) The people who wrote the control logic knew this number would never move. Lucky them.

On the screen, the old control logic keeps metering the blanket to a fixed value. The ratio dips. The logic corrects. The ratio dips again.

VERA Look at it. A dog chasing its tail around the core.

Her slate chimes. A message from the reactor office: MAINTENANCE WINDOW IN 47 MINUTES.

VERA Forty-seven minutes. Fine. I've annotated whole genomes in less.

VERA (to herself) That's a lie. But it's a motivating lie.

She loads her layer into the sandbox. A copy of the reactor blooms on the middle screen in thin white lines.

VERA Test one. Here's the drift we measured. Hold the ratio.

The layer reads the drift. Its gates flicker as they rewrite themselves. The ratio flattens at one point zero seven nine and holds.

VERA Good. Boring. I love boring.

VERA Test two. A collapse. Five percent in a hundred seconds.

The cross-section line drops like a stone. On the screen, the gates rewrite themselves again and again, thirty times. The ratio dips, wobbles, and climbs back.

On screen: Vera (leaning in, lips moving as she counts, one hand gripping the stylus)

VERA (whispering) Twenty-eight. Twenty-nine. Thirty. And back.

VERA Okay. Now something the universe hasn't done yet.

She types a number she has made up. The cross-section on the screen jumps all at once, a cliff instead of a slope.

VERA A step. Everything changes in one instant. Show me what you've got.

Picture: Vera on the floor of the Bio-Integration Lab, her face lit white by a screen where hundreds of logic gates rewrite themselves in a storm of light around a calm reactor diagram.

The gates blur. Two hundred rewrites in three seconds.

The simulated reactor does not even notice.

VERA (breathing out at last) Oh, you beautiful thing.

She signs the layer as a delta patch. She checks it against the firmware manifest, the way every patch on the Keres has been checked for three centuries.

Close-up: The patch manifest on Vera's screen

It reads: DELTA PATCH VL-001 / ADAPTIVE BLANKET METERING / VERIFIED / TARGET: 214 BLANKET NODES / PUSH?

VERA Push.

She presses her palms back to the deck. Through the plating, the plant takes the change: a hitch, then a smoother beat.

VERA (eyes closed) Thump. Thump. Thump.

On the screen, the breeding ratio stops falling and stays.

VERA (into her slate) Command Node. Tell Kaelen the blanket will hold.

VERA Tell him the fire in the walls has a valve now. It opens and closes on its own.

She lowers the slate. Her smile fades.

On screen: Vera (alone on the floor, smile gone, looking at her hands)

VERA (quietly) Every fall I could invent. And none I couldn't.

Observational Core, Thursday, before dawn

Ambience: the cold hum of life support, a quarter tone flat

The chamber is a cave of cyan. Every wall is covered with plots, overlays, crossed-out models. A cup sits on the console, empty, never filled.

On screen: Aris (coat hanging off one shoulder, eyes too bright, unshaven, hands shaking slightly)

ARIS (reciting) Thirty hours awake. Everything is very clear. Every number hums against its neighbours.

ARIS It's almost nice. A fever with a filing system.

He pulls the raw scintillation counts beside the stored logs. He compares them line by line.

ARIS If it's a message, someone could be editing my logs. Sharpening it.

He checks a third time. The logs match.

ARIS (flat) No one is editing them.

ARIS That should make me feel better.

He drags a new plot to the centre wall: gap length against cycle.

ARIS First week, the gaps run the primes. Two, three, five, seven, eleven, thirteen, seventeen, nineteen. Days apart.

ARIS Then it starts again, shorter. Then again, shorter still.

The points on the wall fall in a smooth, steep curve.

ARIS (softly) Like a stone dropped from rest.

He tries to make it something else. He says each idea out loud and kills it.

ARIS A beat between two clocks. No. The clocks agree.

ARIS Heat aliasing in the counter housings. No. The housings are flat.

ARIS The dark count locking to the drift. No. I checked that on Monday.

ARIS Me. My own code. A bug.

He reads his code from the top, line by line, his lips moving.

ARIS (quiet) No.

Only the curve is left on the wall. At the bottom of it, where the gaps reach zero, a number glows.

ARIS (reading it) Fifty-nine hours.

He sits down very slowly in the worn chair.

On screen: Aris (in the chair, the empty cup in both hands, face lit by the number on the wall)

ARIS Not a message. A countdown.

ARIS I waited my whole life to be answered. And the answer comes with a deadline.

He waits to feel afraid. What comes is quieter and older.

ARIS (almost tender) You did hear me. You just don't talk. You schedule.

He opens a note to the Command Node and types fast.

ARIS (reading as he writes) Projection of drift interval collapse. Confidence intervals attached. Request full meeting before the window closes.

He reads it back twice.

ARIS (uneasy) It's a physics note.

ARIS I can't find the place where it stops being one.

Sound: the soft click of the note going out

The clock in the corner reads 04:10.

Command Node, Thursday, early morning

Ambience: soft fans, the slow pulse of status lights

The schematic of the Keres glows white on the wall. The forward spindle is still amber. On the long table, a row of cold tea cups, and the handwritten numbers of three watches: four, eleven, and more below.

On screen: Kaelen (jacket off, sleeves pushed up, standing, reading a slate, tired but straight)

WATCH OFFICER (off) Note from Dr. Thorne, Chief. Flagged for you. Four eleven.

KAELEN I'm reading it.

He reads it standing. Then he takes a ration slip from his pocket and a pencil, and does sums on the back.

KAELEN (half aloud) Reservoir at thirty-four percent. Down from fifty-six on Tuesday. The lavender at full draw did that.

KAELEN The analog goes faster than synthesis can replace it. The precursor comes from the gene banks. The gene banks have the same drift Vera's chasing.

KAELEN Everything on this ship has the same slope now. I'm starting to know it like a face.

WATCH OFFICER (off) And the Faithful?

He looks at the feed. Three thousand people in the blue light at the AI Core, humming, as they have since Tuesday night.

KAELEN Fifty-nine hours to Aris's zero, if Aris is right.

KAELEN Full dose, I can hold the Core for eleven of them. Quarter dose, about fifty-five.

WATCH OFFICER (off) And after that?

KAELEN Four hours short. After that, whatever comes at the end of the count comes to a ship with empty tanks.

He folds the slip once.

KAELEN Start a drawdown schedule. The Core concourse and Rings 3 and 7 at full dose until hour eleven.

KAELEN Then I want a written plan for which concourses go cold first. In order. Signed. With my name on it.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Chief, that means choosing—

KAELEN (calm) Which corridors get a calming agent and which get nothing. Yes. Write it.

WATCH OFFICER (off) And if someone asks why their corridor?

KAELEN Then they ask me. That's why my name is on it.

On screen: Kaelen (watching the crowd feed, jaw tight)

On the physiological feed, three thousand heart rates line up into one slow, shared rhythm.

KAELEN (quiet) Look at their hearts.

WATCH OFFICER (off) The feed calls them calm.

KAELEN The feed calls them calm. They're synchronising. Three thousand hearts finding one beat.

KAELEN The way a marching column finds the frequency of a bridge.

WATCH OFFICER (off) A patrol, Chief. Please.

KAELEN A patrol is a shock to the system. It might scatter them. It might lock them together tighter.

KAELEN I won't roll that die on three thousand people.

He moves markers on the map.

KAELEN Core concourse pressure up half a percent. Heavy and sleepy. Let them think about their bodies, not their hearts.

KAELEN And start drawing precursor from the agricultural reserve.

WATCH OFFICER (off) That breaks the food-reserve protocol.

KAELEN Then log that it broke. And that I ordered it.

KAELEN That protocol was written for a universe that held still.

KAELEN If the count is real, the worst hour is the last one. I mean to have something in the tank for it.

He looks at the feed once more. Under the calm, he sees the slope again: ground still tilting toward an edge no one can see.

KAELEN Get me Thorne and Lin. Here. Now.

Command Node, Thursday, midday

The cool white lights are up. Three chairs are pulled close around the end of the long table. Aris's curve fills the schematic wall, the reactor and the rings drawn faintly behind it.

On screen: Kaelen (seated, forearms on the table, calm), Vera (slumped in a chair, stylus behind her ear, tea in hand), Aris (standing, too wired to sit, slate glowing)

ARIS Fifty-nine hours from four ten this morning. The intervals reach zero. I've broken every other explanation.

VERA Every one you could think of.

ARIS (sharp) Every one anyone could think of. Name one I missed.

VERA (sipping) I can't. That's not the same as there not being one.

KAELEN Stop. I didn't call you here to argue about God. I called you because I have to choose something today.

They both look at him.

KAELEN (to Vera) Your layer is on two hundred and fourteen blanket nodes. How many does the ship have?

VERA About five thousand. Containment. Life support. Gene banks. Your air mix.

VERA Every one running logic that thinks the numbers never move.

KAELEN Then they all need your layer. What does it cost to put it there?

VERA (setting the tea down) Each node has to restart on the new firmware. A few seconds dark per node. Air handlers, lights, vents.

VERA Do it slowly and it takes a week. Do it before Saturday and whole concourses blink at once.

KAELEN (quiet) Including the Core concourse.

VERA Including the Core concourse.

On screen: Kaelen (very still, eyes on the crowd feed), Vera (watching him), Aris (lowering his slate)

On the feed, three thousand Faithful sway in the blue light Kaelen has spent two days building.

ARIS You'd turn their lights off. In front of the Core. With a countdown on every wall.

KAELEN For a few seconds at a time. Over and over. For two days.

KAELEN (low) That's a fever spike every hour. And I'll be paying for each one out of the tank.

VERA There's something else. One thing I can't patch at all.

KAELEN Say it.

VERA The pressure valves on the Ring 7 vapour line are mechanical. Springs and steel. No node, no firmware.

VERA If the physics jumps and the controls lag, that line spikes, and my layer can't touch it. Your people would have to.

KAELEN Then I'll have people at Ring 7 on Saturday. Not a patrol. Engineers.

ARIS (looking up) And I'll give you a fall you couldn't invent.

VERA What?

ARIS My curve. The real shape of the drift. Test your layer against what the data says, not what you imagine.

VERA (surprised) That's actually useful.

ARIS (faint smile) I'm told I'm good at my job.

On screen: Kaelen (standing, decided), Vera (sitting up, alert), Aris (sitting at last, slate on the table)

KAELEN Then here's the choice. Vera's layer on every node before zero. The concourses blink. I take the cost.

KAELEN I'll hold the Core on quarter dose between the blinks and spend the agricultural reserve on the blinks themselves.

WATCH OFFICER (off) That burns the reserve by Saturday morning, Chief.

KAELEN Then we meet zero with nothing in the tank but the light. Log it. My name.

VERA (quiet) Eleven coders and me. Five thousand nodes.

KAELEN Eleven coders and you.

She laughs once, tired. Then she nods.

VERA Before Saturday, then.

KAELEN Aris. I need it exact. When is zero?

Aris pulls the curve toward him. He runs the fit once more, his lips moving.

Sound: a soft chime as the fit converges

ARIS (very quietly) Saturday.

He looks up at them both.

ARIS Fifteen ten, ship time. Give or take four minutes.
