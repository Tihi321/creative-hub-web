Zero

Bio-Integration Lab, Thursday, night

Ambience: incubator fans, a soft beat of the cooling loops through the deck

The lab is lit only by screens. Cups of cold tea stand along the bench like a row of small grey moons. The reactor sandbox glows on the middle screen.

Down the hall, eleven borrowed coders sit at borrowed benches, pushing her layer node by node, their keys clicking like rain.

On screen: Vera (on the floor again, palms on the deck, hair falling out from behind her ears, focused)

VERA Fine. No more falls I invent. Here's the drift as it really runs.

She feeds the layer Aris's curve: the measured slope, steepening, bending toward zero.

The gates rewrite themselves to the drift. Then they do something she did not write. They run ahead of it.

VERA (frowning) What are you doing?

On the screen the layer mutates against a cliff that is not in the input yet. The display floods green.

Sound: a flat dead tone as the sandbox clock stops answering

VERA (very still) You bet on the future.

She stares at the frozen sandbox for a long moment.

VERA If the countdown's noise, you go on bracing for a fall that never comes. And one night you rewrite yourself into a shape that fits nothing.

VERA And you take the reactor with you.

VERA (quiet) A body that panics at a fever that isn't coming. That's how you get an allergy.

She pulls up the sensor banks and starts a filter.

VERA (typing) New rule. You get told what is. Not one second before.

VERA No projections fed to you as truth. Whatever happens at zero happens to you the way it happens to the ship. All at once.

She writes it. It comes out three lines longer than it should.

VERA (dry) No time for pretty. Ugly and right will do.

She runs the steepening profile again. This time the layer holds the drift as it arrives and does not run ahead. The green points settle.

On screen: Vera (leaning back against a bench, eyes closed for a second, relieved)

VERA (to the empty lab) A layer that adapts to bad data adapts to nothing.

VERA Now you only know what's true.

Observational Core, Friday, morning

Ambience: the cold hum of life support

The cyan walls are cleaner now. The old plots are gone. In their place, a field of five thousand small dots, each one a node of the ship, each one waiting.

On screen: Aris (coat off, shirt sleeves rolled, slate on the console, almost calm)

ARIS The harness. The one job left that doesn't ask what I believe.

ARIS Node by node. What the layer says its gates are doing, against what they're actually doing.

ARIS And if I can't tell the difference, it fails loudly.

He runs the harness. The dots blink, one by one, then all together. Green.

ARIS Clean.

He should stand up. He should send it to Vera. Instead he sits and looks at his own checker for a long time.

ARIS (quietly) Backwards, then. Do it backwards.

He feeds it the residuals, the gaps between the gaps. Then, after a pause, the dark count from ampoule seven.

ARIS Ampoule seven. Failed its heat calibration all week. I've been leaving you out of every fit.

ARIS (bitter) Because you were inconvenient.

The checker thinks. A sequence appears. It is not the primes.

ARIS That's tighter than mine.

He opens the maintenance log beside it. A name repeats down the page every fourteen days for eleven years.

Close-up: The ampoule rack maintenance log

It reads: SERVICED BY: K. OSEI. EVERY 14 DAYS. TIMES VARY. NOTE: COUNTERS SLOW TO SETTLE AFTER HANDLING.

ARIS (reading) Osei. Every fourteen days. At slightly different hours.

ARIS And the counters never quite settle after his hands.

He laughs once. It is not the laugh from Monday.

ARIS The ship has a rhythm. A man with a cleaning cloth. And I heard it as a voice.

He runs a third fit, a fourth. They come back loose as noise.

ARIS Two tight fits out of a hundred. And I showed the whole ship the one I wanted.

On screen: Aris (head in his hands, slate dark, alone)

He sits like that for a long time. The hum of life support goes on around him, a quarter tone flat.

Then he lifts his head, saves the second fit, and gives it a plain name.

ARIS (typing) Finding. Second periodicity. Source: ampoule maintenance cycle. As strong as the first.

ARIS Not in a subfolder. In the standing watch log.

ARIS (quiet) Where Vera will see it the minute she looks.

Command Node, Friday, nine at night

Ambience: soft fans, status lights pulsing

The countdown runs in white digits in the corner of the schematic wall: 18:10:00, and falling. Beneath it, Aris's curve. Cold tea everywhere.

On screen: Aris (standing at the wall, slate in hand, tired), Vera (in a chair, knees up, stylus in her teeth), Kaelen (at the head of the table, hands flat on it)

ARIS Eighteen hours. It hits zero tomorrow at fifteen ten.

VERA (taking the stylus out) It's a fit to a sparse series. You grew it by hunting for a period until you found one.

ARIS It's too clean to be noise. I've fitted a hundred curves. One of them—

He stops, half a breath too late.

ARIS (lower) Two of them fit like this.

VERA (quietly) Two.

She does not press. She read the watch log an hour ago. She has stood where he is standing, once, and she knows the floor there.

On screen: Vera (looking at Aris, gentle now), Aris (not meeting her eyes)

VERA The ship's in a new phase, Aris. The forces are moving. In a new phase the noise gets louder.

VERA A curve fitted to loud noise looks like a countdown because you ask it to.

VERA It's a fever. You've been reading the fever as a letter.

KAELEN Doesn't matter.

They both turn to him.

KAELEN Countdown or fever. The tank's at twenty-two percent. The blinks cost more than I priced them.

KAELEN Quarter dose carries the Core to about four hours short of zero. After that, the light and nothing else.

KAELEN I need the ship held together for the next hour. And the hour after that. That's the only question I've got time for.

Vera turns her chair to the console and feeds the layer the newest stretch of drift.

VERA Four thousand eight hundred nodes patched and verified. The last two hundred by morning.

VERA It holds the drift it's given. It'll hold the snap, if a snap comes all at once.

VERA It won't tell us what's on the other side.

ARIS Nothing will tell us that.

VERA No. That's the point.

Command Node, Saturday, afternoon

Ambience: the steady hum of life support, very loud in the quiet room

The ship is calm. On the feeds, the galley lines are short, the corridors smell green from the vents, the concourse lights are lilac. At the AI Core, a few hundred Faithful pray in shifts.

The countdown in the corner of the wall reads 00:00:10.

On screen: Kaelen (at the habitability console, earpiece in, still as a post), Vera (sitting on the deck with her palms flat on the plating), Aris (at a side console, harness running, slate gripped hard)

WATCH OFFICER (off) Ten seconds.

Nobody speaks.

The digits run down. Three. Two. One. Zero.

Nothing happens.

ARIS (whispering) Nothing.

VERA Wait.

Then it happens.

The hum drops out of the air. In the silence Aris can hear his own heart.

Picture: The Command Node plunged into white emergency light, Kaelen braced at his console, Vera flat-palmed on the shaking deck and Aris clinging to his side console as the schematic wall flashes red across the whole ship.

Sound: a hard metallic shudder through the whole hull

The deck kicks under them. The ration slip flies off Kaelen's console. Beyond the bulkhead, a rack of parts crashes down in the crawlway.

The hum comes back a quarter tone sharp, then flat, then as if it does not know what it is.

VERA (through her teeth) Fine-structure constant. It moved.

ARIS How far?

VERA A part in ten thousand. Not enough to break a bond. Enough to shift every bond on the ship at once.

VERA Every protein. Every membrane. The water in every body on board.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Reactor plasma's hitting the bottle! Containment's wavering!

Sound: a rising pressure alarm from Ring 7

WATCH OFFICER (off, shouting) Pressure spike in Ring 7! The vapour line valves are lagging! Hatch four's sealed with people on the wrong side!

KAELEN (calm) Vera.

VERA Working.

On screen: Vera (palms on the deck, eyes fixed on the wall, voice gone flat), Kaelen (one hand up at the map), Aris (watching his harness scroll)

The schematic wall shows five thousand nodes as a field of green. Six hundred go red in the first second. Eight hundred in the second.

VERA (flat) It's getting an input it never imagined.

The red spreads. Then it stops spreading.

VERA It isn't falling.

VERA (half a breath) It's mutating.

Each node reads its own drift, rewrites its own gates, passes the rewrite along the mesh. Red turns to green, patch by patch, like a wound closing.

VERA Reactor metering just relearned lithium-6. It jumped a tenth of a percent and it isn't coming back.

VERA Blanket re-metered. They're arguing the plasma back into the bottle.

KAELEN The air. Ring 7.

VERA Scrubbers relearned their kinetics in four seconds. But that vapour line is springs and steel. Your engineers have to close it.

Kaelen is already moving markers. He does not send a patrol.

KAELEN Engineers at Ring 7, close the valves by hand. Vent the hatch-four corridor into the agricultural plenum. Drop the far side two percent so we can crack the hatch without a blowout.

KAELEN And that corridor's lights at full white. Don't soften it. I want them to see the way out.

KAELEN Hatch four open. Walk them through. Slow.

The room waits. Eleven seconds. They take longer than the whole rewrite.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Through, Chief. Forty-one minor injuries. No crush. An old man with a bruised shoulder. A girl who won't let go of the rail.

KAELEN Get the rail out of her hands and get her warm.

He keeps moving markers. On the AI Core feed, the few hundred Faithful stand frozen in the white emergency light, staring up at the steel eye.

KAELEN (low) And the Core. All I have left. Lavender and blue, full draw, until the tank's dry.

WATCH OFFICER (off) That empties it.

KAELEN That's what it was for.

On the feed the white light softens into blue. The Faithful sink to their knees, not in panic, but slowly, holding each other.

VERA Hydrolase rates up a percent. Repair pathways relearning their proofreading. Three hundred nodes still renegotiating.

VERA (making herself read the numbers) It took the jump. It'll take the next one.

On screen: Aris (watching the harness, face pale), Vera (still on the deck)

ARIS The harness crashed. Nine hundred alerts in a tenth of a second.

ARIS It's restarting.

The harness comes back. Five thousand green points, each one saying what it does is what it claims.

ARIS (quiet) Clean. Your patch is honest.

He should look away from the screen. Instead he opens the drift intervals from the minutes around zero and feeds them to the checker.

A spread comes back. Not a sequence.

He feeds it the intervals from the new plateau. Another spread.

Picture: Aris alone at his side console, the column of glowing primes from Monday breaking apart into a loose cloud of scattered points on the screen before him.

ARIS (barely audible) A noise floor. Where the signal was.

ARIS The stone was the slope all along.

ARIS And there was never anyone on the other side of the cliff.

VERA (careful) Aris. Did the harness hold?

ARIS It crashed, it restarted, it held.

He takes a breath.

ARIS The countdown was the drift, steepening. I dressed it up because I needed it to be a letter.

KAELEN (not turning from his console) You built the harness.

KAELEN If you'd spent those hours in despair, the layer could be lying to itself right now and I'd never know.

KAELEN The fever put you at your console. I'll take that.

On screen: Kaelen (turning at last, calm, tired), Aris (looking up at him), Vera (between them on the floor)

KAELEN New baseline. And the physics will drift again on a schedule nobody knows. I need a standing watch on the constants.

ARIS I'll take it.

He looks at his display: a slope, rising and falling around a new mean.

ARIS I'll log it as a slope. Not a countdown.

Vera lets her hands go still on the deck. Under them, the ship breathes, a quarter tone sharp of where it lived for thirty years.

VERA (softly) New constants.

She pushes a delta patch to the sensor banks and watches it settle.

VERA New firmware.

Ambience: the hum of life support, steady again, slightly higher than before

WATCH OFFICER (off) Chief. Lights.

On the schematic wall, the dark decks start to glow again. Not all at once. One at a time.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Deck one. Deck two. Concourse coming up. Ring 3. Ring 7.

On the feed, the long curve of the concourse fills with ordinary yellow light, a ring at a time, rolling around the ship like a sunrise.

KAELEN (very quietly) There.

ARIS Deck by deck.

VERA (smiling, eyes wet) Like it's waking up.
