The Local Patch

Command Node, Sunday, early morning

Ambience: the hum of life support, steady, a quarter tone sharp

The harsh white is gone. The lilac of Kaelen's evening band lies across the floor, soft and even, and makes the consoles look like furniture in a warm room.

The schematic wall glows green from end to end. Cups of cold tea, a dropped ration slip, a rack of parts still lying where it fell.

On screen: Vera (at the table, hands folded, hair loose, too tired to move), Kaelen (standing at the wall, jacket back on), Aris (at his side console, hands on the keys, quiet)

VERA (reading) Breeding ratio, one point zero eight. Holding.

VERA Lithium-6 capture at a value nobody on the Keres ever wrote down. And the layer meters to it like it's always been there.

VERA (very softly) The reactor's breathing.

She watches the number settle and does not say what she feels.

KAELEN Ring report.

He pulls it onto the wall. Ring 3, cortisol at a low plateau, loiter time back to normal. Ring 7, falling, not yet fallen.

KAELEN No breaches. Chem-bank at nineteen percent. Not draining.

On the AI Core feed, the Faithful have broken into ordinary noise: people arguing, gossiping, asking where breakfast is.

KAELEN (a ghost of a smile) Listen to that. Three thousand people remembering they have stomachs.

VERA Is that a medical term?

KAELEN It's the best one I know.

He turns to the room.

KAELEN We have a new baseline. The physics has stopped moving. I don't know on what schedule it moves next.

KAELEN I intend to find out.

On screen: Aris (still at his console, the slope on his screen, not looking up)

On Aris's screen there is a slope: the drift, risen and fallen around a new mean. Six hours of it. Not a single prime.

ARIS No one spoke.

They both look at him.

ARIS I want that in the standing watch log, Kaelen. In plain words.

ARIS So that when the next scholar finds primes in the noise, they find the correction underneath.

KAELEN Write it.

Aris writes it. The date. His name.

Close-up: The standing watch log entry

It reads: NO ONE SPOKE. THE PRIMES WERE NOISE WITH A SHAPE I WANTED. A SECOND RHYTHM FIT AS WELL. — A. THORNE

ARIS (breathing out) There.

VERA How does it feel?

ARIS (thinking) Like putting down something I've been carrying since I was a boy.

VERA (dry, kind) Good. Now sleep, before you find a pattern in the ceiling tiles.

ARIS There is one. Every fourth tile is—

VERA Aris.

ARIS (a real smile) Going.

Observational Core, Thursday, before dawn a week later

Ambience: the cold hum of life support

The chamber is dark and quiet. One plot glows on the black glass: the slope, rising and falling around its new mean. A cot has been dragged in beside the console.

Sound: a soft chime, then a second, sharper alarm

On screen: Aris (barefoot, hair flattened from the cot, blinking, half awake)

ARIS (mumbling) Harness. Something changed and it can't tell me what.

He is at the console in ninety seconds, bare feet on the cold deck. He reads.

ARIS Cross-sections wobbled. A part in a hundred thousand. Gone in under two seconds.

ARIS The layer caught it. Rewrote its gates. Settled.

ARIS And the harness flagged it because the rewrite didn't match its prediction. That's all.

ARIS (quietly) Nothing failed.

He sits and looks at the wobble for a while. His hand rests near the old brute-force checker.

ARIS Once upon a time, this was a knock on the door.

ARIS I'd have run the long checker. Found Osei's fourteen days. Called it a hand reaching through the physics.

He does not run the checker. He logs the wobble as a wobble, attaches the layer's rewrite, and sets the watch for the next shift.

He is almost back to his cot before he realises.

ARIS (surprised) I wasn't even tempted.

Sound: the door hissing open

On screen: Aris (turning, startled), Sera (in the doorway, grey Faithful clothes, a folded ration slip in her hands, unsure)

A woman stands in the doorway as if she has come a long way and is not sure of the last step.

SERA Doctor Thorne?

ARIS Yes.

SERA I'm Sera. I kept the primes.

SERA (quiet) I kept them in my bunk and prayed to them. I told my daughter they were the Programmer's countdown.

She unfolds the slip. Lines of careful handwriting.

SERA They've started again. Last night, at the end of the watch. The drift came back, and it spaced itself the way it did before.

SERA I came before the Faithful hear it. Because I don't know what I'd do if they heard it from someone else.

Aris takes the slip. On it are the intervals from the wobble he has just logged, and beneath them, worked out by hand, a spacing that looks exactly like the old countdown.

ARIS (very quietly) Of course it does.

ARIS Any slope you feed it comes back as a countdown. It did for me too.

On screen: Aris (holding the slip, gentle), Sera (hands clasped, waiting)

ARIS I know how this feels, Sera. I built the whole thing.

ARIS Here's the truth, and I want you to hear it plain before anyone gives you a version you like better.

ARIS The primes had a rival that fit just as well. A man named Osei, cleaning the counters every fourteen days.

ARIS There's no one on the other side of the cliff.

ARIS Last night the drift spaced itself the same way because it's the same drift. The shape isn't a letter. It never was.

Sera looks at the slip in his hand for a long moment.

SERA And the ones who prayed?

ARIS You gave them something to hold in a bad hour. You held it too.

ARIS That isn't a crime, and it isn't a lie. As long as you put it down when you know better.

ARIS You put it down before it hurt anyone. That's the watch, Sera. That is exactly the watch.

He steps back from the console.

ARIS The slope's on the main display. It reads out plainly. Would you like to see how the night shift is done?

Sera folds the ration slip. She does not answer at once.

Then she sits down in the second chair.

Bio-Integration Lab, Saturday, night a week later

Ambience: incubator fans, the soft steady pulse of the cooling loops

The lab lights are back to warm white. Fresh plants green the hydroponic trays. On the wall screen, the DNA repair statistics are calm, long flat lines.

On screen: Vera (at the bench, fresh tunic, stains still on her hands, stylus behind her ear, rested at last)

VERA A plateau. Harder than a fall.

VERA A fall is a cliff. A cliff is easy. A plateau is a slow lie the gates have to be talked out of every hour.

She patches. Re-patches. Then she opens the layer's header.

VERA (typing) Header. Version one point nine. Name.

She pauses. Then she types it.

Close-up: The header of Vera's firmware layer

It reads: THE LOCAL PATCH. SMALL, SIGNED, PUSHED TO THE NODE THAT NEEDS IT. THE KERES IS THREE CENTURIES OF THESE. NOTHING IN THIS LAYER IS FINAL.

VERA That's what the ship always called a firmware fix. A local patch.

VERA (dry) Turns out we're made of them.

She pulls up the gene banks. A fault light blinks amber.

VERA Oh, hello.

A repair cascade is running ahead of its input, trying to fit a proofreading rate to a plateau that is not there yet.

VERA (leaning in) You're betting on the future again.

On the screen, the filter she wrote before zero catches it, three lines longer than it should be. The gates refuse the bet. They hold at what is.

The cascade runs down and stops. The green points settle.

On screen: Vera (sitting back, a long breath out, a small smile)

VERA (to the empty lab) You don't get to know the future.

VERA You get to know the next second. That's enough.

She picks up a cup of tea. For once, it is hot. She drinks it.

Concourse, Sunday, evening a week later

Ambience: the murmur of an ordinary evening crowd

The long curved promenade glows warm yellow. Shops are open. Overhead, across the cylinder, the hanging gardens are lit gold. A small crowd has gathered around a bench by the central display, not praying, just listening.

On screen: Kaelen (at the back of the crowd, hands in his jacket pockets, at ease), Sera (on the bench, slate in hand, reading aloud)

SERA (reading) End of shift. The slope held within four parts in a million. One wobble at oh three seventeen on Thursday, caught and settled.

SERA Gene banks steady. Reactor steady. Next reading at the end of the night shift.

She looks up. A few people nod. A child yawns. Someone asks a question about the reactor, and she answers it plainly.

KAELEN (to himself) A population that can't see the numbers invents them.

WATCH OFFICER (off, in his earpiece) She's good, Chief.

KAELEN She's very good.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Same time tomorrow?

KAELEN Every shift. Read it as arithmetic. Not a sermon and not a prophecy.

KAELEN When the drift comes back hot, they'll already know the language for it.

On screen: Kaelen (watching the crowd thin and drift home, calm)

The crowd breaks up into ordinary evening noise. Somebody laughs. A choir is rehearsing two shop fronts down.

KAELEN I moved the old patrol protocols to the archive today.

WATCH OFFICER (off) With the fixed-constant firmware.

KAELEN With a note saying I ordered it, and why.

KAELEN I wrote no protocol for the Faithful. I punished no one.

WATCH OFFICER (off) Some people say you should have.

KAELEN A fever that isn't punished breaks on its own. A fever that's punished learns to hide.

He watches Sera fold her slate away and walk home with her daughter.

WATCH OFFICER (off) You sound pleased, Chief.

KAELEN I'm not pleased. Pleased means the problem's over.

KAELEN It's never over. But the ship's holding. And holding is enough.

Observational Core, Sunday, late night a week later

Ambience: the hum of life support, a quarter tone sharp and steady

The cold round chamber at the ship's spine. The slope glows on the black glass. Dust turns slowly in the projector beams, as it always has.

On screen: Aris (shaved, coat buttoned, slate set down on the console, at peace)

Aris logs the drift. Then he sets his slate down and does not pick it up again.

ARIS (listening) A quarter tone sharp of the one I grew up with.

ARIS Steady. Not counting down to anything.

ARIS (softly) Just the Keres. Breathing.

Picture: The Arkship Keres seen from outside, a long slow cylinder turning against the stars, every ring lit warm yellow, carrying its people on toward port.

ARIS All of us, carried through a universe that changes its mind.

ARIS And something inside it learned to bend.

Sound: the door hissing open

On screen: Aris (turning in the chair), Sera (in the doorway, slate in hand, ready for her shift)

SERA Evening, Doctor.

ARIS Evening. It's all yours.

He hands her the watch. She sits down in the second chair and opens the log.

SERA (reading aloud, plainly) Night shift. Week two of the new baseline. The slope held. No wobble since Thursday.

Her voice does not rise or fall with the numbers. It just reads them.

Aris listens, and for the first time in years, he closes his eyes in this room and lets himself rest.
