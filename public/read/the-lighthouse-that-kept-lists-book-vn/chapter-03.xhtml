Boats That Did Not Come Back

Service room, a minute past ten

Ambience: a teleprinter clacking slowly under a moaning wind

The machine types at the speed of a careful old man, sixty words a minute if that. Each line climbs out of the platen and waits to be read.

The keys strike one at a time. Between the letters there is time to breathe.

On screen: Mara (standing at the desk, bent close to the paper, crate still on its side behind her)

MARA (reading as it comes) Galeb. Nineteen sixty-one.

MARA Galeb. A gull. Somebody's gull.

MARA Dva Brata. Seventy-four.

MARA Two brothers.

The carriage slams back. She flinches every time, and every time she is annoyed with herself for flinching.

MARA Sveti Nikola. Eighty-three.

MARA Ljubica. Nobody knows when.

MARA (quiet) Nobody knows when.

The carriage returns. One more line comes up, and she stops reading aloud.

Close-up: the teleprinter list of lost boats, the last line set a little apart

GALEB, 1961. DVA BRATA, 1974. SV. NIKOLA, 1983. LJUBICA, NOBODY KNOWS WHEN. And last, a little apart: MARICA (WENT TO ITALY).

The carriage returns. The paper feeds up twice.

Ambience: wind moaning round a stone tower, a faint electrical hum

The green lamp dies, then the red. The room holds only the bora and her breathing.

MARA (very quiet) Marica.

She rights the crate and sits down on it, slowly, with the paper across her knees.

MARA I was eight.

MARA You carved her out of a bit of pine. This long.

She measures it against her forearm without thinking, from the wrist to the elbow.

MARA Blue. Royal blue, you said, because a navy needs a proper blue.

MARA A sail cut out of a tea towel. The one with the lemons on it. Baka was furious.

MARA MARICA on the stern in white paint.

MARA (almost a smile) What you called me when nobody was listening.

MARA I sailed her off the causeway on a string. Off the third step. You said the third step was the harbour.

MARA The bora took the string.

She looks at her own hand, as if the string might still be in it.

MARA I cried until I was sick on your shoes.

The beam goes round overhead. She watches the wall where it would cross, if there were a window.

MARA You crouched down. Hands on my shoulders. Very serious. The serious face, the one for relays.

MARA You said she'd gone to Italy, and she'd send a postcard.

MARA Three weeks later a postcard came. Ancona. A church. An Italian stamp.

MARA (reciting) "Arrived safely. Food is all right. Marica."

MARA I kept it in my pencil case until the corners came off.

She folds the paper once along its length, then flattens it again on her knee.

MARA I never told anybody. Not Mum. Not the kids at school in Zagreb, because they'd have laughed. Not anybody.

MARA And neither did you. I'm almost sure.

MARA (to the teleprinter, unsteady) So who told you?

The teleprinter says nothing, as is its right.

MARA (trying for brisk) Signal to noise. Right. The signal-to-noise ratio of...

She has nothing after it.

She pulls the raincoat tighter and stays on the crate with the paper on her knees. Overhead, the beam keeps going round.

Service room, much later

The bulb is still on. The wind has not let up. The thermos is empty and lying on its side.

On screen: Mara (on the crate, back against the curved wall, eyes half closed, paper on her lap)

MARA (drowsy) Could be a recorder.

MARA Timer and a tape. Anybody could set a timer.

MARA Anybody who knew about the boat.

MARA Which is nobody.

Her eyes close. They open again.

MARA Five bits a letter. Thirty-two possible codes. Letters shift, figures shift...

MARA (mumbling) Baudot. Murray. He'd want me to say Murray.

Her head tips back against the whitewash. The beam goes round above the tower. The sea hits the rock and the floor hums, and the hum is almost a lullaby.

She sleeps sitting up, in the raincoat, with her hand flat on the list.

Service room, seven in the morning

Grey light comes in round the edges of the steel door. The bulb is still on and looks foolish in the daylight.

Sound: a steel door grinding open and the wind rushing in

On screen: Mara (asleep sitting up on the crate, head against the wall, paper in her lap), Dino (in the doorway, clipboard in a plastic bag)

Dino stands in the doorway and clears his throat. Then he clears it again, more officially.

DINO Ahem.

MARA (jerking awake) I'm awake. I'm... what time is it?

DINO Seven. For the inventory.

He looks at her, at the crate, at the empty thermos.

DINO You stayed the night in a lighthouse.

MARA (rubbing her neck) I stayed the night in a lighthouse.

DINO That is not on any form I have.

MARA I'm sure you could find one.

DINO I will look.

He holds out his hand. There is no point pretending she has not been reading it all night, so she gives him the paper.

Picture: Dino reading the strip of teleprinter paper by the bare bulb in the service room, Mara watching from the crate

On screen: Mara (stiff, tired, watching his face), Dino (holding the paper at arm's length, reading)

He reads standing. She watches him read the first line, and the second.

She watches him reach the third line and stop.

His thumb moves over the paper, the way you would smooth a child's hair.

DINO (quiet) Sveti Nikola.

MARA Did you know her?

DINO She was my father's.

He holds the paper further from his face, the way Ivka holds a phone. Dino has never needed glasses.

DINO (plainly) Twelfth of December. Bora.

DINO He went out for the nets, because the nets cost more than the boat.

DINO They found the nets.

He folds the strip along its length, carefully. Then he unfolds it, because folding it was a mistake.

DINO I was fourteen. I was on the riva with my mother. All night. There was a lamp on the boat, and then there was not.

DINO The inquiry form was four pages. I have read it many times.

DINO It has a box for the cause. Somebody wrote "weather" in it.

The wind leans on the door.

MARA Dino. I'm sorry.

DINO It is old news, Mara.

He writes something on the clipboard. She is nearly certain it is nothing, just a line to give the pen something to do.

DINO (formal again, slightly hoarse) For the record. I am now assisting this investigation.

MARA Is there an investigation?

DINO There is now.

He looks at the last line.

DINO Who is Marica?

MARA A boat.

DINO Which went to Italy.

MARA It sent a postcard.

He looks at her. She looks hard at the receiver, at the two dark lamps, at the masking tape.

He lets it go. She will remember it later as the first kind thing anybody did for her on Galebnjak.

Service room, Wednesday morning

Dino has the inventory form out again, because a form once started must be finished. Item fifteen, desk, steel, one. He crouches to look under it.

On screen: Mara (shaking the last drops out of the thermos), Dino (on one knee by the desk)

DINO Item sixteen. Box, wooden, one.

DINO (peering) Stencilled. D, N, E, V... Dnevnik.

MARA Logbooks.

He hauls the crate out by its rope handles and lifts it onto the desk.

Sound: a heavy wooden crate thumping down on a steel desk

The desk groans. Inside, packed upright like books on a shelf, are forty black oilcloth notebooks, their spines lettered in white with years.

MARA (reading the spines) Seventy-nine. Eighty. Eighty-one.

MARA All the way to last year. One for every year.

DINO Forty.

MARA Forty years.

She opens one in the middle. Each page is a day.

Wind, direction and force. Sea state. Ships passing, by name when he could read them and by colour when he couldn't. Bulbs changed. The time the light came on.

And between them, lists.

MARA (reading) "Red tanker, Greek, going north." "Blue yacht, too fast, idiots."

DINO That is a fair description of yachts.

MARA "Figs for Katica."

MARA "Ring the doctor about Katica."

She turns pages faster, years going by under her thumb.

MARA "Mara's birthday. What does a ten-year-old want. Ask Ivka."

DINO What did you want?

MARA A soldering iron. Ivka must have told him. I got one.

DINO At ten.

MARA I burned the table. He said it was a good burn. A clean burn.

She turns another page and stops. Dino reads over her shoulder.

DINO (reading, slowly) "Things I should have said to my father."

DINO One item.

It is crossed out so hard the pen has gone through the paper.

Neither of them says anything for a moment. The wind says it for them.

DINO He wrote everything down.

MARA He said if you write it down, you don't have to carry it.

DINO Did it work?

Mara looks at the forty logbooks, all in a row.

MARA (dry) Look at the size of this crate.

Service room, nearly eight

The last logbook is thin. The final entries lean in a shakier hand, capitals propping each other up.

On screen: Mara (sitting on the crate, the thin logbook open on her knees), Dino (standing at her shoulder, clipboard against his chest)

MARA March. April.

MARA "Wind N 4. Light on 19.12. Tired."

MARA Then it stops.

She closes it. Then she opens it again from the back, the way he used to read the newspaper, sport first.

Close-up: the inside back cover of a black logbook, a pencil note boxed twice

In pencil, boxed twice: HOME LINK. 45.45 BD. 170 HZ SHIFT. TEST WITH I. Under it, a frequency in the two-metre band.

MARA (sitting up) Oh.

MARA Oh, you sneaky old...

She takes a pen from Dino's clipboard without asking and copies the frequency onto the back of her hand, pressing hard.

DINO That is my pen.

MARA It's evidence.

DINO Forty-five point four five what?

MARA Baud. Symbols a second. Old teleprinter speed. Slow.

MARA And a hundred and seventy hertz between the two tones. Mark and space.

MARA (fast, in her meeting voice) It's a private link. He set this receiver to listen for something of his own. Not the coast stations. Not the weather. His.

DINO Something where?

MARA That's what I'm going to find out.

DINO And I?

MARA You're the harbour master. You're Dino.

DINO (tapping the pencil line) Not me. "I". Test with I.

MARA I don't know.

She closes the logbook and holds it against her chest, the way he holds the clipboard.

MARA Could be anyone.

MARA (dry) Could be Italy.

DINO Italy does not have a transmitter on Galebnjak.

MARA Not yet.

She gives him back his pen. He looks at it, and at the ink on her hand, and puts the pen away.

DINO The inventory says, item seventeen, logbooks, forty.

MARA Thirty-nine. I'm taking one home.

DINO (writing) Thirty-nine. One on loan.

She stands, pushes the logbook into the raincoat, and looks at the frequency on her hand.

MARA Tonight at ten, I'm not sitting on a crate.

MARA Tonight I'm listening.
