The Back Room

The back room, a few minutes past ten

Ambience: two warbling radio tones from a small speaker, a paraffin heater ticking

The back door opens on a narrow warm room. Tinned tomatoes on shelves to the ceiling, a calendar from 2011 crooked on a nail, a paraffin heater ticking in the corner. The air is thick with paraffin and coffee.

On a card table under the window: a silver cassette recorder with a carrying handle, and beside it a black radio with a microphone on a hook. A cable runs from the recorder into the microphone socket. Another climbs the wall and out through a hole drilled in the window frame.

Picture: Ivka on a kitchen chair beside the card table, coffee cup in one hand, the other holding the silver cassette recorder still, Mara and Dino in the doorway

On screen: Ivka (black cardigan, grey knitted hat, coffee in one hand), Mara (dripping on the lino), Dino (holding the fishbone antenna)

Ivka sits on a kitchen chair beside the table, in her black cardigan and a knitted hat. She has a cup of coffee in one hand. With the other she is holding the cassette recorder still, as though it might wander off.

She looks at Mara. She looks at Dino, and at the fishbone in his arms.

IVKA Shut the door. You're letting the bora in.

Sound: a back door banging shut against the wind

Dino shuts the door.

The tones squabble on through the little speaker.

Mara stands and listens to them as if she has never heard anything like them, though she has heard them every working day of her adult life.

IVKA (with distaste) It sounds like a kettle arguing.

MARA (faintly) It's frequency-shift keying.

IVKA That's what I said.

The tones stop. The tape hisses. Ivka presses a button hard with one finger, and the hiss stops too.

Ambience: a paraffin heater ticking, wind outside a small window

She puts her coffee down.

IVKA You'll want coffee.

MARA Ivka.

IVKA Sit. Dino, put down the fish. You look like a procession.

Dino looks for somewhere to put a three-element Yagi in a room full of tomatoes, and ends up leaning it gently against the shelf, where it rests among the tins like a saint in a niche.

DINO It is not a fish.

IVKA It's a tape measure. I know that tape measure. He measured my shop for a shelf in 1988.

IVKA The shelf didn't fit. He said the shop was wrong.

The back room, Wednesday night

Three cups of coffee, poured from a small copper pot on a camping stove. Ivka has the only chair. Mara sits on a crate of tinned tomatoes. Dino stands, because there is nowhere else, and because he would anyway.

On screen: Ivka (upright on her chair, glasses on their chain), Mara (on the crate, raincoat open), Dino (by the door, clipboard flat against his chest)

MARA How long?

IVKA How long what?

MARA How long have you been sitting in here pressing buttons at ten o'clock at night?

IVKA Since Monday. When else?

Ivka reaches behind her, between the tomatoes and a box of candles, and takes down a shoebox. She sets it on the card table between the recorder and the radio, and squares it up with both hands.

Close-up: a shoebox lid with a rule written in black marker capitals, underlined twice

In Ante's capitals: ONE A NIGHT, WHEN MARA COMES HOME. NOT BEFORE. PLAY. STOP. DO NOT TOUCH THE KNOBS. IVKA THIS MEANS YOU.

MARA (quiet) That's his writing.

IVKA Whose else? He underlined it twice. He underlined everything twice.

DINO (reading over Mara's shoulder) "Ivka, this means you."

IVKA I didn't touch the knobs.

She lifts the lid. Four cassettes stand in a row, numbered on their spines in the same black marker.

One, two and three have been turned round in their cases to face the wrong way. The fourth says LAST.

MARA You turn them round. When they're done.

IVKA How else would I know?

MARA You could write it down.

IVKA I'm not your grandfather.

IVKA March. He came in for lemons. He was already thin by then. You saw him at Easter.

MARA I saw him.

IVKA He had that radio under one arm and this box under the other.

IVKA (indignant) And he took over my back room for an afternoon like it was his own house. Moved my tomatoes. Drilled a hole in my window. He put a wire on my roof.

IVKA He told me it was for the television.

MARA Did you believe him?

IVKA I don't have a television on the roof. I have a television in the kitchen. Of course I didn't believe him.

DINO (suddenly) Test with I.

They both look at him.

DINO In the logbook. Test with I.

His neck goes pink above his collar.

DINO For the record.

IVKA He made me press play. Then he walked all the way out to the lighthouse in the rain, and all the way back again.

IVKA He came in with water running out of his boots, and he was so pleased with himself he bought two lemons he already had.

She folds her hands on the lid of the box.

IVKA Two buttons. Play. Stop. That's all he taught me.

IVKA He said, when Mara comes home. One a night. Ten o'clock.

IVKA He said, don't tell her.

MARA How did you know I'd come?

IVKA Monday, a yellow coat gets off the ferry.

IVKA (shrugging) Who else wears that coat? You can see it from Italy.

MARA And why not tell me?

IVKA He said, if you tell her, she'll explain it.

Mara opens her mouth. Then she shuts it again.

Dino looks at the ceiling, very carefully, as if there were a crack in it that needed his attention.

DINO (slowly) So you have been sitting here. Every night. Sending messages to the lighthouse.

IVKA I have been sitting here with a cup of coffee, pressing a button.

IVKA What it sends, how would I know? It's a kettle. I never knew a single word.

For the first time all week she sounds offended. Not with them. With the situation.

IVKA Eighty years on this island. I know who is in debt, and who is in love, and who is putting water in the wine.

IVKA (jabbing the box) And this, the one thing, he puts in a kettle.

The back room, later

The heater ticks. The window has steamed over completely. Outside, the bora has found a loose gutter and is playing it.

On screen: Ivka (holding a cold cup, not drinking), Mara (leaning forward on the crate), Dino (still standing, very quiet)

MARA The first one said bread and lemons.

IVKA I saw. On your telephone.

IVKA And one apology to Ivka. One.

IVKA (bitter and amused) Fifty-five years, and he gives me one.

MARA What was it for?

Ivka picks up her coffee, finds it cold, and drinks it anyway.

IVKA In 1971 there was a dance in Split. The Saturday before Easter.

IVKA A proper one. A band from Zagreb, with a trumpet. Not three cousins and an accordion.

IVKA He asked me in January. In January! Three months I had to wait.

IVKA I bought a dress in Split. Apricot. With a belt. And shoes that hurt.

MARA Apricot.

IVKA Apricot. Do you know what apricot cost in 1971? Nobody on this island wore apricot. They wore black and they wore navy blue.

IVKA Saturday comes. I'm on the riva for the ferry. Everybody's on the riva for the ferry. Half the island in their good shoes.

IVKA No Ante.

IVKA The ferry waits five minutes. Then the ferry goes.

IVKA I go home and take off the shoes.

She puts the cup down, precisely, on its own ring on the card table.

IVKA He said the light needed him.

IVKA (sharp) There were two keepers on that rock in those days, mala. Two. Ante and old Marinović. The light did not need him.

MARA What did you do?

IVKA I married Frane the next spring.

IVKA (fondly) Frane danced like a goat falling off a wall, God rest him. And I loved him thirty-eight years.

IVKA Ante came to the wedding. He married Katica. We all grew old. He bought his lemons.

IVKA And he never said one word about it. Not one. Not in fifty-five years.

IVKA Every Monday across that counter. Bread, two lemons, how is the wind, how is your knee. Never one word.

She flicks the shoebox with one finger.

IVKA And now a kettle says he's sorry.

Nobody speaks. The gutter plays on. The heater ticks.

MARA (quietly) Tonight's. Number three. It's printed by now.

Ivka looks at her over her glasses.

IVKA Well?

IVKA Go and get it. I'm eighty. I don't walk on that thing in a bora.

DINO I will go with her.

IVKA Of course you will. Take the fish. Leave it, it's ugly. Go.

The causeway, half past ten at night

Ambience: a hard gusting wind over open sea, waves breaking on stone

The causeway is a long dark shove. Overhead the lamp goes round, and each time its beam passes it turns the spray white.

On screen: Mara (hood up, head down), Dino (walking on the sea side of her, between her and the rail)

Dino walks on the sea side, between Mara and the rail. She notices, and decides not to mention it.

MARA (over the wind) You don't have to.

DINO Have to what?

MARA Nothing.

DINO Good.

A wave breaks over the edge and soaks his left side to the knee. He does not change sides.

MARA Two keepers.

DINO What?

MARA She said there were two keepers. The light didn't need him.

DINO (after a while) My father said Ante was the only man on the island who was afraid of nothing except dancing.

MARA You never told me that.

DINO You never asked. And it was not relevant to the inventory.

They walk on. The tower gets bigger. The beam goes over them and the spray lights up like snow.

Service room, nearly eleven

Ambience: wind moaning round a stone tower, a faint electrical hum

The lamps are dark. The paper has come up a hand's width out of the teleprinter and stopped.

On screen: Mara (at the desk under the bare bulb), Dino (at the door, dripping)

Sound: teleprinter paper tearing cleanly along a metal bar

Mara tears it off along the bar and reads it under the bulb.

Then she reads it again.

Then she holds it out to Dino without a word, and he reads it with his lips moving, the way Ivka did.

DINO (hoarse) Oh.

MARA Yes.

DINO Binoculars.

MARA Yes.

DINO The old goat.

MARA (very nearly laughing) Dino.

DINO (stiff) That is what she will say. I am only saying it first. For the record.

He hands it back, holding it very flat, as if it might spill.

DINO We should not fold it.

MARA No.

She slides it inside the borrowed logbook, against the back cover where HOME LINK is boxed twice in pencil. Then she buttons the logbook into the raincoat.

The back room, ten to midnight

Ambience: a paraffin heater ticking, wind outside a small window

Ivka has not moved, except to make fresh coffee and to put out a plate of dried figs, which on Galebnjak is the same as a brass band.

On screen: Ivka (on her chair, hands in her lap), Mara (standing with the paper), Dino (by the door, clipboard against his chest)

IVKA Read it. I left my glasses somewhere.

The glasses are on the chain round her neck. Nobody says so.

Close-up: a teleprinter sheet headed FOR IVKA. ONE APOLOGY, IN PARTS. and signed ANTE

FOR IVKA. ONE APOLOGY, IN PARTS. Six numbered lines, and at the bottom, ANTE.

Picture: Mara reading the teleprinter sheet aloud in the warm back room, Ivka very still on her chair beside a plate of figs

MARA (reading) One. 1971. The dance. The light did not need me. I said it did.

MARA Two. I was afraid of you. You were the best dancer on the island, and I dance like a winch.

MARA Three. I saw the dress from the gallery with the binoculars. Apricot. It was very good.

MARA Four. Frane was the right man. I said so at the wedding, but the band was too loud.

MARA Five. For fifty years you gave me the good lemons from the bottom of the box. I knew.

MARA (softly) Six. Sorry.

MARA Ante.

Nobody speaks. Dino studies his clipboard. The heater ticks.

IVKA (at last) A winch.

IVKA That's true. He danced like a winch. I saw him at Frane's cousin's wedding.

IVKA A winch with a bad bearing.

MARA Ivka.

IVKA (shaking her head) Binoculars. Fifty-five years.

IVKA The old goat.

Dino coughs into his fist, very carefully, and looks at the calendar from 2011.

Ivka holds out her hand. Mara gives her the paper. Ivka folds it in four and puts it in the pocket of her cardigan without looking at it.

IVKA Read number five again.

MARA (without the paper) For fifty years you gave me the good lemons from the bottom of the box. I knew.

IVKA (briskly) I give everybody the good lemons.

She takes the fourth cassette out of the shoebox and turns it over in her fingers. LAST, it says, and nothing else.

MARA Ivka...

IVKA Tomorrow. It says one a night.

MARA I know what it says.

IVKA Then you know.

She puts the cassette back in the box, facing the right way, and puts the lid on, and both hands on the lid.

Dino holds the clipboard flat against his chest, the way he held it on Mara's doorstep. Except now it looks less like a breastplate and more like something he is keeping warm.

In ten minutes it will be Thursday. The Plovput crew comes on Friday's ferry.

Somewhere under the berthing forms and the fuel forms is the one with the soft corners. Mara watches him not take it out.

DINO For the record...

He seems unable to think what the record wants.

He eats a fig instead.
