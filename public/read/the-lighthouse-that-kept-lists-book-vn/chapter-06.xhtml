A List for Mara

Harbour office, Thursday evening

Ambience: wind against a window, a strip light buzzing

The harbour office: a metal desk under a strip light, ring binders, a nautical chart, a fax machine nobody has faxed since 2015. Outside, the wind has dropped to something the island calls calm.

On screen: Ivka (black cardigan, knitted hat, arms folded), Dino (behind his desk, clipboard squared in front of him), Mara (leaning on the door frame)

IVKA I'm coming.

DINO It is four hundred metres of wet stone.

IVKA I have walked on wet stone for eighty years.

DINO Not that stone. Not at night.

DINO (consulting the clipboard) You are a resident in a high-risk category.

IVKA Which category?

DINO Over seventy-five.

IVKA Then I've less to lose than you.

That settles it, except that she still has to sign for the life jacket.

Dino takes it down from a hook: orange, from the harbour office, made for a fisherman twice her size. He lays a form beside it.

DINO Equipment, flotation, one. Sign here. And here.

Ivka signs with a flourish, as if it were a treaty.

On screen: Ivka (swamped in a huge orange life jacket over her black cardigan, very cross), Dino (holding the form), Mara (biting the inside of her cheek)

MARA You look...

IVKA Say it.

MARA Like a very small buoy.

IVKA A buoy that knows where you live, mala.

The back room, half past nine at night

Ambience: a paraffin heater ticking, wind outside a small window

The heater ticks. The shoebox is open on the card table. Three cassettes face the wrong way. One faces the right way.

On screen: Luka (large and gentle, green jumper with net twine in the wool, crossword under his arm), Ivka (in the life jacket, the cassette marked LAST in her hand)

Ivka puts the cassette into the recorder herself, and closes the lid with one finger.

IVKA Ten o'clock.

IVKA Play. Stop when it stops. Don't touch the knobs.

LUKA I know, teta Ivka.

IVKA Luka.

IVKA (tapping the shoebox lid) This means you.

LUKA (patiently) I know, teta Ivka.

She looks at him a moment longer than she needs to, and then at the recorder.

IVKA (quieter) Not one second early.

LUKA Ten o'clock. Play. Stop when it stops.

He sits down in her chair and unfolds his crossword, and takes a pen out of his pocket.

IVKA Not in pen.

The causeway, a quarter to ten at night

Ambience: a stiff wind over open sea, small waves on stone

The bora has blown itself down to a stiff wind. The sea is black and only a little restless. Above the tower the lamp turns, and its beam keeps walking over them.

Picture: Mara, Ivka and Dino walking the stone causeway three abreast at night, Ivka in the middle in her orange life jacket, the lighthouse beam sweeping over them

On screen: Mara (raincoat), Ivka (in the life jacket, one hand through each of their arms), Dino (on the sea side)

They walk three abreast. Ivka is in the middle, a hand through each of their arms.

DINO Slowly.

IVKA I am going slowly. You are going slowly. I am going at a normal speed.

Halfway out, at the third set of steps down to the water, Ivka stops.

She looks down at the water by the third step, and says nothing.

MARA Ivka?

Ivka does not say why. She walks on.

At the door Dino bends, tips the third flowerpot, and comes up with the key as if he has been doing it for years.

MARA (watching him) You've done that before.

DINO For the inventory.

MARA For years?

DINO For the inventory, for years.

Service room, ten o'clock at night

Ambience: wind moaning round a stone tower, a faint electrical hum

Dino has set the crate upright and folded his jacket on it. Ivka sits on it like a queen on a fish box.

Mara stands by the teleprinter. Dino plants himself by the door, in case anyone needs stopping.

On screen: Ivka (enthroned on the crate in the life jacket), Mara (at the teleprinter), Dino (by the door, clipboard in its plastic bag)

DINO What if Luka presses the wrong button?

IVKA There are two buttons.

DINO He is my nephew.

The red lamp lights. Then the green. Then both steady and agree.

Sound: a teleprinter clattering awake with a crash like a filing cabinet falling downstairs

The machine wakes with its crash. RYRYRYRYRY rattles up out of the platen.

IVKA (flinching) Every night it does that?

MARA It's a test pattern. It works every part of the printer, so you know it's all...

She hears herself, and stops.

The machine types a heading. FOR MARA.

The paper climbs. Nobody moves.

Close-up: a long teleprinter sheet headed FOR MARA, seven numbered lines, signed NONO

FOR MARA. Seven numbered lines in Ante's slow capitals, and at the bottom, NONO.

MARA (reading, steady) One. Do not sell the house to the man from Split. He wants to put in a jacuzzi.

DINO (scandalised) A jacuzzi.

MARA Two. The light relay is twenty-four volt. Spares in the jar marked relays. Not the jar marked buttons.

MARA Three. The stove draws better with the window open one finger.

She reads four silently first. Her face does not change. Then she reads it out.

MARA Four. Marica did not go to Italy. I found her in the morning, in pieces under the causeway.

MARA I could not mend her, and I could not give you pieces. I put a stone in her and sank her by the third step.

MARA The postcard was Captain Božić, on the Ancona boat. I dictated.

MARA Sorry. Same as Ivka.

Ivka's hand goes to the pocket of her cardigan, under the life jacket, where the third list is folded in four.

MARA Five. Eat. Ivka says you are thin.

IVKA (to the ceiling) I did say that.

MARA Six.

She stops.

MARA (quietly) You can stop explaining things now. I know how it works. I built it.

MARA Seven. Mondays, bread and two lemons. You don't know she isn't.

MARA Nono.

The carriage returns. The paper feeds up twice. The lamps die, green, then red.

IVKA (after a while) Captain Božić. I knew it.

IVKA His wife said he had a girlfriend in Ancona.

Service room, a little after ten

The room is very quiet. The wind circles the tower. The beam circles above it.

On screen: Mara (holding the sheet flat on the desk with both palms, very composed), Ivka (on the crate), Dino (by the door)

Sound: teleprinter paper tearing cleanly along a metal bar

Mara tears the paper at the bar. Her hands are steady. She notices that, and it seems important, so she keeps both palms flat on the sheet.

MARA (brisk, too fast) The tape is a recording of the audio.

MARA He'd have generated the tones on the old terminal unit at the house. It's in the cupboard under the stairs.

MARA Recorded them onto the cassette through the line out. Then the recorder at Ivka's plays them into the microphone socket of the radio...

IVKA Mala.

MARA ...and the radio sends them up the wire on her roof, so it's actually a very simple...

DINO Mara.

On screen: Mara (stopped mid-word, eyes wet), Dino (holding out an ironed handkerchief), Ivka (looking at the ceiling)

He holds out a handkerchief. Ironed. He has brought it in the plastic bag, with the clipboard.

She takes it.

The explaining runs out somewhere around the microphone socket. What comes after is wet and undignified and goes on until the handkerchief is no longer ironed.

Ivka studies the ceiling. Dino studies his clipboard.

MARA (muffled) I knew about the postcard.

IVKA Of course you did.

MARA Boats can't write.

IVKA No, mala.

When she is finished she gives the handkerchief back. Dino folds it and files it away under the berthing forms.

Service room, later

Mara reaches up to the shelf above the desk. Two jam jars stand side by side, labelled in his capitals: BUTTONS. RELAYS.

On screen: Mara (face blotched, eyes clear, holding a jam jar up to the bulb), Dino (at her shoulder), Ivka (standing now, rustling in her life jacket)

MARA Buttons. Full to the lid.

MARA Relays.

Inside, wrapped in newspaper from 2009, are four small black relays with 24V stamped on their cases. She holds one up to the bulb.

DINO Good?

MARA Four spares. Contacts clean.

MARA (almost laughing) Because of course they are.

Ivka gets off the crate without being asked. Mara pulls it to the desk and sits at the teleprinter keyboard.

DINO Does it go anywhere?

MARA No. The receiver here can't send. It'll only print on this paper.

She switches the machine to local. The motor hums.

MARA Doesn't matter.

Picture: Mara typing on the teleprinter keyboard with two fingers in the round service room, Ivka in her life jacket and Dino watching over her shoulders

She types with two fingers, slowly, the way he did, the keys coming down hard, the letters rising in capitals.

MARA (as she types) For Nono.

MARA One. House not for sale.

MARA Two. Spares found, four of them. Not the buttons.

MARA Three. Window open one finger.

MARA Four. I knew about the postcard. Boats cannot write. I was eight, not stupid.

MARA Five. Same as Ivka.

MARA Mara.

She tears it off and looks at it. Then she slips it into the logbook crate, on top of the forty black books, where whoever looks next will find it.

IVKA (reading over her shoulder) Good.

IVKA Now help me back over that thing. I want to sit down somewhere with a back.

Harbour office, half past six on Friday morning

Ambience: wind against a window, a strip light buzzing

Grey light over the ferry ramp. The strip light buzzes. On the desk, under the lamp, lies the form with the soft corners, flat at last.

On screen: Dino (in shirtsleeves, tie loosened, pen in hand, very awake), Mara (in the yellow raincoat, a coffee in each hand)

Dino has been in his office since six. Mara arrives at half past with two coffees from Ivka, who was up, because Ivka is always up.

DINO You are late.

MARA It's half past six.

DINO I said six.

MARA You said "early". I heard "early" as half past six.

He takes the coffee without looking at it and turns the form round to face her.

DINO Proposal for registration of a cultural good.

DINO Box one. Name of the good.

MARA The lighthouse.

DINO (writing) Galebnjak Lighthouse Radio Station.

DINO Box two. Description.

MARA Lighthouse with... a radio-teletype receiving station. Historic.

DINO In working order?

MARA In working order.

DINO (writing, slowly) In working order. Demonstrated.

He underlines "demonstrated" twice.

DINO Box nine. Responsible technician.

He holds out the pen.

MARA Dino, I live in Zagreb.

DINO The box does not ask where you live.

DINO It says "volunteer".

She reads the box twice. Then she takes the pen and signs: Mara Bašić. Responsible technician, volunteer.

MARA There.

DINO And here. And here. And initials on every page.

She signs where he points, page after page. He blots each page with a sheet of paper, the way nobody on the island has blotted anything since the typewriter was new.

DINO At seven, I send it to the conservation office in Split. With a request for preventive protection.

DINO At eight, I phone to make sure someone has read it.

MARA And at nine?

DINO At nine, I phone to make sure they have read all of it.

MARA (smiling into her coffee) For the record.

DINO For the record.

Ferry ramp, a quarter to five on Friday

Ambience: a light wind over a harbour at dusk, a ferry engine idling

The ferry swings in against the ramp. The light came on twenty minutes ago, by itself, and its beam already sweeps over the deck.

On the foredeck lies a galvanised pole, lashed down like a lance. Two men in orange jackets come down the ramp with a trolley.

On screen: Dino (at the foot of the ramp, clipboard held out in front of him), the older man (orange Plovput jacket, pen in his chest pocket), Mara (in the yellow raincoat, a little behind Dino)

The form with the soft corners lies on top of his clipboard, every box filled. Split has read it. Split has read all of it.

THE OLDER MAN (reading the form) Preventive protection.

THE OLDER MAN So we can't touch anything.

DINO Until it is assessed.

THE OLDER MAN How long's that?

DINO It is November. They are in Split.

The older man looks up at the lamp sweeping the causeway. At the pole on the foredeck. At Mara in the raincoat.

Then he takes the pen from his jacket and signs the bottom of Dino's form.

Dino takes it back and clips it at the front of the clipboard, where the berthing forms used to be.

DINO (formal) Thank you. It is noted.

THE OLDER MAN Is there coffee on this island?

MARA One place. Ask for the good lemons.

The ferry turns at a quarter past five, which gives the crew half an hour in Ivka's shop, drinking her coffee standing up.

Each of them comes out with a paper bag of lemons. Mara, at the window, watches Ivka reach to the bottom of the box for them.

The crew goes back to Split on the same ferry, without the pole. It stays on the riva. Someone will come back for it in spring.

Service room, Friday evening

Ambience: a light wind round a stone tower, a faint electrical hum

On the way out, with bread under her raincoat, Mara stops at the third step. The water is black and shows nothing. She finds she does not mind.

Now the bulb is on, and the lamp turns overhead, and the teleprinter sits quiet with its paper ready.

On screen: Mara (alone on the crate, raincoat sleeves rolled twice, a new black notebook open on her knee)

She has a new notebook from Ivka's shelf, between the batteries and the candles. Black oilcloth.

MARA Right.

MARA Date first. In capitals. Because that's how it's done.

She writes. Then she reads it back to the empty room, the way he would have.

MARA Light on, sixteen twenty-five.

MARA Wind north, dropping.

MARA Teleprinter OK.

She looks at the two dark lamps side by side, waiting.

MARA (softly) Bread. Two lemons.

She closes the notebook and does not explain anything.
