The Man with No Hands on His Watch

The foot of the atelier stairs, rue Vieille-du-Temple. Tuesday, ten to eight in the morning.

A stone stairwell, grey light from the street door. Dec sits on the bottom step with two paper cups of coffee and a folded newspaper, like a large untidy dog told to wait.

On screen: Dec (standing up too fast, slopping coffee), Inès (trench coat, scarf, unimpressed)

DEC (happy) I brought coffee. One black, one with everything in it. You take whichever.

He holds out the black one. She never said which. He guessed wrong. She takes it, mostly to stop him holding it out.

INÈS (neutral) How did you find me?

DEC You told Pichon. I've a very good memory for addresses. It is what we do in my line of work. You remember where the doors are.

DEC (sad) And there is a bad thing.

He hands her the newspaper, folded to page four.

On screen: The newspaper: NOTARY FOUND DEAD NEAR THE PONT MARIE. Maître Gérard Lassalle, 62, of the quai d'Orléans. Police believe a heart attack. No signs of violence.

Inès reads it twice. The coffee goes cold in her other hand.

INÈS (worried) Lassalle. He telephoned Morel on Saturday to ask if we could be faster.

DEC (worried) The man who sent me the gear and the man who sent you the painting. In the river. Of a heart attack. The day the clocks stopped.

Four floors up, a door bangs. Morel starts down the stairs and stops on the first landing.

INÈS (thinking) I am going to the quai d'Orléans.

DEC (happy) Grand. I will come with you.

INÈS I did not ask you.

DEC No, I know. But I have a good feeling about this door.

Lassalle's office, 18 quai d'Orléans, second floor. Mid-morning.

The small door set in the green carriage door stands an inch open. Upstairs, the office door stands open all the way.

Inside, panelled walls and tall windows over the river. Every book lies open on the carpet, spine up, in rows. Every filing drawer is pulled out to exactly the same length. Every picture stands against the wall with its brown paper backing slit down the middle.

On screen: Inès (in the doorway, mouth dry), Dec (behind her, quiet)

INÈS (scared) This is not a burglary. Burglars are stupid. This is someone going through a room square by square.

DEC (thinking) And they did not find it. They have gone round in a ring. Bookcase, cabinet, pictures. Every drawer of the desk is out.

DEC (thinking) But they have not done the desk itself. Either they were interrupted, or they do not know what this is.

He steps between the open books to a heavy mahogany bureau with a high back of pigeonholes, all emptied onto the floor.

DEC (happy) Second Empire. A workshop in the Faubourg Saint-Antoine made hundreds of these for notaries, and every one has a secret drawer.

DEC (happy) Because they only knew the one trick. Take out the middle pigeonhole, reach in to the elbow, and at the back on the left there is a peg...

A click. A panel Inès would have sworn was solid drops forward on a hidden hinge. Behind it, a drawer no deeper than a book.

DEC (happy) Every desk of that make. Every single one.

On screen: Dec (very pleased), Inès (leaning in)

In the drawer there are three things.

On screen: A silver pocket watch on a chain: white enamel face, twelve black numerals, and no hands at all, not even a post where hands would go

On screen: A cream card: La Fondation Vaulmont vous prie d'assister à la Veillée de l'Éclipse. Panthéon. Le premier avril. Quinze heures précises.

On screen: A scrap of notebook paper in a cramped old hand: Barrière d'Enfer. La neuvième niche.

DEC (worried) The gate of hell. The ninth niche.

INÈS (thinking) The Barrière d'Enfer was an old toll gate in the city wall. At Denfert-Rochereau. Where the catacombs are.

DEC (scared) Oh, that is cheerful.

Inès takes the watch. It is warm. Her thumb finds the catch on the back of the case.

The floor of the landing creaks. One plank, under one big careful weight.

On screen: Dec (frozen), Inès (frozen, watch in hand), the bald man (filling the doorway: enormous, bald, black wool coat, small pale eyes, a faded blue cuckoo clock tattooed on his neck)

His small pale eyes go to the open drawer, then to the watch in Inès's hand. Without a word, he comes into the room.

DEC (scared) Right. Okay. Window.

The roofs of the Île Saint-Louis. Moments later.

Grey zinc slopes wet with drizzle, chimney pots, a satellite dish, a pot of dead geraniums. The Seine far below on one side, a courtyard of bins on the other.

On screen: Dec (crawling along the ridge), Inès (clinging to the zinc, white-faced)

DEC (worried) Do not look down.

INÈS (scared) Basszus.

She has looked down.

Behind them the zinc booms under a great weight. The bald man is not running. He walks, as if he has all day.

At the end of the ridge, the roof stops. A light well drops between two buildings, a metre and a bit wide. On the far side, a lower roof with a skylight propped open on a stick.

Dec jumps it, skids, catches the skylight frame, and turns round with both hands out.

DEC (worried) Come on.

INÈS (scared) No. I cannot. I am afraid of heights. I clean skies. I do not jump over holes.

DEC You can. It is a metre. You have walked further for a coffee.

The zinc booms behind her. She looks round.

On screen: Inès (scared), the bald man (crouched by the gutter, three metres back)

He is not looking at her. A grey pigeon is caught by one foot in the bird netting along the gutter, beating itself against the zinc.

The huge man reaches out slowly, the way you reach for a sleeping child. He folds its wings. With one thick finger he works the net off its foot, holds it a moment against his coat, and opens his hands.

The pigeon goes up past Inès's face in a clatter of wings.

And Inès, without deciding to, jumps.

Dec catches her by both wrists. They go over sideways against the skylight, the stick falls out, and the skylight bangs shut on Dec's elbow.

DEC (angry) Ah, Jaysus, Mary and the holy saints and all their cousins...

Across the gap, the bald man studies the drop, then takes out a phone and walks slowly back the way he came.

The Pont de Sully, early afternoon.

A stone and iron bridge over a green-grey Seine, the tip of the island behind them, rain on the parapet.

On screen: Dec (rubbing his elbow, grinning), Inès (glasses off, wiping them on her scarf with hands that will not keep still)

DEC (happy) You jumped.

INÈS (angry) I could not. And then I did. Do not make a thing of it.

DEC (happy) It was only a metre.

INÈS It was a metre and twenty.

DEC I will grant you the twenty.

Her other hand is still clenched round the silver watch. The chain has printed links into her palm. She presses the catch. The back springs open.

On screen: Inside the watch: no works at all. On the inner lid, nine tiny rings engraved side by side like wheels, each one ringed with the alphabet in letters smaller than grains of rice

INÈS (worried) Dec. What is this?

It is the first time she has used his name. He bends over her hand and, for once, goes quiet.

DEC (thinking) That is a lock.

Roux & Fils, four in the afternoon.

The shop is ticking again like light rain. Two hundred clocks back at their business, the cat clocks eyes going left and right. Someone has wiped the chalk out of the longcase.

On screen: Roux (behind the counter, furious, loupe on its chain), Dec (on a stool), Inès (on a stool, the silver watch on the counter)

ROUX (angry) A day! A whole day! At eighty-one I have counted how many I have left. And the police send me a boy with a pimple to ask if I have been drinking!

DEC Had you?

ROUX (angry) You are the thief who brought me my water. Sit. Mind the cuckoo, he bites.

Roux will not touch the watch on the counter. He looks at it the way a man looks at a dog that once bit him.

DEC (thinking) Anselme. Before Sunday. Did anyone ask you for something you would not do?

ROUX (angry) Everybody. That is what a clockmaker is. A man who says no.

ROUX (thinking) Two jobs this winter. The gentleman came once. After that, only a young man in a grey coat with a parcel.

INÈS What gentleman?

ROUX (thinking) Grey suit, very good. Silver hair brushed back. A voice like warm oil. He had one of those on a chain. A very good watch to have no hands.

Dec and Inès keep their eyes on Roux. It takes some effort.

ROUX (neutral) The first job was a lock. A copy of a fourteenth-century letter lock. Nine wheels. Turn each to a letter, and if they spell the word, the bar drops.

DEC What was the word?

ROUX (angry) I do not know! The drawings give the gates by number, not letter. The young man brought the last wheel's position in a sealed envelope and stood over me while I cut it.

ROUX (angry) He said it was a matter of faith. Faith! In my shop!

INÈS (thinking) And the second job?

ROUX (angry) The one I did not do. Station clocks, hospital clocks, the receivers in the traffic lights. They all listen to Allouis, the long-wave transmitter. It sends out the real time and every radio clock in France obeys.

ROUX (angry) They wanted the receivers changed to listen to a different voice. For an exhibition. I said a clock listens to Allouis as a priest listens to God, and I put him out of my shop. Ten days ago.

The cuckoo over the workshop door shoots out and says cuckoo four times. Dec jumps.

DEC (worried) Then on Sunday someone takes a day off you. Because you said no.

Roux's silence is answer enough.

The same shop. The counter, under the lamp.

Roux spreads five sheets of old drawings from a cardboard tube, weighed down with a carriage clock, a jar of screws, his loupe, and after a moment's thought, the handless watch.

On screen: The drawings: nine thin wheels on a spindle, each ringed with letters, each with a tiny gate at a numbered position; in the corner a circle with IX at the top and the words Fr. Anselmus Vey me fecit, MCCCXLIX

INÈS (thinking) Brother Anselm Vey made me. Thirteen forty-nine.

ROUX (neutral) The plague year. Everybody was dying and this one made locks. The word was never on the drawings. The young man said so. Vey kept it in his book.

DEC (thinking) It is the same lock. On the watch. It is a picture of this lock.

ROUX (neutral) Of course. It is their sign. Every one of them carries the lock, and none of them carries the word. You see, thief? You are not so stupid.

ROUX (sad) Take the tube. Every time I open it I taste cloves.

INÈS (neutral) Anselme. Show me your tongue.

With enormous dignity, he does. Still grey, fainter, like the shadow of a thumbprint on glass.

INÈS (thinking) I restored an apothecary's sign once. Sixteen hundreds. One panel was a man in bed, tongue grey, spiced wine, cloves round the jug, a plant with white trumpet flowers.

INÈS (thinking) The rhyme said: give this to a man and he will owe you a day.

ROUX (surprised) White trumpets. Datura. My grandmother grew it for the rats.

The anger goes out of his face. What is left is an old man in a work coat.

ROUX (sad) Somebody gave me that? And drew in my longcase? My father's clock.

Dec's hand is on the old man's shoulder. It feels like a bird's.

DEC (sad) We will find out who. I promise you.

ROUX (happy) Thief. You promise like a thief.

The rue des Gravilliers, dusk. A street lamp coming on.

On screen: Dec (carrier bag with the tube), Inès (unfolding the scrap of paper under the lamp)

DEC (worried) The warm-oil man wants the clocks of Paris to hear a different voice. Roux says no and loses a day. Lassalle is in the river. And they want my room finished for an eclipse at the Panthéon.

DEC I do not know what any of that means.

INÈS Neither do I. But I know where this is.

On screen: The scrap: Barrière d'Enfer. La neuvième niche.

DEC (scared) Under the ground. With the bones.

INÈS Six million of them, more or less. The ninth niche will not be in the part with the gift shop.

She scrolls to a number and looks at it as if a wasp has landed on her hand.

INÈS (worried) My mother's hairdresser has a cousin who goes down there at night. Illegally. A philosophy student. They call her La Taupe.

DEC The Mole. Is she good?

INÈS (neutral) I have never met her. My mother's hairdresser says she is very good, and also completely insane.

Her thumb hovers over the call button.

What do you choose?

  • Call La Taupe and go down to the ninth niche
