Dec Stopped

Room four at Le Balancier. Saturday, a few minutes past four.

The white quilt, Roux's drawings, the tray on the bed.

On screen: Dec (cup in hand, frowning at it)

DEC (thinking) Spiced. The Swiss put cloves in everything. Roux said something about cloves. Roux says a lot of things.

DEC (thinking) It's the cold. That's all it is.

He drinks it down. His bones are still cold from the tower, so he pours a second and drinks that too.

Then the room tilts. Gently. Like a boat leaving a quay.

He puts the cup on the drawings and misses. Tea runs across Vey's nine wheels. He watches it soak in and can't think why it matters.

He lies back on the quilt. The ceiling is miles away.

Room four. Dusk at the window.

A key turns in the lock. It sounds like it's in another building.

On screen: Fabien (grey coat, canvas bag on his shoulder, Madame Jobin's big iron key ring in one hand)

Dec sees him in pieces, the way you see a street from a train. A hand on a pot. A chair dragged to the cuckoo clock. His own wrist lifted, his watch turned on it, set down again.

DEC (scared) Wha... who...

The words come out in the wrong order and lie down.

FABIEN (neutral) Shh. It's all right, Monsieur Hartley.

Cool fingers press Dec's thumb to his phone. Then the wardrobe creaks, and somebody is drawing on wood.

At the door Fabien turns, kind and earnest.

FABIEN (neutral) You owe a day, that's all. Everyone does. Mutuo datum.

The door closes. Dec means to get up. He means it hard. He watches the ceiling go soft at the edges until it's the moon.

The corridor outside room four. Sunday, nine in the morning.

Inès knocked last night at ten and again at eleven, and got nothing. After the tower she thought he was avoiding her. She thought that was fair.

This morning Madame Jobin hands her the key without a single honeymoon joke.

On screen: Dec (at the window in yesterday's clothes, one hand on the glass), Inès (in the doorway)

DEC (happy) There you are. Is it Saturday? I've to meet you for breakfast. We're going to talk about the weather.

INÈS (scared) Dec. Show me your tongue.

He does, as obliging as a boy at the doctor's.

On screen: Dec's tongue, dull grey, as if he has been sucking a pencil

She reads the room the way she reads a painting, one thing at a time. The travel clock says nine. The cuckoo says nine. Tea has dried brown on Roux's drawings. The wardrobe door stands ajar.

On screen: Inside the wardrobe door, in white chalk: a circle, twelve small marks, and IX at the top

She picks up his phone. The photographs from the tower are gone, every one, and the phone's clock says 09:00.

INÈS (angry) Basszus.

DEC (worried) Have I done something?

INÈS (sad) No. Somebody has.

The forester's house, at the edge of the fir forest. Twenty minutes later.

She runs the whole way in the wrong boots. Clothilde said the young man in grey rented it.

On screen: Inès (in the snow, out of breath), the forester's house (door open, no smoke from the chimney)

Tyre tracks show where the black four-by-four stood, already filling with fresh snow. Inside: a cold stove, a swept floor, a smell of candle wax. Nothing else.

Atelier Morel. Tuesday evening.

She brought him home on the Sunday night train, his head on her shoulder. Every hour he asked whether they were nearly at Saturday.

On screen: Dec (asleep on the green velvet sofa under Inès's trench coat), Inès (at her bench, phone in hand)

The big easel is empty. A docket on the bench says the Fondation's courier took the Allegory on Friday, while they were in the snow. Morel signed for it.

Morel hasn't answered his phone since Sunday. The atelier is hers now, and the sofa, and the man on it.

On her own phone she has the REMIT page and the list of tests, saved before it died in the tower.

The Panthéon line she has only in her head. She read it over Dec's shoulder by his torch, the night the trap banged shut, and said it aloud: five kilometres.

On screen: Inès's memory of the page: Panthéon, galerie du dôme. 1er avril. 14h51 à 15h00. Neuf minutes.

A restorer remembering a notebook she no longer has. Nobody will believe that, and she knows it.

At ten an email comes.

On screen: Email from Mlle Ferrand, Fondation Vaulmont: Monsieur de Vaulmont has heard Monsieur Hartley is unwell, and releases him from his contract with every good wish. He sends his warm regards to Madame Varga, and to her mother in Lyon.

Inès reads the last line four times. She puts the phone face down on the bench and doesn't touch it again until morning.

Atelier Morel. Wednesday 1 April, twenty-five past two.

The skylight has gone a strange thin grey, like light through a dirty window. On the bench, her laptop shows the live broadcast from the Panthéon.

On screen: The laptop: gilt chairs under the dome, guests in dark suits, the pendulum sliding past the stage, and on an easel the Allegory of the Sundial

On screen: Dec (sitting up on the sofa, a blanket round his shoulders), Inès (beside him, the laptop on her knees)

DEC (thinking) Is that your painting?

INÈS (neutral) Yes.

DEC (happy) It's lovely. When did they take it?

INÈS (sad) While we were in the snow.

Waiters move along the front row with little pewter cups. The minister drinks. The prefect drinks. A banker raises his cup to the camera.

DEC (thinking) Mulled wine. In April. Your man's very fancy.

By a quarter to three the front row has gone soft. Heads tilt. A journalist smiles at nothing. Inès knows that smile. She has watched it on Dec for four days, and now she knows what was in the cups.

Nobody on the broadcast seems to notice.

Above them, the skylight dims to blue. The light drains from the room as if someone were drawing it off with a cloth.

On the laptop the nave goes dark. Phones light up in it like fireflies.

On screen: The laptop clock, Inès's phone on the bench, the oven in the kitchenette: 09:00, all of them

Down in the rue Vieille-du-Temple a car horn starts, then another, and they don't stop.

On the screen a tall man stands at the lectern, silver hair swept back, perfectly calm. The camera loves him.

VAULMONT (neutral) Do not be afraid. The debt is called. For nine minutes, my friends, we will know what we owe.

VAULMONT (neutral) Mutuo datum. Reddetur.

Behind him, in the dark, the pendulum swings past and back, and nobody stops anything.

At three the light comes back, silver and slow. The clocks lurch and start. In the front row the minister lifts his head, tears on his face, and tells the camera he saw the hand of God.

On screen: Dec (looking at his watch, still stopped at nine), Inès

DEC (worried) Inès. What day is it?

She looks at the skylight, and the laptop, and the man on the sofa who once told her a twenty-minute story about a cat called Pádraig to keep her alive.

INÈS (sad) It's later than that.

From her bench drawer she takes an old steel watch with a cracked strap. Zoltán's. She winds it, twelve slow turns, the way her father did at red lights. Then she takes off Dec's stopped watch and buckles her father's on in its place.

It ticks. He holds it to his ear and smiles, as if she has given him the time.

The End
