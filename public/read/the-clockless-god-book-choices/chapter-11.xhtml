The Vigil

The side door of the Panthéon. Wednesday 1 April, seven in the morning.

A trolley of three wooden crates, stencilled FRAGILE and CECI N'EST PAS UNE BOMBE. It seemed funny in the workshop in February.

On screen: Dec (contractor's pass round his neck), a security guard (black fleece, unimpressed)

DEC (happy) Escape room. For the guests. Pour s'amuser.

DEC (worried) The stencil's a joke. Magritte? The pipe?

Nobody wants to argue about Magritte at seven in the morning. The guard waves him through.

The nave of the Panthéon. Morning.

Pale stone, columns like oak trees, a dome so high its saints are the size of thumbnails. From the crown a steel cable comes down sixty-seven metres to a brass sphere swinging above a brass ring.

On screen: The sign by the pendulum: Foucault, 1851. It is the floor, the city and you that move beneath it.

Beside it stand a low grey stage, a lectern, and an easel under grey cloth.

On screen: Kessler (at the foot of the roped-off south dome stair, hands folded, still as a piece of the building), Dec (pushing his trolley)

They look at each other across the nave. Neither moves. The pendulum swings between them, and back.

The Clockmaker's Cell, north-east chapel of the crypt. A quarter past eleven.

His room: stone-effect walls, a bench of brass gears, a real iron lock. On the wall, a clock with nine hours on its face. The client's brief asked for it. He thought it was a quirk.

On screen: Dec (flat on his stomach, one arm down through a hatch in the floor)

Through the hatch in his planks he reaches the old iron grille. Its lock takes nine minutes. He can't stop counting in nines.

The bolt goes back. He leaves the grille closed but unlocked, and puts the hatch back.

DEC (thinking) Nobody'll need it. But it's there.

The nave. Half past one.

A hard blue sky, but the light has gone wrong, every shadow thin and slightly blue. Outside, hundreds hold up cardboard eclipse glasses.

Inside: a tall tired minister, the prefect of police in silver braid, two bankers in navy, a priest with a purple sash, a presenter with a famous tan.

On screen: Vaulmont (moving among them, touching elbows), Dec (behind a column in the south aisle, with a clipboard to look busy)

Everyone Vaulmont speaks to walks away a little straighter, as if something has been lent to them.

On screen: Inès (dark green dress, navy coat, mustard scarf, clipboard held to her chest like a shield, beside the draped easel)

She doesn't look at Dec. That's the arrangement. Once, as the pendulum swings between them, her eyes meet his and go back to her clipboard.

The catering chapel off the south transept. Two o'clock.

Agency waiters, young and bored. At the back table, a man in a white jacket that doesn't suit him, with a narrow back Dec would know anywhere.

On screen: Fabien (white caterer's jacket, earpiece, back to the room)

Steel urns of ordinary vin chaud. And apart, four pewter jugs of something almost brown, with a grey sheen like the bloom on a plum.

On screen: The far end of the table: four pewter jugs, a hundred small pewter cups each engraved with a clock face with IX at the top, and a card in copperplate: Le vin de la Veillée

The whole chapel smells of cloves.

Dec turns away before Fabien can turn round, and walks straight into a waiter with a tray of spoons.

On screen: Moussa (about twenty, tall and gangly, shaved head, white jacket, name badge), Dec

MOUSSA (worried) Pardon, monsieur. Ça va? You're white.

DEC (neutral) I'm Irish. We're always white.

DEC (worried) Moussa. The brown wine in the pewter. When does it go out?

MOUSSA (thinking) Half past two. Only the guests on his list. Any waiter who drinks it is sacked.

MOUSSA (worried) It smells like a church at Christmas. This morning I saw him tip something into the jugs from a paper bag. Grey, like ash.

DEC (sad) It's a drug. It makes you lose a day, and remember only what you're told.

DEC (angry) Then the minister and the prefect say it on the telly.

DEC (worried) I need to swap the jugs and keep the real ones for the police. I can't do it on my own.

MOUSSA (thinking) How much do you think I'm paid for this?

DEC (neutral) Not enough.

MOUSSA (angry) Eleven euros forty an hour. Before tax. And he shouted at Fatou for folding the napkins wrong.

MOUSSA (neutral) What do you need?

The passage outside the catering chapel. Twenty-two minutes past two.

A crate stencilled CECI N'EST PAS UNE BOMBE stands by the wall. Moussa knocks it over with great force and theatrical surprise.

Four hundred brass gears burst out and ring across the stone, like all the coins in the world dropped at once.

On screen: Fabien (on his knees, white with fury, scrabbling for gears), Moussa (apologising)

Every waiter goes down on hands and knees. For forty seconds every eye is on the floor.

Dec builds rooms where forty seconds is a whole puzzle.

Behind the screen, the four pewter jugs go into the empty crate. In their place go four jugs of Moussa's vin chaud, a spoonful of cocoa stirred into each. Brown-grey and dull. Under a lid, it will do.

Dec shuts the crate and sits on it.

Fabien stands up with his hands full of brass. The jugs are where they always were. Moussa apologises at length, in French and Wolof.

The nave. Half past two.

On screen: Dec (behind his column), Vaulmont (by the stage, watching)

The silver trays go out. The guests on the list look at the little clock on the cup, smile, and drink.

Across the nave, Vaulmont's calm face relaxes very slightly, like a man hearing the last wheel of a lock fall into place.

Dec's phone buzzes.

On screen: Text from a Paris number: Where are you. Pichon.

He types without looking.

On screen: Dec's reply: Inside. Swapped the wine. 4 jugs of the real thing in a crate, south transept, behind the screen. Need you. Now.

On screen: Pichon: Lunch.

A second later:

On screen: Pichon: Coming.

The nave. Fourteen forty.

The sun is a thin sickle behind the moon. The light drains as if someone is turning down a dimmer on Paris.

On screen: Vaulmont (at the lectern under the dome, the pendulum cable rising behind him, the camera's red light on)

VAULMONT (neutral) Time is not ours.

The voice fills the nave. Warm oil, poured slowly.

VAULMONT (thinking) Every hour of every life is lent. Today, for a few minutes, the light that measures our hours will be taken away.

VAULMONT (happy) And we will stand together in the dark, and remember it was never ours to keep.

Up the south aisle comes a thin pale figure in a white jacket, scanning the columns.

On screen: Fabien (stopped in the aisle, eyes on Dec, a hand at the wire in his collar), Dec (behind his column)

The narrow face finds his and stays blank. Fabien's lips move, into the earpiece.

At the foot of the roped stair, Kessler's great bald head turns slowly toward the south aisle.

Dec can't go up now. Every step he takes, two men will take with him.

He looks across the nave for Inès. She has already put her clipboard down on the easel.

The north aisle of the Panthéon. The same minute.

Inès walks the north aisle without hurrying, in her green dress and mustard scarf. Nobody watches a woman with a clipboard, or notices she has left it behind.

On screen: Inès (at a narrow door behind a pillar, one hand on its frame)

Behind it is the other stair, the service stair inside the wall: sixty metres round and round, a greasy rope for a handrail and nothing on the other side.

It is the stair she is most afraid of.

Up there, where the pendulum cable meets the crown, waits the transmitter from Morel's notebook.

She looks back once and finds Dec behind his column. He can't nod. Fabien is watching him.

INÈS (scared) Basszus.

She puts her hand on the rope and looks up into the dark.

What do you choose?
