Under the Varnish

Atelier Morel, rue Vieille-du-Temple. Monday, just before eight in the morning.

A top-floor workshop under a grey skylight. The radiator knocks like a polite ghost. On the big easel, a dark old painting, a third of its sky cleaned to a cold clear blue.

On screen: Inès (apron over a grey jumper, gloves tacky with solvent, glasses pushed up with one knuckle)

An Allegory of the Sundial, French, about 1650. A woman in a garden rests one hand on a stone sundial and points at the sky with the other.

INÈS (neutral) You had better not have been retouched in the eighteen hundreds. I don't have time for it.

The odd thing is the sundial. Its shadow falls on a smooth blank band where the hours should run.

On screen: Inès (cleaning), Morel (thin, anxious, cashmere worn through at the elbows, half-moon glasses)

MOREL (worried) How far have you got?

INÈS A third of the sky.

MOREL (worried) En somme, Lassalle telephoned again. The client asks if we can finish by the end of the month.

INÈS (angry) We said the fifteenth of April.

MOREL (worried) Voilà, we said. But the client has an event on the first.

INÈS I am not taking a scalpel to a seventeenth-century sky because somebody is having a party. Who is the client?

MOREL Lassalle doesn't say. A private collection.

His eyes go straight to the sundial, and stay there.

MOREL (worried) And there's nothing unusual? Underneath? Old repairs, overpaint?

INÈS (thinking) Why would there be?

MOREL No reason. I'll be in the office. If there's anything.

He shuts himself in his glass office and sits with his hands flat on the desk.

INÈS (thinking) People don't ask that idly. They ask it about paintings they already know the answer to.

The atelier, half past eight. The blind is drawn; only the UV lamp glows.

Under ultraviolet the old varnish shines a milky green. Except in one place.

On screen: Inès (bent close to the canvas, lamp humming)

On the face of the sundial the glow breaks into a faint dark ring, with twelve small marks round its rim.

She swaps the lamp for a raking light, almost flat to the canvas, so every ridge in the paint throws a shadow.

INÈS (thinking) Paint remembers.

On screen: The sundial in raking light: a clock face painted out under the varnish, twelve hour marks, no hands, and at the top, where XII belongs, the numeral IX

Below the dial runs a line of Roman capitals, also painted out. One word in the middle has been scraped away with a blade, down to the ground.

On screen: The motto under the varnish: MVTVO DATVM . . . [scraped] . . . HORA NONA

INÈS (thinking) Mutuo datum. Given as a loan. And hora nona, the ninth hour.

She takes forty-one photographs and logs every one. A photograph without a note is a rumour.

Through the glass, Morel has not come out to ask why the blind is down. He is not looking at her, very hard.

At nine thirty-five the radio over the sink says rue des Gravilliers.

RADIO An elderly clockmaker found confused in his shop... he had, quote, lost a day... all the clocks had been stopped...

INÈS (surprised) Roux.

She looks at the painted clock with nine at the top. Then she pulls off her gloves, grabs her mustard scarf and takes the stairs at a speed her mother would not approve of.

MOREL (worried) Inès? Where are you...?

INÈS Coffee.

The rue des Gravilliers, outside Roux & Fils. Late morning.

A police car half on the pavement. A small crowd with shopping trolleys, and a man with an elderly dachshund. The dachshund looks at Inès. She goes round the other side of the trolleys.

On screen: Inès (navy trench, mustard scarf, wary of the dog), Pichon (beige raincoat, glasses on a cord)

Two officers steer a tall man out of the shop. He ducks a beat late and hits his head on the bell.

On screen: Inès, Pichon, Dec (hands behind his back, auburn hair in four directions, talking)

DEC (worried) No, I understand, I'd arrest me too, on paper I look desperate...

He sees Inès and gives her an enormous hopeful smile.

DEC (happy) Madame! Could you telephone to my client? J'ai un rendez-vous à onze heures! Je suis une serrure!

INÈS (neutral) Je ne peux pas. Je vais être en retard.

INÈS And you are not a lock. You are a serrurier. A serrure is the thing you open.

DEC (surprised) Right. Thanks. That's actually very helpful. I'll know for next time.

PICHON (sad) Walk, Monsieur Hartley.

As they fold him into the car, something falls from his jacket and rings across the cobbles to the toe of her grey boot.

On screen: The brass gear: new-cut, the size of a two-euro coin, stamped very small with a circle of twelve ticks and IX at the top

Through the rear window the Irishman looks back at her, apologising or asking for help.

Inès pockets the gear and goes to find out where the police judiciaire has its lunch.

Police judiciaire, rue du Bastion. An interview room, a quarter past twelve.

A bare table, a camera, and a door with a card reader and no keyhole, which Dec takes personally.

On screen: Dec (hungry, slumped), Pichon (napkin in his collar, a bubbling cassoulet on a tray, a folder)

PICHON (neutral) You don't mind. If I don't eat at twelve I become unjust. My wife says so.

PICHON Your landlord says you are polite and sing in the shower. Your bank says less.

PICHON (thinking) And your back door was faked by someone who wishes the police to find a burglar. And go home.

He puts down his spoon and slides three photographs across the table.

On screen: Three photographs: chalk clocks with IX at the top inside a judge's clock case, a kitchen clock, a church mechanism

PICHON (worried) October, a retired judge. January, a bookseller. February, a curate at Saint-Médard. Every clock at nine. All said everything tasted of cloves.

PICHON (neutral) None of this was in the papers. Then a man on his knees in the fourth case tells me about the chalk and the cloves before I ask.

DEC (worried) I just looked at what was there.

PICHON (sad) Yes. That is the trouble. Most people don't.

An officer looks in. A woman downstairs has something of the prisoner's. She says the lobby coffee machine is a crime and should be arrested.

The same interview room, minutes later.

On screen: Dec (sitting up), Pichon (eating), Inès (coat buttoned, looking the room over as if it has failed an inspection)

INÈS (neutral) Commissaire. Inès Varga, restorer, Atelier Morel. This fell out of his pocket.

She sets the brass gear on the table.

DEC (happy) Oh, thanks. That's the pattern piece. It came with the parts list from my client.

PICHON (surprised) Who?

INÈS (surprised) Who?

They say it together, and look at each other with mutual irritation.

DEC The Fondation Vaulmont. Please match all wheels to this pattern. I thought it was their logo...

He turns it to the light, sees the IX for the first time, and puts it down.

INÈS (worried) I saw this mark this morning. Under the varnish of a painting from about sixteen fifty.

INÈS (worried) A clock with nine at the top, painted out. A Latin line about a loan and the ninth hour, with one word scraped away. I have photographs.

Pichon takes off his reading glasses and presses the bridge of his nose.

PICHON (sad) Of course you do.

The steps of the police judiciaire. Twenty past two, fine drizzle.

On screen: Dec (released, hands in his jacket pockets), Inès (winding her scarf), Pichon (buttoning his raincoat)

PICHON (neutral) Monsieur Hartley, I have nothing against you but bad luck and a bag of little knives. Madame Varga, I keep the gear. Send me your photographs.

PICHON (worried) Then go home, both of you. If I find you in the fifth case, I will be very sad, I will eat a large lunch, and then I will put you in a cell.

DEC (surprised) There's a fifth?

PICHON (sad) There is always a fifth.

He goes back inside.

On screen: Dec (awkward), Inès (looking at the ring road as if it has disappointed her)

DEC Thanks. For coming.

INÈS I didn't come for you. I came for the mark.

DEC We're not, you know. Partners or anything.

INÈS (neutral) No. We are not partners.

DEC (happy) Grand. Glad we sorted that.

They stand in the drizzle not being partners. A bus goes by.

His phone rings. A Paris number.

FERRAND Monsieur Hartley? Mademoiselle Ferrand, Fondation Vaulmont. We heard about this morning's unpleasantness.

FERRAND The clockmaker's cell must be installed by the thirty-first. The vigil is on the first of April, the day of the eclipse. Nothing about it can move.

FERRAND Monsieur de Vaulmont sends his regards.

The line goes dead.

DEC (scared) They knew I'd been arrested. Before I'd told a soul.

INÈS (thinking) Who sent you the parts list?

Dec digs a crumpled envelope out of his satchel. The sender's stamp is a notary's.

On screen: The envelope: Étude Lassalle, 18 quai d'Orléans, Paris 4e

INÈS (surprised) Lassalle. That's the broker for my painting.

Drizzle beads on her glasses.

INÈS (neutral) We are still not partners.

DEC (happy) Course not.

What do you choose?
