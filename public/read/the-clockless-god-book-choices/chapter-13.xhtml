Bail Before Dawn

Roux & Fils, Horlogerie, the rue des Gravilliers. Thursday, nine in the morning.

Two hundred clocks on the walls, and every one of them stopped. Roux stands at the top of a stepladder with a winding key.

On screen: Roux (on the stepladder, brown work coat, eyebrows raised like awnings), Dec and Inès (on two stools below)

By the door stand Madame Ferré from the bakery and an old lady in a fur hat, who came in uninvited and won't leave.

ROUX (angry) I stopped them myself. Get that straight.

ROUX (angry) Yesterday at three I'm sitting here with the radio. The radio says the stations have all stopped at nine. Austerlitz, Montparnasse, Gare de Lyon. The traffic lights to the Seine. The hospitals.

ROUX (angry) The man on the radio is frightened. So I look at my clocks.

ROUX (happy) Not one stopped. Not one of my clocks listens to Allouis, or to anybody. My clocks listen to me.

ROUX (neutral) So this morning I stop them all. By hand. At nine. To see how it feels to do it to myself.

INÈS (thinking) And how does it feel?

ROUX (angry) Stupid. Watch.

He touches the pendulum of the big walnut regulator on the top shelf, and it swings. Then the carriage clock, and the next, and the next, his small spotted hands working along the shelves, a touch or a turn of the key for each.

At the bottom of the ladder he opens the little door in the longcase, his father's clock, the chalk long wiped away. He gives the pendulum a push, the way you push a child on a swing.

The ticking comes up from every side like rain starting on a roof. One drop, then a dozen, then all of them, none together.

On screen: The row of cat clocks, every pair of eyes swinging left and right again

The cuckoo over the workshop door shoots out, says cuckoo once, as if clearing its throat, and goes back in.

Roux listens with his head on one side until he is satisfied.

ROUX (happy) There. That's the only miracle I've ever approved of.

The old lady in the fur hat bursts into tears.

MADAME FERRÉ (neutral) There, madame. Have a croissant.

DEC (happy) Could I have a croissant?

MADAME FERRÉ (neutral) No.

A very serious restaurant in the sixth. Half past twelve.

White cloths, and the same eleven dishes since nineteen fifty-two. Pichon has been awake for thirty hours. He won't discuss the case before the second course, because at present he is not a just man.

On screen: Pichon (napkin tucked firmly into his collar, a plate of blanquette de veau), Dec and Inès (across the table)

The blanquette comes in a sauce so white and smooth Inès could lay gold leaf on it. Pichon puts down his fork.

PICHON (neutral) Fabien Ruel has confessed. To everything.

PICHON (neutral) The tea at the inn, Monsieur Hartley. Madame Aubry. The curate. Monsieur Roux's coffee. Monsieur Morel's tea. The clocks, every time.

PICHON (thinking) In great detail, with some pride, in the form of a sermon. My sergeant typed for four hours.

PICHON (neutral) He believes he will be forgiven. He was very struck by the word on the lock.

PICHON (neutral) Your young waiter is pressing no charges for the bite.

DEC (surprised) He bit Moussa?

PICHON (happy) Moussa was sitting on him.

PICHON (neutral) Also, the Centre des monuments nationaux wants to keep your clockmaker's cell in the crypt until the end of the summer. An educational exhibit. They ask your fee.

PICHON (neutral) I told them it was very high.

Dec opens his mouth, and shuts it again.

PICHON (neutral) Kessler has said nothing at all. I'm told it's his custom. His hands match the marks on Maître Lassalle's arms. That will do.

PICHON (thinking) He asked one thing last night, through an interpreter. He speaks only Swiss German, and not much of that.

PICHON (neutral) He asked if someone could check the netting in the dome. For birds.

On screen: Inès (eyes down, pushing a mushroom slowly round the sauce with her fork)

She keeps pushing it until she trusts her face again.

PICHON (sad) Jean-Paul Morel woke at six this morning at the Hôtel-Dieu. He asked for his wife. Then he asked what day it was, and when they told him, he wept.

PICHON (neutral) The doctors think he'll be well. He wants to talk to me, at length. And to you, Madame Varga, when you can bear it.

PICHON (neutral) I told him that might be some time.

INÈS (surprised) It might not.

She is surprised to hear herself say it.

PICHON (happy) At seven this morning the minister rang the prefect to thank him for his long service and accept his wish to spend more time in Normandy.

PICHON (thinking) The prefect didn't know he had that wish until seven. He has it now.

PICHON (neutral) The wine. Four jugs. Datura, clove, and the ash of burnt paper. They're still working out what paper.

DEC (worried) And Vaulmont?

Pichon puts his fork down again. From his raincoat on the back of the chair he takes a small evidence bag and sets it on the cloth between the bread basket and the salt.

On screen: In the bag: a plain, heavy silver pocket watch on a short chain. White enamel face, twelve black numerals, no hands.

PICHON (sad) Released at four this morning. Bail of two million euros. The magistrate is a sensible woman. She wished to keep him.

PICHON (angry) Paid in full at three fifty-one, from a foundation in Liechtenstein. It didn't exist on Tuesday. It doesn't exist this morning.

PICHON (neutral) A car was waiting at the quai des Orfèvres. No plane. No train. He has simply gone.

PICHON (thinking) He left this. They took it from him at the Panthéon with his tie and his belt. At four, he didn't ask for it back.

Inès opens the back of the watch through the plastic with her thumbnail, the way she did on the Pont de Sully, a week and a lifetime ago. Where Lassalle's watch had nine little wheels, this one has a single engraved line.

On screen: Inside the lid, in a fine old-fashioned script: VII de IX

INÈS (scared) The seventh of nine.

Dec sets his glass down on the cloth very carefully, as if it might go off. At the next table someone laughs. A waiter drops a spoon. In the kitchen, somebody is singing.

DEC (worried) So there are eight more.

PICHON (neutral) There are eight more.

PICHON (neutral) The Swiss searched the forester's house last night. The black notebook and the board were in the stove, half burnt. Vey's ledger was not.

PICHON (thinking) Fabien says it went to the Seventh. So it went with him.

PICHON (thinking) One of the others is called the Keeper of the Last Hour. Though I'm told that's a story nuns tell each other in the evenings.

He slips the bag back into his raincoat.

PICHON (neutral) I'll keep this. As a reminder. I have a very bad memory.

PICHON (angry) Now. The cheese, the tarte Tatin, and nineteen hours of sleep. And neither of you is going to be in the next case of anything. You hear me?

DEC (happy) We hear you.

Pichon, who doesn't believe a word of it, calls for the cheese.

The staircase up to Atelier Morel. Four o'clock.

On screen: Inès (groceries on one hip, phone to her ear), Dec (two steps below, carrying a toolbox)

CLOTHILDE (happy) They've taken the wall down! The commune, this morning. The fire brigade did it. You can see the whole dial from the village. All nine hours.

CLOTHILDE (happy) Madame Jobin's nephew went up the ladder with a pot of grease and a library book. He's put the old twelve-hour wheel back. The one my sisters took out in sixteen ten.

CLOTHILDE (happy) It struck four just now. Four times. Not thirteen. The whole valley came out to listen. I cried.

CLOTHILDE (neutral) I've been on the plum brandy since lunch. Is he there? Your not-husband?

INÈS (neutral) He's carrying a toolbox.

CLOTHILDE (happy) Good. Give him a kiss from me. On the forehead. Don't let him make it into anything.

She rings off.

DEC (thinking) What did she want?

INÈS (neutral) Nothing. Coffee.

Atelier Morel. Five o'clock.

Low gold light comes through the skylight and lies across the benches and the jars of pigment like varnish.

On screen: The Allegory of the Sundial, back on its easel, police tape across one corner of the frame and an evidence tag on a string

It will be evidence for months, maybe years. But the magistrate agreed that a half-cleaned seventeenth-century canvas can't wait for the courts. Over the cheese, Pichon told Inès: she goes on, under seal. Something in her chest came loose that she hadn't known was tight.

She won't paint the word back in. A restorer doesn't. She'll stabilise the scrape, clean round it, and leave the gap honest. Anyone who looks properly will see where someone took a knife to the thing they were most afraid of.

Behind her, Dec kneels at the atelier door with the old lock half out, the one that has stuck every winter for four years. He has been humming for twenty minutes. He is very happy. She can tell from the humming.

On screen: Inès (in her apron, facing the easel), Dec (on his knees at the door, screwdriver in hand)

DEC (happy) This is a terrible lock. Who put this in? It's a crime against doors. I'm going to put in a five-lever mortice with a...

INÈS (neutral) Dec.

DEC (neutral) Hm?

INÈS (neutral) We're not partners.

The screwdriver stops.

DEC (worried) No.

INÈS (thinking) But this building has fourteen doors. Every lock is terrible. And I'm going to need somebody who can get into places.

She turns round. He is sitting back on his heels, mouth a little open, the gold light in his untidy hair.

INÈS (neutral) It's a job. That's all. It pays very badly.

DEC (thinking) How badly?

INÈS (neutral) Very.

DEC (thinking) Will there be tea?

INÈS (happy) There might be tea.

Then the grin comes, the whole enormous Galway grin, the one that looked at her through the back window of a police car eleven days ago.

DEC (happy) Ah, go on, then. Purely professionally.

INÈS (happy) Purely professionally.

Four floors down, the street-door bell rings.

Atelier Morel, the bench under the skylight. A few minutes later.

A courier in a motorcycle helmet wouldn't come up, wouldn't say who sent it, and was gone before Dec reached the last flight.

On screen: A small square parcel in plain brown paper, addressed in a neat old-fashioned hand in grey ink: Mme I. Varga. M. D. Hartley. Atelier Morel, rue Vieille-du-Temple.

They look at it without touching it. Then Inès puts on her white cotton gloves, because that's what she does, and slits one edge with her scalpel.

On screen: In grey tissue, an old enamel clock face the size of a saucer. Twelve black marks round the rim. No hands, and no hole for them. At the top, where twelve should be, a careful IX.

On screen: Under the IX, in the same grey ink, one word: HUIT

INÈS (scared) Eight.

Across the roofs of the Marais a church bell begins to strike six. She finds herself counting. Beside her, under his breath, in English, Dec is counting too. They both stop at six, and look at each other, and go on looking.

DEC (thinking) Well. That's not a partnership. That's an invitation.

Inès lays the clock face back in its tissue and closes the box. She takes off her gloves and pushes her glasses up with one knuckle.

INÈS (neutral) Get your coat.

The End
