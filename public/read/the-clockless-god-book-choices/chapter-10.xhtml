Pichon Listens, Sort Of

Pichon's office, police judiciaire, rue du Bastion. Monday 30 March, a quarter past twelve.

Fourth floor of a glass building. On the desk, an andouillette that smells like a farmyard that has been through something.

On screen: Pichon (napkin in his collar, fork in hand), Dec (talking fast), Inès (very still, hands in her lap)

Dec tells him everything, from the chapel to Vincennes. Pichon stops chewing only once, at the Seventh will collect. Inès puts her phone and a memory stick on the desk. He reads one photograph twice.

On screen: The notebook page on Inès's phone: 26/03 Gare d'Austerlitz + bd de l'Hôpital, 07h51, 9 min, OK. Aucune réaction.

PICHON (sad) Morel is at the Hôtel-Dieu. He doesn't know his name. Datura, much more than the others. He may come back. He may not.

PICHON (angry) And Lassalle. Datura in the blood, water in the lungs. He was alive when he went into the Seine. Marks on his arms where two hands held him. Large hands.

DEC (worried) And?

PICHON (neutral) On Sunday the prefect's office telephoned. Very politely. The prefect expects the heart attack to be confirmed.

PICHON (neutral) He'll be at the vigil, as guest of a friend. Whose foundation complains of an Irishman trespassing in Switzerland.

PICHON (sad) Your name is on a list, Monsieur Hartley. Not my list. Theirs.

DEC (scared) So what happens now?

Pichon folds his napkin.

PICHON (neutral) Now I arrest you.

The open-plan floor outside Pichon's office.

Twenty officers at twenty desks.

On screen: Pichon (cold, gripping Dec's arm), Dec (stunned), Inès (on her feet)

PICHON (angry) Declan Hartley. You are under arrest for obstruction of a criminal inquiry. Stand up.

INÈS (angry) Commissaire, that is not...

PICHON (angry) Sit down, Madame Varga, or you will join him.

He walks Dec slowly past every desk. By the lifts, a man in a good suit who doesn't look like a policeman holds a coffee, watches them go by, and takes out his phone.

In the lift Pichon lets go of his arm and watches the numbers go down, his face grey and old.

A holding cell in the basement.

A bench, a steel toilet, frosted glass. A steel door with a card reader. No keyhole.

On screen: Dec (on the bench), Pichon (in the open doorway, hands in his raincoat pockets)

DEC (angry) I hate this building.

PICHON (neutral) Everybody does. It was designed by a man who had never been arrested. I'm sorry for upstairs.

PICHON (thinking) The man by the lifts is the prefect's. For perhaps a day, they'll think I know which way the wind blows.

PICHON (neutral) On paper you were released at one. I'll sign after my dessert.

DEC (surprised) You believe us.

PICHON (sad) I believed you on the first day. When you told me about the tongue.

PICHON (angry) But if I go upstairs with photographs of a notebook I don't have and a sermon about a monk, they take my badge. Then there's no one.

He holds out a folded sheet of paper.

PICHON (neutral) A police officer reads a great many boring documents. Sometimes he leaves them lying about.

On screen: The photocopy: Autorisation de travaux, Panthéon. Contractor: Horlogerie Vaulmont Frères, Genève. Installation temporaire d'un dispositif d'observation de l'éclipse. Galerie haute du dôme, à proximité du point d'ancrage du pendule.

PICHON (thinking) The pendulum hangs from the crown of the dome. Sixty-seven metres of cable. A fine thing to hang other things from, if one were a radio amateur from Brittany.

PICHON (happy) I know nothing about radios. I know about lunch. There's a tarte Tatin in the canteen, and I've had a difficult morning. Mind the door. It's heavy.

His shoes go away up the stairs. The steel door stands wide open, its card reader blinking. Dec had very nearly worked that reader out.

DEC (sad) That's not fair. I'd have had that.

The steps outside. Thin March sunshine.

On screen: Inès (coat buttoned to the throat, angry and still as a painting), Dec (holding up the photocopy)

INÈS (thinking) The pendulum cable. It's a sixty-seven-metre aerial hanging in the middle of Paris.

DEC (worried) And I'm on a list. If the prefecture sees me near the Panthéon on Wednesday, I'm done.

His phone buzzes. He reads the email and starts to laugh, quite helplessly.

On screen: The email from Mlle C. Ferrand, Fondation Vaulmont: the Clockmaker's Cell will be installed in the north-east chapel of the crypt. Attached, a pass with Dec's grinning photo: PANTHÉON, and in red, ACCÈS TOUTES ZONES.

INÈS (worried) He wants you there.

DEC (thinking) Where he can see me. In the crypt, in a little room I built with a door that locks, while he's up under the dome with his radio.

DEC (happy) Nobody arrests a man Vaulmont put on his own guest list. The pass is the trap, and it's the door. And I know every way out of that room. I drew them.

Atelier Morel. Monday, eleven at night.

One lamp on the bench, orange cloud in the skylight. Dec is asleep on the green sofa with his boots on, talking.

DEC (happy) Ah, Mrs Kilbride, sure it's no trouble.

On screen: Inès (at the bench, pencil and loupe), Dec (asleep on the sofa)

Under the lamp: photograph twenty-three, her notebook, the REMIT page, Roux's drawings.

INÈS (thinking) Ne csak nézd, lásd. Don't just look. See.

On screen: The drawings: gate numbers XVII, IIII, XII, VIII... and nine rings of letters, each ring starting at a different letter

INÈS (surprised) Not decoration. A table. Count round each wheel from its own first letter.

She counts like stitches. Seventeen on the first wheel: R. Four on the second, which starts at B: E.

On screen: The notebook, letter by letter: R E M I T T E R

The ninth gate has no number. Only a blank square, and Roux's furious biro.

On screen: In the blank square: enveloppe scellée!!

She turns to photograph twenty-three. Nine ghost ticks, and at the foot of the ninth a short flat stroke. E or L. No word ends remitterl.

On screen: The notebook: REMITTERE

INÈS (sad) To send back. To let go. To forgive a debt.

INÈS (thinking) Mutuo datum remittere hora nona. What was given as a loan, to forgive at the ninth hour.

INÈS (angry) Not reddetur. Not repaid. Vey wrote the other word, and made it the word for his lock.

INÈS (angry) Their own lock says forgive. And in six hundred years they never counted round the letters.

She takes off her glasses, presses her hands to her eyes, and puts them back. Then she kicks the sofa.

DEC (surprised) Wha'? Mrs Kilbride? What?

INÈS (neutral) Get up. I've got the word.

The atelier. Midnight.

On screen: Dec (hair in four directions, elbows on knees), Inès (standing over him with the page)

DEC (happy) That's mad. You did that with a pencil.

INÈS (neutral) Don't make a thing of it.

DEC (thinking) And Roux's copy, up in the dome on the radio, opens with the same word. I was going to pick it.

DEC (worried) Vey cut by candlelight. Roux had a lathe and a grudge. I'd be there till Easter.

INÈS Now you won't have to.

DEC (sad) Remittere. Forgive.

A café on the rue Mouffetard. Tuesday, nine in the morning.

On screen: La Taupe (yellow raincoat, woollen hat, a hand-drawn map on tracing paper), Inès, Dec

LA TAUPE (happy) Soufflot found the hill full of holes and nearly had a heart attack. An old gallery under the rue Clotilde runs up to the east end of the crypt.

LA TAUPE (happy) To a shaft with a grille nobody's opened in two hundred years. It comes up in a little chapel off the crypt.

DEC (surprised) The north-east chapel. That's where they're putting my room.

That afternoon, four metres up a brick shaft, Inès clings to the rungs and doesn't look down. Through the rusted bars: the underside of pale new planks, stamped in blue by a timber yard in Montreuil.

DEC (happy) That's my floor. They've built my room right on top of their own back door.

DEC (thinking) I'll open it tomorrow, from the top. Nobody'll need it. But I like to know there's a way out.

Atelier Morel. Tuesday evening, under the north light.

Mademoiselle Ferrand answered Inès in twelve minutes: of course the restorer may stay with her painting. Monsieur de Vaulmont would be delighted.

On screen: Inès, Dec

DEC (worried) We're both inside, with a word for a lock in the roof and a back door in the cellar. And then what?

INÈS (thinking) You look at the door. I look at the painting. We see what's wrong.

DEC That's a terrible plan.

INÈS (neutral) Yes.

DEC (happy) Grand. I'm in.

What do you choose?
