Night Train to the Snow

The chapel under the Barrière d'Enfer. The deep niche behind the altar, in the dark.

Three people pressed into a hollow of cold stone, the stone door pulled almost shut. Inès feels Dec's heart hammering through his jacket.

The door swings open. Candlelight spills in.

On screen: Grey hooded figures (two by two, candles in their hands, faces lost in shadow)

Inès counts. Eighteen. Eight pairs stop on the stone steps in front of eight niches. The ninth pair goes on to the altar.

The niche with three idiots in waders pressed into the back of it, they leave empty.

The chanting stops. Somebody at the altar turns the pages of the ledger.

VOICE Lassalle has been remitted.

A man's voice, quiet, cultivated. Someone who has never had to shout, because people lean closer to hear him.

INÈS (scared) Warm oil. Roux said a voice like warm oil.

VOICE He was afraid. Fear is a debt like any other. He has paid it. Pray for him.

VOICE Brothers. Our restorer is close to the word. Our clocks are ready.

VOICE In seven days the moon will cover the sun. At the hour of None the voice from Allouis will speak for us, and the city will hear its debt called in.

VOICE And the Seventh will collect. Mutuo datum.

HOODS Reddetur.

INÈS (thinking) Given as a loan. It shall be repaid.

INÈS (scared) Our restorer. Our restorer is close to the word.

In the dark, Dec's hand finds hers. She grips it hard enough to hurt them both.

The candles go out pair by pair, the chant fading down the galleries. The last pair stops in the doorway to pull the stone door shut. One of them is far larger than the other.

On screen: The last hooded figure (turning back in the candlelight: a huge bald head, a little blue cuckoo clock tattooed on his neck)

The door closes. On the other side, a heavy iron sound, like a bolt going home.

The chapel, pitch dark, then one headlamp.

On screen: La Taupe (scared), Inès (shaking), Dec (headlamp on, breathing hard)

LA TAUPE (scared) We're locked in a cult's bedroom under the fourteenth and nobody knows we're here.

DEC (thinking) Ah, now. Locked's my department.

He puts his whole long body against the stone door. It won't move a whisper. His lamp swings round the walls, and stops.

On screen: Behind the altar, low in the wall, a rusted wrought-iron grille over a black hole the size of a dog kennel, with a big square box lock rusted almost solid. Cold air and running water come through the bars.

Dec unrolls the cutlery on the chapel floor like he is laying a table for Sunday dinner. His hands are shaking. By the second pick, they are not.

DEC (thinking) Old lever lock. Two levers. Not been opened since Napoleon was a boy. She's going to be a right...

The lock makes a noise like a kicked gate.

DEC (happy) ...there she is. Good girl.

LA TAUPE (happy) Drainage. Eighteenth century. Mon Dieu, it's beautiful. Follow me exactly. Exactly.

A closed courtyard behind a school, fourteenth arrondissement. Ten past two in the morning.

On screen: La Taupe (sitting on a bin, soaked), Dec (hair plastered flat, yellow mud on his forehead), Inès (glasses steamed solid)

Wet to the neck, all three of them start to laugh, so hard that a light comes on in a window above.

Inès laughs until it turns into something else, and then back. She wipes her glasses on the inside of her collar.

INÈS (neutral) I'm going to the Jura.

DEC (happy) Right. I'm going with you.

INÈS Why?

DEC Professional reasons. It's Vey's lock. If his clock's in the Jura, his lock's in the Jura, and I want to see it.

INÈS (neutral) Purely professionally.

DEC Totally.

The train to Basel. Thursday 26 March, morning.

Grey suburbs give way to green fields.

On screen: Dec (turning his cup round and round), Inès (notebook open, writing nothing)

DEC (sad) My father was a locksmith in Galway forty years. Hartley and Son on the van in red, before there was a son to speak of.

DEC (thinking) He used to say every lock's a story about what somebody's afraid of. Three deadbolts on an empty flat, he's not afraid of burglars. He's afraid of someone in particular.

DEC (sad) He died in twenty-one. Turns out I'm a very good locksmith and a very bad businessman. Somebody's painted the van white and does plumbing in it.

DEC So. Escape rooms. You build a story about being afraid, and people pay to get out.

He looks up. She is watching him over her glasses.

DEC (worried) Sorry. I talk. It's a thing.

INÈS (neutral) I know. Go on.

Basel station, under the big departures board. Twelve minutes to change.

A thin young man in a long grey coat steps off their train three carriages down, glances at the board, and looks away very quickly at nothing.

On screen: Dec (holding a pretzel the size of his head), Inès (her own bag of books), the young man in grey (pale, fair hair cut short, a choirboy's earnest face, a silver chain across his grey waistcoat)

DEC (worried) Inès. Don't look round.

She looks round. Of course she does.

DEC (thinking) The chain on his waistcoat. I'll bet you the rest of this pretzel there's a watch on it with no hands.

INÈS (neutral) I don't want the rest of your pretzel. Platform four. Walk normally.

DEC I always walk normally.

INÈS (neutral) You walk like a stepladder somebody's trying to carry.

The train to Delémont. Snow in the shadows under the trees, then everywhere.

The young man in grey sits at the far end of the carriage behind a Swiss newspaper.

On screen: Dec (peering past her), Inès (reading)

DEC (thinking) He's been on the same page since Basel. Pharmaceutical share prices. That's not a share-prices face.

INÈS (neutral) Then stop looking at him. You are less discreet than the mountains.

Her phone buzzes. She reads it, goes still, and turns the screen to him.

On screen: A text: Mme Varga. This morning at 07:51 every clock in the Gare d'Austerlitz and the traffic lights on the boulevard de l'Hôpital showed nine o'clock for nine minutes. The SNCF calls it a technical fault. Please tell me you are in Lyon with your mother. A. Pichon.

DEC (surprised) He gave you his number?

INÈS I took it off his card while he was eating.

She types with one finger: I am with my mother. Sends it. Puts the phone face down.

DEC (worried) Nine minutes of nine o'clock, in a station at rush hour. And the warm-oil fella said our restorer. Like you're theirs.

INÈS (angry) I'm not theirs. I'm nobody's.

At the far end of the carriage the choirboy turns a page. It is the same page.

Les Bréviaires, a halt on the snowbound plateau. Late afternoon.

Farmhouses with roofs almost to the ground, an onion-spired church. High on the hill, a square black tower and a broken wall.

On screen: Dec (in trainers, snow to the shins), Inès (in boots)

DEC (sad) Ah, God.

INÈS I told you to bring boots.

DEC I'm from Galway. We don't have weather. We have rain.

The young man in grey walks past close enough to touch. As his coat swings, Dec sees the round edge of a watch in the waistcoat pocket.

He walks up the single street, turns in at a gate, and is gone.

Le Balancier, the inn opposite the church. Evening.

Woodsmoke, melted cheese and wet dog. An old Bernese mountain dog asleep on the hearth. Inès gives it a wide berth anyway.

On screen: Madame Jobin (big and solid, edelweiss apron, beaming), Inès (firm), Dec (ears going hot)

INÈS (neutral) Two rooms. Please.

MADAME JOBIN (happy) Of course! On your honeymoon? Everyone comes to the plateau on honeymoon. It's the quiet.

INÈS (angry) No.

MADAME JOBIN (happy) Rooms three and four. They have a door between. It locks. From both sides.

She winks at Dec. The dog opens one eye at him with enormous pity and closes it again.

MADAME JOBIN (worried) And nobody goes up to the priory in winter. Two metres on the track. If it's the tower you want, ask Mère Clothilde at the museum. She knows everything up there.

Inès's room, a quarter to ten. The lights off.

On screen: Inès (long grey jumper, hair flattened at the back), Dec (beside her at the window, not touching)

High on the hill, a small light moves through the ruin. It goes behind the broken wall. Then it climbs slowly, as if carried up a stair inside the tower, and rests near the top.

It burns there, steady and patient, the way the chalk was patient.

At exactly nine o'clock by Dec's phone, it goes out.

DEC (worried) Somebody's up there right now. We could follow the tracks while they're fresh.

INÈS (angry) In trainers. At night. Into two metres of snow, after a man who stops clocks.

DEC (thinking) Or we trust a woman who thinks we're on honeymoon, and wait for a nun.

INÈS (thinking) She was right about the snow. I'll take her word on the nun.

DEC (worried) And the fella in grey knows where we're sleeping.

Inès looks at the dark where the light was. Then at the door between the rooms, and its lock.

What do you choose?
