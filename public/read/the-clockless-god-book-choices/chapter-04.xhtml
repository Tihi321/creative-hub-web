The Ninth Niche

A gap in a fence above an old railway cutting, fourteenth arrondissement. Wednesday, eleven at night.

Orange street light, wet brambles, a black cutting below. Somewhere a dog barks and Inès flinches.

On screen: La Taupe (small and wiry, shaved head under a black woollen hat, headlamp, olive chest waders, backpack held together with silver gaffer tape), Inès (navy trench, scarf tucked in), Dec (leather jacket, satchel)

La Taupe looks Dec up and down.

LA TAUPE (neutral) Too tall.

DEC (surprised) I'm what?

LA TAUPE For the galleries. One metre forty in spots. You'll crack your skull and moan. The tall ones always moan.

LA TAUPE (thinking) Your mother's hairdresser says you're afraid of everything.

INÈS (angry) Not everything. Heights. Dogs.

LA TAUPE Underground there's no heights and no dogs. Only water and the dead. You're fine with the dead?

INÈS (neutral) I restore paintings from the sixteen hundreds. Everyone in them is dead.

La Taupe considers this, and seems to find it acceptable.

LA TAUPE (happy) Bon. Waders.

She pulls two pairs from the backpack. They smell powerfully of old rubber.

LA TAUPE Headlamps. If you lose me, stand still and shout. If you don't stand still, I find you in three weeks and you won't be interesting any more.

Dec hops into his waders and catches Inès's eye.

DEC (happy) Your mother's hairdresser has a very complete family.

A dead railway tunnel, then a hole at the foot of its wall.

Black brick, dripping. A hundred metres in, a low gap half hidden by rubble. La Taupe lies on her back and slides into it feet first, like a letter into a postbox.

On screen: Inès (staring at the hole, jaw set)

Inès goes next, because if she thinks about it she won't.

The quarry galleries under Paris. Near midnight.

A crawl through yellow rock opens into a gallery as tall as a church nave. Pale limestone sweating with damp, black water round their ankles.

On screen: La Taupe (headlamp swinging), Inès (standing up slowly, amazed)

On screen: A plaque carved in the rock at a corner: Rue Saint-Jacques, 1781

LA TAUPE (happy) The street above us. They dug out the stone to build Paris, and every gallery is named for the street on top. Up there, someone's eating a kebab.

Behind them Dec crawls out, stands up, and hits his head on a ceiling exactly as tall as he is not.

On screen: La Taupe, Inès, Dec (rubbing his head, smiling bravely)

DEC (sad) Ow. No, it's fine. It's grand. I'm not complaining.

LA TAUPE (neutral) He's complaining.

INÈS (neutral) He's Irish. It's how they say thank you.

They walk for an hour. Inès loses her sense of direction in ten minutes. La Taupe talks the whole way.

LA TAUPE (angry) Spinoza was badly treated by everyone. Including Spinoza.

LA TAUPE (happy) And drainage. Nobody respects drainage. It's the most important art in Paris.

DEC (thinking) Is that you and Spinoza on the drainage side, or...

LA TAUPE Turn left.

They wade to the knees twice. In one gallery Dec has to turn sideways and hold his breath, and not one complaint comes out of him. Inès finds herself almost sorry.

A side gallery, the Barrière d'Enfer section.

The walls change. Long brown bones stacked like firewood, ends outward, and set into the stack at chest height a row of skulls, all looking the same way.

On screen: Inès (stopped, very still), La Taupe (quieter), Dec (close behind)

LA TAUPE (sad) The overflow. When they emptied the old cemeteries, the tourist part filled up. The rest went wherever there was room. The old tollgate's right above us.

Inès has cleaned the faces of martyrs and a Flemish field of plague dead. This is different. The skulls all face one way, patient, as if waiting for a bus.

DEC (worried) Niches. Look.

Along the walls of bone, at intervals, arched hollows a metre high, each with a stone sill. Some hold a scratched cross. One holds a jam jar with a dead flower in it.

INÈS (thinking) The ninth niche.

They count. La Taupe counts with them out loud, in French, as if counting sheep.

LA TAUPE ...sept, huit, neuf.

The ninth is empty. No cross, no jar. Smooth stone sill, smooth sides, a back wall of yellow limestone.

Dec puts his hand flat on the back wall and moves it slowly, the way Inès runs her fingertips over a canvas to feel where the paint changes. He stops. He pushes.

Deep in the rock, something goes clunk, like a big key turning in a big door. The back of the niche swings inward.

LA TAUPE (surprised) Oh, putain.

For the first time tonight she sounds about twelve.

The chapel under the Barrière d'Enfer.

A round room cut from the rock, the size of the atelier, under a low dome. The floor is dry and swept. Nine tall empty niches stand round the walls, each with a little stone step in front, like a place for a person to stand.

On screen: Inès (headlamp on the far wall, breath held), Dec (in the doorway), La Taupe (behind him)

On screen: Over a block of limestone altar, a clock face a metre wide carved into the rock and painted dull silver: twelve marks, no hands, and at the top a great IX

On the altar, on a wooden stand, lies a ledger bound in leather the colour of dried blood.

Inès goes to it before she can think, and turns the pages with the end of her pencil, as in an archive.

INÈS (thinking) The first half is old. Brown ink, the hands changing every few pages. Horae solutae. Heures payées. Hours paid.

INÈS (worried) The second half is new. Black ink. One neat hand. And a different heading.

On screen: The ledger page, headed DÉBITEURS: Delorme, Jacques, magistrat honoraire. Octobre. Une heure. / Aubry, Marthe, libraire. Janvier. Une heure. / Girardin, Abbé Luc, Saint-Médard. Février. Une heure. / Roux, Anselme, horloger. Mars. Une heure.

And under it, the ink so new it still seems wet:

On screen: Lassalle, Gérard, notaire. Mars. Remis.

INÈS (scared) The judge. The bookseller. The curate. Roux. Pichon's four cases.

INÈS (scared) And Lassalle. No hour after his name. Just remis. Delivered. Returned. Paid off.

On screen: Inès (pale), Dec (reading over her shoulder, knuckles white on the altar)

DEC (angry) Somebody wrote him off. In a book. Like a bill.

At the bottom of the page, set apart from the names, a note in the same neat hand.

On screen: The note: Selon la règle de l'Horloge de la Dette. Frère Anselme Vey. Prieuré de Combe-Noire, Jura.

INÈS (thinking) By the rule of the Debt Clock. Brother Anselm Vey. The Priory of Combe-Noire, in the Jura.

INÈS (surprised) Vey. The man who made Roux's lock. Roux said it: Vey kept the word in his book.

LA TAUPE (scared) Inès. Lamps off. Now.

The chapel, a heartbeat later.

Far off down the galleries, getting nearer: footsteps in water. Many of them, slow and even.

And voices, low, rising and falling. It takes Inès a breath to understand. They are chanting.

On screen: La Taupe (one hand on the stone door, the other reaching for Inès's lamp), Inès (frozen at the altar), Dec (staring at the doorway)

LA TAUPE (scared) There's nowhere to run. Only the niches. The one behind the altar is deepest. In, all of us, and not a sound.

LA TAUPE (angry) The cataphiles who see things down here are the ones they never find.

A light flickers far down the gallery. Candles, moving in pairs.

Dec stays where he is, eyes on the ledger: Roux's name, and the word after Lassalle's.

DEC (angry) Whoever wrote that is about to walk through that door. And we'd be in a hole, in the dark, not even knowing his face.

DEC (thinking) Roux said silver hair. A voice like warm oil. A watch with no hands. One look under the hood and we'd have a face to give Pichon.

LA TAUPE (scared) One look and they look back. I know these galleries. You don't.

LA TAUPE (angry) Keep that lamp on and you're on your own. I don't wait for anybody.

INÈS (scared) Dec.

DEC (worried) Tell me to switch it off and I will.

The footsteps reach the bone wall outside. The candlelight finds the edge of the open stone door.

Inès's thumb is on the switch of her headlamp. Dec's lamp is still burning, pointed at the door.

She has known La Taupe for one hour. She has known Dec for three days.

What do you choose?

  • Trust La Taupe: lamps off and into the niche
  • Keep the lamps on and see the face under the hood
