The Debt Clock

Inès's room at Le Balancier. Friday, six in the morning.

Dark blue window, the church bell across the road striking six, very slow. A laptop screen turned down as dim as it goes.

On screen: Inès (grey jumper, two pairs of socks, a cold cup beside her)

She counts the strokes and is relieved when they stop at six. Then she opens photograph twenty-three, raking light from the left, and enlarges the gap in the Latin line until it fills the screen.

INÈS (thinking) Whoever scraped the word went through the paint. Not through the ground underneath.

INÈS (thinking) And a painter who letters a motto draws it first. He marks the spacing with little ticks, so the word will fit.

On screen: Photograph twenty-three, enlarged: in the chalky ground under the gap, nine faint spacing ticks; at the start, the ghost of a curve that could be R, P or B; at the end, a foot stroke that could be E or L

She counts them three times. Nine. She writes it in her notebook.

On screen: The notebook: R/P/B _ E/L

INÈS (thinking) Nine letters. A word for what happens to a loan, at the ninth hour.

Through the connecting door Dec falls over something, swears, and starts singing in the shower with astonishing confidence and very little tune.

INÈS (neutral) His landlord was right about that.

The Musée de l'Heure, the old schoolhouse at the top of the village. Mid-morning.

A hand-painted sign on the door: Ouvert quand c'est ouvert. Inside it ticks, louder and less politely than Roux's shop. Painted farmhouse clocks, watchmakers' benches left as if for lunch a century ago, a whole wall of cuckoo clocks that Dec eyes with deep personal suspicion.

On screen: Clothilde (small and sturdy, red cardigan, steel spectacles, navy veil, snow boots, a coffee pot in one hand)

CLOTHILDE (happy) The couple at Madame Jobin's. The honeymoon that isn't. Coffee?

INÈS (angry) We're not...

CLOTHILDE (happy) Of course not. Nobody is, at first. Sit, sit. You're here about the priory.

On screen: Clothilde (pouring into three cups that don't match), Inès (suspicious), Dec (holding his cup in both hands)

INÈS How do you know?

CLOTHILDE (neutral) Nobody comes to Les Bréviaires in March except for the priory. And the young man who has rented the forester's house asked me yesterday whether two people from Paris had been asking.

CLOTHILDE (angry) Monsieur Ruel. Fabien. He signed the commune register like a schoolboy. I don't like him. He reads timetables for pleasure.

Inès puts the silver watch on the table between the cups. The amusement goes out of the old nun's face like light out of a valley under a cloud.

CLOTHILDE (sad) Ah. Yes. Then you'd better have the whole of it.

The museum, at the coffee table.

On screen: Clothilde (grave, hands folded well away from the watch), Inès (listening), Dec (very still)

CLOTHILDE (sad) Combe-Noire was a little priory. Twelve monks and some sheep. In thirteen forty-nine the plague came up from Basel and half the plateau died.

CLOTHILDE (thinking) One of the monks was a clockmaker, a new and strange thing to be. Anselm Vey.

DEC (thinking) The lock.

CLOTHILDE (angry) The lock, the clock, everything. He preached that God was a lender. That He had lent us every hour as a loan, and now the loan was being called in.

CLOTHILDE (angry) So Vey built the Debt Clock to keep the account. Nine hours on its dial, because the ninth was the hour of reckoning. He rang the valley's hours. When to work, to pray, to pay.

CLOTHILDE (sad) Whoever holds the clock holds the time of everyone who can hear it.

Inès thinks of Roux: every radio clock in France listens to the one voice. She can feel Dec not looking at her.

INÈS (worried) And those who didn't pay?

CLOTHILDE (sad) They were given the Grey Communion. Datura seed steeped in wine, cloves to hide the taste, and ash from the burnt accounts of the dead. That is what turns the tongue grey.

CLOTHILDE (sad) A man drank it and lost a day. He woke with the clock stopped at the ninth hour, and the monks said the hour had been taken as payment. He had the day missing. He had the taste in his mouth. So he paid.

The cuckoo clocks strike ten, one after another. Nobody speaks until they finish.

CLOTHILDE (happy) In sixteen ten my order, the Visitation sisters, came up with the bishop's soldiers. The brothers were turned out, the clock was walled up and the stair broken.

CLOTHILDE (neutral) The Brotherhood went down to the cities and became bankers. Much the same thing, with better shoes. I'm the last sister in the Jura. My turn to forget.

INÈS You haven't, though.

CLOTHILDE (happy) No. I'm very bad at it.

The museum, later. The coffee pot refilled twice.

On screen: Clothilde (thoughtful), Inès (notebook open)

CLOTHILDE (thinking) The sisters told a story that Vey repented at the end. That he wrote one last line in his great ledger, a confession, and it was scraped out.

CLOTHILDE (sad) My sisters burnt half the ledger when they walled up the clock. The other half they never found.

INÈS (surprised) Vey kept it in his book. That's what Roux was told.

She shows the nun the notebook page and tells her about the painting: the clock with IX at the top, mutuo datum, hora nona, the scraped word.

CLOTHILDE (thinking) Hora nona. In a monastery that is not nine o'clock. The monks counted from dawn. None, the ninth hour, is three in the afternoon. The hour of the reckoning.

Inès's hand goes to the cream card in her pocket. She knows it by heart. Panthéon. Le premier avril. Quinze heures précises.

INÈS (scared) Fifteen hundred. The ninth hour.

CLOTHILDE (worried) They've bought the priory, you know. Last autumn. A foundation from Paris with a name like a wine. Valmont. Vaumont.

INÈS Vaulmont.

CLOTHILDE (neutral) They changed every lock they could. Not this one. It's in the listed wall and the commune wouldn't let them.

She puts a black iron key as long as her hand on the table.

CLOTHILDE (happy) The abbess's key, to the tower door. It's been in my drawer forty years and I'm tired of it.

The far end of the museum, before a small glass case.

On screen: Dec (hands in pockets, reverent), Inès (beside him)

On screen: The reliquary case: a dark wooden casket bound with iron, and set in it a brass lock with nine thin worn wheels, its shackle open

DEC (thinking) It's his. Vey's. The little brother of the one Roux made. Look how thin he cut those wheels. With a file, by candlelight, in a plague.

INÈS (thinking) If it's open, the letters are still set. Can you read the word?

DEC (sad) Somebody's spun them. All nine. After. It doesn't tell you anything except he was very good.

On screen: Dec, Inès, Clothilde (coming up quietly in her snow boots)

CLOTHILDE (sad) The sisters found it in his cell, open, as if he had opened it himself before he died. My predecessor called it the only honest thing in the priory. A lock that had given up.

Through the tall window, the black tower of Combe-Noire stands against a pewter sky. A very long way up.

The priory of Combe-Noire. Friday afternoon.

A shelf of open hillside above the spruce. A broken grey wall iced with snow, and in its corner a square black tower. High on its face, a great iron dial three metres across, with a single iron hand.

On screen: Dec (snowshoes, borrowed boots stuffed with newspaper, snow on his knees), Inès (navy coat, mustard scarf, not a speck of snow on her)

Fabien was seen taking the little red train to Delémont at noon. Dec has fallen over four times on the way up. Inès waited each time without turning round, and he is grateful.

INÈS (worried) Count them. There are only nine.

On screen: The tower dial: nine marks where twelve should be, the single hand pointing straight up

The old oak door has a lock plate the size of a dinner plate. Dec rocks the abbess's key gently, feeling the levers like a tooth with his tongue. Something in the door groans like an old man getting out of a chair, and turns.

Inside the tower, then the chamber of the Debt Clock.

Hollow all the way up like a chimney. The old stair broken into stubs. A brand-new steel ladder zigzags up the walls, bolts still bright.

DEC (thinking) They've spent a lot of money on a heritage nobody's allowed to visit.

Near the top, behind him on the rungs, Inès says something quiet in Hungarian that is either a prayer or the opposite.

Through a trap in a wooden floor, the clock fills the room: a cage of black iron the size of a garden shed, huge hand-cut wheels, a stone weight on a chain hanging down into the dark.

On screen: The Debt Clock: at the front of the iron cage, a riveted iron box, and in its door a brown-brass cylinder with nine thin wheels

On screen: Dec (bare hand on the brass, like a hand on a horse), Inès (behind him, breath smoking)

DEC (surprised) It's the one in the drawings. The actual one. Six hundred and seventy-five years.

INÈS Can you open it without the word?

DEC (thinking) Machine-cut gates, you need the word or a drill. But hand-cut, by candlelight, by a man in a hurry because the world was ending? There's play. A hair.

DEC (thinking) Pull on the shackle, turn a wheel slow, and you feel the gate go past the bar. Give me a minute. Don't talk to me.

INÈS I wasn't going to.

DEC I know. I'm talking to me.

Eyes shut, he turns the first wheel. Nothing. Nothing. A tiny give.

DEC (thinking) One.

Snow blows in through the slits. He warms his fingers in his armpits and starts again. Four. Five. Six. The seventh is stuck with six centuries of grime. The ninth takes longest of all.

A sound so small he feels it more than hears it. The bar drops.

INÈS (thinking) What does it say? What word did you open it with?

DEC (surprised) Nothing. Q, F, Z, A... I went by the gates, not the letters. Somebody's had the wheels off and put them back in a different order. Recently.

The chamber of the Debt Clock. The box open.

On screen: Inès (turning brittle pages with her pencil), Dec (holding the box door)

Inside, a huge red leather book, its back half burnt to a black crust.

INÈS (thinking) Vey's ledger. Horae solutae. The hours paid. And the last page before the burn, in a big shaking hand...

On screen: The ledger's last page: lines of sprawling Latin, and on the very edge of the burn, half eaten by it, one word going off into the char: REMIT...

DEC (thinking) Remit. That's English.

INÈS (worried) It's Latin first. Remittere. To send back. To forgive a debt. It's cut off. It could fit the gap.

The other two things are new. A fat black notebook in neat blue biro, and a circuit board in a silver bag.

On screen: The notebook's first page: ALS162. Essais de substitution locale.

On screen: The circuit board, white paint pen: ALS162 / PROTO 3 / 162 kHz / 9 min

DEC (worried) Allouis. The voice the clocks listen to. Local replacement.

Inès turns the pages fast, the way she never turns pages.

On screen: The tests: 12/02 Bois de Vincennes, 400 m, 9 min, OK. / 05/03 Quai de la Rapée, 1,2 km, 9 min, OK. / 26/03 Gare d'Austerlitz + bd de l'Hôpital, 2,5 km, 07h51, 9 min, OK. Aucune réaction.

DEC (scared) That's Pichon's text. That was them. A test.

One line alone on the next page, written carefully, the thing he was working towards.

On screen: Panthéon, galerie du dôme. 1er avril. 14h51 à 15h00. Neuf minutes. Rayon prévu: 5 km.

INÈS (scared) Five kilometres. The whole centre of Paris. Every station, every traffic light, every hospital clock. At three o'clock, in an eclipse, with the city in the street looking up.

DEC (angry) And a man in a nice suit on the telly saying look, the clocks have stopped, God's calling in the debt. It's not a miracle. It's a radio.

INÈS (worried) Photograph it all. Mine's on four per cent.

He photographs every page as fast as he has ever done anything. He is checking the last when, far below, the oak door groans open.

The chamber of the Debt Clock. The light going.

Feet on the steel ladder. One light and quick. One so heavy the whole ladder hums.

INÈS (scared) Phones inside your clothes. Not your pockets.

He shoves both down his jumper against his skin. They are so cold he gasps.

On screen: Fabien (rising through the trap, grey coat, snow on his shoulders, face smooth as a plate), Inès (notebook in hand), Dec

FABIEN (neutral) Every hour is lent. Every hour lent is owed. You are in a private building, Madame Varga. Monsieur Hartley.

DEC We had a key.

FABIEN Of course you did.

On screen: Fabien, the bald man (surfacing through the trap, stooping under the beams, the cuckoo tattoo moving when he swallows), Inès

The bald man holds out his hand to Inès, palm up. Not threatening. Waiting, the way you wait for a child to give back what it should not have picked up.

She puts the notebook in it.

FABIEN And the board, please. And the book.

The bald man carries the ledger down flat on both palms, as if it were a sleeping animal. Fabien shuts the iron box and spins the nine wheels with one sweep, like shuffling cards.

From the trap, only his head and shoulders showing, he looks back.

FABIEN (neutral) Minus twenty tonight on the plateau, they say. The Clockless One lends us every hour. Tonight, perhaps, you'll learn the price of one.

The trap bangs down. Underneath it, the bright modern snap of a steel padlock. Far below, the oak door, and the abbess's key turning in it from outside.

On screen: Dec (staring at the trap), Inès (arms wrapped round herself, snow blowing in through the slits)

DEC (sad) Well. That's my key gone.

The wind rises to a howl. Inès moves a step closer to him, and then another.

What do you choose?
