Totality

The service stair inside the wall of the dome. Fourteen forty-one.

A stone spiral barely wider than her shoulders. A greasy rope for a handrail. On the inner side, a hand's-width gap and darkness dropping away.

On screen: Inès (one hand on the rope, eyes on the next step)

She doesn't look down. She counts, the way she counted Vey's wheels. One step, one number.

INÈS (scared) Forty-one. Forty-two.

By a hundred the stair has begun to tilt. She presses her forehead to cold stone and shuts her eyes.

INÈS (scared) Basszus. Basszus. Basszus.

At nine she froze on the first landing of a tower in Lyon. Her father sat beside her, sang a long song about a horse, asked: up or down? She said down.

INÈS (angry) I'm not nine.

She opens her eyes and climbs.

The gallery of the dome. Fourteen forty-seven.

A narrow stone ring, a waist-high balustrade, sixty metres of darkening air below. The pendulum's sphere a pinhead. The steel cable rises past her to the crown; from the crown a second cable, black and new, runs down the dome's ribs.

On screen: A tall brass cabinet against the gallery wall, domed like a clock case, green light glowing through its seams. On its doors, a cylinder of nine thin wheels. On its lid, an engraved clock face with no hands and IX at the top.

A hum she feels in her teeth.

VAULMONT ...and when the light is taken from us, my friends, we will see what we truly owe...

She walks to it with her hand on the wall, giving the drop nothing. Roux's wheels are crisp, perfect. She steadies her wrist, as for gold leaf on a halo, and turns the first.

On screen: The first wheel: R

Behind her, from the south stair, footsteps.

The gallery. Fourteen forty-nine.

On screen: Kessler (stooping out of the stair arch, filling the gallery from wall to rail), Inès (backing away)

He comes on slowly, the way you follow a cat you don't want to frighten into the road. She backs away round the ring, shaking from her teeth to her knees.

VAULMONT ...nine minutes, my friends. Only nine. The hour of None...

Below, a hundred voices gasp. Outside, thousands. A long rising sigh, like wind through trees.

The light goes out.

The gallery in totality. Fourteen fifty-one.

The dome turns a deep glowing blue, the inside of a mussel shell, a ring of pale fire in the windows.

Through the great doors, the traffic lights of the rue Soufflot flash red again.

On screen: On the wall beside the stage, a big station clock painted FONDATION VAULMONT. Its hands sweep round by themselves and stop at nine. Phone screens light the nave like fireflies. Someone cries that every board at Montparnasse says nine.

VAULMONT Mutuo datum. Reddetur.

Something explodes into her face. Wings, dust. Her hip hits the balustrade; for one endless second there is nothing on her right but blue dark. Then she is on her knees, arms round a baluster.

On screen: A grey pigeon caught by a wing and a foot in the conservators' netting between the balusters, beating itself against the stone

For a few seconds the sixty metres vanish. Something is more frightened than she is. She folds its wings under her palm and works the mesh free. Her hand shakes. The bird lets her.

Kessler has stopped two metres away, his head turned toward the bird. She holds it out to him in both hands, palm up, the way he held out his hand for the notebook in the tower.

On screen: Kessler (huge hands closing round the pigeon, over hers), Inès

His hands are very warm. He stands with the bird against his chest, still as a man in church, and she walks past him. Then she runs.

At the far stairhead a torch beam swings up out of the arch, and Dec behind it, gasping.

DEC (scared) Inès!

INÈS (angry) The cabinet! It's Roux's lock. You can't pick it. Use the word!

The torch finds the nine wheels. And behind Dec, coming up out of the same arch, unhurried, a small dark pistol in one gloved hand: Vaulmont.

Inès doesn't stop. A low iron door in the east pier opens on a conservators' stair, down to the nave, the microphone, the painting.

INÈS (thinking) You look at the door. I look at the painting.

She takes the steps two at a time into the dark.

The gallery. Fourteen fifty-six.

On screen: Vaulmont (silver hair a little disordered, pistol held loosely like reading glasses), Dec (back against the cabinet)

VAULMONT (neutral) Monsieur Hartley. Please don't touch that. Monsieur Roux is a genius, and a very rude one. You could be there with your picks until Easter.

DEC (worried) Yeah. I noticed.

VAULMONT (neutral) In four minutes the debt is called. Afterwards nothing bad will happen to you. We are not monsters. We simply keep accounts.

Dec doesn't step away. When he's frightened, he talks.

DEC (thinking) Can I tell you something about locks? My da used to say every lock's a story about what somebody's afraid of.

Behind his back his fingers find the first wheel. Already on R. The second: four, from B. They went over the offsets thirty times at the bench.

DEC (sad) This one's a monk in a plague. Built a whole religion on everyone owing everyone. And he's terrified. Of his own bill.

DEC (thinking) So he puts in his lock the one word he wants and doesn't think he'll get. And you lot keep his nine numbers six hundred years like a relic.

Third wheel. Fourth. Fifth.

DEC (angry) And not one banker or bishop among you ever counted round the letters.

VAULMONT (worried) What are you doing?

DEC (neutral) Opening it. With the word.

VAULMONT (angry) There is no word. There are numbers. The Rule...

DEC (thinking) Seventh. Eighth. All those people, to find a word that was in your lock the whole time.

DEC (neutral) It's not pay, Monsieur de Vaulmont.

He turns the ninth wheel.

DEC (sad) It's forgive.

On screen: The nine wheels: R E M I T T E R E

A small, deep, perfect click. The brass doors swing open an inch, the hum comes out loud, the green light.

On screen: Inside: a neat rack of black boxes with green lights, a thick cable from the dome, a car battery, ALS162 in white paint pen, a big red rotary switch

He turns it, hard, all the way round, until it clunks. The green lights go out down the rack. Silence fills the dome.

Vaulmont stares at the wheels. His lips move, spelling the word letter by letter, like a child.

Below, the traffic lights of the rue Soufflot turn green. The FONDATION VAULMONT clock lurches, sweeps round, stops.

On screen: The station clock: 14:58

Two minutes short of None. A puzzled murmur goes through the nave: people promised a miracle, watching it tidy itself away.

Then a woman's voice comes out of the loudspeakers.

The gallery, looking down at the stage.

Far below, in the TV light, a small figure in a mustard scarf pulls the cloth off the painting and holds photograph twenty-three to the camera.

INÈS (neutral) My name is Inès Varga. I restore this painting. This is their founder's last line. Someone scraped one word out. I've put it back.

INÈS (thinking) Mutuo datum remittere hora nona. What was given as a loan, forgive at the ninth hour.

INÈS (angry) And the clocks were not a miracle. There's a transmitter in the dome, built by the Fondation Vaulmont. Someone has just turned it off.

INÈS (angry) The wine you drank at half past two was meant to hold datura. It was swapped. The real wine is in a crate in the south transept.

In the silence, sixty metres up, Dec hears the pendulum go past.

The minister, empty pewter cup in hand, begins to laugh helplessly. The prefect has gone the colour of a church candle. The camera's red light stays on.

Dec turns. Vaulmont has lowered the pistol.

VAULMONT (sad) Forgive. Six hundred years.

DEC (sad) Yeah. Funny old thing.

Vaulmont looks at him with enormous patience, like a man considering a bill. Then he turns the pistol round and holds it out, butt first. Dec takes it like a dead fish.

VAULMONT (neutral) I'll go down by the stair. You'll forgive me if I don't wait for you.

The nave. Three minutes past three.

The light returns as a long silver dawn. Outside, the crowd cheers, a little embarrassed by its relief.

On screen: Pichon (beige raincoat, napkin still in his collar with a small brown stain, four officers behind him), Vaulmont (wrists held out together, like a ticket at a barrier)

PICHON (neutral) Auguste de Vaulmont. I'm arresting you for the murder of Gérard Lassalle.

PICHON (angry) And for poisoning Anselme Roux, Judge Delorme, Madame Aubry, the Abbé Girardin and Jean-Paul Morel. All alive, I'm happy to say, and very angry.

PICHON (thinking) You like to keep accounts. So do I.

Kessler comes out of the south stair with his hands empty and holds out his great wrists to the nearest officer, who goes white.

From the south transept, a crash, a yelp, a delighted young voice.

MOUSSA (happy) Monsieur l'agent! The thin one tried to take the crate. I'm sitting on him. Do you want him?

A young officer takes the pistol from Dec's fingers like scissors from a child. The pendulum swings past, and back. It never stopped. It kept its own plane while the city turned underneath.

On screen: Inès (on the stage, glasses crooked, scarf unwound to her knee, perfectly still), Dec (across the nave)

He lifts his hand, a little, uncertain.

After a moment, so slightly nobody else in the Panthéon would see it, she lifts hers.

What do you choose?
