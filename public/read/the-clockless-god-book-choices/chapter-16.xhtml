The Miracle at None

Atelier Morel. Monday, eleven at night.

One lamp on the bench. Orange cloud sits in the skylight. Inès's notebook lies open under the lamp.

On screen: Inès (at the bench, phone and pencil), Dec (on the green sofa, his phone in his hand)

Pichon's name glows on the screen. Dec's thumb hovers. He turns the phone face down on the cushion.

DEC (sad) No uniforms. We agreed.

INÈS (neutral) We agreed.

She has what the tower left on her phone, and what the Jura left in her notebook.

On screen: Inès's phone: the burnt ledger page, the last word running into the char: REMIT...

On screen: The notebook: R/P/B _ E/L

INÈS (thinking) Nine ticks. Five letters in Vey's own hand. Four to go, and the last is an E or an L.

She rings Les Bréviaires. Clothilde picks up on the second ring. A television mutters behind her.

CLOTHILDE (happy) The honeymoon that isn't. Is he alive?

INÈS (neutral) Asleep on a sofa. Ma Mère, I need a word. Nine letters. REMIT, then three I can't read, and the last is an E.

CLOTHILDE (thinking) The sisters always said Vey repented at the end. A lender who repents does only one thing, child.

CLOTHILDE (neutral) Remittere. He lets the debt go. We say it at every absolution. In remissionem peccatorum.

Inès writes it under the ticks. It fits, letter for letter, down to the flat foot of the E.

On screen: The notebook: REMITTERE

INÈS (sad) Mutuo datum remittere hora nona. What was given as a loan, forgive at the ninth hour.

CLOTHILDE (sad) And they scraped it off. Of course they did. Bankers.

CLOTHILDE (worried) Inès. Drink nothing they pour you.

She hangs up. Dec is sitting up, hair flat on one side.

DEC (happy) You did that with a phone call.

INÈS (neutral) Clothilde did it with a prayer. Don't make a thing of it.

His phone buzzes against the cushion.

On screen: Email from Mlle C. Ferrand, Fondation Vaulmont: the Clockmaker's Cell, north-east chapel of the crypt. Attached, a pass with Dec's grinning photo, and in red: ACCÈS TOUTES ZONES.

DEC (thinking) He wants me inside. Grand. Galerie du dôme, the notebook said. That's the walk round the outside, on every tourist map.

Fabien wiped the photograph. But Inès read that line over Dec's shoulder in the tower, by his torch, and said it out loud. She has said it to herself every night since.

On screen: Inès's memory of the page: Panthéon, galerie du dôme. 1er avril. 14h51 à 15h00. Neuf minutes. Rayon prévu: 5 km.

INÈS (thinking) Wait.

She opens the list of tests on her phone, all she kept of Morel's notebook.

On screen: Inès's phone: Bois de Vincennes, 400 m. / Quai de la Rapée, 1,2 km. / Gare d'Austerlitz, 2,5 km.

INÈS (thinking) Every test reaches further. Jean-Paul checked the range. On long wave, range means a long wire, hung high.

In Morel's drawer of gift-shop rubbish she finds a postcard of the Panthéon.

On screen: The back of the postcard: Le pendule de Foucault. Câble de 67 mètres, suspendu au sommet de la coupole.

INÈS (scared) Sixty-seven metres of steel, hanging free in the middle of Paris. It isn't only a pendulum, Dec. It's an aerial.

INÈS (scared) Not the gallery outside. The one inside, at the top, where the cable hangs from.

DEC (thinking) Then I swap the wine at half past two, the way Fabien said on the phone. And then I go up with the word.

The Panthéon. Wednesday 1 April, eleven in the morning.

The cell in the crypt is finished, and with the pass nobody stops him.

On screen: Dec (in the north aisle, beside a narrow door behind a pillar)

Kessler fills the arch of the roped south stair. The narrow north stair inside the wall has a new chain and a shiny brass padlock.

DEC (happy) Two minutes, that. Later, when nobody's looking.

The service passage by the south transept. Twenty-two minutes past two.

On screen: Moussa (tall, white jacket, tray under one arm), Dec, Fabien (on his knees in a flood of brass gears)

The CECI N'EST PAS UNE BOMBE crate goes over with theatrical surprise. Four hundred gears ring across the stone like all the coins in the world.

Behind the screen, four pewter jugs of the real wine go into the empty crate. Four jugs of vin chaud with a spoon of cocoa take their place.

MOUSSA (happy) Eleven euros forty an hour. For this, I want twelve.

At half past two the trays go out. The minister drinks. The prefect drinks. The cardinal's secretary asks for a second cup.

Dec watches them drink harmless wine and breathes out. Then he walks to the north aisle, not hurrying.

The north stair. Fourteen forty-six.

Vaulmont's voice fills the nave behind him. Everyone is facing the stage.

VAULMONT ...and when the light is taken from us, my friends, we will see what we truly owe...

On screen: Dec (kneeling at the padlock, two picks in his fingers)

Seventy seconds. The shackle drops, and he goes up into the dark two steps at a time, the word ready in his mouth.

By the easel, Inès feels a hum in her back teeth. It comes down the steel cable from the dome, and the brass sphere carries it past her.

INÈS (scared) Go on, Dec. Go on.

Forty steps up, a torch hits him from below.

On screen: Two men in dark suits with earpieces (on the stair below), Dec (blinking into the light)

Not police. The prefect's own protection detail, who answer to the man in the front row and not to any commissaire. They have just watched a contractor pick a lock twenty metres from a minister.

DEC (scared) There's a radio up there! On the cable! Two minutes, I only need two minutes!

They don't need two minutes. They need four seconds, and one of them twists his wrist until the picks fall.

The nave in totality. Fourteen fifty-one.

They are marching him down the north aisle when the light goes. The dome turns deep blue, and outside thousands of people sigh at once.

On screen: On the wall beside the stage, a station clock painted FONDATION VAULMONT. Its hands sweep round by themselves and stop at nine.

Phones light up all over the nave, every screen at 09:00. Through the great doors the traffic lights of the rue Soufflot flash red.

And the guests see it, wide awake and sober, cups in their hands. They will remember every second, because Dec made sure of it.

The minister stands up. The cardinal's secretary crosses himself.

VAULMONT Do not be afraid. For nine minutes the city will hear its debt. Mutuo datum.

VAULMONT Reddetur.

DEC (angry) It's a radio! It's a box in the dome! It's a radio!

DEC (angry) Inès! Tell them!

INÈS (scared) Nobody's listening.

At three the light comes back, silver and slow. Vaulmont lifts one hand, and the station clock lurches round to three.

VAULMONT And now, my friends, it is given back.

The nave stands and applauds. Some of them are weeping. The camera's red light stays on.

Roux & Fils, Horlogerie. Thursday, nine in the morning.

Two hundred clocks are ticking, every one of them right.

On screen: Roux (behind the counter, loupe on its chain, newspaper held at arm's length), Inès (on a stool, very tired)

ROUX (angry) LE MIRACLE DU PANTHÉON. Front page. With a photograph of a clock.

ROUX (angry) The Bourse lost billions in nine minutes. And somebody, it says here, has made billions.

ROUX (angry) Nom de Dieu de nom de Dieu. It's a radio. I told him I wouldn't build it.

INÈS (sad) Somebody else did.

ROUX (sad) Where's the Irishman?

INÈS (neutral) The prefecture let him go at six this morning. Monsieur de Vaulmont declined to press charges.

INÈS (sad) A foreigner, overexcited by an eclipse. He told them he'd hate to spoil such a beautiful day.

Atelier Morel. Thursday, five o'clock.

The easel is empty. Dec sits on the sofa, a bruise coming up on one wrist.

A courier has left two envelopes: one cream, one with a bank's blue logo.

On screen: Inès (holding a small cream card), Dec (watching her face)

On screen: The card, in a neat old-fashioned hand: With thanks for your cell. A. de V.

Clipped to it is a cheque: the fee for the Clockmaker's Cell, exactly as Dec invoiced it, to the cent.

DEC (sad) He paid me. And he let me go.

INÈS (neutral) Of course he did. He keeps accounts.

She opens the blue envelope.

On screen: The bank's letter: the loan on Atelier Morel is called in. The premises to be vacated by Saturday 11 April.

INÈS (sad) He's called in the atelier. My bench, my lamps, Jean-Paul's sofa. Nine days.

DEC (worried) Your painting?

INÈS (sad) Lent to the Panthéon, it says in the paper. By popular demand.

On her bench, under the lamp, her notebook still lies open at one word in pencil. REMITTERE.

She closes it.

The End
