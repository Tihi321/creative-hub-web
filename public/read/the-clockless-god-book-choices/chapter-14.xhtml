Caught by the Brotherhood

The chapel under the Barrière d'Enfer. Wednesday night, a heartbeat later.

La Taupe kills her lamp. Inès's thumb stays on her switch, and she doesn't press it. Dec plants himself in the doorway and holds his beam high down the gallery.

On screen: Dec (in the stone doorway, headlamp blazing), Inès (beside the altar, lamp still lit), La Taupe (flat against the wall, eyes shut)

LA TAUPE (scared) Putain. T'es un con. Un beau con.

The chanting stops. The candles stop. Then they come on again, faster.

DEC (worried) Bonsoir, les gars. Je suis... un touriste.

On screen: Grey hooded figures (pouring through the stone door two by two, candles in hand, faces lost in shadow)

Inès counts. It's all she has. Eighteen. Tonight they don't take the niches. They make a ring, and the three of them are inside it.

The biggest hood steps out. A shovel hand lifts Dec's satchel and pulls out the canvas roll.

On screen: The bald man (hood pushed back, the little blue cuckoo clock on his neck, Dec's roll of picks in one fist)

DEC (sad) Ah, non. Pas les couverts.

The ninth pair reaches the altar. One of them turns.

VOICE Monsieur Hartley.

Quiet. Cultured. Warm oil, poured slowly.

VOICE You were engaged to build my cell. Not to visit my chapel.

DEC (surprised) You know my name.

VOICE We know everyone's name. It is the nature of a ledger.

Dec swings his lamp full on the hood, and Inès's beam joins it. The man doesn't flinch. He lowers the hood himself, as if they had asked politely.

On screen: The face under the hood (about sixty, silver hair swept back, a calm, handsome face, amused grey eyes; under the robe, a silver chain across a grey waistcoat)

He draws a watch from the waistcoat and opens it toward them.

On screen: The watch: a white enamel face with no hands

DEC (scared) Silver hair. A voice like warm oil. A watch with no hands.

DEC (scared) You're Roux's gentleman.

VOICE (happy) Look as long as you like, Monsieur Hartley. You won't keep it.

The bald man lifts the lamp off Dec's head, gently, like taking scissors from a child. Then Inès's. He turns them both to the floor.

The chapel, by candlelight.

On screen: Dec (backing toward the altar, hands behind him, talking), Inès (beside him, white), the silver-haired man (at the altar, pen in hand)

Dec backs until his heels hit stone. He doesn't stop talking.

DEC (happy) Total malentendu. Mauvais virage rue Saint-Jacques. Signalisation pourrie, ici.

Behind his back, low at the rusted grille, Inès hears the tiniest scratch of steel. One pick, kept up his sleeve.

DEC (happy) Et puisque vous êtes là. La cellule. La porte s'ouvre dedans ou dehors? Dedans pour le drame. Dehors pour les pompiers.

VOICE (neutral) Dehors, je pense.

Scratch. Two hoods near the altar turn toward the sound.

Inès steps into the candlelight before her knees can vote.

INÈS (scared) Mutuo datum.

Every hood turns.

INÈS (angry) It's on my painting, under the varnish. Mutuo datum. Donné en prêt. Given as a loan.

INÈS (angry) Un prêt demande un prêteur. A lender, a borrower, a contract. Who signed for the city? Who signed for Roux?

VOICE (happy) Madame Varga. Jean-Paul told me you were minutieuse. Thorough.

He is enjoying this. She can hear it: a man who has waited years for someone worth explaining to.

VOICE (neutral) God signed. He lent the hours. Brother Vey only kept the accounts.

INÈS (angry) Datum. Given. Done. Then a hole where somebody took a knife. You've built a church on half a sentence.

VOICE (neutral) Then you know how the sentence ends.

Behind her, a small dry click. The grille screams open, and the sound circles the chapel three times.

DEC (scared) Taupe! Go!

La Taupe dives into the black hole headfirst, like a letter into a postbox. Water splashes. Then nothing.

The bald man's hand closes on Dec's shoulder. It isn't rough. It doesn't need to be.

On screen: Dec (on his knees by the open grille, one pick still in his fingers), the bald man (hand on his shoulder), Inès

A hood moves toward the drain.

VOICE (neutral) Laissez-la. Qui croirait une cataphile? Elles voient des fantômes ici tous les week-ends.

INÈS (thinking) Leave her. Who would believe a cataphile? They see ghosts down here every weekend.

VOICE (neutral) Mutuo datum.

HOODS Reddetur.

INÈS (sad) It shall be repaid.

The altar. Later. Candles only.

The silver-haired man opens the ledger to the debtors' page and uncaps a fountain pen. The nib whispers.

On screen: The ledger, two new lines in neat black ink: Hartley, Declan, serrurier. Mars. Une heure. / Varga, Inès, restauratrice. Mars. Une heure.

A pewter cup passes hand to hand round the ring. Two hoods hold Inès's arms. She clamps her mouth shut. A gloved thumb presses her jaw, almost kindly, until it opens.

On screen: A pewter cup engraved with IX

Sweet and dusty. Then flat and mineral at the back of the tongue, like cold wet stone.

INÈS (scared) Cloves. Cloves and slate. Roux said cloves.

Across the ring, Dec swallows, coughs, and finds her eyes.

DEC (scared) Inès. His face. Hold on to his face.

She looks. Silver hair. Grey eyes. The watch with no hands. She says it over to herself like a phone number.

VOICE (neutral) Don't be afraid. You owe us only a day.

The candles go very far away.

The Pont de Sully. Early morning.

Low sun on the Seine, green-grey water, bare trees at the tip of the Île Saint-Louis. A jogger passes and doesn't look twice.

On screen: Inès (sitting up on a bench, glasses crooked, trench coat damp), Dec (asleep against her shoulder, dried yellow mud to his knees)

Her mouth tastes of slate. She doesn't know why.

INÈS (worried) Dec. Dec. Why are we on a bridge?

DEC (scared) What... I don't know. What time is it? We're meeting La Taupe tonight. It's Wednesday.

INÈS (neutral) It's Wednesday.

She checks her watch.

On screen: Inès's watch, and Dec's beside it: both stopped at nine

Dec licks dry lips. She grabs his chin.

On screen: Dec's tongue, dull and grey, as if he's been sucking a pencil

He looks at her mouth and goes white.

He rips open the satchel. No pick roll. No cardboard tube of Roux's drawings. No silver watch.

DEC (scared) Gone. The watch. The drawings. Everything.

INÈS (thinking) Who took them?

He opens his mouth. Nothing comes.

DEC (worried) There was a face. I looked right at it. Silver... something.

DEC (scared) I had it, Inès. I had it, and it's gone.

She reaches for it too. Candles, a ring of hoods, a face. Where the face should be there is a smooth blank band, like the sundial before the raking light.

Her phone wakes in her pocket.

On screen: Friday 27 March, 08:58. Fourteen missed calls: La Taupe, Thursday 01:40 to Thursday 23:55. One message: je vous ai attendus à la sortie toute la nuit. T'ES VIVANTE??? appelle-moi

INÈS (surprised) Friday.

DEC (scared) It can't be Friday. It was Wednesday. Where's Thursday?

INÈS (scared) She waited for us at the way out. She rang all day Thursday. And we weren't anywhere.

His phone buzzes. He reads it and holds it out.

On screen: Text from Mlle Ferrand, Fondation Vaulmont: Good morning, Monsieur Hartley. Monsieur de Vaulmont looks forward to seeing you at the vigil on Wednesday. He hopes you are rested.

DEC (thinking) Vaulmont. The fella paying for my cell.

The skin on Inès's arms rises under her coat. She can't say why.

INÈS (worried) Say the name again.

DEC (worried) Vaulmont.

Neither of them knows why it makes them want to run.

Across the river, a church bell starts to strike. Inès counts every stroke without meaning to.

It stops at nine.

The End
