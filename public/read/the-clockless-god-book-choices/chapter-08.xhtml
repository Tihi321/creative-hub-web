The Grey Communion

Room four at Le Balancier. Saturday, a few minutes past four.

The white quilt, Roux's drawings, the tray. The cup is half empty.

On screen: Dec (cup in a shaking hand)

DEC (scared) Cloves. Cloves and slate.

Roux, in his head, a bit deaf and very angry: everything tastes of cloves. Dec sets the cup down with great care. Then he crosses the room in two strides, sticks two fingers down his throat and is sick into Madame Jobin's geraniums, thoroughly. He will later call it professional.

He kneels by the window, eyes streaming. His head feels light, like after the second pint. The tea or the fear, he can't tell.

DEC (thinking) Whoever left that tray is coming back. To see if it worked. To do the clocks.

He looks at the pot, still nearly full. Five seconds.

He pours it down the sink, all but a finger's depth. He half fills the cup with water and leaves it where a man would who drank half and fell asleep. He eats both biscuits, because that man would have.

Then he lies down on the quilt, facing the door, one arm hanging off the bed, eyes almost shut.

Room four. Forty minutes later.

Twice the room tilts, gently, like a boat, and tilts back. He bites the inside of his cheek.

The key turns in the lock.

On screen: Fabien (grey coat, a canvas bag on his shoulder, Madame Jobin's big iron key ring in his hand)

Calm and attentive, almost kind, like a nurse on his rounds. He looks into the cup, then the pot, and nods to himself.

Then he goes to work, quick and silent, like a man laying a table he has laid a hundred times. Dec's watch, set to 09:00. The travel clock, stopped at nine, a sliver of brass pushed into the movement. The cuckoo clock by the door, set to nine from a chair, its pendulum wedged.

Then he picks up Dec's phone.

Dec's heart stops, and starts again twice as fast.

On screen: Fabien (holding Dec's limp hand), Dec (eyes almost shut, not breathing)

The phone is locked. Fabien takes Dec's hand gently, the way you'd take a child's, and presses his thumb to the button. It opens.

He scrolls the photographs without a flicker, deletes them, empties the deleted folder, and sets the phone's clock to nine.

Last, he opens the wardrobe, takes out a stick of chalk and draws on the bare pine inside the door with his head on one side.

On screen: Inside the wardrobe door, in white chalk: a circle, twelve small marks, and IX at the top

At the door he looks back at Dec, and his lips move. Latin, very quiet. A prayer, perhaps. For Dec, or for himself.

The key turns. Dec lies staring at the chalk, so angry he can no longer feel his hands.

The village street of Les Bréviaires. Blue dusk.

Dec counts sixty, climbs out over the geraniums and slides off the porch roof into a snowdrift. It is not a rooftop chase. He will be firm about that afterwards.

On screen: Dec (crouching behind a woodpile, snow on his jacket)

Fabien walks up the street openly, past the lit windows of the workshops, to a small dark house at the edge of the forest. Smoke from the chimney. A big black four-by-four outside.

DEC (thinking) The bald man. He didn't take the train. He drove.

Fabien stops by the woodpile and makes a call, low and respectful, a junior clerk to a senior partner. Dec crouches behind a stack of logs ten metres away.

FABIEN (neutral) Yes, the Irishman. Half the pot at least. He'll sleep until tomorrow night. The photographs are gone. I saw to it.

FABIEN (neutral) The woman is at the museum with the old nun. She'll find him in the morning. She'll find the chalk.

FABIEN (thinking) And Morel? He'll have the word by Sunday? Good. And then... yes. Of course. As the rule says.

Behind the logs, Dec goes cold all over again.

FABIEN (neutral) The guests. The minister. The prefect, yes, he's confirmed. Two bankers from the Bourse, the journalist from the television, the cardinal's secretary.

FABIEN (happy) The catering is ours. The wine at half past two, half an hour before None. They'll wake remembering the eclipse, the clocks stopping and your voice. Only that. And they'll tell it on camera, all the same way. A miracle.

For the first time, there is warmth in his voice.

FABIEN (happy) And the Seventh will collect. Nine minutes of every signal in Paris, the markets in a panic. The fund's positions are... Forgive me. Not my concern.

FABIEN (happy) The train worked beautifully. I got off at Saignelégier and our brother drove me up. They walked straight in. Mutuo datum, Brother.

He hangs up. Dec shifts to ease his cramp, and the German geologist's boot slips off a frozen log. The top of the stack rolls into the snow, knock, knock, knock.

Fabien's head comes round.

A vaulted cellar under the hay barn. Dark.

Five stone steps down, and a smell so thick Dec could lean on it. In the grey light from a grating, rows of tall round cheeses ripen in perfect silence.

On screen: Dec (crouched between two racks, folded as small as six foot two will fold)

A torch beam comes down the steps. Over the cheeses. Over the wet flagstones. It stops a metre from his knees, on his footprints. Then it goes away.

FABIEN Rien. Cheese.

A grunt. A very large, very low grunt.

The door shuts. A wooden bar drops across it outside, clunk. The four-by-four coughs into life and its lights sweep away down the valley.

DEC (sad) Locked in. Again. That's three times this week. That's a personal best.

The cheeses don't reply. A bar on the outside. On the inside, no handle, no keyhole.

DEC (sad) At least it's not minus twenty. It's about minus two, though. I'm going to die in a cheese cave, and they'll put that on my headstone in Galway.

Outside the cellar. Twenty minutes later.

An engine, old and clattering, a fan belt screaming on the corners. Boots crunch. The bar lifts, the door opens, and Dec falls backwards into the snow.

On screen: Inès (torch in one hand, for reasons never established a ski pole in the other), Dec (flat in the snow), Clothilde (in the driver's seat of a battered blue van marked Fromagerie Cattin, cigarette in her mouth)

DEC (surprised) How did you...

INÈS (neutral) I went to your room to tell you about the page. Chalk in your wardrobe. Sick in Madame Jobin's geraniums. Your window open.

INÈS (neutral) Footprints from the window, up the street, to the logs, into here. Your feet are very easy to follow. They're very big.

DEC (worried) They know we know, Inès. And they've wiped my phone.

CLOTHILDE (happy) Get in the van. Arguing in the snow is for Protestants.

The back of the cheese van, down the switchbacks to Delémont. Night.

Cheese crates and diesel. Clothilde takes the hairpins far too fast for the night train to Basel.

On screen: Dec (on a crate), Inès (on the crate beside him, phone glowing)

He tells her everything, down to the wine at half past two, and Morel, and as the rule says.

INÈS (thinking) Your phone's wiped. Mine isn't.

INÈS (neutral) Mine had four per cent in the tower. Before it died I took the ledger, the REMIT page and the list of tests. Vincennes, the quai de la Rapée, Austerlitz.

On screen: Inès's phone: the burnt ledger page ending REMIT..., then the notebook's list of tests, ending Gare d'Austerlitz, 9 min, OK

INÈS (sad) Not the flyleaf. Not the Panthéon page. Those were on yours. It's not enough.

DEC It's not nothing.

The van swings round a hairpin. The crates slide, and she slides with them against him, and stays.

DEC (scared) I was frightened. When he took my hand to open the phone. I've never been so frightened in my life.

Inès says something in Hungarian, low and quick.

DEC (thinking) What does that mean?

INÈS (neutral) Nothing. It's Hungarian. It doesn't count.

In the dark her hand finds the sleeve of his jacket, and holds on all the way down the mountain.

What do you choose?

  • Go back to Paris and face Morel
