The Evidence

The vault

Ambience: water slapping concrete, a bar hammering the hatch above

The lamp is turned up on the plinth. Black water stands at the ankle and climbs. Above, boots cross the plates. A steel bar comes down once, then again, patient as a clock.

On screen: Mara (soaked, jaw scabbed, canvas bag on her shoulder), Idris (water to the knee, hair flat to his forehead, listening up)

IDRIS They've found a rhythm. I liked the anger better.

MARA A man can't hammer forever. Water can.

She sets the lamp down and turns the wick up until the mantles whiten. Her hand comes to rest on the plinth.

MARA The sluice is open. The sump will take us to the ledge, and the batteries sit high enough. That's the whole of the plan. I'll not improve it.

IDRIS And if they get the bolts?

MARA They can pull that hatch and come down the ladder at us. Or they can pull it and let the sea come down with them. They'll know which.

She swings the canvas bag onto the dry ledge and opens the lid herself.

The vault

Ambience: water slapping concrete, the bar still working overhead

The recorder lies open on the plinth, its spool bright under the lamp. She lifts the head block to check the tape path. Her hands stop.

Close-up: the tape spool in the lamplight, a clean transverse cut three-quarters across, the ends butted and held with thin copper foil.

MARA A splice.

Idris's humming stops.

IDRIS That wasn't on the transcript she read out. A bump like that would have gone on the page as a gap.

MARA It plays across a splice. It's a bump, that's all. But the sea doesn't cut tape in a straight line.

She turns the spool with her thumb.

MARA One join means somebody opened the spool to look and joined it back. Or spliced two records end to end. One recorder, two voyages.

IDRIS Play the whole of it. From the head. We've taken a snatch and stopped every time before this, or read another woman's transcript.

IDRIS (quieter) This time we take it end to end and do not stop until the spool runs out.

MARA Then stand where I can see you. And don't tell me what I'm hearing till I've heard it.

The vault

Ambience: water slapping concrete, the bar fallen silent above

He clips the recorder's line output to the small amplifier. The battery reads eleven point nine and will fall all night. Mara threads the tape, frees the stiff take-up reel, resets the counter, and presses play.

Sound: a wash of carrier hiss, a ship's bell, an engine's low beat

The first voice is not her brother's. A flat inventory runs under the bell and the weather.

A VOICE ON THE TAPE Watch changed at two. Pumps normal. Weather thickening from the southwest.

MARA A commercial recorder. Inventory, watch, the lot.

IDRIS The whole night's in it. Hours.

Mara lets it run. The spill on the floor finds her boot tops and keeps coming.

A VOICE ON THE TAPE Captain. The Levenia is on our port quarter and she's been there since the light failed. She's made no signal.

Mara's hand goes flat on the plinth and stays there.

DAN (on the tape, calm) How far?

A VOICE ON THE TAPE Half a mile. Closing. She's not showing running lights and she hasn't answered the lamp.

DAN (on the tape) Get off a flare.

A breath of silence, then a hiss and a distant pop.

Sound: a flare hissing up, then nothing answering, then cast iron on steel, the speaker overloading into a roar

Mara does not move. The roar cuts off clean and the water comes in behind it.

DAN (on the tape) She's holed us below the waterline. Two frames aft of the engine room.

DAN (on the tape) She didn't shear. She held the contact. She's backed off and putting the lamp on us now.

A VOICE ON THE TAPE Is she offering assistance?

DAN (on the tape) She's asking us to stand by and make no signal. That's a steamer that ran us down and now she wants to be our friend.

A VOICE ON THE TAPE Then she wants us quiet. How long have we got?

DAN (on the tape) Not an hour. Not if the bulkhead holds.

The voice goes away and comes back with a smaller number each time. Idris has both hands on the plinth's edge and is looking at nothing.

DAN (on the tape, closer now) This is the Amity Rose, sinking in the Farrow Sound, by the running-down of the Levenia, and it was no weather.

DAN (on the tape) She came up on us in the squall and she ran us down and she means to let us go, because the sea's tidier than a court.

DAN (on the tape) If the boat hears this, the boat hears it on the tide, the way the old man used to work it, and gets it to the air. Not to the office. Not to a file. To the air, with witnesses.

DAN (on the tape) Two of you, on the air. A man alone is weather. Two men is a court.

Sound: a ship's bell struck slow, three times, then the tape running into silence

The spool turns. The head comes round and bumps across the joined seam, and the carrier starts its inventory again, flat and patient, as if nothing had happened.

Mara reaches out and presses stop.

MARA Two takes. He recorded it twice. The first with the hull going. The second after the water took the power, on the recorder's own battery.

MARA It's all still there. The splice is where the power failed and he started again.

The vault

Ambience: water at the ledge, the lamp's low hiss

Idris has gone to the far wall and stands with his palm on the cold stone. When he speaks it is slowly, and not about the tape.

IDRIS All these months I've known the hull was cut and known my father's charge made the cut. I made my peace with that the night I told you the rest.

IDRIS What I didn't know was the name in the box.

He comes back to the plinth.

IDRIS A ship's name in a dead man's mouth is a rumour. A ship's name on a bill of sale is a fact.

Mara's fingers have found a waxed tube clipped under the battery. She unrolls a log page on the plinth and weights it flat with the lamp.

Close-up: a ship's log page, upright clenched writing, a smaller colder hand beneath the last entry.

Mara reads it aloud, low.

MARA 0200. Levenia four points on the port bow, no lights. 0212. Masthead lamp out. 0230. Struck aft of number two bunker. Signal from Levenia: stand by, make no signal.

Idris turns the page sideways so the light falls across the small hand at the foot.

IDRIS That's not his writing. That's a man writing cold, with the sea coming in under him.

MARA That's his hand at the foot of the page. He writes what he did with the evidence after the strike. He says: cutter rigged forward on her port bow. Steel mount recovered and stowed, marked as per the note.

MARA Then he says: saw to it the office had the ship's name and not his own. The ship above the water, not the ship beneath. Her registry in the box with the tape.

MARA Signed A.V.

IDRIS Alun Vane.

His voice is not his own.

IDRIS He wrote the registry in with the tape. He did that, and then he gave the office the name, and they killed him for a fall down a stair.

MARA Keep reading. There's a packet in the lid.

She has the oilcloth open on the plinth before he can answer, a brass tally and a folded paper, the ink gone brown along the creases, a company seal stamped hard at the foot.

The vault

Ambience: water at the ledge, the hatch above shifting once and going quiet

Idris reads the paper twice before he says anything at all.

Close-up: a bill of sale, the ink brown at the folds, the printed seal of Rourke Salvage and Towage Limited at the foot, a signature in a firm upright hand.

IDRIS The tally matches the note in my father's tide table to the numeral.

IDRIS The Levenia, sold in the spring to a company at St. John's. Rourke Salvage and Towage Limited.

IDRIS And Sella Rourke was the signing officer.

He sets it down very carefully, as though it might go off.

MARA Read me the date again.

IDRIS The spring after the sinking. After the loss.

Mara reads the small hand's last line once more in the lamplight, and the phrase stops her the way a steel dogging bar stops a door.

MARA The ship above the water, not the ship beneath.

She puts her finger on the three facts and holds them in a line, and says the shape of it aloud, because saying it is how she sees it.

MARA The Levenia struck and kept afloat long enough to put her lamp on them. Then she went down in the same weather. A ship on the bottom can't be sold in a spring sale.

IDRIS So what the office sold was not the ship.

MARA It was her papers. Her registry, her hull lines, her name, no questions asked. Sella's firm raised her, or says she raised her, emptied her, and put a hull to sea under that name with the numbers ground off.

MARA And a bill to prove she was the thing she wore.

IDRIS It wasn't the Levenia that struck us.

MARA It was the thing made to wear the Levenia's name. Two of them that night, and only one stayed long enough to offer a lamp.

MARA Your father saw which was which and wrote it plain in a cold room.

Idris puts his hand flat on the bill.

IDRIS And Alun Vane was on the sealing of her. He'd have known the certificate didn't match the steel.

IDRIS Sella told you there were three. Two are dead. If he was the third—

MARA Then Sella was the fourth.

Idris looks up sharply.

MARA I marked it when she said it and let it lie. It can lie a while longer. We can't prove a sealing from a paper in a box.

MARA We can prove the sale and the ship and the voice. A court can find the rest once a coast officer is in the room with her.

She folds the bill of sale and the log page and puts them inside her shirt, flat against her ribs.

The vault

Ambience: water slapping concrete, a steady drip from the bracket

She sets her pocket logger beside the big recorder. Idris watches her thumb the switch.

MARA Four minutes, maybe five. We say it once and we say it well.

She folds her hands and presses record.

MARA This is Mara Kell, engineer aboard the Steadfast, at the Farrow Islands. I have recovered a ship's recorder from a wreck in the Farrow Sound in about ninety metres.

MARA The hull is cut by a shaped charge. It is not a collision.

MARA The voice on the tape is Dan Kell, my brother, master of the Amity Rose, and it names the Levenia as the ship that ran him down.

MARA The papers with it name the Levenia again, sold after her loss by Rourke Salvage and Towage. That sale and the tape go together.

MARA The sea did not do this. If you are hearing it, make it official, because we can't.

She stops the logger and puts it inside her coat.

IDRIS You said if you're hearing it. You didn't say if once before that.

MARA I don't say things that can't be proved. The sky can prove it or it can't.

She feels the cold of the concrete through her boots and the water at her heels.

MARA Now the transmitter.

The vault

Ambience: water slapping concrete, wind audible in the hatch rim

They work at the plinth a metre at a time. Mara follows the wiring with her lamp, saying it back as she goes, the way a diver says back a schedule.

MARA One rule. The set doesn't choose, the key chooses, and the key is the tide.

MARA Float lifts, relay pulls, carrier runs while the water holds it. The float holds a level, not an hour. On a night like this the surge holds a foot of water out of the Sound and the key runs out with it.

MARA A tape would break in pieces. A man's words cut off in the middle.

IDRIS So we don't use the float.

MARA We don't cut it either. It's our proof, and the court will want it turning as his father left it. We bridge it.

IDRIS Find something that lifts where the tide lifts and stays lifted while the wind says so.

He looks up at the vinegary tangle of cable he hung in the ceiling in the years when nobody was listening.

IDRIS The anemometer. The cups come round on the wind, and a spindle with a contact makes a switch that keeps time to the weather and not to the sea.

MARA Both switches into one circuit. The float's contact stays where it lies, the cups' contact sits beside it in the same loop.

MARA A break in any part of a circuit stops the current. The key opens only when both switches are open. The one that's closed keeps its hand on the key.

MARA Water and weather take turns at it. Wire it.

He wires. She crimps. The pair goes up the wall and into the conduit to the regulator's house, out to the roof, along the guy wire to the spindle that hasn't been asked to do a day's work since the office stopped paying for it.

The battery reads eleven point two when they are done.

The vault

Ambience: water at the ledge, wind rising in the hatch rim

The recorder sits at the foot of the plinth with its head open and the tape wound back to the first word. Idris winds the spool and checks the path. His hands are steady.

IDRIS Play it end to end. The first take and the second after the splice.

IDRIS He asked for two of us on the air.

MARA You take the head of it. I'll take the tail.

She puts one finger on the tape and thinks about the sky.

MARA The ionosphere's a wall tonight. Everything under thirty meg swallowed, and no way to know when the storm punches a door through its own absorption.

MARA The tape's five minutes. The door gives you three of them if you're lucky, and the ship that's listening needs a watchkeeper awake.

She looks at the drip on the bracket. It has found a new seam and works at a tick in four seconds, and it no longer sounds like a clock.

MARA That sounds like a tide coming back.

IDRIS It is a tide coming back.

MARA Then we don't wait for the door. We open it.

IDRIS How does a woman open the sky?

MARA The fleet's been sitting on seventeen all night with nothing to hear. They'll hear a carrier come up.

MARA We run the tape and the inventory and the bell, and the first one who writes down the time and the frequency is the witness. Then the storm does what it does.

Above them the rock thuds. Water comes into the sump at a steady rate, and the hatch above is a metre of concrete and two men who are by now out of patience.

IDRIS It won't stand open for a woman who's late.

MARA Then we're not late. Thread it.

Picture: Mara Kell and Idris Vane in the flooded vault, the tape threaded through the open recorder on the plinth, the lamp whitening the concrete, both of them bent over the machine with the black water at their boots.

He winds the spool and checks the path a second time.

IDRIS The battery will run the tape and the lights. The lamp over the hatch is the last thing.

MARA Then we save it for the last thing.

She sets her thumb on the key. The carrier meter answers, and the needle climbs and sits steady at fifty watts.

The wind finds the cups on the roof. The wire to the anemometer carries the spindle's turning down into the vault. The needle stays climbed, and the machine keeps its one rule.

IDRIS I've never asked you what you'd do after.

MARA After what?

IDRIS After this. If the sky opens.

MARA I'd stand a court and hear a name read out, and then I'd want my boat and a straight run of weather.

MARA Ask me again when we're dry.

He almost smiles. Then the hammering on the hatch stops, and they both look up.

IDRIS They've stopped.

MARA Then they've gone for a spanner.

She leans to the microphone, the head of the tape comes round, and the station's old oscillator puts its rising tones out into the storm.

MARA Now. Run it.

Above them, boots run across the plates toward the lighthouse stair.
