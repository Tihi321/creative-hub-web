The Light

The weather station, morning

Ambience: wind at the eaves, a kettle ticking, gulls on the rock

Sunlight on the salt-stiff window. The Sound beyond runs long grey swells with white on their shoulders. A pot of porridge steams on the paraffin stove, stirred with a screwdriver.

On screen: Mara (coat for a blanket, hair flat, four hours' sleep in her face), Idris (sleeves up, bowl in hand, stirring badly)

IDRIS There's more of everything than there was. Or I'm seeing it fresh.

MARA The wind dropped at six. Barometer's climbing. That's the weather, and nothing else.

He sets a bowl in front of her and sits.

IDRIS The Petrel comes back at ten. Reeve's on the radio. Coast guard launch with her or behind her. And a cutter out of St. John's nobody told me about till it called St. Bride's at four.

MARA Cutter's for her. The launch is for the evidence.

IDRIS Or the launch is for us. We took a ship's recorder and went into a vault we were forbidden.

MARA I know what we did.

IDRIS I'm not sorry. I want that written down somewhere, so it's clear.

She eats. The porridge is bad, and neither of them says so.

The west slip

Ambience: swell against stone, a launch engine idling

The Petrel stands off the head, grey and patient. The coast guard launch comes in behind her, low and grey, a rib on its davits. Sun on wet concrete.

On screen: Mara (hands wrapped in grey rag, standing straight), The officer (young, careful, notebook already open), Reeve (coming up the slip with his log), The young deckhand (cap in his fist, waiting behind)

The launch touches. The officer steps off with two men and a case.

MARA Mara Kell. Engineer. Steadfast, off the lee.

THE OFFICER I'll take your account in the station, Ms. Kell. Twice, if you don't mind. First for the spoken record, then for the book.

MARA Once to the book and once to the machine. You'll have both.

She has the canvas bag open before he asks.

Close-up: the bench in the station, and on it the recorder in its foam bed, the log page under a brass tally, the bill of sale brown at the folds, the pocket logger beside them.

THE OFFICER And this is everything?

MARA Everything from the bottom and everything from her lid. My own voice is on the small one. I made the account at four in the morning and I'll stand to it.

He writes twice, once fast and once slow, and Mara watches him do it.

The vault

Ambience: drip on the bracket, a lamp's low hiss

The water is down to a skin of silt on the concrete. Two men with a camera and a lamp tent. The transmitter sits on its plinth, quiet, its wiring tied neat.

On screen: Mara (lamp raised, pointing), The officer (crouched at the plinth, camera strap round his neck)

MARA There's the set. Bolted through the plinth, wired to the bank behind it. His father left it like that and I left it like that.

THE OFFICER And the float switch?

MARA Still in the sump. It's turning with the tide this minute. I didn't cut it. I bridged it.

She takes his lamp and holds it for him herself.

MARA A break anywhere stops the current. The float's contact and the cups' contact sit in one loop. Whichever one is closed keeps its hand on the key.

THE OFFICER So the wind ran your broadcast. Not the tide.

MARA The wind ran the key. The tide ran it when the wind failed. Neither one chose a word.

He photographs the rim of the flood hatch where a bar has bitten the steel, and she stands back and lets him.

THE OFFICER Who's the certified party on this site?

MARA There isn't one. There's me, and I'm an engineer. That's all I'll sign to.

He writes that down too.

Little Farrow, 12:00

Ambience: water at the steps, wind light from the west

Full sunlight on the black rock. The rib waits at the foot of the slip. Sella comes up the path from the station alone, coat brushed, hair set, folder under her arm.

On screen: Sella (grey coat, gold pin, pale eyes steady), Mara (on the steps above her), The officer (at the rib, warrant in hand), Reeve (top of the slip, log under his arm), The young deckhand (behind Reeve, cap in hands)

There is no hurry in any of it, and no guard at her elbow.

SELLA Mara Kell. You'll find I kept records. Every meeting, every hour.

SELLA You'll find your own signature on a contract with a clause about securing the site. You'll find it does not say what you decided it said.

MARA Then a court will read it out. That's what courts are for.

Sella arranges her coat and looks past her at the tower, at its white lamp turning.

SELLA Little Farrow was my father's station, girl. I signed a paper over that lamp when I was twenty-two years old.

She says it the way a keeper reads a barometer, and the officer puts her aboard.

The rib goes out toward the cutter.

On screen: Reeve (coming up level with Mara, log closed), The young deckhand (watching the rib shrink)

REEVE Let her talk. In that room she's the injured party, not the accused.

REEVE She was raised on this island's books. The Rourkes were lighthouse people before they were salvors. She knew the name Vane off a station list.

REEVE She knew what a tide switch was as soon as she could read one. Nobody told her what his father built. She knew the shape of it and it frightened her, and that's why the Petrel came out so fast.

MARA You'll put that in your log?

REEVE It's already in it.

The west slip

Ambience: gulls, water slapping the steps

The coast guard launch holds off the slip. The young deckhand stands with his cap turning in his hands.

On screen: Mara (hands folded, patient), The young deckhand (cap turning, jaw working)

THE YOUNG DECKHAND Second engineer. Forty-one. The Amity Rose was the ship my uncle went down in, ma'am.

MARA Say it to the man with the book, not to me.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND I have. He'll have it. He asked me would I stand to it in a room with a judge in it and I said I would.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND We were told it was weather, and we were paid not to wonder. Every watch for two years I've wondered.

He turns the cap round once more.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND There were four went down in her, ma'am. My uncle, and then three from the engine room the water took getting the boats away. Isaac Prouty and the two Aitken boys from Catalina. The office only ever sent us word about the one. My mother wrote twice for the others and got no answer either time.

MARA Put that down as well.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND I'll put it down.

MARA You'll lose your ticket.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND Maybe. I'll have the plate on my mother's wall mean what it says. That's worth a ticket.

He goes up the slip toward the station, cap back on his head.

The weather station, afternoon

Ambience: an anemometer turning overhead, pages turning

Three days of work in one room. A clerk's laptop glows beside the old desk. A grey case with initials on the clasp sits open, and its contents fill three tables.

On screen: Mara (standing at the window, arms folded), Idris (beside the clerk, bent over the transcript), The officer (at the desk, pen moving), The shipping authority's man (chart folder under his arm, doubtful)

THE CLERK The tape's thin here, Mr. Vane. Two words gone.

IDRIS It says she held the contact. Put the gap in the line and write my father's name under it. Alun Vane. He wrote the registry in with the tape.

THE CLERK I've written it three times now.

IDRIS Then you'll write it a fourth.

MARA Fourth time's on the record. Let it be on the record.

The shipping man opens the charts and sets them down at the end of the desk.

THE SHIPPING AUTHORITY'S MAN The lane stays shut until it's surveyed. That's the rule.

THE SHIPPING AUTHORITY'S MAN The rule was made for a reason, though the reason got lost somewhere.

IDRIS It got lost in a contract. The lane was closed because a hull was put on the bottom of it, and then it stayed closed because closed is cheap.

IDRIS Survey two points off the sound. Mark the wreck as a wreck, not an obstruction. Open the lane with a rule that publishes every abandoned hull in it.

IDRIS Not in an office. Published. So the next man who finds the tide running against a bell can look it up.

The man writes it down and says the authority will have to argue about it.

IDRIS I've time.

MARA Then sign me off the survey. I'll not certify a thing I watched her crack the floor with.

The officer looks up, pen poised.

THE OFFICER I'll want that in the book as well.

MARA You'll have it in the book.

The lighthouse, morning

Ambience: sea below, gulls, a spanner on iron

The tower stands white and streaked in the sun. A tarpaulin at its foot is spread with a gearbox in pieces. Sunlight on oil.

On screen: Mara (boilersuit, sleeves rolled, a main bearing in her hand), Idris (on a crate, coat open, holding the old one)

MARA Fixed white, one flash every ten seconds, fifteen miles. That's the specification and that's what the chart will say.

MARA It ran on the tide for twelve years and on the wind for one night. Now it runs on a shaft and a bearing and a winding I can service.

MARA Which means it'll keep running after I'm gone. That's the whole point of a machine.

IDRIS Can it still be keyed? For the record. Not for—

MARA I've left the tap. Put a signal on this tower any night you like.

MARA But the lamp turns steady and the pulse doesn't go out with it. The pulse is finished. That was a message and it got through.

MARA What's left is a light.

IDRIS Good. That's what it was for.

She holds out her hand for the old bearing and sets it down beside the new one.

The west slip

Ambience: a slow engine in the Sound, water at the steps

The Steadfast waits out in the Sound with her engine turning over slow. The tower is lit. The wind is light, and the sky over the west is the colour of a cut on the horizon.

On screen: Mara (bag at her feet, coat on), Idris (hands in pockets, hair pushed back with his wrist)

MARA Nine days and the engine's still running with a bent stanchion. I'll want a fortnight on her before I trust her again.

IDRIS I'm staying.

MARA I know.

IDRIS You knew before I did. It's not that I've nowhere to go. I've a flat with a damp wall and a woman two doors down who'll have given my plants up for dead.

IDRIS It's that the radio room wants someone in it and the station wants a man who can sign for the power, and the tower's got a light in it again.

IDRIS I'll do the weather. The authority will pay a man to keep an observation station and a beacon. If they won't, I'll argue with them.

MARA You're terrible at arguing.

IDRIS I'm better than I was. It's the company I've been keeping.

She holds out her hand, then takes it back and hugs him instead, badly.

MARA His name is on a warrant and a registry and a charge sheet. It's written down in five places and none of them rub out.

IDRIS That's all I wanted. Twelve years. That's all I wanted.

He holds on anyway.

IDRIS Send me a signal when you're past the cape. Any old night. The tap's there.

MARA I will. And if you don't answer it, I'll come back and take the radio room apart looking for the fault.

IDRIS There won't be a fault. It'll just be me, being slow.

MARA Then I'll wait. Machines can wait. So can I.

Steadfast, dusk

Ambience: a steady diesel beat, sea under the forefoot

The wheelhouse glass runs with spray. The chart is squared on the table, the log open, the tide table tucked behind it.

On screen: Mara (at the throttle, coat off, sleeves up, the brass key at her throat)

She writes in her square hand, then reads it back.

Close-up: the ship's log, one new line in Mara's hand: 20:52, Little Farrow abeam to starboard. Lamp burning, fixed white, ten seconds. Lane open. Under way.

MARA Never a fault. A man, and then his father, keeping a light on.

She looks to starboard. Little Farrow stands black against the last of the sky, and the tower puts its white arm out over the water.

MARA Here is the rock. Here is the channel. Here is the way through.

Ahead, the Sound opens into the lane, and a freighter's lights are coming down it out of the west.

MARA Go on, then. Go on.

She opens the throttle, and the Steadfast takes the swell under her forefoot and puts her shoulder into it.

Picture: Mara Kell standing at the throttle of the Steadfast at dusk, the tug's bow lifting into a light western swell, the lit tower of Little Farrow behind her to starboard and a freighter's lights opening ahead in the lane

She watches the light turn, then turns her face to the channel and holds her course.
