The Broadcast

The vault

Ambience: storm against rock, the sump drinking

The lamp burns low. The tape runs. The carrier meter jumps with each gust, and the water in the sump has found a new level and holds it.

On screen: Mara (sleeves cut back, hands raw, watch on the meter), Idris (headphones half on, one ear free, crouched at the receiver)

MARA Cups are turning. The bridge is taking what the float gives it.

IDRIS Hear that squeak in the pipe? That's them. The cups run the key now. Not the sea.

The meter kicks. The oscillator puts out its rising tones and the flat note.

MARA That's a bite. It went out.

IDRIS Flat on forty. Twenty's a wall of noise. Seventeen's got a carrier from Reykjavik, coming and going like a door in a gale.

MARA Then keep the door. Tape's loaded, head's clean, battery says nine point four.

IDRIS Round and a half in the bank. Not two.

MARA Then the window opens in the first hour and we take it in the first hour.

Sound: a wash of garbled voice breaking up, a woman reading a gale warning in pieces

IDRIS There. Hear her? Gale warning out of Reykjavik. Three bars.

MARA Play it.

The vault

Ambience: the recorder running, the sump ticking

Idris hits the play lever. The heads lift, the carrier climbs, and the oscillator takes the voice and hurls it up the conduit.

On screen: Mara (both hands on the plinth, eyes on the meter and not the machine), Idris (microphone taped to his knee, leaning in)

DAN (on the tape, thin, through the key's cut) If anyone picks this up, it's Dan Kell off the Amity Rose.

The five-second key cuts him in the middle of a word. The flat note follows.

MARA That's the first bite. Wait for the cups.

They wait. The wind backs a point and the meter kicks again.

DAN (on the tape) She didn't shear. She held the contact.

Sound: cast iron on steel, the speaker overloading, the key cutting it dead

IDRIS (laughing, raw) That's the collision. On the air. Any man holding a dial from Rockall to the Grand Banks just heard one steamer run down another.

MARA How many ears?

IDRIS Every ear up at this hour with a reason to be listening. It's not a call. It's a transmission. Goes out to whoever's there.

MARA Battery.

IDRIS Eight point nine. Holding.

The vault

Ambience: the recorder running, a storm easing a fraction

The receiver on the corner crate gives them the Sound in fragments.

On screen: Idris (receiver to his ear, face turning), Mara (at the plinth, counting keys by the meter)

A TRAWLER VOICE (over receiver) What the devil's on seventeen? It's a bell and a man's voice, and I've heard a bell like that at a funeral.

A SECOND VOICE (over receiver) Take it down, Erskine. Take it down and put the hour on it.

Sound: a flat American voice cutting across the channel

HALCYON RIDGE (over receiver) This is the Halcyon Ridge, bound for St. John's. We have the signal, the bell and the voice, and we are recording it on the bridge. All ships with a watchkeeper, take this down.

Idris turns from the set, and in the lamplight his face is younger than she has ever seen it.

IDRIS That's a real ship on the lane. She's writing it down.

MARA Keep the tape round. He names the Levenia in the last bite and the last bite hasn't gone yet.

IDRIS Mara. If the window shuts before the end...

MARA Then the next thing on the air is us. Two witnesses, saying it plain.

The Petrel

Ambience: engines labouring, a speaker on the chart shelf

Grey murk, deck lights burning, the Petrel standing off the west slip. Sella comes into the wheelhouse still fastening her coat over her nightdress. Reeve stands at the chart table with the log open and nothing written in it.

On screen: Sella (coat half buttoned, pale eyes hard), Reeve (log open, pencil still), The young deckhand (wet through, cap in his fist), The big man (behind her, torch in hand)

SELLA Turn that off.

REEVE It's every channel, madam. Seventeen. The working channel. A Norwegian asking can we confirm the registry.

SELLA Then name the source as interference. An unlicensed transmitter putting out false evidence, and any man who repeats it repeats a lie. Cut the beacon circuit and it stops. Get the torch man up to the vault.

The young deckhand comes in from the deck and stands by the door and looks at her.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND Mrs. Rourke. Was the Levenia ours?

SELLA Don't be absurd. It's a name on a voice in a storm.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND Only, my uncle served on the Amity Rose. He went down in her. We had a service and a plate on the wall at home, and my mother cried at it.

The bridge goes quiet. The receiver gives them Dan Kell, plain, before the key takes him.

DAN (over receiver) It was no weather.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND Was that a lie? All of it?

SELLA Get out.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND Not until you tell me whether my uncle was let drown by a ship with your name on it.

Sella looks at him. Her voice stays level, and this is the last time that works.

SELLA Reeve. Take their names.

Reeve closes the log.

REEVE (carefully) The Halcyon Ridge has logged it and is bound for St. John's. The trawler's asked the coast station to relay. This is not a valve on a beacon, madam. I'll not be the man who cuts a wire a court is listening to.

He steps back. The men behind her step back with him, and she is alone in the middle of her own deck.

SELLA The torch. You work for me.

The torch man sets the torch down against the bulwark and steps back too.

The vault

Ambience: a bar working concrete somewhere above

The loader stops. The meter lies flat against its stop and the oscillator's tone goes out.

On screen: Mara (output cable in her hand, going still), Idris (at the terminal bed, understanding)

IDRIS That's the feed. They've cut it. We've the battery and the keying, but the tower puts it out. We're a voice in a box.

Mara is quiet a moment. She looks up at the lamp bracket, then at the recorder, then at the hatch.

MARA A beacon isn't a transmitter. A beacon is a light.

IDRIS The anchor light's cut.

MARA The lamp in the tower isn't. There's a battery bank the size of a truck and a pump crew's cable coils and a man with an iron who knows every wire in this rock.

She sets the lamp in the bracket by the hatch and swings the coil onto her shoulder.

IDRIS A line up the outside of the tower. In this.

MARA The tower's a mast. The lamp burns and the tape goes out through the lamp. That's the whole of it.

IDRIS There's another window in forty minutes and it's the last one. Go up the tower and you'll miss it.

MARA Then you'll be down here and I'll be up there.

She puts the pocket logger, the log page and the bill of sale into his hands.

MARA If they take me, you've the evidence and the voice. Say it into the microphone and be two witnesses instead of one. Your father kept the registry. Let him have his say.

IDRIS Mara.

MARA I don't need the window. I need the lamp.

The lighthouse

Ambience: wind on iron, sea on rock

The tower is salt-pitted iron bolted to the seaward end of the rock. The wind on it is a wall. The lamp room at the top is dark.

On screen: Mara (coat knotted, cable coiled across her back, hands wrapped in rag), Idris (below at the tower base, looking up)

Picture: Mara Kell climbing the outside of the lighthouse tower in the storm, a coil of cable across her back and the black sea below her working like poured iron, the lamp room dark above and Idris Vane a small figure at the base

Halfway up, a gust takes her feet off the rung. She hangs from one hand and waits for it to spend itself.

MARA Counted. The trays and the spine conduit and where the salt's eaten the steel. Hands know what the head forgets.

She puts her feet back on the iron and climbs.

At the top she finds the terminal block behind the lamp and lays her wire on it. Her fingers are dead and steady.

MARA Down the spine. Into the cable way. Into the vault. Don't lose the way at the turn.

The wire runs down into the dark. The wind howls through the gallery.

The vault

Ambience: the sump steady, a low hum climbing

In the vault the lamp goes white and the glass above them takes the load. The carrier meter kicks, the oscillator puts out its tone, and the tape comes back with the pulse running ahead of it.

On screen: Idris (microphone in his fist, standing tall, face wet), Mara (entering through the cable way, hands bleeding, coat torn)

Sound: the five-second pulse, then the tape behind it

Idris leans into the microphone and gives the room, and the tower, and the sky the news.

IDRIS This is Idris Vane at the Farrow light. The voice you've been hearing is Dan Kell, master of the Amity Rose, and the hull's cut and it wasn't weather.

IDRIS My father is Alun Vane, keeper of this station. He built this transmitter and he kept the registry with the tape, and he saw to it the office had the ship's name and not his own.

IDRIS Two of us here. That's what he asked for. Two witnesses on the air.

Mara comes down the last rungs and stands beside him.

MARA Say my name too.

IDRIS Mara Kell, engineer of the Steadfast, brother of the voice you're hearing.

MARA Now let the tape finish. It's got one last thing to say and it's the second take.

The vault

Ambience: the recorder running, the wind gone round

The wind backs into the last quarter and the cups ease. The float takes the key with the tide's late surge behind it. The keying finds its own rhythm in the two together, and the tape runs unbroken.

On screen: Mara (at the plinth, rag round her hands), Idris (receiver to his ear, not breathing)

DAN (on the tape, whole and clear) A man alone is weather. Two men is a court.

The last of it goes out through the lamp and down the tower and into the air, and the meter stays up a moment after it, as if the sky were reluctant.

IDRIS The fleet heard it.

MARA The Halcyon Ridge heard it.

IDRIS The coast station at St. Bride's. A watchkeeper took the record to the desk with both hands.

MARA Then it's done. It's out of our hands and into a book.

The west slip

Ambience: rain easing, water at the steps

The rain thins. The Petrel stands off the slip with her boat alongside. The crew are quiet, and none of them look at the slip.

On screen: Sella (coat buttoned to the throat, hat square, pale eyes fixed ahead), Reeve (at her elbow, log under his arm), The young deckhand (by the wheelhouse door, marlinspike in his belt, watching her)

Sella walks down the west slip between her own crew like a woman crossing a court. She says nothing. She steps into the launch without a hand offered.

REEVE Mrs. Rourke.

She climbs in and gives him nothing. The launch turns into the murk and the Petrel's engine beats away toward the Sound.

THE YOUNG DECKHAND She never asked me my uncle's name.

MARA Write it down. The officer will want it, and it'll be the first time anybody's written it in the right place.

The young deckhand nods and turns his medal over and over in his fingers.

The weather station, radio room — 09:20

Ambience: wind at the eaves, a set with the door open to the weather

The bag is on the bench: the recorder, the log page, the bill of sale with its tally, and Mara's pocket logger. Idris stands at the set with the door open to the wind.

On screen: Mara (hands wrapped in clean rag, sitting at the bench), Idris (handset to his ear, listening, still)

Close-up: the bench in the radio room, the recorder in its foam bed, the pocket logger beside it, a folded log page weighted with a brass tally.

The brass tally reads on one face in small worn numerals; the log page carries two hands, the upper one upright and the lower one smaller and colder.

IDRIS (into handset) Say that again.

He lowers the handset and holds it against his chest.

IDRIS It's not a loss in the register any more. It's gone over as a criminal matter.

MARA Say the rest.

IDRIS The Amity Rose, sunk by the running-down of the Levenia. The registrar's office is reopening the registry. The coast guard wants the position and the depth.

IDRIS It's a crime, Mara. It's not weather. It's a crime.

Mara rests her hand on the bench and lets that sit.

MARA He wasn't lost the way they said he was lost. He was run down by a ship with a name and a signature and a bill of sale.

She goes to the door and stands in the wind with the lamp turning above her.

MARA It's a beacon. It always was.

She turns back into the room.

MARA There's work to do before the light can be left. Get the launch loaded and don't drop the logger.
