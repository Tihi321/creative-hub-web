The Storm

Steadfast, wheelhouse — 02:40

Ambience: wind screaming in the rigging, hull groaning

The barometer needle is jammed against its lowest pin. Rain runs sideways across the glass. Every light in the wheelhouse is on, and the wind makes the bulbs stutter.

On screen: Mara (boilersuit on, boots laced, log open but unread), Pavel (storm coat, slumped at the winch drum beyond the door, head back)

Mara climbs the last step and reads the strain meter by the lamp. The needle trembles near the red.

MARA Twenty fathoms out. Holding. That's all she can do and all I can do.

Sound: a gust slamming the tug over, a crack running down the mast

She waits for the roll to give her back, then crosses to the radio.

Sound: sand-coloured hiss on every channel, no edges to it

MARA Survey channel's a blank. Emergency channel's a blank. That's not jamming. Jamming has a rhythm.

She turns the tuning dial slowly, once, twice.

MARA The sun's thrown something at us and the sky's stopped bouncing. No skip, no relay, nothing. Hours, maybe a day. It always comes back.

She takes a mug of tea out into the wind and puts it in Pavel's hands.

On screen: Mara (oilskin over her boilersuit, rain on her face), Pavel (storm coat soaked, eyes red-rimmed, hands wrapped round the mug)

PAVEL I can't raise them.

MARA The Petrel came through at ten. Nothing since.

PAVEL Nothing since.

MARA There's nothing on the air for anyone tonight. Not them. Not the coast station. Not the fleet.

He drinks. The wind takes the steam sideways off the mug.

PAVEL How long, this dead air?

MARA Hours. A day at the worst.

PAVEL Then she cannot call her cutter. She cannot call anybody. That woman will be standing on her own bridge with nobody to order.

MARA She'll have her own crew.

PAVEL She has a crew that wants to be paid and a ship that is dragging.

He says it flatly, not as a warning. Mara studies the rain.

MARA You're supposed to be watching me.

PAVEL I am watching you. I am also sitting down. It is not the same thing.

On screen: Pavel (looking up at her, tired and honest)

PAVEL The chain out there is doing more guarding than I am. You could take the boat. I would have to get up.

MARA You'd fall down.

PAVEL I would fall down.

He drinks the tea. A gust lays the tug over and both of them brace.

MARA Get some sleep in the cabin. I'll watch the chain.

PAVEL I will not sleep. But I will go and be off my feet in a warm place.

He goes below. Through the glass, the station light burns on the hill, steady as a clock in a room where everything else has come apart.

MARA Somebody left that burning. I know who.

Steadfast, foredeck — 03:50

Ambience: wind dropping to a hard breeze, rain easing

The rain thins and stops. Between cloud and sea a strip of cold grey opens in the east, the first light refusing to be morning yet.

On screen: Mara (lamp clipped on, dinghy painter in hand), Pavel (at the rail above, coat undone, not guarding)

The sea has flattened into a hard chop. The tug rides it without slamming.

MARA The centre's coming over. Twenty minutes of this, maybe less.

PAVEL Then it comes back from the other side.

MARA Then it comes back from the other side.

She looks across the water. The west slip is a black line in the grey. Beyond the point, a shape of deck lights swings through ninety degrees and back.

MARA The Petrel's dragging. Look at her. She's working her anchor, and her crew are all up on the foredeck with torches.

PAVEL A lee shore and a survey ship. That is a bad night for a claim file.

MARA Bad night for a bag left in an empty office.

She gets her leg over the rail. Pavel does not move to stop her.

On screen: Pavel (hands on the rail, face set, rope burn pale on his wrist)

PAVEL If she asks me, I will tell her the truth. I watched the chain, and the chain held, and then you were gone. She can put me off the ship for it.

MARA Then don't be on the ship. Go below and sleep like a man who wasn't asked.

PAVEL Go. Before the wind comes back. Then it is her word against weather, and weather is a better liar than me.

She drops into the dinghy and takes up the oars.

Picture: Mara Kell rowing the small dinghy from the Steadfast toward the west slip in the grey lull before dawn, Pavel a dark shape at the rail behind her, the Petrel's deck lights swinging beyond the point as she drags

The west slip

Ambience: slack water knocking at wet steps, wind sighing down the rock

The slip comes out of the dark a length at a time. Weed on the steps is slick and green and shining under her lamp. The skiff is gone.

On screen: Mara (lamp in hand, water to the knee of her boot, looking up the empty slip)

No man stands on the slip, and no man stands at the top of it. Above, the station windows are lit.

MARA Empty. All of them on the water keeping her off the rocks.

She climbs the steps, quick and quiet, and passes the ringbolt where the skiff had lain.

MARA She's out there with torches and men and a dragging anchor, and she left her own house with the lamp on.

The weather station, office — 04:10

Ambience: anemometer turning overhead, a lamp hissing, rain still draining off the roof

A lamp burns on the desk and a folder sits squared to the edge of the wood. The passage beyond is dark and smells of paraffin and salt.

On screen: Mara (dripping, lamp low, eyes moving over the room), The big man (in the passage doorway, coat, heavy), The thin man (behind him, coil of rope on his forearm)

Mara finds the entry closet and the bag where it was stowed, canvas and salt-stained. The recorder is inside its padded case, heavier than a thing that size has any right to be.

Close-up: the canvas bag open on the office floor, a steel capsule inside a foam bed, a pencilled label on the case in a small older hand: AV

MARA (quiet) Twelve years in the water and still warm to the hand.

She puts the strap over her shoulder. The door opens behind her.

THE BIG MAN Put it down.

She takes a step sideways so the desk is between them, and her hip knocks the lamp. The light swings across the wall and comes back.

MARA The boat's dragging. You've got men on the deck and a man on the bridge. Do you want to be counting a recorder with me, or counting your own men off a lee shore?

For a whole second, both of them look out of the window at the swinging lights beyond the point.

On screen: The big man (reaching across the desk, quick for his size), The thin man (rope coming off his forearm), Mara (bag strap wrapped twice round her fist)

The big one gets the strap in his fist and hauls. Mara goes with it, off her feet and into the desk edge, and the room goes down to grey window light.

The weather station, passage — 04:12

Ambience: a rising keening crackle from the radio room, a voice shouting

Down the passage comes a sound like a man dragging a chain across a steel deck, and under it a voice in pure panic.

On screen: Idris (coat torn, face white, an arm through a radio's open back, shouting), The big man (torch swinging toward the sound)

Sound: every light in the station going out at once, a chair clattering

IDRIS Fire! Fire, the set's lit up, get out, get out of the building!

The keening rises. Sparks crack along the passage floor in the dark. The big one lets go of the strap to put his torch on the sound.

Mara drives the heel of her hand into his wrist. The torch spins away and rolls.

On screen: Mara (under the thin man's arm, bag held against her ribs)

She ducks the rope and goes through the window, and the glass gives with her, and then she is on wet turf with the recorder against her side.

She lies a moment while the rain does what it likes. Something hot runs down her jaw. Her hand comes away red.

MARA Shallow. Under the ear. It'll keep.

She gets up and runs for the tower.

The lighthouse

Ambience: wind returning from the southwest, rain coming on again

A low concrete box at the foot of the tower, its door swollen shut with wet. Above it the black gallery waits.

On screen: Mara (bag over her shoulder, blood drying under her ear, shoulder against the door)

She puts her shoulder to the door twice before it gives. The dark inside smells of cold iron and old paint.

MARA Counting. The plan was drawn in the dark and so am I.

She goes along the wall with her hand flat. The pump room. The pipe gallery. The floor plates.

MARA Pump room. Gallery. Plates. And the last square of concrete, set in the floor, with a ring in it.

She finds the ring and gets her fingers through. The hatch comes up an inch and a half and sticks.

Sound: the hatch falling back with the flat crack of a slammed door

She gets her boot in the gap. The second time it slips. The third time it comes all the way up, and the dark below smells of cold concrete and dead water.

On screen: Idris (at the outer door, soaked, out of breath, sparks still in his hair)

IDRIS Mara.

MARA You should be counting your own men off a lee shore like the rest of them.

IDRIS The radio room's not on fire. It only sounds like it. I put a hand across the set's back and the whole lot lit up like a bonfire.

MARA Come down. Fast.

He comes. She swings the recorder over her shoulder and lowers herself into the dark, and he follows her down.

IDRIS The big one's torch is on the roof and the thin one's shouting about the building.

MARA Then shout back at him from the bottom.

The vault

Ambience: wind howling in the flood hatch above, water slapping the sump

Her lamp finds the dry ledge, the rusted cable heads, and on it the steel cabinet with its guts showing. The transmitter is bolted to its plinth, wired to the batteries.

On screen: Mara (lamp set on the ledge, jaw bleeding, bag on the concrete), Idris (below the hatch, hauling on the ring)

IDRIS In? The hatch won't answer to me from underneath.

MARA Get the bar. The iron one in the corner. Then drop it.

He drags it round. She wedges one end under the hatch rim and swings her weight onto the other.

Sound: the hatch slamming shut, the ring dropping into its seat

On screen: Idris (hands on his knees, breathing hard, looking up), Mara (ear against the concrete, listening)

Above them, boots cross the plates. A bar comes down on the hatch once, twice. The hammering stops and voices argue.

IDRIS That bar on the lid. They've found the bolts.

MARA They'll need a spanner and daylight. It's a flood hatch, not a door. It was never made to be opened twice in one night.

IDRIS The sump. The tide's coming back. If I pull the sluice board off the sump wall it'll flood the floor, and they can't come down through this hatch without opening it against the whole rising sea.

MARA Do it.

He wades to the sluice and drags the board free. Black water spills out and spreads across the concrete, cold and quick, rising past her boots to her knees.

On screen: Idris (water to the thigh, lamp angled down, breathing fast), Mara (bag lifted clear of the water, against her chest)

IDRIS It'll be up to the ledge by five. The batteries sit high. The set sits high.

MARA Then we sit high and let them argue with a flood.

She sets the recorder on the dry ledge and stands with her hand on the transmitter's plinth.

MARA The storm's peaking. That means the sky's full of noise and the radio's dead in every direction. No cutter. No Petrel. Nobody listening.

IDRIS Nobody in the world can hear us tonight.

MARA Not tonight.

She looks at the plinth and the float switch and the battery bank, and a slow, cold certainty takes hold of her.

MARA The storm that killed the radio is going to give it back. That's what a geomagnetic storm does. It lifts the sky and then it lets it down.

IDRIS The ionosphere. When it settles, the layer gets low and dense and the low bands carry clean. The old hands used to wait for it.

MARA Then we wait for it. And when the sky answers, we don't send a survey report. We send the tape.

Picture: Mara Kell and Idris Vane sealed in the flooded vault beneath the lighthouse, knee-deep black water around the plinth, the recorder on the dry ledge, both of them looking up at the iron hatch while boots hammer the concrete above them

IDRIS They're hammering again.

MARA Let them. We've got a transmitter, a recording, and a battery bank that doesn't read folders.

IDRIS And a window. There'll be a window.

MARA Then we find the window, and we're ready for it when it opens.

Above them the bar comes down on the hatch again, and this time it does not stop.
