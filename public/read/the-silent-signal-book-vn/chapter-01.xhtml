The Contract

Steadfast, 02:00

Ambience: diesel hum, water slapping steel

The engine room is warm and dim, brass and oil catching the torchlight. Mara walks its length, touching things.

On screen: Mara (navy boilersuit, sleeves rolled, torch in hand, brow furrowed)

MARA Water on the plate. Chain groaning. That shackle forward, still not seized.

MARA Machinery gives back what you give it. Every time.

She rests her palm on the generator housing a moment, then climbs for the wheelhouse.

Steadfast, 02:10

Ambience: heater ticking, wind on glass

Salt streaks the windows. The chart table is spread with a survey chart and tide tables. A kettle steams on the gas ring.

On screen: Mara (seated, tea in hand, log open)

MARA Wind southwest four. Sea moderate. Anchor bearing zero-four-five to the light.

She writes in a square, careful hand, then runs the radio's scan.

Sound: radio static sweep

MARA Coast guard. Trawler on the bank. Weather, reading like a will.

The static thins. Then, at 02:14, something clean.

Sound: five-second pulse — three rising tones, one flat

Mara goes very still.

MARA No speech. No beacon. Four tones, machine-cut.

She looks at the clock, then pulls the tide table across.

MARA High water, 02:11. That's three minutes after.

She writes: 02:14. Five seconds. 4.8 MHz. HW +3.

Steadfast, 08:36

The windows show flat black sea and a low grey sky. Mara has not slept; her tea is cold and untouched.

On screen: Mara (tired, pencil in hand, ruled paper covered in numbers)

MARA Nothing for six hours. Whatever that was, it isn't a chat.

Sound: five-second pulse — three rising tones, one flat

She drops her pencil, checks the clock, checks the table.

MARA 08:36. That's not three minutes after anything. Unless I'm counting wrong.

She murmurs the tones back, whistling softly, marking each gap.

MARA Tone, gap, tone, shorter gap, tone, longer gap, flat one. A full stop.

Close-up: ruled paper with the pulse drawn as gaps against a tide curve

The pulse's gaps sit over the tide's slow rise and fall, matched height to height.

MARA The gaps stand in the same relation as the water's heights. It's keeping the sea's time.

She squares the paper against the chart. Her hands slow.

MARA Dan said it. A signal has a hand behind it. Find the hand.

Steadfast, 08:52

On screen: Mara (standing, handset to her ear, jaw set)

Sound: radio crackle

SELLA (over radio, smooth) Steadfast, this is Rourke. You were due a noon report yesterday.

MARA Signal's intermittent. I'll need to go ashore.

SELLA (over radio) Then go ashore. But remember the secure-the-site clause. Anything you find, I hear first.

MARA Understood.

SELLA (over radio) Is there anything I should hear?

Mara looks at the ruled paper. A pause.

MARA No. Just a bad oscillator, probably.

SELLA (over radio) Report to me before you go ashore. I like to know where my people are.

The line clicks dead.

Hotel bar, St. John's — flashback, evening

Ambience: low piano, glasses, rain on windows

Warm lamplight on polished wood. Beyond the glass, harbour rain. Sella sits straight-backed, a folder and a pen before her, her tea untouched.

On screen: Sella (charcoal coat, gold pin, pale eyes steady), Mara (work jacket, wary)

SELLA The harbourmaster says you can take a dead engine apart in the dark.

MARA He's easily impressed.

SELLA I'm not.

She turns the folder to face Mara.

SELLA A decommissioned station, an active navigation beacon, an intermittent fault. Insurers won't reopen the eastern lane while it broadcasts nonsense. Silence it, certify it, the lane opens.

Mara reads, tea cooling beside her.

MARA Standard language so far. Charter, day rate, survey costs separate.

Then her eyes stop on a paragraph.

MARA Secure the site. All equipment secured. Report only to you. Discuss the site with no other party. Why?

SELLA A formality. One channel for the insurers.

MARA It's not standard.

SELLA It's not a standard job. There are losses outstanding. No loose ends.

She closes the folder, and the interview with it.

SELLA You have a reputation for being thorough, Ms. Kell. Be thorough.

St. John's, waterfront — flashback, rain easing

On screen: Mara (coat collar up, contract folded inside, uneasy)

Rain drips from the lamp posts. Harbour lights wobble in black water.

MARA It's a job. Jobs want doing.

She walks, and does not look convinced.

Steadfast, boat deck — morning

On screen: Mara (oilskin on, lantern clipped to her belt, doubtful)

Wind pulls at her jacket. The dinghy lies on its chocks; she checks the oars and painter.

MARA A beacon with a year-old fault and a power draw that doesn't match its lamp doesn't have a fault. It has a second load.

She looks out at the island — black rock, low roofs, the mast catching the first light.

MARA Whatever made that signal is not a fault. Nobody pays this well to silence a fault.

She lowers the dinghy.

Picture: Mara Kell rowing the small dinghy from the anchored Steadfast toward the black tiers of Little Farrow, wind chopping the grey water, the island ahead and the tug behind

MARA I didn't report. There'll be a price for the delay. Not yet.

The sea pushes back against the oars, patient and heavy.

MARA Somebody built that pulse. Somebody's watching me look at it.
