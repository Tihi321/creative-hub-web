The Recorder

The wreck site

Ambience: low diesel mutter, swell slapping the skiff, wind pulling at the line

The skiff holds her nose into the swell, running lights dark. A hundred metres off, the Petrel's cabin light burns in the black.

On screen: Mara (dive suit half on, hood back, checking the watch on her wrist), Idris (awkward in his father's suit, boots wrong round his shins)

Mara runs her thumb down the wet slate of the plan and reads it twice.

MARA One line down, one line back, both clipped to the stern cleats. Twelve minutes on the bottom. No going inside past the hatch.

IDRIS Twelve minutes.

MARA And no more. Past that, the whole thing comes apart and I'm not paying for it in a wheelchair.

He nods too many times and stops himself.

IDRIS He'd have known the regulator's house is the deepest cupboard on the island. He'd have known the sea would keep a thing if you put it where the sea lives.

MARA That's your father. A man who builds a lock that reads the tide knows where to leave the rest.

She pulls her hood up and looks at him hard.

MARA Get the box up first. Worries after.

They kit in the dark with the lamps off.

The wreck site

Black water closes over them. Ten metres down the wind thins to a hiss; thirty, and it is gone, and the beam of the lamp finds drifting matter and blue going grey and grey going colder.

On screen: Mara (reg in, lamp on the line, one hand checking her watch), Idris (ahead on the line, lamp swinging slow)

At sixty metres the cold gets into her hands. At seventy it gets into her head.

MARA (into her reg, slow) Line. Breath. Line. Breath. Name's Mara Kell. Depth's the depth.

The wreck comes up out of the dark, broken grey litter, and then the beam finds the long line of the hull and she is a ship.

She lies over on her port side, sand drifted to her rail, and six metres from the stern the plate opens inward in petals of rusted grey.

Close-up: Mara's slate, one word scratched in white: CUT.

Sound: the hull giving a soft, deep tick

Idris reads the slate and puts his palm flat on the hull, and the shudder runs down the line to her hand.

MARA (muffled, into the regulator) Weather works a seam and argues with it slowly. This was a charge against the plating. A shape held and fired.

They swim in through a gap where a hatch has burst. Idris's lamp finds a locker door swinging and a white board with a name printed on it.

Close-up: the locker board, a man's hand in grey ink across the corner: Dan Kell

Mara looks at the name and says nothing.

MARA (quiet, to no one) You wrote your name so the crew would know a locker was yours.

Idris tugs her sleeve. A pocket of steel has folded and landed upright and kept what was inside.

Sound: a bracket parting with a crack

The orange casing shows first, then dark plastic and the steel of the cradle bracket. It comes free more easily than it should, and for an instant the full weight hangs off one hand and the bottom is suddenly underneath her.

She gets her regulator in and is moving up before she has decided to.

The wreck site

Ambience: sea on a small hull, wind in the lamp

The skiff's grey hull comes up over them and they break the surface into cold and wind.

On screen: Mara (mask up, reg out, hair flat to her skull), Idris (face white, hauling at the gunwale)

Mara shoves the recorder over the side and makes a pair of breaths sound like a decision.

IDRIS We did it.

MARA Get aboard before the sea hears you boast.

It takes both of them to heave the capsule in. The orange casing is pitted, the label lifted at one corner, the seals intact. Idris wipes his hands twice on his suit and lays them flat and still.

IDRIS She'll put a diver in at first light and find the cradle empty. She'll know who took what.

MARA Then she'll have to work out where it went. Open it.

The access cover comes off with a quarter turn. Inside, a data capsule in a foam bed, no bigger than a thermos, the battery still seated.

The wreck site

Ambience: wind in the lamp, the skiff knocking against slack water

The station's radio sits on the boards between them, cabling coiled out to the playback.

On screen: Mara (crouched over the radio, hands on the gunwale), Idris (feeding the cable, eyes on the capsule)

Sound: a wash of engine room audio — pumps, a man's voice marking the hour

MARA Hours of it. The ship's own beat behind a man giving the watch.

IDRIS Where does she take water? That's the question. Run it.

The machine runs. Zero four hundred, all well. The sea goes on. A man says something about a pump.

Sound: a hammer on a bell, then another, pumps thudding, a voice breaking across

A man who had been calm says where it is, and says it fast. A new voice cuts across the bridge, young and not calm.

A VOICE ON THE TAPE (thin, urgent) It's down. It's in and it's down. Take her to make water where the weather can be blamed.

Mara's hand goes flat on the deck. Salt blows into her eyes and she does not close them.

IDRIS That's not weather.

MARA No.

The tape goes on, orders given and not given, men doing their jobs in a hull condemned from underneath.

IDRIS There's another channel. Someone's left a handset keyed open.

Sound: breathing in a filling space, a pump winding down to nothing

DAN (on the tape, calm) If anyone picks this up, it's Dan Kell off the Amity Rose. It's not the weather, whatever the report says.

DAN (on the tape) They put us under and I saw the men who did it. My ship knows the machine that was in the number and knows the hull that put it there.

Mara's fingers dig into the gunwale.

DAN (on the tape) I'm not the only one who saw. One of us is dead already for saying so, and the man who did it has the office's ear.

DAN (on the tape) So it has to come off the water, and it has to come off alive, and it comes with two of us behind it. Two of us or none. The box comes up with whoever comes for me. If I'm not there, she'll need the rest of you.

The recording runs into the sound of water, and then into nothing, and stops.

On screen: Mara (still, face wet, one hand on the radio), Idris (bent over the set, not looking up)

MARA It was Dan. I told you the transmitter was my brother's and I was wrong. My brother wrote the message.

She takes a breath and makes it count.

MARA He found out what was done to his ship and wrote it into a machine and gave it to a man he trusted. And that man built a lock and made it last twelve years.

IDRIS The cradle, Mara. The bracket. The box came down with the ship.

IDRIS (quieter) My father's workshop has the jig in it yet. Sheet steel, exactly that size, and I found it after he died and I never knew what it was for.

MARA Keep going. The whole of it.

IDRIS The packet came up with the papers twelve years ago. He came home with it and would not say where it had been. He went to the bench and he made a mount, and he put the box where the sea could hold it, and he never told me why.

IDRIS And then he went up to the light and gave them the name, and they gave him a contract and sent him home, and he died for it. And I sat on it because I was frightened and nineteen.

IDRIS I'm telling you the whole of it now because you'll be sending this out. I don't want one thing kept back. Not one.

MARA He didn't keep it back. He put it where it could be found.

She folds the playback cable over her arm.

MARA Get us in to the station. Lamp off the recorder. Don't look at the Petrel.

The west slip

Ambience: wind off the rock, water lapping at wet steps

The skiff comes in past the Petrel's quarter with the engine low and the recorder under a fold of canvas. Two men at the rail in the spill of light see her; one turns and goes into the cabin.

On screen: Mara (at the tiller, canvas half back), Idris (leaping for the steps with the line), Reeve (coming down the slip with a lamp, a cutting rod in his right hand), Sella (buttoned coat, hat in place, coming last)

Sound: chain rattling on concrete

REEVE Hands off the cargo.

Reeve comes down first with the lamp and the rod. Two more behind him. Sella comes last and looks down into the skiff, and surprise is not in her face.

SELLA You used a launch I believed to be chained, and you went to a wreck I believe to be nobody's business.

SELLA Give me the capsule.

MARA It's evidence. Of a crime you're standing in the middle of.

SELLA No. It's property.

SELLA It's on a wreck in a channel surveyed and closed at the insurers' expense, which means every scrap of it belongs to a claim. That is the legal opposite of evidence.

SELLA It is the fact of a matter having been settled. Every hour a thing like this exists unsettled is an hour somebody has to explain. Reeve.

Reeve has a bag open on the concrete. He comes down to the gunwale and puts out his hand.

On screen: Idris (hands loose at his sides, looking at Mara), Mara (looking at Idris, three ways, none of them good)

SELLA Hand it up. There's no version of this night where you don't.

Mara breathes out. She lifts the recorder with both hands and lays it in Reeve's arms, and the weight comes off her fingers like a hole.

Reeve sets it in the bag. The other man zips it and carries it up the steps, and the plastic makes a flat sound on the wet concrete and goes away into the dark.

On screen: Sella (down to the bottom step, water not touching her boots)

SELLA Forty-eight hours. I told you once. I won't tell you a third time.

She turns and goes up the slip after her men. Out in the dark, her boat's engine starts.

Idris lets out a breath he has been holding since the bottom.

MARA Leave the skiff. Get the tide table and your coat and go up to the regulator's house.

IDRIS The vault's been pumped, and she's got the station watched.

MARA Then we go the way the water goes.

MARA There's a second door on the regulator's house and a cut in the concrete the station drawings call the flood hatch. I read it in Sella's own plans and never once looked past it. The vault's not the only way under this rock.

She looks after Sella's boat and the bell buoy tonguing out on the Sound.

MARA She's got the mountain in a bag. She hasn't got the mountain. The transmitter's still wired to the batteries, and the batteries don't care who's holding the case.

MARA Dan said take it out on the air, and don't do it alone. We've got one thing she hasn't counted on. The storm.

Picture: Mara Kell climbing the wet steps of the west slip at night with Idris Vane a step behind her, the empty skiff knocked against the bollards below, Sella Rourke's boat showing one light going out into the dark Sound

IDRIS Mara. Two of us or none was the last thing he said.

MARA I heard it. Now move.
