The Clause

The weather station, evening

Ambience: paraffin lamp hissing, wind working at the glass

A lamp sits on the desk with its wick leaning. The folder is squared to the edge of the wood and a pencil lies parallel to it. A man stands at the door with his hands folded.

On screen: Sella (charcoal coat still buttoned, silver bob neat, pale eyes flat), Mara (dive suit dried stiff, hair flat, hands curled over the chair back), Reeve (at the door, shaved head, patient)

SELLA You'll want dry clothes.

MARA I want my boat back. I want my crew paid off. I want my name off your sheet.

SELLA The Steadfast is chartered to my company until the survey closes. You'll have her. What you won't have, Ms. Kell, is a clean invoice. You broke the terms you signed.

She opens the folder and lays one typed sheet on the desk. The pen is already uncapped beside it.

SELLA Sit down. This is a conversation. It will be shorter if we both behave like professionals.

Mara sits. The chair takes her badly. Her knees are still cold from the dive.

SELLA Addendum to the salvage agreement. It acknowledges that all materials recovered from the site and adjacent waters belong to the claim, that you took possession in good faith and surrendered them intact, and that you cannot attest to the wreck's condition, having dived without survey authorisation.

Close-up: a typed addendum, single-spaced, a signature line at the foot

SELLA Sign it, and the launch goes back to being a launch in the yard. The trespass goes away. The unauthorised salvage goes away. You finish the survey, you cut the circuit, you take your money home.

MARA And I'm the one who says under my hand that I don't know what was in the hull.

SELLA You say you can't attest. You're not lying. Any honest engineer would say the same, in the dark, at ninety metres, without authorisation.

SELLA This is what the secure-the-site clause was always for. Findings through one channel. No discussion with any other party. You're not being asked to lie. You're being asked to file.

Sound: the lamp wick ticking

Mara looks at the pen. Her hand stays where it is on the chair back.

MARA No.

SELLA That's the wrong answer, and it's not your last one.

MARA It's my answer.

Sella sits back, looks past her at the man in the door, and nods once. The man goes out into the passage.

The weather station, evening

Ambience: wind at the eaves, a pencil tapping wood

Sella counts on the desk with the end of the pencil, tap, tap, tap, as if the list were a survey.

On screen: Sella (pencil down, voice level), Mara (sitting forward now, hands flat on her knees)

SELLA Trespass. Theft. Interference with salvage under claim. In the morning I can put a call out on the emergency channel.

SELLA Little Farrow has no constable. St. John's has a good many, and a cutter, and an interest. It's storm season. A day to get here, a day to take you back.

SELLA In those two days a great deal of equipment can be properly secured.

MARA You'd have me watched by a man with a checklist and call it law.

SELLA I'd call it a file. You're not a criminal, Ms. Kell. You're tired and you're grieving and you've decided it's all one thing.

SELLA Sign, and I will keep it from being one thing.

The door opens and Idris comes in with a hand at each shoulder and his coat half off one arm.

On screen: Idris (jaw set, cheek red, coat dragging), Reeve (behind him, hands firm but not rough), Mara (turning in her chair)

IDRIS That paper's a confession.

SELLA Mr. Vane. You're not a party to this contract. Sit down and be quiet, or stand there and be quiet.

IDRIS You can't hold a man in a room and call it quiet.

SELLA Reeve.

Reeve puts Idris in the second chair the way a man moves a table. No force in it. Idris's hands stay still, and a bright line of anger runs under the red on his cheek.

IDRIS What did they pay you for, Mrs. Rourke? For the burying. Whose number was on it?

Sella looks at him a moment, then looks back at Mara.

SELLA You want to know what's on the tape. I don't have to guess. I've heard what there is to hear.

She draws the folder an inch toward her own side.

SELLA There's a ship named. A man's voice giving an order. Enough of one night to sink this survey division and the claim with it.

SELLA It says the sinking was no weather. It says other things.

On screen: Sella (eyes coming up, steady), Mara (very still, the chair forgotten)

SELLA I've known what was on that tape since before it was raised. Take that how you like.

Mara says nothing. Her mind runs the times: four this morning in her own hands, five at the slip in Reeve's bag, no machine between.

MARA Before it was raised.

SELLA My employers paid for two things in this matter. The first was a channel kept closed. It stayed closed three years, because a beacon made a bad noise and no insurer would risk a hull on a bad noise.

SELLA You were hired to make the noise stop. That was the job. All of it. Then it was not.

MARA And the second thing they paid for?

Sella takes her time with it.

SELLA To make sure the first thing stayed done.

SELLA There were three of us on the sealing. I was one of them. Two of the three are dead, and neither death was a storm.

SELLA So don't tell me about the cost of the truth. The cost of the truth is on my invoice, and I am the one of us still standing to carry it with a clean signature.

Mara turns it over once, slowly. Three on the sealing, Sella counted among them, two of the three dead and no weather report to cover them. And a man on this island dead twelve years, who built a lock to keep a tape.

MARA Then she's the third still standing. And Alun Vane makes four who knew.

SELLA The keeper knew what he was given. He knew what it would cost, and he took it, and he paid it. The weather is very useful. It explains so much, and it never answers back.

Reeve shifts his feet. Idris says nothing at all, which is worse than anything he could say.

The weather station, evening

Ambience: wind rising, the window singing in its frame

Sella pushes the sheet a little toward Mara and lays the pen square across it.

On screen: Sella (waiting, hat on now), Mara (looking at the page, not touching it), Idris (held in the second chair, watching her)

MARA I'd want to read it again. In the morning. With the light.

SELLA You have the light now.

MARA You've been up as long as I have, and I've been ninety metres down. I'm not signing a page my hands can't read because there's a man on the door.

MARA If the answer's no, you get it in the morning. If it's yes, you get it in the morning. I want it in daylight and I want my boat's papers in my hand.

Sella considers her the way a gauge gets read twice.

SELLA Twenty-four hours. Six tomorrow evening I call the cutter or I don't.

SELLA You stay on this island until I have your answer. You do not go near the vault, the regulator's house, or the beacon circuit. You do not use a radio except on the survey channel.

SELLA And Idris Vane stays with my crew. For his own safety, and yours. A man who keeps old radios is a man who talks, and I'd rather he didn't.

Idris stands up too fast. The chair goes back, and Reeve has him by the arm and shoulder before he has taken a step.

On screen: Idris (pulled round, coat dragging, eyes on Mara), Reeve (holding him, face flat), Sella (at the desk, immovable)

IDRIS Mara. Don't you dare sign that page.

IDRIS My father died so a woman like her would have to do a thing like this out in the open.

Reeve turns him toward the passage. Idris turns his head back, very clear into the room.

IDRIS The regulator's house is the deepest cupboard on the island. The deep cupboards keep what they're given. That's all I'll say. That's all.

Reeve takes him out. The door stays open and the wind comes in and the lamp leans over on its wick.

Sella settles her hat with two fingers and looks down at Mara.

SELLA You should have taken the money. I'd have slept well.

MARA You wouldn't.

SELLA No. But I'd have slept.

She stands in the doorway with the dark behind her.

SELLA Six tomorrow. Bring the pen. Bring the page signed, and we'll both forget the night.

SELLA That's the whole of the offer, and it will not be made again.

The door shuts on the lamp and the wind. Mara sits in the empty room with her hands in her lap.

MARA Three on the sealing. Two dead. And she's the third still standing.

Steadfast, midnight

Ambience: sea against the hull, wind in the rigging

Sella's man in the storm coat stands at the rail with his collar up. Mara has been let aboard with a lamp and a man behind her, and the Sound is building out of the southwest.

On screen: Mara (oilskin over the stiff dive suit, log under her arm, eyes on the glass), Pavel (at the rail, broad back turned, cropped grey head)

Mara keeps her eyes on the gauges. They tell her the same story in the same order.

MARA Sea running high under the chop. Barometer down eleven since the dive, still falling. Wind not at its quarter yet.

MARA Cloud low and ragged with a shelf to it. That's storm before dawn, and a big one by evening.

She goes below and sets the barograph. The drum turns, the pen draws its slow descent.

Close-up: the barograph drum, the pen drawing a long slope down the paper

MARA The cutter's a day out at best. Two days in a storm, or it won't come at all and she'll have to find another way to make me afraid.

MARA She has the recorder in a bag aboard the Petrel. One bag against the whole weather. A launch can lose that bag over deep water before the cutter's past the cape.

MARA And she has Idris, and a man on my deck, and words that were decided before I ever sat down.

She opens her log to the back and writes nothing. The book has ears now.

MARA She made one error, and it's sitting in the middle of the room like a bucket of water. She said the second payment, and she said it to a woman whose trade is knowing how a system fails.

MARA The regulator's house. The second door. The cut in the concrete the drawings call a flood hatch. A hatch like that has a ring pull and a state it's in, and I can find the state in the dark.

MARA The vault's pumped and the transmitter's still bolted to its plinth, wired to the battery bank. The battery bank doesn't consult a folder.

MARA I don't need to be at that vault. I need to be under it.

She goes up on deck. The man at the rail has not moved.

MARA The storm heaps over the islands and turns me from a problem into a length of static. Static is a wall in front of every cutter and every call she can make. And it cuts both ways.

Pavel turns his head a fraction, then back to the water.

MARA Bad night for a launch. Bad night for a man with a bag. Bad night to be standing on a heaving deck with a case you need to be quit of.

Off to the northeast the sky is blacker than the sea. The barometer has stopped being a gauge and become a countdown.

MARA Six tomorrow. I have a night, a storm, a hatch, and no one I can trust with the words.

She looks at the dark hill and the small shape of the station beneath it.

MARA Deep cupboards keep what they're given. All right.

She says it to the barograph drum, and the wind takes it.

MARA Twenty-four hours.

The man at the rail turns his collar up and says nothing, and the lamp in the station office goes out.
