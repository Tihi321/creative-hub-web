No Hands on His Watch

Interview room, police judiciaire, rue du Bastion. Monday, a quarter past twelve.

A clean white room: a table, three chairs, a camera in the corner. The door has a card reader and no keyhole. Dec eyes it like a personal insult.

The door clicks. Pichon enters with a tray: bubbling cassoulet, half a baguette, a glass of red, a folder.

On screen: Dec (slumped in a plastic chair, hungry, worried), Pichon (napkin tucked in his collar, reading glasses on, neutral)

PICHON (neutral) You don't mind. If I don't eat at twelve, I become unjust. My wife says so. She is right about most things.

He breaks the crust with a spoon. Steam rises: duck fat, garlic, beans. Dec has not eaten since a croissant at seven.

PICHON (neutral) Your landlord says you are polite and you sing in the shower. Your bank says less. Tell me about the back door.

DEC (thinking) Scratches all go in. A pro doesn't rake a lock, he reads it. So the scratches are for show. And it's locked with a thumb-turn, from inside. A door telling you a story, and the story's wrong.

PICHON What story should it tell?

DEC (thinking) That somebody came in the front, easy as you like. Then fitted a cheap new lock, picked it badly from outside, and locked it again from in.

DEC (surprised) That's mad. Who changes a lock in the middle of the night to pretend they broke it?

PICHON (neutral) Someone who wishes the police to find a burglar. And go home.

PICHON And the drawing?

DEC The pendulum was hanging wrong. Held off to one side by a bit of brass. Someone wound it, set it to nine, jammed it, and signed the case.

Pichon puts down his spoon and slides three photographs across the table.

Three chalk clocks with IX at the top: on dark wood beside a maker's plate, on the white plastic of a kitchen clock, on the iron beam of a church mechanism.

PICHON (sad) October, Judge Delorme. January, Madame Aubry, a bookseller. February, a curate at Saint-Médard.

PICHON (neutral) Every clock at nine. A little burglary, nothing taken. All three said everything tasted of cloves. The doctors told them to drink less.

PICHON (thinking) The judge drinks nothing at all.

PICHON (neutral) None of this was in the papers. Not the chalk. Not the cloves. So when a man tells me both before I ask, Monsieur Hartley, I become interested in that man.

A young officer puts his head round the door: a woman downstairs has something of the prisoner's, and says the lobby coffee machine is a crime and should be arrested.

PICHON (neutral) Friend of yours?

DEC I don't have friends in Paris. I've a landlord who likes my singing.

On screen: Dec (surprised), Pichon (eating, neutral), Inès (trench coat, mustard scarf, inspecting the room and finding it wanting)

INÈS (neutral) Commissaire Pichon. Inès Varga, paintings restorer, Atelier Morel. This fell out of his pocket outside the clock shop.

She sets the brass gear on the table.

DEC (happy) Oh, thanks. That's the pattern piece. It came with the parts list.

PICHON (thinking) Who sent it?

INÈS (thinking) Who sent it?

They say it together, and look at each other with mutual irritation.

DEC The Fondation Vaulmont. My client. It's their logo, isn't it, stamped on there...

Inès leans close to the photograph of the judge's clock and pushes her glasses up with one knuckle.

INÈS (worried) I've seen this mark. This morning. Under the varnish of a painting from about sixteen fifty.

INÈS (worried) A clock on a sundial, painted out. A Latin line under it about a loan and the ninth hour. And one word scraped away with a knife.

INÈS (neutral) I have photographs.

Pichon takes off his glasses and pinches the bridge of his nose. For the first time today he looks his age, and a little more.

PICHON (sad) Of course you do.

The steps of the police judiciaire. Twenty past two, fine drizzle.

Glass and pale stone beside the ring road. A drizzle that does not fall so much as hang about.

On screen: Pichon (buttoning his raincoat, almost fatherly), Dec (released, hands in pockets), Inès (winding her scarf, neutral)

PICHON (neutral) Monsieur Hartley, I have nothing against you but bad luck and a bag of little knives. Neither is yet a crime in France.

PICHON (neutral) Madame Varga, I'll keep the gear. You will send me your photographs.

PICHON (sad) Then go home, both of you. Mend your painting. Build your rooms. If I find you in the fifth case, I'll eat a very large lunch and put you in a cell.

DEC (surprised) There's a fifth?

PICHON (neutral) There is always a fifth.

He goes back inside.

On screen: Dec (awkward, worried), Inès (looking at the ring road as if it has disappointed her)

DEC Thanks. For coming. You didn't have to.

INÈS (neutral) I didn't come for you. I came for the mark.

DEC (worried) It's not as if we're, you know. Partners or anything.

INÈS (neutral) No. We are not partners.

DEC (happy) Grand. Glad we sorted that.

INÈS Good.

DEC Good.

They stand in the drizzle, not being partners. A bus goes by.

DEC (thinking) What did it say? The Latin.

INÈS (thinking) Mutuo datum. Given as a loan. Then the word someone took a knife to. Then hora nona. At the ninth hour.

Dec's phone rings. An unknown Paris number.

FERRAND (pleasant) Monsieur Hartley? Mademoiselle Ferrand, Fondation Vaulmont. We heard about this morning's unpleasantness at Monsieur Roux's. We do hope you are all right.

FERRAND (neutral) The clockmaker's cell must be installed by the thirty-first. The vigil is on the first of April, the day of the eclipse. Nothing about it can move. You understand.

DEC (worried) Yes. Of course.

FERRAND (pleasant) Wonderful. Monsieur de Vaulmont sends his regards.

The line goes dead. Inès is watching him, drizzle beading on her glasses.

INÈS (thinking) Who was that?

DEC (scared) My client. The ones with the logo. They knew I'd been arrested. Before I'd told a soul.

The staircase of Atelier Morel. Tuesday, ten to eight.

A dim stone stairwell. On the bottom step, a tall untidy man with two paper cups and a newspaper, like a large dog told to wait.

On screen: Dec (standing too fast, slopping coffee, happy), Inès (trench coat, wary)

DEC (happy) I brought coffee. To say thanks, properly. One black, one with everything. Have whichever.

INÈS (thinking) How did you find me?

He holds out the black one. She has not said which. He has guessed.

DEC (worried) Also I've two things to show you. One thing and a bad thing.

He hands her the paper, folded to page four: a photograph of the Seine and a police launch.

INÈS (sad) Notary found dead near the Pont Marie. Maître Gérard Lassalle. A heart attack, they think. He fell in.

INÈS (worried) He was the broker for my painting. He telephoned Morel on Saturday to hurry us.

DEC (thinking) Thought he might be. That's the other thing.

He shows her a creased brown envelope. In the corner, in small capitals: Étude Lassalle, Notaires. 18, quai d'Orléans.

DEC (worried) The Fondation's parts list came from his office. Who reads the back of an envelope?

INÈS (neutral) I do.

DEC (happy) Well, you would.

DEC (worried) So the man who sent me the gear and the man who sent you the painting is the same man. And he's in the river. The day the clocks stopped.

INÈS (thinking) I'm going to the quai d'Orléans.

DEC Grand. I'll come with you.

INÈS (angry) I didn't ask you.

DEC (happy) No. But I've a good feeling about this door.

Lassalle's office, 18 quai d'Orléans, Île Saint-Louis. Tuesday morning.

The dark green carriage door stands an inch open. Upstairs, the office door is wide.

Inside, the room has been searched square by square: books laid open in rows, every file drawer pulled to the same length, pictures turned to the wall with their backing slit.

On screen: Inès (in the doorway, scared), Dec (stepping between the open books, thinking)

INÈS (scared) Patient. Careful. Like the chalk.

DEC (thinking) Somebody's been looking for something. And they didn't find it.

DEC (thinking) They've done the drawers of that desk, but not the desk. They don't know what it is.

A heavy mahogany bureau, its leather top worn pale by forty years of elbows, its pigeonholes pulled out and stacked on the floor.

DEC (happy) Second Empire. Made by the hundred for notaries, and every one has a secret drawer in the same place.

He reaches into the middle pigeonhole up to his elbow, frowning at the ceiling. A click. A slim panel drops forward on a hidden hinge.

In the shallow drawer lie three things.

A heavy silver pocket watch on a short chain. White enamel face, twelve numerals, and no hands at all, not even a post where hands would go.

A cream card: the Fondation Vaulmont requests your presence at the Eclipse Vigil. The Panthéon. The first of April. Fifteen hundred precisely.

A torn scrap in a cramped old hand: Barrière d'Enfer. La neuvième niche.

DEC (worried) The gate of hell. The ninth niche. That's cheerful.

INÈS (thinking) The Barrière d'Enfer was an old toll gate. At Denfert-Rochereau. Where the catacombs are.

DEC (scared) Oh, that's cheerful.

Inès takes the watch and finds the catch on its back with her thumb.

A single floorboard creaks on the landing.

On screen: Inès (watch in hand, scared), Dec (scared), Kessler (the bald man: huge, bald, black wool coat, cuckoo-clock tattoo on his neck, neutral)

He fills the doorway. Small pale eyes go to the open drawer, then to the watch in Inès's hand.

He says nothing. He simply comes into the room.

DEC (scared) Right. Okay. Window.

The roofs of the Île Saint-Louis. Moments later.

Wet grey zinc, chimney pots, a satellite dish, a pot of dead geraniums. The Seine forty feet down on one side, a courtyard of bins on the other.

On screen: Dec (holding Inès's hand, scrambling along the ridge, scared), Inès (on hands and knees, scared)

DEC (worried) Don't look down.

INÈS (scared) Basszus.

She has looked down.

The zinc booms behind them under a great weight. The bald man does not run. He walks. He does not need to hurry.

The ridge ends at a light well, a square shaft dropping past drainpipes to a tiny yard. On the far side, a lower roof with a skylight propped open on a stick.

Dec jumps it, long-legged, skids, catches the skylight frame, and turns with both hands out.

DEC (worried) Come on.

INÈS (scared) No.

INÈS (scared) Not over a hole! I'm afraid of heights. I clean skies. I don't do this.

On screen: Inès (at the edge, scared), Kessler (crouched by the gutter, hands cupped, neutral)

Three metres back, the bald man has stopped. A grey pigeon is caught by one foot in the anti-bird netting, beating itself against the zinc.

He folds its wings with both huge hands, slowly, the way you lift a sleeping child, and works the net off its foot with one thick finger.

He opens his hands. The pigeon clatters up past Inès's face.

And Inès, without deciding to, jumps.

Dec catches her by both wrists. They tumble against the skylight, and it bangs shut on Dec's elbow.

DEC (angry) Ah, Jesus, my whole arm, who props a window on a stick...

Across the gap, the bald man looks at the drop, and at them. He does not jump. He takes out a phone and walks slowly back the way he came.

The Pont de Sully. Late morning.

The middle of a stone and iron bridge, the grey-green Seine below, gulls, the pale houses of the island behind.

On screen: Dec (holding the parapet, out of breath, grinning), Inès (wiping her glasses on her scarf, hands shaking, neutral)

DEC (happy) You jumped.

INÈS Yes.

DEC You said you couldn't.

INÈS (neutral) I couldn't. And then I did. Don't make a thing of it.

DEC (happy) I'm not. It's a tiny thing. It was a metre.

INÈS (angry) It was a metre and twenty.

DEC (happy) I'll grant you the twenty.

She opens her other hand. The watch chain has printed little links into her palm. She never let go of it.

She presses the catch. The back springs open.

No wheels, no spring, nothing to make it go. On the inner lid, engraved in the silver: nine tiny rings side by side, each one circled by the alphabet in letters smaller than grains of rice.

INÈS (thinking) Dec. What is this?

It is the first time she has used his name. He bends over her hand, and his face goes serious in a way she has not seen before.

DEC (worried) That is a lock.
