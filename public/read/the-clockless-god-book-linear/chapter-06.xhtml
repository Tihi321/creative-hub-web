The Grey Communion

Room four, Le Balancier. Saturday, four in the afternoon.

A small pine room under a huge white quilt. Roux's drawings spread across it. A cuckoo clock by the door, a travel clock on the bedside table, red geraniums on the windowsill.

A knock. No one in the corridor. Only a tray on the floor: white teapot, cup, two biscuits on a doily, a card. Avec les compliments de la maison, pour vous réchauffer.

On screen: Dec (alone, jumper and socks, carrying the tray in, happy)

DEC (happy) Ah, Madame Jobin. You're a dote.

He pours, adds milk, drinks half the cup in one go. Still cold from the tower.

Then he stops. Cup halfway to the saucer. Tongue moves round his mouth.

Cloves. Sweet and dusty under the tea. Behind them, something flat and cold. Wet stone.

DEC (scared) Cloves and slate. Like licking a church roof.

He sets the cup down. Hand shaking. Crosses to the windowsill in two strides. Sick into Madame Jobin's geraniums with great thoroughness.

DEC (thinking) Whoever left that tray will come back to check.

He pours the pot down the sink, keeps a finger's depth. Fills the cup halfway with water. Eats both biscuits. A man who drank the tea would have eaten the biscuits.

Then he lies on his side on the quilt, facing the door. One arm hanging off the bed. Eyes half closed.

The key turns.

On screen: Fabien (grey coat, Madame Jobin's iron key ring, canvas bag, calm as a nurse on a ward, neutral), Dec (sprawled on the bed, eyes half shut, scared)

Fabien looks into the cup. Then the teapot. Nods once to himself. Goes to work, quick and practised. Like a man laying a table he's laid many times.

Dec's digital watch: set to 09:00, back off with a tiny screwdriver, closed. The travel clock: hands to nine, a sliver of brass in its movement. He stands on a chair and jams the cuckoo clock at nine.

He picks up Dec's phone. Locked. Lifts Dec's limp hand, presses the thumb to the button.

Scrolls through the photographs of the black notebook. No change in his face. Deletes them. Deletes them again from the bin. Sets the phone's clock to nine.

Last, he opens the wardrobe. Chalks on the bare pine inside the door: a circle, twelve marks, IX at the top. Then he's gone. The key turns.

Dec is so angry he cannot feel his hands.

The forester's house above Les Bréviaires. Blue dusk.

A dark wooden house at the forest edge. Smoke from the chimney. A big black four-by-four in the snow. Woodpile. Hay barn.

Dec has slid down the porch roof into a snowdrift. Crept the length of the village behind cars, a woodpile, the church wall. In the German geologist's boots.

On screen: Dec (crouched behind a stack of logs, snow to his knees, scared), Fabien (by the woodpile, phone to his ear, respectful, neutral)

FABIEN (neutral) Yes, the Irishman. Half the pot at least. He'll sleep until tomorrow night.

FABIEN (neutral) The photographs are gone. I saw to it.

FABIEN (thinking) And Morel? He'll have the word by Sunday? Good. And then, as the rule says.

Behind the logs, Dec goes cold all over again.

FABIEN (neutral) The guests, yes. The minister. The prefect, confirmed.

FABIEN (neutral) Two bankers from the Bourse. The television journalist. The cardinal's secretary.

FABIEN (happy) The catering is ours. The wine at half past two, half an hour before None.

FABIEN (happy) They'll remember the eclipse, the dark, the clocks stopping, and your voice. Only that. On camera. A miracle.

FABIEN (happy) And the Seventh will collect. Nine minutes of every station and signal in Paris, and the markets in a panic.

FABIEN (worried) The fund's positions are... Forgive me. Not my concern.

FABIEN (happy) The train worked beautifully. Off at Saignelégier, Brother Kessler picked me up, and they walked straight in.

FABIEN (neutral) Mutuo datum, Brother.

Dec shifts to ease a cramp. The too-big boot slides off a frozen log. The stack groans. Three logs roll into the snow like somebody knocking at a door.

Fabien's head comes round.

The cheese cellar under the hay barn. Evening.

A low door, unlocked. Five stone steps down into a long vault, black and damp. Smell thick enough to lean on.

Tall round cheeses like little drums ripen in silence on wooden shelves from floor to ceiling.

On screen: Dec (crouched between two racks, scared)

A torch beam comes down the steps. Moves along the cheeses and the wet stone floor. Stops a metre from his knees, on his footprints. Then it goes away.

FABIEN (neutral) Rien. Cheese.

Above, a very large, very low grunt.

The door shuts. A wooden bar drops across it outside. The four-by-four coughs into life, its lights sweep the grating, and it goes away down the valley.

DEC (sad) Locked in. Again. Three times this week. Personal best.

The door has a bar outside and nothing inside: no handle, no keyhole.

Twenty minutes later an old engine clatters up the track, fan belt screaming. Boots crunch. The bar lifts, and Dec falls backwards out of the door into the snow.

On screen: Inès (torch in one hand, a ski pole in the other, neutral), Dec (flat on his back in the snow, surprised), Clothilde (at the wheel of a battered blue van marked Fromagerie Cattin, red ski jacket over her habit, cigarette, happy)

DEC (surprised) How did you...

INÈS (neutral) I went to your room to tell you about the page. Chalk in your wardrobe. Sick in the geraniums. Window open.

INÈS (neutral) Footprints from the window, up the street, to the logs, to here.

INÈS (neutral) The German geologist's boots. Your feet are very easy to follow. They're very big.

DEC (scared) They know we know, Inès. And they've wiped my phone.

CLOTHILDE (happy) Get in the van. Arguing in the snow is for Protestants.

In the back of the van, on empty cheese crates, careering down the switchbacks to Delémont and the night train, Dec tells her everything: the tea, the keys, the thumb, the call by the woodpile.

On screen: Inès (on a cheese crate, phone lit in the dark, thinking), Dec (beside her, worried)

INÈS (thinking) Mine isn't wiped. It died in the tower but kept what was on it.

INÈS (thinking) The REMIT page. The list of tests, Vincennes to Austerlitz.

INÈS (sad) Not the flyleaf. Not the Panthéon page. It's not enough. It's not nothing.

The van takes a hairpin. The crates slide. She slides against him. Stays.

DEC (scared) When he took my hand to open the phone... I've never been so frightened in my life.

She says three or four quick words in Hungarian.

DEC (thinking) What does that mean?

INÈS (neutral) Nothing. It's Hungarian. It doesn't count.

But her hand has found the sleeve of his jacket in the dark, and it holds on all the way down the mountain.

Atelier Morel. Sunday, ten past eleven in the morning.

Nobody works Sundays at Atelier Morel. The skylight is lit anyway.

On Inès's stool, in her apron, under her UV lamp and raking light, sits Morel. Scalpel in hand. Nose almost on the canvas. Scraping at the gap in the Latin line.

On screen: Morel (her apron over his worn cashmere, scalpel in hand, scared), Inès (in the doorway, bag of books, no sleep, angry)

INÈS (angry) Jean-Paul. Stop. You're taking off the ground.

He jumps so hard the scalpel skitters across the floor. His face is grey and wet and old.

MOREL (scared) Inès. You said a week. Your family.

INÈS (neutral) I lied. So did you.

He puts his face in his hands and cries quietly, the way a tap drips.

MOREL (sad) Twenty nineteen I opened a gallery. The partner went to Brazil. Then the virus. I owed three banks.

MOREL (sad) My wife still thinks the flat in Vincennes is ours.

MOREL (sad) Then Lassalle came. His client could buy my debts. All I had to do was small favours for a foundation.

INÈS (angry) Vaulmont.

He flinches like she hit him.

MOREL (sad) Last autumn they asked if I remembered my radio. As a boy I built long-wave transmitters in my father's shed.

MOREL (sad) They gave me figures. Power, frequency, range. A son et lumière, they said.

MOREL (sad) I checked them. I wrote my name in the front of the notebook like a schoolboy, because it was a nice notebook.

MOREL (sad) I didn't ask. En somme, that's what I was paid for. Not to ask.

INÈS (thinking) And the painting?

MOREL (worried) A word under the varnish, for their ceremony on the first. I'm not good enough, Inès. You know I'm not.

MOREL (worried) So I gave it to you. I thought nobody would be hurt.

INÈS (angry) Gérard Lassalle is in the river. Anselme Roux lost a day.

MOREL (scared) I know! On Friday a polite young man telephoned. He said Lassalle had been afraid at the end.

MOREL (scared) Fear is a debt like any other, he said. He hoped I wasn't afraid.

MOREL (scared) He'll expect the word by tonight. I can't see what you see. I never could.

Four floors down, the street bell rings. Long and polite and unhurried. Morel's face goes the colour of old paper.

Atelier Morel. Half past eleven.

Even footsteps on every one of the four flights. At the top, he is not out of breath.

On screen: Vaulmont (grey three-piece suit, silver hair swept back, silver chain across his waistcoat, calm, neutral), Inès (hands fisted in her apron pockets, scared), Morel (shrinking on his stool, scared)

VAULMONT (neutral) Madame Varga. Forgive me for coming on a Sunday. Auguste de Vaulmont.

The voice. Warm oil, poured slow. She heard it in the dark under the Barrière d'Enfer, saying Lassalle has been remitted.

VAULMONT (neutral) I've come for the Allegory. It's needed for the vigil on Wednesday.

VAULMONT (neutral) My people are downstairs with a van.

He stands before the easel, hands clasped behind him, and looks at the clean square of cold blue sky.

VAULMONT (happy) Beautiful. You have very good hands.

VAULMONT (happy) I've owned this picture thirty years and never knew the sky was that colour.

INÈS (surprised) You're the anonymous buyer.

INÈS (angry) It isn't finished.

VAULMONT (neutral) Nothing is ever finished. That is rather the point.

VAULMONT (thinking) You were away, I think. The air on the plateau is very clear in March. One can hear bells for miles.

Her fists close in her apron pockets.

VAULMONT (sad) I knew your father. Zoltán Varga. He drove the twenty-eight in Lyon, past the Hôtel-Dieu.

VAULMONT (sad) He sang in Hungarian at red lights. A very fine baritone.

VAULMONT (sad) To lose one's days like that, one by one, never knowing which.

VAULMONT (sad) Grief is a kind of interest, isn't it? We go on paying it long after the loan is gone.

VAULMONT (neutral) Your Monsieur Hartley is building us a charming room. I do hope nothing interrupts his contract.

VAULMONT (neutral) It's so hard for a young foreigner in Paris.

VAULMONT (neutral) And Jean-Paul's affairs, so complicated. And this atelier, mortgaged to a bank on whose board I sit.

VAULMONT (happy) We all hold a little of each other's time.

He opens the silver watch like any man checking the hour. Looks at a white face with no hands. Closes it.

VAULMONT (thinking) Did you find the word?

INÈS (neutral) No.

VAULMONT (neutral) No. I don't suppose you did.

At a quarter to twelve two men in grey overalls carry the blanket-wrapped painting down. By the van at the bottom of the stairs waits Kessler, who lifts it in as gently as if it were asleep.

On screen: Inès (alone before the empty easel, sad), Morel (behind her, scared)

She has her forty-one photographs on her phone, in the cloud, and on a memory stick in her coat. She has nine ticks, an R, an E, and REMIT. She does not have the painting.

MOREL (scared) What do I do, Inès?

INÈS (worried) Go home. Lock your door. Don't drink anything anyone gives you.

Morel's flat, Vincennes. Sunday, past eleven at night.

No answer to her calls at ten, at half past, at eleven. Dec is outside her building on a hired bicycle in nineteen minutes, which is not possible. The concierge lets them in, frightened and furious, in her dressing gown.

The third-floor door is not locked. A beige armchair, a lamp, and on the low table a cup with a finger of cold tea.

On the mantelpiece, the shelves, the oven, the cable box under the television: 09:00.

On screen: Morel (in his armchair, hands flat on the arms, staring at the wall, neutral), Inès (kneeling by the chair, holding his hand, scared), Dec (by the mantel clock, angry)

INÈS (scared) Jean-Paul. Jean-Paul, can you hear me?

MOREL (neutral) Sunday.

His pulse is slow and far away. When his mouth falls open, his tongue is grey, much darker than Roux's.

Dec is on the phone to the emergency services in his terrible French, spelling the address twice.

She holds the cold hand of the man who gave a restorer from Lyon with a Hungarian name a job when nobody else would, and who sold her to the Brotherhood because he was frightened.

INÈS (sad) Morel will have the word by Sunday. And then, as the rule says.

INÈS (thinking) It isn't anger. It's housekeeping. A debt that stopped being useful, written off.

INÈS (scared) And now I'm the only restorer they have left.
