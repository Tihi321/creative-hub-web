What the Varnish Kept

Pichon's office, police judiciaire, rue du Bastion. Monday 30 March, a quarter past twelve.

There are files on every surface and clock faces pinned to the board. On the desk sits an andouillette that smells like a farmyard that has been through something.

On screen: Pichon (napkin in his collar, chewing slowly, neutral), Dec (hands going as he talks, worried), Inès (very straight in her chair, neutral)

Dec tells him everything, from the chapel to the armchair in Vincennes. Pichon eats and listens with his eyes on Dec's face. He stops only once, at "the Seventh will collect", and lays his knife across the plate.

Inès puts her phone on the desk with a memory stick beside it. Pichon puts on his reading glasses and goes through the photographs.

PICHON (thinking) Gare d'Austerlitz. Seven fifty-one. Nine minutes.

PICHON (thinking) "Aucune réaction."

PICHON (sad) Morel is at the Hôtel-Dieu. He does not know his name.

PICHON (sad) Datura, a much larger dose than Roux had. He may come back. He may not.

PICHON (angry) And Lassalle. Water in the lungs. He was alive when he went into the Seine.

PICHON (angry) There are marks on both arms where two hands held him. Large hands.

PICHON (neutral) I told my superiors it was murder. In writing, on Saturday.

DEC (worried) And?

PICHON (neutral) On Sunday a very polite young man telephoned from the prefect's office.

PICHON (neutral) The prefect is confident it was a heart attack.

PICHON (neutral) The prefect is also a guest at Wednesday's vigil.

PICHON (neutral) And the foundation has complained about an Irishman trespassing on its property in Switzerland.

PICHON (sad) Your name is on a list now, Monsieur Hartley. Not my list. Theirs.

DEC (scared) So what happens now?

Pichon takes the napkin out of his collar, folds it, and stands.

PICHON (neutral) Now I arrest you.

He opens the door on twenty officers at twenty desks, and his voice goes loud and cold.

PICHON (angry) Declan Hartley, you are under arrest for obstruction. Stand up.

INÈS (angry) Commissaire, that is not...

PICHON (angry) Sit down, Madame Varga, or you will join him.

He walks Dec slowly across the middle of the floor. By the lifts, a man in a good suit with a coffee watches them pass and takes out his phone.

In the lift, Pichon lets go of Dec's arm. His face has gone grey and old.

The holding cell, basement. Ten to one.

The cell is clean and white, with a bench, a steel toilet and frosted glass. The steel door has a card reader and no keyhole.

On screen: Dec (on the bench, eyeing the lock, sad), Pichon (in the open doorway, hands in his raincoat pockets, neutral)

DEC (angry) I hate this building.

PICHON (neutral) Everybody does. It was designed by a man who had never been arrested.

PICHON (sad) I'm sorry for upstairs. The man with the coffee is from the prefect's office.

PICHON (sad) He telephoned your arrest from the lift lobby. I watched him.

PICHON (thinking) For a day, they will think I know which way the wind blows.

PICHON (thinking) On paper, you were questioned and released.

DEC (surprised) You believe us.

PICHON (neutral) Since the first day. Since the tongue.

PICHON (worried) But I have photographs of a ledger, and no ledger.

PICHON (worried) And on Wednesday the minister and a television crew are the guests of a man who gave eleven million to the hospitals of Paris.

PICHON (sad) If I go upstairs with a radio and a medieval monk, they give my chair to a man who likes his lunch less.

PICHON (sad) Then there is no one.

DEC (worried) So what do we do?

PICHON (neutral) We do nothing. I am a police officer. You are a prisoner.

He holds out a folded sheet.

PICHON (neutral) But a police officer reads many boring documents.

PICHON (neutral) Sometimes he leaves them lying about.

It is a photocopied works permit, dated 16 March, for Horlogerie Vaulmont Frères, Genève: a temporary eclipse observation apparatus in the upper gallery of the dome, near the anchorage point of the pendulum.

PICHON (thinking) Sixty-seven metres of cable from the crown of the dome.

PICHON (thinking) A fine thing to hang other things from. An aerial, for instance.

PICHON (neutral) I know nothing about radios. I know about lunch.

He checks his battered steel watch, the kind with hands.

PICHON (happy) Twelve fifty-one. There is tarte Tatin in the canteen, and I shall be some time.

PICHON (happy) Mind the door, Monsieur Hartley. It's heavy.

His shoes go away up the stairs, and the steel door stands wide open.

Dec looks at the card reader, at its faceplate, and at the chewing-gum wrapper in his pocket that he was saving for exactly this.

DEC (angry) That's not fair. That's not fair at all.

DEC (angry) I'd have had that.

The steps of the police judiciaire. Twenty past one.

Thin March sunshine lies on the glass. Inès waits at the bottom with her scarf wound three times round her neck, so still that she looks like a painting of herself.

On screen: Inès (coat buttoned to the throat, angry), Dec (coming down with the photocopy held up, happy)

INÈS (angry) He let you go.

DEC (happy) He let me go. And he gave me this.

INÈS (thinking) Near the anchorage of the pendulum.

INÈS (thinking) A sixty-seven-metre aerial, hanging in the middle of Paris.

DEC (neutral) That's what he said. Well. He said he knows nothing about radios.

INÈS (neutral) He knows everything about radios.

DEC (worried) I'm on their list. If they see me near the Panthéon on Wednesday, I'm done.

INÈS (sad) So you can't go.

His phone buzzes. He reads the email and starts to laugh helplessly, until she takes the phone out of his hand.

Dear Mr Hartley, we are delighted to confirm your Clockmaker's Cell for the Eclipse Vigil, in the north-east chapel of the crypt of the Panthéon. With Monsieur de Vaulmont's personal good wishes. Mlle C. Ferrand.

A white pass is attached. It has PANTHÉON across the top, Dec's photograph grinning like an idiot, and in red: ACCÈS TOUTES ZONES.

INÈS (surprised) The prefecture has you on a list. And Vaulmont gives you every room in the building.

DEC (thinking) He wants me where he can see me. In the cellar, in a little room I built myself, with a door that locks.

DEC (neutral) But nobody arrests a man on Vaulmont's own guest list.

DEC (neutral) His pass is the trap, and it's the door. Same thing.

DEC (happy) He thinks I'm a fella who builds rooms for other people to get out of.

DEC (happy) I am. I know every way out of that room. I drew them.

Atelier Morel. Monday, eleven at night.

The studio is dark except for the lamp on Inès's bench. On the old green sofa by the door, Dec sleeps with his boots on, talking.

On the bench lie photograph twenty-three, her notebook open at R/P/B _ E/L, the REMIT page on her phone, and Roux's drawings pinned flat by a pigment jar and a coffee cup.

On screen: Inès (bent over the drawings, pencil in hand, thinking), Dec (asleep on the sofa, blanket kicked off, happy)

DEC (happy) Ah, Mrs Kilbride, sure it's no trouble.

She hears her father's voice, at a red light in Lyon. Ne csak nézd. Lásd. Don't just look. See.

Sheet four shows the nine wheels in section. Beside each notched gate is a number in faded brown: XVII, IIII, XII, VIII.

Sheet three shows the same wheels from outside, each ringed with tiny letters. It is the old Latin alphabet, with no J and no W, and U and V the same letter.

INÈS (thinking) It isn't decoration. It's a table.

INÈS (thinking) The key, drawn right beside the lock.

Wheel one, gate seventeen. She counts round the ring with the pencil point, as carefully as stitches on a needle.

INÈS (surprised) R.

Wheel two, gate four, gives A, B, C, D, and that is wrong. Under the loupe, she sees that the second ring does not start at A. Each wheel begins on a different letter.

INÈS (thinking) B, C, D, E. You careful old man.

She counts every wheel and checks every letter twice: R, E, M, I, T, T, E, R. Eight wheels give eight letters.

The ninth gate has no number, only a blank square and, in Roux's furious biro, enveloppe scellée!!

She turns to the photograph. At the foot of the ninth ghost tick is the faint short stroke that could be an E or an L. No word ends in remitterl.

INÈS (surprised) REMITTERE.

INÈS (thinking) Mutuo datum remittere hora nona.

INÈS (thinking) What was given as a loan, to forgive at the ninth hour.

INÈS (sad) Not repaid. Forgiven.

INÈS (sad) Their own lock says forgive, and in six hundred years nobody counted round the letters.

She presses the heels of her hands to her eyes. Then she walks to the sofa and kicks it.

DEC (scared) Wha'. Mrs Kilbride. No. What?

INÈS (neutral) Get up. I've got the word.

DEC (surprised) That's mad. You did that with a pencil.

INÈS (neutral) Don't make a thing of it.

DEC (thinking) So if they had Roux cut his gates to these numbers, and the last one from the envelope...

INÈS (neutral) Then his copy opens with the same word.

DEC (worried) And I was going to pick it. Vey filed his gates by candlelight.

DEC (worried) Roux had a lathe and six weeks and a grudge. You couldn't get a thought between those gates.

DEC (worried) I'd be up that stair till Easter, in front of Vaulmont.

INÈS (neutral) Now you won't have to.

DEC (thinking) Remittere. Forgive.

INÈS (neutral) Forgive.

A café on the rue Mouffetard. Tuesday, nine in the morning.

The café has a zinc counter, marble tables and croissants. Across one table lies taped tracing paper in four inks: the streets of the fifth in grey, and under them, in black and red and blue, something like the roots of a tree.

On screen: La Taupe (woollen hat, bright yellow raincoat, happy), Inès (coffee in both hands, worried), Dec (leaning over the map, thinking)

LA TAUPE (happy) Soufflot finds the hill full of holes and nearly has a heart attack.

LA TAUPE (happy) He fills half of it. Not all of it.

LA TAUPE (happy) There's an inspection gallery under the rue Clotilde, right up under the crypt.

LA TAUPE (happy) At the end, a shaft. With a grille.

DEC (neutral) Of course there's a grille.

LA TAUPE (happy) Seventeen nineties, gorgeous. It comes up into a little room off the crypt.

LA TAUPE (happy) A chapel that isn't one any more.

DEC (surprised) The north-east chapel.

He shows them Mademoiselle Ferrand's email.

DEC (thinking) They're putting my escape room on a grille nobody's opened since the Revolution.

LA TAUPE (happy) Only one way to find out.

She folds the map and finishes Inès's croissant without asking.

The shaft under the Panthéon crypt. Late morning.

The gallery is so low that even Inès stoops. At its end, a round brick shaft goes straight up, with iron rungs rusted into its side.

Four metres up is a wrought-iron grille. Grey light shows through the bars, and across the top lie pale new planks with a timber merchant's stamp in blue ink.

On screen: La Taupe (above them on the rungs, headlamp blazing, happy), Inès (eyes on the rung in front of her, scared), Dec (squeezed beside her, face to the bars, surprised)

INÈS (scared) The whole Panthéon is on top of us. Is it safe?

LA TAUPE (happy) Safest place in Paris. It's not going anywhere. Probably.

Dec looks up at the planks and starts to laugh very quietly, his forehead against the iron.

DEC (happy) That's mine. That's the floor of the cell.

DEC (happy) My timber, from the yard in Montreuil. I'd know that stamp anywhere.

DEC (happy) They've built my room right on top of their own back door. And they don't know it's there.

INÈS (worried) Can you open it?

The grille has a big square box lock, eighteenth century and rusted nearly solid. He looks at it as if he has spotted an old friend in a crowd.

DEC (neutral) Tomorrow. From the top, when I'm inside.

He reaches through the bars and touches the underside of his own planks with his fingertips.

DEC (thinking) Nobody's going to need it. But I like to know there's a way out.

Atelier Morel. Tuesday afternoon.

North light falls on the bench: the map, the drawings, the permit, the pass, and the word written in pencil at the bottom of a page.

On screen: Inès (arms folded, leaning against the bench, neutral), Dec (across from her, worried)

INÈS (neutral) I told Mademoiselle Ferrand no restorer leaves a painting alone with a buffet.

INÈS (neutral) She answered in twelve minutes. "But of course. Monsieur de Vaulmont would be delighted."

DEC (worried) He wants you there too. Where he can see you.

INÈS (neutral) Yes.

DEC (worried) So. A word for a lock in the roof, a back door in the cellar, a minister, a radio.

DEC (worried) And a fella the size of a wardrobe on the stairs. And then what?

INÈS (neutral) Then we stop it.

DEC (surprised) How?

INÈS (thinking) I'll know when I see it. You look at the door. I look at the painting.

INÈS (thinking) We see what's wrong.

DEC (neutral) That's a terrible plan.

INÈS (neutral) Yes.

DEC (neutral) It's the worst plan I've ever heard.

INÈS (neutral) Yes.

DEC (happy) Grand. I'm in.

High over the Marais, a grey cloud slides south towards the dome. Behind it the sun, the one that goes out tomorrow at three, breaks through and lays a square of cold clean light across the word.
